What is AbarVa?

AbarVa is an AI program operating system. It helps executive teams and operations leaders run AI initiatives with the same discipline they bring to capital programs — structured phases, gate reviews, evidence-backed decisions, and a verified value trail from hypothesis to realized outcome.

Most AI programs fail for predictable reasons: no confirmed sponsor, no measurable baseline, scope that keeps expanding, governance left until it's too late. AbarVa's job is to catch these failure modes early — before they become expensive — and give every stakeholder a shared view of where the program stands.

One sentence version AbarVa is a calm intelligence layer that enforces evidence citation, governance gating, and provenance tracing on every program decision — so your AI investments actually deliver.

What AbarVa is not

  • Not a chat app. The chat interface is a means to an end — it structures your program, not just your conversation.
  • Not generic RAG. Every response is grounded in your tenant's data, program history, and cited evidence. Nothing is fabricated.
  • Not a project management tool. AbarVa manages program intelligence — the decisions, gates, evidence, and value trail — not tasks and timelines.

Core concepts

These four ideas underpin everything in AbarVa. Read them once and the rest of the product will make sense.

1 · Strategic Moves

An AI initiative is called a Strategic Move in AbarVa. A Move begins as an idea (P0 Originate) and ends when value is measured and verified (P5 Validate). Every Move has an owner, a hypothesis, a sponsor, and a value stake. AbarVa tracks all of these across the full lifecycle.

2 · Gate-driven phases

Moves advance through six phases. Each phase has gate criteria — specific, verifiable conditions that must be met before advancing. You cannot promote a Move by clicking a button; the gate criteria must be closed. This is deliberate: it prevents "false starts" where programs are promoted before they're ready.

3 · Evidence integrity

AbarVa enforces evidence labeling on every dollar figure and benchmark. An unverified estimate is always marked [UNVALIDATED_HYPOTHESIS]. A confirmed measurement is marked [MEASURED_RESULT]. This prevents the most common failure in AI programs: confusing projected ROI with realized value.

4 · The 10 failure modes

AbarVa's intelligence is built around the 10 failure modes that end AI programs — patterns derived from published research (Gartner, RAND, MIT/BCG, McKinsey). Nexus watches for signals of these failure modes in every conversation and surfaces them before they become crises. See the full reference below.

P0 · Originate

Origination is where a Strategic Move is born. You start with nothing more than an idea — a CEO note, an email thread, a problem statement — and end with a structured brief that Nexus helps you build through conversation.

2

P1 · Charter

The Charter phase converts the P0 brief into a formal program record with a confirmed sponsor, a measurable success metric, and a scoped delivery plan. Think of it as the "signing ceremony" — after Charter, the program is real.

Key decisions in P1

  • Confirm the sponsor. The sponsor candidate from P0 must be upgraded to a confirmed sponsor with explicit budget authority. Nexus flags FM-1 (no confirmed sponsor) until this is done.
  • Define the success metric. A KPI must be defined with a plan to measure the baseline. "Model accuracy" is not a valid business metric. Nexus flags FM-4 and FM-9 if the metric is output-only.
  • Lock the scope. What's in scope and out of scope must be formally recorded. Scope changes after this gate require a gate review.
  • Assess foundation readiness (F1–F4). Four criteria must be assessed before advancing: data access confirmed, sponsor confirmed, platform available, team capacity confirmed. Any red criteria block the gate.

P2 · Diagnose

Diagnose is the evidence-gathering phase. The hypothesis from P0 is tested against reality. Baseline measurements are established. Gate criteria are closed one by one through structured diagnostic work.

What "gate partial" means

If a Move shows amber status ("Gate partial"), it means some gate criteria have been met but at least one remains open. The gate panel in the workspace shows exactly which criteria are open and what evidence is needed to close them. Nexus helps you identify the fastest path to closing each outstanding criterion.

Baseline integrity rule A baseline measurement must be live — not projected, not estimated. AbarVa treats a projected baseline as a gate blocker (FM-9). The baseline exists to make the P5 value attribution defensible.

P3 · Design

Design is where the solution is architected and the delivery plan is written. By the end of P3, you should know exactly what you're building, who's building it, with what tools, on what timeline.

Scope lock

Once the P3 gate is approved, scope is locked. Any addition to scope triggers a gate review. Nexus enforces this by flagging scope additions with FM-10 (scope sprawl) and asking whether a gate review is needed before accepting the change.

P4 · Deliver

Deliver is the build phase. The solution is being built, tested, and deployed. Nexus runs ongoing risk monitoring against all 10 failure modes, tracks pace against the P3 delivery plan, and helps close governance gates (security, HITL, privacy).

Governance gates in P4

Before P4 can close, governance sign-offs must be on record: security review, privacy impact assessment, HITL plan, and compliance review. These cannot be deferred to post-launch. Nexus flags FM-5 if any of these are still open at phase completion.

P5 · Validate

Validate is the moment of truth. The P0 hypothesis is compared to measured outcomes. Value is attributed to the AI intervention. The program either closes or cycles to the next opportunity.

Nexus — your AI program coach

Nexus is the brain behind AbarVa's Moves module. It is the only AI voice you interact with directly. Behind Nexus, dozens of specialized agents analyze your program — checking gate criteria, scanning for failure mode signals, extracting structure from your input, tracking value — but you only ever see Nexus.

What Nexus does

  • Holds the full longitudinal context of your program across all six phases
  • Extracts structure from unstructured input (CEO emails, meeting notes, problem descriptions)
  • Routes silently to specialists based on what you're working on and which phase you're in
  • Enforces evidence integrity on every dollar figure and benchmark before responding
  • Emits structured artifacts that update the canvas pane (brief sections, gate status, value state)
  • Surfaces failure mode signals before they become blockers

What Nexus will not do

  • Invent facts, sponsors, or metrics not grounded in your conversation or tenant data
  • Promote unvalidated dollar figures without the [UNVALIDATED_HYPOTHESIS] label
  • Assert a sponsor is confirmed before you've confirmed it
  • Close a gate criterion based on assertion alone — evidence is required
  • Allow scope to expand without flagging the gate review requirement
Nexus is scoped to your program Every Nexus session is scoped to a specific Move. The header shows which program Nexus is working in. Nexus will not apply knowledge from other programs to your session unless cross-program signals are explicitly relevant (e.g., shared sponsor conflicts or dependency overlaps).

How to chat with Nexus

Nexus is a conversational interface, but it responds to structured intent. The best conversations are specific, concrete, and grounded in what's actually happening with the program.

Starting a new Move

What to do

Navigate to /strategic-moves/new and paste any source material — a CEO note, an email, a board memo, a problem description. Nexus will extract the hypothesis automatically.

"From: Marcus Webb, CEO... I want us to evaluate whether AI-assisted triage in the contact center can bring our average handle time under 6 minutes..."
Nexus extracts: hypothesis (AHT 9min → 6min target), detects existing programs for overlap, classifies archetype, identifies sponsor candidate, begins filling the 7-section brief.

Advancing a gate

What to do

Ask Nexus directly which criteria are blocking the gate. Then work through each one in conversation.

"What's blocking the P2 gate?"
Nexus lists each gate criterion with its current state (met / unmet), what evidence is needed for each unmet criterion, and the fastest path to closing them.

Closing a gate criterion

"We've confirmed baseline AHT with the contact center platform — 8.7 minutes average across the last 90 days, inbound voice only."
Nexus records the baseline (marks measurement as live, not projected), updates the gate panel, and checks if this closes the outstanding criterion.

Handling a failure mode signal

When Nexus flags a failure mode

If Nexus detects a failure mode signal, it surfaces a warning card. Do not dismiss these — address them.

Nexus flags: "FM-1 · No confirmed sponsor — Marcus Webb named Sarah Chen for the business case, but budget authority hasn't been confirmed. Who holds decision rights over the contact center transformation budget?"
Answer the question directly. Once budget authority is confirmed, the FM-1 flag is resolved and the sponsor is marked confirmed in the charter.

Updating scope

After P3 scope lock

"We also want to include the chat channel in the AI triage rollout."
Nexus flags FM-10 (scope sprawl): "The original scope was inbound voice only. Adding chat adds approximately 40% to the surface area. This requires a gate review before we can accept it — do you want me to initiate one?"

Glossary

Key terms used throughout AbarVa. When in doubt, refer to these definitions — they have precise meanings in the context of the platform.

Strategic Move
An AI initiative tracked through AbarVa's 6-phase lifecycle. Every program, whether $1M or $50M, is a Strategic Move. Abbreviated as "Move."
Archetype
The category of AI program: workflow automation, AI product enablement, intelligent analytics, data modernization, etc. Nexus classifies the archetype in P0 with a confidence band. The archetype determines which patterns and benchmarks apply.
Sponsor
The executive who holds budget authority and operating-model decision rights over the program outcome. Not the same as the "business case lead" or "project manager." Nexus distinguishes these and will not mark a sponsor as confirmed until budget authority is explicit.
Gate criterion
A specific, verifiable condition that must be met before a phase can close. Gate criteria are defined per phase. Some are binary (sponsor confirmed: yes/no); others require evidence (baseline measurement on record).
Gate state
The current status of a phase gate: ready (all criteria met), partial (some criteria met, some outstanding), or blocked (a hard blocker is preventing progress).
Brief-progress artifact
A structured signal Nexus emits when a scaffold section is filled during origination. It updates the canvas pane and the gate summary in real time. You don't type into the canvas — Nexus fills it from conversation.
Failure mode
One of the 10 categories of program failure patterns that AbarVa is designed to prevent. Nexus watches for signals of each failure mode and surfaces warnings before they become blockers. See the full reference.
Foundation readiness (F1–F4)
Four criteria assessed in P1 Charter: F1 data access confirmed, F2 sponsor confirmed, F3 platform available, F4 team capacity confirmed. All four must be green (or have a plan to green) before the P1 gate can close.
Value hypothesis
The preliminary estimate of value at stake, established in P0. Labeled [UNVALIDATED_HYPOTHESIS] until measured. AbarVa tracks the value from hypothesis through to realized outcome across the full lifecycle.
Baseline
The measured pre-intervention state of the success metric. Must be live measurement, not an estimate. AbarVa blocks P2 gate closure if the baseline is projected rather than measured, because a projected baseline makes P5 value attribution indefensible.
Deliverable
A named output produced during a phase — a charter document, diagnostic report, solution design, governance sign-off, etc. Nexus tracks deliverable completion state across the program lifecycle.
Evidence receipt
AbarVa's record of every piece of evidence cited in the program — where it came from, when it was captured, what it supports. Every assertion Nexus makes is traceable to an evidence receipt.
Display code
A short alphanumeric identifier for a Move, visible in the breadcrumb (e.g., APX-001). Useful for referencing programs in steering committee discussions, Slack, and email.
HITL
Human-in-the-loop. A governance requirement for AI systems that affect consequential decisions — contact center triage, lending decisions, clinical recommendations. AbarVa tracks the HITL plan as a governance gate in P4.
Nexus
AbarVa's AI program orchestrator for the Moves module. The only AI voice you interact with directly. Behind Nexus: dozens of named specialists that analyze different aspects of the program silently.
Specialist
A narrow, function-specific AI agent that operates behind Nexus. Specialists are never user-facing. Examples: DuplicateDetector, GateChecker, CitationGuard. See the agent catalog.
Tenant
Your organization's AbarVa account. All programs, people, data, and evidence are scoped to the tenant. No data crosses tenant boundaries.
Broker bundle
The package of tenant context that Nexus loads for every session: org structure, program inventory, executive bench, cross-program signals. It enables Nexus to check for duplicates, sponsor conflicts, and resource contention across the portfolio.

The 10 failure modes

These are the patterns that end AI programs. AbarVa's intelligence layer exists to catch these early. Nexus monitors every conversation for signals of these failure modes and surfaces them before they become crises.

Source: Gartner (2023), RAND Corporation, MIT/BCG "Winning with AI" (2021), McKinsey Technology (2022), Forrester AI Adoption Report (2023).

1
No executive sponsor Lack of confirmed budget authority and executive ownership of the outcome. Programs without a real sponsor have no mechanism for resource decisions, escalations, or accountability. Signal: sponsor "assumed," budget "pending approval," no named owner.
2
Unclear problem definition Vague objective without a testable hypothesis. "Improve operations" or "leverage AI" are not hypotheses. Signal: no measurable bet stated, starting to build before the problem is validated, hypothesis never written down.
3
No data ownership Unknown data custodian, no confirmed data access, quality undefined. The most common technical failure point — discovered mid-P2 or mid-P4. Signal: "we think we have the data," no data lineage, quality checks not planned.
4
Undefined outcome measurement Success defined as "the model runs" rather than a business outcome. No KPI. No A/B comparison after go-live. ROI projected but not tracked. Signal: model accuracy reported as the success metric, no post-launch measurement plan.
5
Governance and security deferred Security, privacy, and compliance reviews left until late or post-launch. HITL plan absent. Signal: "we'll fix it in production," privacy impact not assessed, security not engaged until demo day.
6
Infrastructure not confirmed Build started before vendor or infrastructure decision is settled. Architecture rework mid-program. Signal: cloud vendor not selected, infra sizing not done, build started before architecture review.
7
Stakeholder misalignment Critical teams not engaged in design. Adoption assumed, not planned. Change management skipped. Signal: "they'll come around once they see it," training plan absent, operations team not in design reviews.
8
No delivery plan No defined build milestones for P4. Timeline undefined. "Agile will figure it out." Signal: no sprint cadence, no milestone dates, phases not sequenced, handoffs not defined.
9
Baseline and KPI integrity failure No business KPI baseline. Outcome measurement deferred. Contaminated or unreliable measurement after go-live. Signal: baseline "estimated," no way to attribute value to the AI intervention, measurement plan absent from charter.
10
Scope sprawl Simultaneously running too many pilots. Scope keeps expanding. New features bolted on after design lock. Signal: 10+ concurrent AI pilots, original use case diluted, "while we're at it" additions after P3 gate.
