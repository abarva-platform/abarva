AbarVa · Product Vision

The operating system for AI programs that actually deliver.

Most AI programs fail for the same ten reasons — every time, at the same points in the program lifecycle. AbarVa exists to make that failure pattern visible before it is irreversible, and to give every team the industry-specific knowledge they need to avoid it.

We believe the future of enterprise AI is not more models — it is better programs. A model without a confirmed sponsor, a measured baseline, a governed rollout, and a verified outcome trail is just a pilot that will eventually be cancelled. AbarVa is the layer that turns pilots into programs, and programs into realized value.

The platform is built on three ideas:

1

Failure is predictable — and therefore preventable

73% of AI pilots never reach production (McKinsey, 2023). The reasons are the same ten patterns, every time. AbarVa encodes prevention mechanisms for each one directly into the program lifecycle — not as advice, but as gate criteria that must be closed.

2

Industry depth changes outcomes

A contact center AI program in retail is fundamentally different from one in healthcare or financial services. The benchmarks differ. The regulatory constraints differ. The baseline metrics differ. Generic AI program frameworks fail because they ignore this. AbarVa's knowledge layer brings vertical-specific patterns, benchmarks, and evidence requirements to every program.

3

Evidence integrity is the trust signal

Every recommendation, every gate decision, every value claim in AbarVa is traceable to a cited source. Not a projection. Not a model output. A named piece of evidence with provenance. This is what makes the platform safe to bring to a board or an audit committee.

The knowledge layer: where we are going

The knowledge layer is the most strategically important part of AbarVa. Today it is a curated pattern fabric — a library of industry-specific playbooks, benchmarks, workshop structures, and evidence requirements that load into the agent context on every session. But that is only the beginning.

Where the knowledge layer is going

Today — Wave 1
Curated pattern fabric

Hand-authored archetypes with specific benchmarks, SME role catalogs, workshop agendas, and evidence requirements. Six patterns across retail, enterprise knowledge work, and customer service. Research-anchored against McKinsey, Gartner, MIT/BCG, RAND, Forrester.

Near-term — Wave 2
Living catalog + feedback loop

Pattern catalog grows with every completed program. Closed P5 programs feed back into the pattern library — real baselines, real outcomes, real attribution data. Healthcare, insurance, and manufacturing patterns added. Cross-industry contradiction signals harvested automatically.

Future — Wave 3
Embedded vertical intelligence

Specialist SLMs and LLMs fine-tuned on AbarVa's proprietary program corpus — one per industry vertical. A healthcare intelligence model that reasons about FDA approval pathways, HIPAA data handling, and CMS reimbursement mechanics. A financial services model that knows Basel III/IV capital requirements, model risk management, and SR 11-7. A retail model trained on demand elasticity, loyalty economics, and seasonal pattern recognition. These are not general-purpose LLMs doing vertical reasoning — they are narrow, domain-expert models that plug into the specialist layer as hyper-accurate domain validators, available to any agent, on any program, in that vertical.

The embedded SLM/LLM vision Each vertical SLM would do things a general LLM cannot: classify an archetype from a two-sentence description with 95%+ confidence, predict gate failure risk from P1 signals, validate that a baseline meets decision-grade standards for that industry, and surface the specific regulatory constraint that applies to this program's use case. The knowledge layer becomes a living, self-improving intelligence fabric rather than a static reference library. Over time, AbarVa's proprietary program corpus — thousands of real programs, real outcomes, real failure signals — becomes a competitive moat that no general-purpose platform can replicate.

Why this matters for the enterprise

The enterprise AI market is converging on a commodity layer — every platform will have the same foundation models, the same embeddings, the same RAG pipelines. The differentiation will come from domain knowledge depth and program governance rigor. AbarVa is building both: the knowledge layer (vertical SLMs, pattern fabric, real-program corpus) and the governance layer (gate criteria, evidence integrity, failure mode prevention). Together they produce something no point solution can offer — a verifiable chain from a CEO's question to a board-ready outcome attribution.

What is AbarVa?

AbarVa is an AI program operating system — a structured, evidence-driven platform that takes an AI idea from the first conversation to a verified, board-reportable outcome. It is not a project management tool, not a chatbot, and not a generic AI framework. It is purpose-built for the specific workflow of running AI programs in the enterprise: originating ideas, building business cases, gathering diagnostic evidence, designing solutions, delivering them, and proving they worked.

The platform has four surfaces — Moves, Source, Tower, and Intelligence — each with a dedicated AI agent (Nexus, Sentinel, Atlas, Steward). These agents are backed by a shared knowledge layer of industry-specific patterns and a shared governance layer of gate criteria and failure mode detection.

One sentence for the boardroom AbarVa helps CIOs, CFOs, and Value Office leaders run AI programs with discipline — from origination to verified value realization — without losing the narrative chain from a steering question to the evidence behind every recommendation.

Core concepts

Strategic Moves

An AI initiative is called a Strategic Move in AbarVa. Every Move has an owner, a hypothesis, a sponsor, a value stake, and a six-phase lifecycle. The platform tracks all of these from the first idea to the final verified outcome.

Gate-driven phases

Moves advance through six phases. Each phase has gate criteria — specific, verifiable conditions that must be met before the next phase unlocks. This is not ceremony; it prevents the most common failure pattern in AI programs: promoting a program before it is ready and discovering the gap in P4 when it is expensive to fix.

Evidence integrity

Every dollar figure and benchmark in AbarVa carries a label: [UNVALIDATED_HYPOTHESIS], [PRELIMINARY_ESTIMATE], or [MEASURED_RESULT]. The label cannot be removed without the evidence that justifies it. This is what makes AbarVa's value claims defensible in front of a CFO or audit committee.

The knowledge layer

AbarVa's knowledge layer is what makes it different from a generic program framework. For each program archetype — contact center AI, demand forecasting, CDP activation, loyalty optimization, AI coding productivity, M365 Copilot deployment — the platform holds industry-specific benchmarks, workshop structures, SME role catalogs, and evidence requirements. When you run a Contact Center AI program at Apex Retail, Nexus knows that AHT benchmarks differ from healthcare call centers, that containment % is the leading indicator, and that 50–100 audited call transcripts are the evidence bar for P2.

Response gates — why Nexus never guesses

Before any agent response leaves the platform, it passes through four gates:

  • Disclosure gate: If context has gaps, a disclosure banner is required before the response.
  • Pattern label gate: If the response is grounded in a pattern, it must be labeled as such.
  • Refusal gate: If context is insufficient for a decision-grade response, the agent refuses and explains what evidence is needed.
  • Citation gate (CitationGuard): Any dollar figure or benchmark without an evidence label is blocked before the response is sent.

Duplicate detectionBefore filling any brief section, Nexus cross-references the new idea against your existing programs. If there is scope overlap, it surfaces a deduplication question before proceeding. This prevents duplicate investment in the same program under different names.

P1 · Charter

Charter converts the P0 brief into a formal program record. Three decisions must be locked before P1 can close: confirmed sponsor (not a candidate — budget authority confirmed), a measurable success metric with a baseline plan, and a scoped delivery boundary.

The three-day P1 workshop pattern applies to all archetypes:

DayDurationFocusRequired participants
Day 13 hoursDecision locking — scope, cohort, first intervention defined; "everywhere charter" blockedSponsor (budget authority), Operating lead, Technical architect
Day 23–4 hoursBaseline establishment — decision-grade baseline with verified lineage; not hand-waved aggregate metricsData engineer, Domain SME, Privacy counsel
Day 32 hoursSynthesis prep — stakeholder map with named dissenter; P2 readiness recommendation (proceed / re-scope / stop)Sponsor, Operating lead, Measurement lead
FM-1 gate: no confirmed sponsorNexus will not allow P1 to close until the sponsor is confirmed with explicit budget authority. "I think Sarah Chen is the sponsor" is not confirmation. "Sarah Chen confirmed — she controls the contact center transformation budget and has operating-model decision rights" is confirmation.

P2 · Diagnose

Diagnose is the evidence-gathering phase. The P0 hypothesis is tested against reality. Baseline measurements go live. Gate criteria are closed one by one through structured diagnostic work grounded in evidence that meets the pattern's evidence bar.

Baseline integrity rule (FM-9)A baseline must be live and measured — not projected, not estimated from a dashboard. AbarVa treats a projected baseline as a gate blocker. The baseline is the foundation of the P5 value attribution; if it is not credible, the entire value case collapses.

P3 · Design

Design is where the solution is architected, the delivery plan is written, and scope is formally locked. Any scope addition after this gate requires a gate review. Nexus flags FM-10 (scope sprawl) on any addition and quantifies its impact on the P4 timeline before accepting it.

P4 · Deliver

Build is underway. Nexus runs ongoing failure mode monitoring against all 10 signals. Governance gates (security review, HITL plan, privacy impact assessment) must be closed before the P4 gate can open. Pace is tracked against the P3 delivery plan.

P5 · Validate

The moment of truth. The P0 hypothesis is compared to measured outcomes. Value must be attributed to the AI intervention specifically — not to market effects, seasonality, or concurrent programs. The [UNVALIDATED_HYPOTHESIS] label on the P0 value stake is only removed when measured evidence and attribution are on record.

Atlas · Tower agent

A

Atlas

Portfolio intelligence · Tower surface

Atlas is the executive-facing agent. It synthesizes across all active Strategic Moves to give the CIO/CFO/CAIO a portfolio view: aggregate value at stake, program health signals, cross-program risk, and steering-ready briefings.

What Atlas surfaces in Tower

  • Portfolio health scorecard: Phase velocity, gate adherence, and risk signal per program — in one view
  • Value at stake aggregate: Sum of projected, committed, measuring, and realized value across all Moves — by phase, by vertical, by sponsor
  • Cross-program risk signals: Shared sponsor overload, resource contention, overlapping scope, conflicting timelines
  • Steering brief: Monthly / quarterly executive synthesis — what's on track, what needs a decision, what should be stopped
  • Failure mode heat map: Which programs are showing the most FM signals, by failure mode type
Atlas vs. NexusNexus is program-scoped — it works inside one Move at a time. Atlas is portfolio-scoped — it works across all Moves. They share the same evidence integrity rules and the same citation discipline, but Atlas speaks to steering committees and boards while Nexus speaks to program leads and sponsors.

How to chat with Nexus

Nexus responds best to specific, concrete, grounded input. The best conversations are ones where you tell Nexus what you know — and let it tell you what you're missing.

Starting a new Move

Paste any source material

"From: Marcus Webb, CEO... I want us to evaluate whether AI-assisted triage in the contact center can bring our average handle time under 6 minutes..."
Nexus: detects Contact Center AI archetype (PAT-PRG-CC-AI-001 — tentative), checks for duplicate programs in the portfolio, extracts hypothesis (AHT 9min → 6min), identifies sponsor candidate (Sarah Chen from CEO note, not confirmed), begins filling the 7-section brief. Canvas updates in real time.

Closing a gate criterion

Give Nexus the evidence — with lineage

"We confirmed baseline AHT: 8.7 minutes average across the last 90 days of inbound voice calls. Source: WFM platform, not the dashboard aggregate — verified by the contact center ops lead."
Nexus: records baseline as live measurement (not projected), notes WFM lineage, updates gate criterion status, checks if this closes the P2 outstanding criterion. CitationGuard confirms the baseline is labeled as measured evidence.

Responding to a failure mode flag

Address it directly — don't dismiss it

Nexus flags: FM-3 · "Data ownership is unclear — we haven't confirmed who is the data custodian for the WFM system or whether the AI team will have API access to call transcripts."
You: "The WFM custodian is Rachel Torres, data engineering lead. API access to call transcripts is confirmed — BAA is in place and the contact center platform team has approved the integration."

Nexus: FM-3 flag resolved. Records data custodian and access confirmation. Updates foundation readiness F1 criterion.

Glossary

Strategic Move
An AI initiative tracked through AbarVa's 6-phase lifecycle. Every program is a Move.
Archetype
The category of AI program: contact center AI, demand forecasting, CDP activation, loyalty optimization, M365 Copilot, AI coding. Nexus classifies the archetype in P0 with a confidence band. The archetype determines which knowledge pack, benchmarks, and evidence bar apply.
Sponsor
The executive who holds budget authority and operating-model decision rights. Not the business case lead. Not the project manager. Nexus will not mark a sponsor as confirmed until budget authority is explicitly stated.
Gate criterion
A specific, verifiable condition that must be met before a phase can close. Some are binary (sponsor confirmed: yes/no); others require evidence (baseline measurement on record with lineage).
Gate state
Ready (all criteria met), Partial (some met, some outstanding), or Blocked (a hard blocker is preventing progress).
Failure mode
One of the 10 research-grounded patterns that end AI programs. Nexus monitors for signals of each and surfaces them before they become crises. See the full reference below.
Foundation readiness (F1–F4)
Four P1 gate criteria: F1 data access confirmed · F2 sponsor confirmed · F3 platform available · F4 team capacity confirmed. All must be green before P1 closes.
Knowledge pack / phase pack
The archetype-specific and phase-specific coaching context Nexus loads for every session. Contains the relevant benchmarks, SME role catalog, workshop structure, and evidence requirements for this archetype at this phase.
Pattern fabric
The full library of industry-specific patterns in AbarVa's knowledge layer. Six active patterns today; expanding with each Wave.
Evidence receipt
AbarVa's record of every piece of evidence cited: source, timestamp, what it supports. Every assertion traces to an evidence receipt.
Baseline
The measured pre-intervention state of the success metric. Must be live measurement, not an estimate. A projected baseline is a P2 gate blocker.
Value lifecycle
The four states of value in AbarVa: Projected → Committed → Measuring → Realized. Value can only move forward. Each state has a specific evidence label.
Brief-progress artifact
A structured signal Nexus emits when an origination scaffold section is filled. Updates the canvas pane in real time. You don't type into the canvas — Nexus fills it from conversation.
Specialist
A narrow, function-specific AI agent operating behind a front agent. Never user-facing. Examples: DuplicateDetector, GateChecker, CitationGuard. Cataloged in architecture docs but not visible in the product UI.
SLM
Small Language Model — a narrow, domain-fine-tuned model for a specific vertical (healthcare, financial services, retail). Planned for Wave 3 of the knowledge layer. Plugs into the specialist layer as a hyper-accurate domain validator.
Broker bundle
The package of tenant context loaded for every agent session: org structure, program inventory, executive bench, cross-program signals. Enables duplicate detection, sponsor conflict detection, and dependency analysis.
Context receipt
Record of what context was in scope for a given agent session. Part of the audit trail — allows reconstruction of why the agent responded the way it did.
HITL
Human-in-the-loop. Required for AI systems that affect consequential decisions. AbarVa tracks the HITL plan as a governance gate in P4.
SR 11-7
Federal Reserve / OCC supervisory guidance on model risk management. Applies to financial services AI programs. Requires independent model validation, ongoing monitoring, and documented model risk controls.

The 10 failure modes

These are the patterns that end AI programs. AbarVa's prevention mechanisms for each are embedded in gate criteria — not advice. Sources: McKinsey, MIT/BCG, Gartner, RAND, Forrester.

1
No executive sponsorNo confirmed budget authority; sponsor assumed not verified. Signal: "we got funding, now what?" · sponsor name appears in meeting notes but not in RACI · budget approved but no executive owns the outcome. McKinsey: active AI ownership is 3× more likely in high-performing programs.
2
Unclear problem definitionVague objective without a testable hypothesis. Signal: "improve operations" or "leverage AI" without a measurable bet · starting to build before the problem is validated. RAND: misunderstanding the problem is the single most common AI failure pattern.
3
No data ownershipUnknown custodian, no confirmed access, quality undefined. Signal: "we think we have the data" · no data lineage · quality checks not planned. Gartner: 43% cite data availability as the top obstacle; ~60% of AI abandonments through 2026 are data-related.
4
Undefined outcome measurementSuccess defined as "the model runs." No business KPI. No A/B comparison after go-live. Signal: model accuracy cited as the success metric · ROI projected but not tracked · no measurement plan in charter. Forrester: only ~15% of enterprises can demonstrate EBITDA-level financial gain from AI.
5
Governance and security deferredSecurity, privacy, compliance, and HITL left until late or post-launch. Signal: "we'll fix it in production" · privacy impact not assessed · security not engaged until demo day. Gartner: inadequate risk controls are a leading driver of post-launch incident costs.
6
Infrastructure not confirmedBuild started before vendor or infrastructure decision is settled. Signal: cloud vendor not selected · infra sizing not done · architecture rework mid-program. MIT/BCG: internally built GenAI succeeds at ~⅓ the rate of purchased solutions.
7
Stakeholder misalignmentCritical teams not engaged in design. Adoption assumed, not planned. Signal: "they'll come around once they see it" · training plan absent · operations team not in design reviews. McKinsey: workflow redesign is the single biggest EBIT-driving practice; skipping it is the single biggest failure accelerator.
8
No delivery planNo milestones for P4. Timeline undefined. Signal: no sprint cadence · no milestone dates · "agile will figure it out." McKinsey: 73% of AI pilots never reach production — delivery planning gaps are the most common structural cause.
9
Baseline and KPI integrity failureNo business KPI baseline. Contaminated or unreliable post-launch measurement. Signal: baseline "estimated" · measurement plan absent from charter · no way to attribute value to the AI intervention specifically. Forrester: inability to attribute value is the most common reason AI programs lose executive support after launch.
10
Scope sprawlToo many concurrent pilots. Scope keeps expanding after design lock. Signal: 10+ active AI pilots · "while we're at it" additions post-P3 · original use case diluted. Gartner: 57% of AI failures are characterized by "expected too much too fast."
