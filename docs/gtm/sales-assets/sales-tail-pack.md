## T276: Surekha Pilot SOW Starter

Working title: Surekha Governed AI Execution Pilot

Duration: 90 days.

Primary sponsor hypothesis: Surekha.

Pilot intent:

- prove that AbarVa can turn executive AI pressure into a governed operating cadence,
- select 2 to 3 high-value Moves with named business owners,
- produce evidence packets for consequential recommendations,
- create a board-ready decision and value narrative,
- and demonstrate that AI outputs stay in decision-support mode with human approval before any commitment.

Success criteria to personalize with Surekha:

| Criterion | Draft success measure | Evidence required before external use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive clarity | Sponsor can explain the top 3 AI-enabled operating priorities and why they matter. | Sponsor discovery notes. |
| Governed action | Every Move has a named owner, assumption log, missing-data banner, and approval checkpoint. | Product evidence packet or screen proof. |
| Value path | Each Move has a value hypothesis, required data, confidence level, and next decision. | Client actuals or approved synthetic assumptions. |
| Responsible AI | No autonomous business decision is made by the agent layer. | Human-in-the-loop control language. |
| Board readiness | Day-90 packet is ready for sponsor review and can be converted into a board narrative. | Founder and sponsor approval. |

Draft pilot phases:

| Phase | Timing | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery and scope | Days 1-15 | Sponsor goals, data dimensions, 2 to 3 candidate Moves, risk boundaries. |
| Evidence build | Days 16-45 | Move packets, corpus gaps, assumptions, approval paths, value hypotheses. |
| Operating cadence | Days 46-75 | Weekly executive packet, decision log, Source or Atlas view if included. |
| Board-ready close | Days 76-90 | Roadmap, ROI narrative, risk controls, next-step SOW. |

Out of scope until separately approved:

- live sensitive-data load,
- autonomous decisions,
- legal/procurement commitments,
- production integration with client systems,
- and any statement implying AbarVa replaces the sponsor's decision rights.

### Backup 1: Delta Technology Modernization And Source Opportunity

Public evidence supports a modernization and operational-resilience conversation, but not a public renewal calendar. Delta's 2025 Form 10-K says technology is a strategic differentiator, names investments in customer-facing applications, infrastructure and architecture, data access, AI-supported operations, Delta Concierge, and cloud-based technologies. It also discusses disruption risk from internal, third-party, SaaS, cloud, and infrastructure dependencies.

Business case hypothesis:

- AbarVa helps Delta convert a broad technology-modernization agenda into a governed portfolio of Moves.
- Source can support one supplier, AMS, cloud, AI tooling, or resilience-related decision only after discovery identifies an active sourcing moment.
- The pitch should avoid claiming known contract renewals, incumbent vendors, or budget ownership unless a public source or direct discovery confirms it.

Source discovery ask:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Which modernization or resilience program has an active 2026 decision? | Avoids pitching generic transformation. |
| Is there an AMS, cloud, AI tooling, customer-app, or operations-tech sourcing event? | Determines whether Source belongs in the pilot. |
| Who owns decision quality: CIO, CISO, sourcing, operations, finance, or product? | Shapes the executive packet. |
| What evidence must be present before a recommendation can advance? | Defines the decision packet. |

Useable outbound line:

> Delta's public filings show a major technology and AI agenda, plus explicit risk around cloud, SaaS, third-party, and operational technology dependency. AbarVa's opening is not "more AI." It is governed decision support for the next modernization or sourcing decision that already matters.

## T065: Founder Health Coverage Decision Packet

This is decision prep only. It is not insurance, tax, or legal advice.

Official-source baseline as of 2026-06-04:

| Option | When it may apply | Founder action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual Marketplace | Solo founder, freelancer, consultant, or self-employed with no employees. HealthCare.gov says self-employed people with no employees can use the individual Marketplace and may qualify for savings based on income and household size. | Compare plans on HealthCare.gov or state exchange; estimate 2026 income carefully. |
| COBRA | Founder recently lost job-based coverage and wants temporary continuation. DOL says COBRA can allow workers and families to temporarily keep job-based coverage after qualifying events, usually with the full premium cost. | Compare COBRA premium, network continuity, and Marketplace alternatives before deadline. |
| SHOP / small business group coverage | Business has at least one eligible employee beyond owners/spouses/family in the way HealthCare.gov describes. | Use broker or SHOP eligibility tools once hiring creates eligibility. |
| Broker / PEO | Future option when payroll, benefits administration, or group coverage complexity increases. | Defer until employee plan design is real; get quotes before first employee benefits commitment. |

Decision checklist:

- confirm current coverage end date and any COBRA election deadline,
- estimate founder household income for Marketplace subsidy modeling,
- compare networks, deductible, out-of-pocket max, drug coverage, and HSA eligibility,
- decide whether family coverage is needed,
- ask tax advisor whether any self-employed health insurance deduction applies,
- document the chosen option and renewal date in the founder operating system.

Recommended founder next step:

Use a licensed benefits broker or HealthCare.gov assister before binding coverage. Mark this row Done only after the founder records the actual coverage decision, carrier, effective date, and renewal reminder.
