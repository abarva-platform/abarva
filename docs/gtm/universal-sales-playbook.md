# Universal Sales Playbook

Status: candidate

Owner: AbarVa founder/operator

Backlog rows: T269, T277

Purpose: codify two repeatable sales motions that do not depend on a specific account research packet: the "no AI leader ready" play and the "new leader" play. Account-specific claims must still be researched before use.

## Use Rules

- Do not invent buyer facts, current events, budgets, vendors, or org structure.
- Use this playbook as a narrative and qualification scaffold.
- Add account-specific evidence before sending any client-facing note.
- Keep AbarVa positioned as decision-support and program operating infrastructure, not an autonomous decision-maker.
- Route specific SOW, legal, pricing, or data commitments through the relevant approved playbook.

## Play 1 - No AI Leader Ready

### When To Use

Use this play when a prospect has modernization pressure, scattered AI experiments, or data-platform work in motion, but no clearly empowered AI leader or operating model.

Common signals:

- The buyer talks about modernization, cost, delivery discipline, or data readiness before AI strategy.
- AI ownership is fragmented across IT, data, innovation, business units, and vendors.
- The organization has platform work underway but no visible path from platform investment to executive decisions.
- Procurement is more comfortable buying governance, measurement, and program acceleration than a broad AI transformation promise.

### Positioning

Lead with modernization operating discipline:

> AbarVa helps leadership turn modernization work into an evidence-backed operating system for decisions, priorities, and measurable value. AI becomes the accelerator, not the headline.

### Wedge

Use Source as the first wedge when the prospect has vendor, sourcing, managed-services, application-modernization, data-platform, or transformation decisions already happening.

Use Moves when the prospect needs an executive decision rhythm: what to start, stop, fund, sequence, or escalate.

Use Atlas/Tower when the prospect needs a portfolio view of value, risk, spend, and leadership accountability.

### Discovery Questions

- What modernization decisions are already funded but under-instrumented?
- Where does leadership lose confidence: data quality, vendor claims, execution tracking, value proof, or ownership?
- Which decisions need a repeatable evidence packet instead of another dashboard?
- Who can approve a decision-support pilot if there is no named AI owner?
- What would make the current data/platform program more valuable in 90 days?

### Pilot Shape

Pilot title: Modernization Program OS

Scope:

- Select one high-pressure modernization lane.
- Load only the minimum approved context needed for that lane.
- Build a decision ledger with cited evidence, human approvals, and measurable outcomes.
- Produce weekly executive packets that show risks, next decisions, and value movement.

Success criteria:

- Leadership can name the top decisions, blockers, and value levers.
- Decision packets include evidence, assumptions, and human owner.
- The buyer sees how existing data/platform work becomes operationally useful.

### Risks To Avoid

- Do not sell "AI strategy" as the first product if the buyer lacks an AI owner.
- Do not position AbarVa as replacing Databricks, Azure, Snowflake, ServiceNow, Jira, or enterprise BI.
- Do not imply autonomous execution or automatic vendor decisions.
- Do not make account-specific claims without sourced evidence.

## Play 2 - New Leader

### When To Use

Use this play when a newly appointed or newly empowered leader needs to establish control, credibility, and a first-100-days agenda across IT, data, transformation, innovation, sourcing, or operations.

Common signals:

- New CIO, CTO, CDAO, COO, transformation leader, or operating executive.
- Mandate to assess inherited programs quickly.
- Board or CEO pressure for sharper priorities and measurable value.
- Fragmented portfolio, unclear ownership, or weak decision records.

### Positioning

Lead with executive operating leverage:

> AbarVa gives a new leader a governed first-100-days operating layer: what matters, what is at risk, what evidence supports it, and which decisions need human commitment.

### First-100-Days Framework

| Window | Leader question | AbarVa support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1-30 | What did I inherit? | Context map, portfolio inventory, risk/value scan, stakeholder decision map. |
| Days 31-60 | What should I change first? | Move candidates, decision packets, sourcing/vendor risk, value sequencing. |
| Days 61-100 | How do I prove momentum? | Executive packet, action ledger, board-ready narrative, measurable outcomes. |

### Discovery Questions

- What inherited programs must be understood in the first 30 days?
- Which decisions are politically or financially difficult to unwind?
- Where does the leader need a neutral evidence spine?
- Which stakeholders must believe the 100-day plan?
- What weekly artifact would make the leader feel in control?

### Pilot Shape

Pilot title: First-100-Days Decision OS

Scope:

- Select one portfolio or operating lane.
- Build the initial context map and decision ledger.
- Generate weekly executive packets with citations, confidence, assumptions, and human owner.
- Create 3-5 candidate Moves, but require human approval before commitment.

Success criteria:

- The leader has a defensible view of inherited work.
- The leader can communicate priorities with evidence.
- The organization sees a repeatable decision rhythm, not a one-time AI exercise.

### Risks To Avoid

- Do not over-personalize without actual relationship context.
- Do not claim private company data or internal politics unless supplied by the client.
- Do not turn the 100-day framework into generic thought leadership; anchor it to decisions, evidence, and operating cadence.

## Shared Narrative Ladder

1. Modernization pressure is real, but evidence-to-decision flow is weak.
2. Dashboards and data platforms do not automatically create executive commitment.
3. AbarVa binds approved context to decision-support agents, human gates, and auditable outcomes.
4. The buyer gets a safer way to move from "we have data and AI experiments" to "we know what to do next and why."
