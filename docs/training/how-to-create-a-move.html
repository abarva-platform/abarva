01

What is a Strategic Move?

A Strategic Move is a phase-gated transformation program — a structured way to take an AI or operational initiative from hypothesis to handoff.

Every Move has the same anatomy: five phases (P1–P5), a gate at the end of each phase that must be cleared before advancing, and a set of consulting-grade deliverables generated at each phase. Nexus is your AI program manager — you talk to it in natural language; it fills the scaffold, tracks gates, and generates documents.

ℹ
One voice: Nexus
All chat on the Strategic Moves surface goes to Nexus, your front agent. Behind it are 12 specialist agents (gate enforcement, evidence indexing, deliverable versioning, etc.) — you never interact with them directly.
02

Before you start

Have these four things ready before opening the origination screen. They're the minimum required to create a move.

  • The bet / outcome — What are you trying to achieve? One clear sentence. Not a project name; an outcome. e.g. "Reduce contact center handle time by 30% using AI routing."
  • An executive sponsor — Who has accountability for this program? Name and title. Without a sponsor, the move can't advance past P1.
  • A scope boundary — What's in and what's explicitly out? Scope creep is a primary failure mode; Nexus will push you on this.
  • A rough value hypothesis — What's the magnitude of impact? This doesn't need to be a business case yet — a directional range is enough.
✓
You don't need everything upfront
Three optional enrichment fields (evidence family, value hypothesis seed, foundation readiness) are filled through conversation with Nexus — you don't need to prepare them in advance.
03

Originate a new move (P0)

Origination is a conversation. Nexus asks you questions; your answers populate the scaffold in real time. When the four required fields are filled, you promote to P1.

1
Navigate to Strategic Moves
From the top navigation bar, click Programs → Strategic Moves, or go directly to the portfolio home.
/strategic-moves
2
Click "New Move"
The black "New Move" button is in the top-right of the portfolio home. This opens the origination workspace.
/strategic-moves/new
3
Read Nexus's opening question
Nexus opens with: "To start a new Strategic Move, I need four things from you: the outcome you're targeting, who cares about it, what evidence you have, and a rough sense of what value is at stake. Where do you want to start?" — You can start anywhere.
4
Have a conversation with Nexus
Talk naturally. As you describe the initiative, Nexus extracts structured data and populates the right-hand scaffold fields automatically. You can see them fill in as you type.
5
Fill all four required fields
Watch the scaffold panel on the right. Fields 1–4 are required (marked with solid borders). Fields 5–7 are optional enrichments. Once all four required fields show content, the promote button activates.
6
Give your move a name
Before promoting, enter a program name in the name field at the bottom of the scaffold. This becomes the move's identifier across all surfaces.
7
Click "Promote to P1 Charter"
The large black button at the bottom of the scaffold. This commits the draft into an active move and opens the P1 Charter workspace. The origination draft is saved in full.

04

The seven scaffold fields

These are the exact fields Nexus populates during origination. The first four are required to promote; the last three are optional enrichment that improves document quality.

Step Field What to provide Status
01
What's the bet / hypothesis
 The outcome you're targeting — one clear sentence. Not a project name. Not a workstream. An outcome with a direction and magnitude. Required
02
Archetype classification
 The type of move — e.g. Cost Reduction, Revenue Growth, Risk Mitigation, Operational Excellence. Nexus will suggest based on your description. Required
03
Sponsor candidate
 The executive with accountability. Name + title. This person will be cited in the P1 Charter as the program sponsor. Required
04
Scope / boundary
 What is explicitly in scope, and what is explicitly out. Both sides matter — Nexus will generate a scope boundary statement for the charter from this. Required
05
Evidence family selection
 The categories of evidence that are available (operational data, financial data, customer data, etc.). Used to frame the P2 data readiness assessment. Optional
06
Value hypothesis seed
 Your early directional view of value: which levers, rough ranges, confidence level. This seeds the P4 financial model — even a rough estimate is useful. Optional
07
Foundation readiness
 Organizational readiness signals: change capacity, prior program outcomes, data maturity. Helps Nexus calibrate risk in the P2 diagnosis. Optional
⚠
Scope is the most important field
Vague scope is the primary reason moves fail at P2 and P3. If your scope boundary isn't specific enough, Nexus will prompt you to sharpen it before accepting the field.

06

Understanding gates

Every phase ends with a gate. Gates exist to prevent programs from advancing without the evidence and decisions needed for the next phase to succeed.

Cleared

All required gate criteria are met. You can advance to the next phase. The gate artifact deliverable has been generated and signed off.

Pending

Gate is open but criteria are not yet met. You can still work in the phase, generate documents, and have Nexus help populate criteria — but you cannot advance.

Blocked

A hard-severity criterion is unresolvable. Requires either descoping the program or escalating to the sponsor. Nexus will flag this explicitly.

Hard vs. Soft criteria. Each gate has a mix of hard (blocking) and soft (advisory) criteria. Hard criteria must be cleared before you can advance. Soft criteria are tracked but won't block you — they appear in your phase status as watch items.

⚠
Gates cannot self-approve
A program cannot clear its own gate. Gate advancement requires a human to review the gate criteria and confirm advancement. Nexus will remind you of any unmet criteria but cannot approve the gate on your behalf.
07

Generating deliverables

Each phase has a set of consulting-grade documents that Nexus generates from the program context. You can generate each document individually or all at once.

1
Open the phase workspace
Navigate to the phase (e.g. /strategic-moves/{id}/phase/1). Find the Generate tab in the right-hand canvas.
2
Click "Generate all [Phase] documents" — or generate individually
The Generate all button runs all documents for the phase in sequence. Or use the per-document generate button to regenerate a single doc without overwriting the others.
3
Wait 30–90 seconds per document
Generation assembles full program context and drafts a consulting-grade document. A spinner shows while it's running. Do not refresh the page.
4
Download in your preferred format
Once generated, download links appear for each format the document supports.
↓ HTML ↓ Word ↓ Excel
5
View in Evidence Hub
Every generated document is saved to the Evidence Hub for this move. Click "View in Evidence Hub →" to see all documents across all phases in one place.
✓
Re-run is safe
If a document doesn't look right, click re-run next to the green "✓ Generated" chip. Regenerating a single document doesn't affect any other documents in the phase.
08

The Evidence Hub

The Evidence Hub is the single document store for a move — all generated deliverables and uploaded attachments, organized by phase.

/strategic-moves/{id}/evidence

The hub shows every deliverable across P1–P5 with its current status (Draft, In Review, Signed Off), generation date, and download links. You can also upload supporting evidence files that get attached to the relevant phase.

Gate artifacts

Documents with a blue left border are gate artifacts — they're required for the phase gate to clear. These show a Gate label.

Working documents

Documents with a grey left border are working documents — supporting analysis and design artifacts. Not required for gate, but tracked for completeness.

⚠
Download requires generated content
Download links only appear after a document has been generated. If a document shows "not generated," go back to the phase workspace and generate it first.
09

Tips & common mistakes

✓
Talk to Nexus like a colleague
Don't fill scaffold fields manually — describe the initiative naturally in chat. Nexus extracts structure from conversation. "We're trying to cut call handle time by 30%, CFO is the sponsor, scope is just the inbound routing layer" fills three fields in one message.
✓
Use the P2 continue/discontinue decision explicitly
The P2 gate includes a continue/discontinue decision. If the diagnosis surfaces a fundamental blocker (data doesn't exist, sponsor has left, scope is too large), the right answer is often to stop here — not push through. Nexus will surface this honestly.
⚠
Don't skip the optional scaffold fields
Fields 5–7 are optional but they meaningfully improve P2 and P4 document quality. Spend 5 extra minutes on value hypothesis and evidence family during origination — it saves time in later phases.
⚠
Regenerating doesn't break other documents
Each document in a phase regenerates independently. If the business case looks wrong, click re-run on just that document. The execution roadmap and financial model are untouched.
ℹ
The phase rail shows where you are
The horizontal phase rail (P1 → P2 → P3 → P4 → P5 → Tower) at the top of every move detail view shows your current phase (larger dot) and the gate status of each completed phase. Red = needs attention, Amber = watch item, Green/Blue = clear.
ℹ
Uploading files to chat
Use the paperclip icon in any phase workspace to attach .pdf, .docx, .xlsx, .csv, .txt, .md, or .json files. Nexus reads the content and uses it to improve deliverable quality for the current phase. Files are scoped to the chat session, not globally persisted unless saved to Evidence Hub.
