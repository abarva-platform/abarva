AbarVa · User Guide

Welcome to AbarVa.

AbarVa is the operating system for enterprise AI programs — it helps strategy leads originate, validate, and govern AI initiatives with evidence grounded in your own data.

Surface: All modules Audience: New users Time: ~45 min full read
Getting Started · 01

How the platform works

AbarVa has four interconnected surfaces. Each has a primary AI agent. Start with Setup, explore Intelligence, then run Moves — they all feed each other.

Setup ⚙️ Configure Connect data, define your org context
Intel 🔭 Discover AI patterns, signals, pressure cards
Moves 🎯 Execute Phase-gated transformation programs
Tower 📡 Monitor Portfolio tracking, live signals
💬
One agent per surface
Steward runs Setup. Sentinel runs Intelligence & Source. Nexus runs Strategic Moves. Atlas runs Control Tower. Each agent has a front-of-house voice — behind each are specialist functions you never interact with directly.

Your first 30 minutes

The recommended path for any new user. Takes about 30 minutes. Sets you up to run your first Move with real data behind it.

Go to Setup — check connectors and tenant profile
Before anything else, see what data is already connected and who the named executives are. This is the foundation everything else builds on.
/admin → Connectors & Tenant Profile
Open Intelligence — read 3 pressure cards
Intelligence surfaces AI-detected patterns in your data. Reading pressure cards tells you what the system already knows about your operational state — before you've done any work.
/intelligence → Pressure Cards
Notice which patterns could become Moves
Look for the "→ Originate Move" prompt on any pressure card. This is the fastest path from signal to action — Intelligence shows you the problem, Moves is how you fix it.

Click "New Move" and describe the initiative in chat. Nexus handles the rest. You don't need perfect information — a one-sentence hypothesis is enough to start.

Setup

Setup — before you begin

Setup is where you wire AbarVa into your organization's data and context. Without Setup, Intelligence has nothing to analyze and Nexus produces generic documents. Good Setup = high-quality everything else.

Setup · Connectors

Connectors & data sources

Connectors are the pipes from your organization's systems into AbarVa's intelligence substrate. They're what makes signals specific to your business rather than generic industry patterns.

🗄️
Data warehouses
Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift, Databricks. These feed operational metrics that Intelligence uses to detect cost, efficiency, and risk patterns.
/admin → Connectors → Data
☁️
CRM & ops systems
Salesforce, ServiceNow, SAP. Transaction data and process logs that give Intelligence context about where friction and opportunity actually live.
/admin → Connectors → Systems
📁
Document stores
SharePoint, Google Drive, Confluence. Strategy documents, past project files, and reference materials that Nexus can draw on when generating deliverables.
/admin → Connectors → Documents
📊
BI & reporting
Power BI, Tableau, Looker. Pre-built dashboards and KPI definitions help Intelligence interpret metrics in business context rather than raw numbers.
/admin → Connectors → BI
⚠️
Foundation readiness matters
The "Foundation readiness" field you fill during Move origination (P0) is partly a reflection of Setup completeness. If connectors are sparse, Nexus will flag a readiness gap that will slow P3 design and P4 business case quality. The more connected Setup is, the better every Move document becomes.
Setup · Tenant Profile

Tenant profile & org context

The tenant profile is your organization's context layer — who the key executives are, what the strategic priorities are, and how the business is structured. This is how AbarVa learns to talk about your company correctly.

Add your executive team
Name and title for each C-suite and VP-level stakeholder. When you name a sponsor in a Move, Nexus can look up their role and ensure the charter addresses their reporting context.
Set your strategic priorities
2–5 top-level priorities your organization is working on (e.g. "Cost efficiency", "Customer experience transformation"). Intelligence uses these to weight which patterns are most relevant to surface.
Define your business units
A flat list of major divisions or functions. Used to scope Moves correctly and filter Intelligence signals by area of impact.
Setup · Key Terms

Key terms in Setup

Connector
An integration between AbarVa and an external data system. Connectors pull data into the intelligence substrate. Without connectors, Intelligence has no evidence and Moves produce generic content.
Substrate
The combined data layer that all agents query. It includes connector data, document embeddings, and tenant context. Think of it as the shared memory of the platform.
Foundation readiness
An assessment of how complete and current your substrate is for a given initiative. Low foundation readiness = higher risk of thin documents and missed risks.
Tenant
Your organization's isolated instance of AbarVa. All data, agents, and Moves are scoped to your tenant — no data is shared across tenants.
Intelligence

The art of the possible.

Intelligence is the most powerful surface for strategic discovery. It continuously analyzes your connected data to surface patterns, risks, and opportunities you might not have seen — and turns them into Move hypotheses with one click.

Intelligence · Signals

Reading signals & pressure cards

Signals are AI-detected patterns in your operational data. They appear as pressure cards — structured observations with severity, evidence, and a recommended action.

📉
Cost pressure signal
A sustained cost trend that deviates from plan — e.g. per-transaction costs rising 12% quarter-over-quarter with no corresponding revenue lift.
⚠️
Risk concentration signal
Over-reliance on a single vendor, technology, or process — e.g. 73% of a workflow depending on a contract ending in Q3.
🚀
Opportunity signal
A gap where AI capability could create significant value — e.g. a manual classification task consuming 2,400 hours/month that ML could automate at 96% accuracy.

Each pressure card shows you: what the signal is, the data evidence behind it, which business unit it affects, the magnitude of impact, and a recommended action — usually "Originate a Move."

Cost pressure · High severity Routing inefficiency — Contact Center
What the signal says: Average handle time in the inbound routing layer has increased 18% over the past two quarters. The top 3 call categories (authentication failures, billing disputes, routing timeouts) account for 61% of excess time. ML-assisted routing could recover $2.8–4.1M annually based on volume and cost-per-minute data.
Evidence sources
Call center ops data · 6mo IVR routing logs · Q3–Q4 Industry benchmark · AHT peers
→ Originate a Move Deep dive Dismiss
✓
Pressure cards pre-fill Moves
When you click "Originate a Move" from a pressure card, Nexus pre-populates the bet/outcome, value hypothesis, and evidence family fields from the card's data. You start P0 already 60% complete instead of from scratch.
Intelligence · Pattern → Move

From pattern to Move

The most powerful workflow in AbarVa. Intelligence detects a pattern → you validate it → one click sends it to Strategic Moves as a pre-loaded origination.

Browse Intelligence signals for your business units
Filter by business unit, archetype, or severity. High-severity patterns with strong evidence backing are the best candidates for immediate Moves.
/intelligence → Pressure Cards → filter by severity
Open a pressure card and read the evidence
Check the evidence sources. Are they recent? Is the data from connected systems you trust? The stronger the evidence, the more defensible the business case in P4 will be.
Ask Sentinel to go deeper
Use the chat to ask "What's driving the 18% AHT increase?" or "Which teams are most affected?" Sentinel can drill further into the substrate before you decide to act.
Click "→ Originate a Move" to promote the signal
This opens the P0 origination screen with the pressure card data pre-loaded into the scaffold. You confirm the fields, name the Move, and promote it to P1.
Pressure card → Originate a Move → /strategic-moves/new
Intelligence · Key Terms

Key terms in Intelligence

Signal
An AI-detected pattern in operational or financial data. Signals are surfaced automatically from connected data sources. You don't need to ask for them — they appear when the substrate finds something worth noting.
Pressure card
The structured presentation of a signal. Includes type, severity, evidence, impact estimate, and recommended action. Think of it as an AI-authored executive brief on a specific problem.
Archetype
The category of a signal or Move — Cost Reduction, Revenue Growth, Risk Mitigation, or Operational Excellence. Nexus uses archetype to choose which deliverable templates and gate criteria to apply.
Sentinel
The front agent for Intelligence and Source. Answers questions about signals, drills into evidence, and coordinates specialist functions for deep analysis. You talk to Sentinel — it orchestrates everything behind the scenes.
Provenance
The data lineage behind a signal — which connector, which dataset, which time period. High-provenance signals have strong, recent, specific evidence. Low-provenance signals are more uncertain.
Strategic Moves

What is a Strategic Move?

A Strategic Move is a phase-gated transformation program — a structured, evidence-backed way to take any AI or operational initiative from a one-line hypothesis to a handed-off delivery with full documentation and governance.

P0 ✏️ Originate 7-field scaffold
P1 📋 Charter Formalize scope & governance
P2 🔍 Discover As-is, root cause, diagnosis
P3 🎯 Design Target state + solution
P4 📊 Roadmap Execution plan + biz case
P5 🚀 Mobilize Handoff → Tower
Strategic Moves · P0

P0 — Originate

Where a Move is born. You describe the initiative in natural language chat with Nexus. It extracts seven structured fields into a scaffold panel on the right. Once four required fields are filled, you name and promote the Move to P1.

/strategic-moves/new
Left pane · Nexus chat
Nexus
Let's build your Move. Tell me what outcome you're aiming for — one sentence is ideal. What changes if this program succeeds?
You
We want to cut contact center handle time by 30% using AI-assisted routing. COO is sponsoring it. Scope is just the inbound routing layer.
Nexus
Got it — filled bet/outcome, sponsor, and scope. This looks like Cost Reduction. Confirm? And do you have a rough value estimate?
Right pane · Scaffold
01 · Bet / outcome
Reduce contact center handle time by 30% using AI routing.
02 · Archetype
Cost Reduction
03 · Executive sponsor
COO (confirm full name)
04 · Scope boundary
In: inbound routing. Out: agent training, CRM.
05 · Value hypothesis
Awaiting…

The seven scaffold fields

1. Bet / outcome REQUIRED
The result you're targeting — one sentence, outcome-level, with direction and magnitude. Not a project name. "Reduce contact center AHT by 30%" not "CC AI Project."
2. Archetype REQUIRED
Cost Reduction, Revenue Growth, Risk Mitigation, or Operational Excellence. Nexus suggests based on your description — confirm or correct it.
3. Executive sponsor REQUIRED
Name and title of the accountable executive. Without a named sponsor, the P1 Charter gate cannot clear — it's a hard criterion.
4. Scope boundary REQUIRED
Explicit in-scope and out-of-scope. "In: inbound routing. Out: agent training, CRM." Vague scope is the #1 cause of program failure.
5. Value hypothesis
Order-of-magnitude range. Not a full business case yet — that's P4. "$2–4M annual cost avoidance if 30% handle time reduction achieved."
6. Evidence family
The type of data that will validate this Move. Helps Nexus index uploads to the right evidence specialist.
7. Foundation readiness
Honest assessment of current-state data/infra. "Routing system on legacy platform (EOL 2027). No ML infra in-house."
Strategic Moves · P1
P1 Charter Gate: Charter Approval
Formalize the program scope, objectives, and governance. Define who owns what and establish the baseline timeline. The Charter Gate requires a named sponsor, a defined scope, and a signed-off charter document before you can advance to discovery.
Example chat with Nexus
"Nexus, help me draft the stakeholder map. The COO is sponsor, the VP of CX is program lead, and we'll need IT and Finance as working stakeholders." → Nexus populates the stakeholder section, assigns RACI roles for each name, and flags two decision-rights gaps that need resolving before the gate can clear.

Terminology: Charter
The Program Charter is the formal contract for the Move. It locks scope, confirms the sponsor, and establishes decision rights. Once signed off, changing scope requires a formal charter amendment — Nexus will enforce this.
Strategic Moves · P2
P2 Discover & Diagnose Gate: Diagnosis Sign-Off
Map the current state, identify root causes, and build a structured problem statement. This phase includes a critical Continue / Discontinue decision — if diagnosis reveals a fundamental blocker (wrong scope, wrong sponsor, non-viable economics), you record it here and can formally discontinue the Move rather than letting it stall.
Example chat with Nexus
"Nexus, I'm uploading last quarter's AHT data. Can you identify the top 3 call categories driving high handle time?" → Nexus parses the file, surfaces that authentication failures, billing disputes, and routing timeouts account for 61% of excess handle time, and pre-fills the Root Cause Analysis with supporting data points.
Deliverables generated
📊 Current State Assessment GATE 🔍 Root Cause Analysis 📌 Problem Statement ⚠️ Risk Register
Gate criteria
As-is process documented with data evidence Hard
Root causes validated, not just symptoms Hard
Continue/Discontinue decision recorded Soft
Risk register populated Soft
⚠️
The Continue/Discontinue decision
P2 is the last low-cost exit point. If discovery reveals the economics don't work, the scope was wrong, or there's no sponsor alignment — record a Discontinue decision with rationale. A formally discontinued Move is better than a stalled one. Say: "Nexus, record a Discontinue decision for this Move. Rationale: [reason]."
Strategic Moves · P3
P3 Design Future State Gate: Design Approval
Define the target state architecture and operating model. Address each root cause from P2 with a specific design decision. Define the sourcing strategy — build, buy, or partner — and complete solution design for all in-scope components. Every design decision should trace back to a P2 root cause.
Example chat with Nexus
"The routing engine needs ML-based intent classification. We'll buy a vendor solution — the foundation isn't there to build in-house. Nexus, add that to the Sourcing Strategy with the rationale." → Nexus documents the build-vs-buy decision, links it to the Foundation Readiness gap from origination, and adds a vendor evaluation criterion to the P4 roadmap.
Deliverables generated
🏗️ Target State Architecture GATE 🎨 Solution Design ⚙️ Operating Model Design 🤝 Sourcing Strategy
Gate criteria
Each P2 root cause addressed in design Hard
Sourcing decision documented with rationale Hard
Architecture reviewed with technical stakeholders Soft
Operating model changes identified Soft
💡
Terminology: Build / Buy / Partner
The sourcing decision in P3 determines P4 economics. Build = internal development (high cost, high control). Buy = vendor solution (faster, less custom). Partner = joint delivery with a SI or specialist firm. Nexus will ask you to justify the choice against the Foundation Readiness assessment from P0.
Strategic Moves · P4
P4 Roadmap & Business Case Gate: Investment Approval
Translate the P3 design into an execution roadmap and build the business case economics. The P4 gate is the investment approval gate — the highest-stakes gate. It requires a sponsor-signed business case, a milestoned roadmap with named owners, and a financial model before any implementation begins.
Example chat with Nexus
"Nexus, the Financial Model needs updating — vendor cost came in at $420K not $380K. Recalculate the payback period." → Nexus updates the model, recalculates break-even (now 14 months not 12), flags the Business Case needs re-review, and adds a note to the executive summary explaining the revision.
Deliverables generated
🗺️ Execution Roadmap GATE 💼 Business Case GATE 📈 Financial Model EXCEL 📡 Tower Metrics Plan
Gate criteria
Financial model with 3-year projection Hard
Roadmap with milestones & owners Hard
Executive sponsor sign-off on business case Hard
Tower metrics defined & agreed Soft
💡
Terminology: Financial Model (Excel)
The P4 Financial Model is a 5-sheet Excel workbook: Assumptions, 3-year P&L projection, NPV/IRR, Sensitivity analysis, and Payback curve. The value hypothesis seed you entered at P0 pre-fills the assumptions — update it with actuals from vendor quotes and internal cost data before gating out.
Strategic Moves · P5
P5 Mobilize & Handoff Gate: Tower Acceptance
Assemble the delivery team and RACI. Build the handoff package. Verify execution readiness. Secure Tower acceptance. Gating out of P5 closes the Move and transitions it to Control Tower — where Atlas tracks execution progress, KPIs, and ongoing signal health.
Example chat with Nexus
"Nexus, generate the Handoff Package. Include the charter, roadmap, business case, and the risk register from P2. Delivery team: PM is Priya, tech lead is Marcus." → Nexus assembles the package, pre-fills the RACI with their roles, and flags two open risks from P2 that need owner assignment before Tower acceptance can confirm.

Gates & Evidence

Understanding gates

Gates are the quality checkpoints at the end of each phase. They're not bureaucratic formalities — they're the mechanism that ensures each phase has genuine outputs before you commit to the next investment level.

🔒
Pending
Phase not started or hard criteria not yet addressed. Advance button is locked.
⚠️
Open
In progress. Some criteria met, some still outstanding. Advance button shows what's blocking.
✅
Cleared
All hard criteria met. Advance is active. Soft criteria show as advisory warnings.
Hard criterion
A blocking gate requirement. The gate cannot clear and the Advance button stays locked until this is satisfied. Examples: named sponsor, scope boundary, gate document signed off.
Soft criterion
An advisory gate requirement. The gate can clear even if this is open — but it appears as a warning in the gate view and in the audit trail seen by Control Tower. Examples: stakeholder review completed, technical sign-off obtained.
Gate approval
The human action that clears a gate. In the current pilot, any user can self-approve. In production, only admin or Maestro-role users can approve. Nexus (the AI) cannot approve a gate on your behalf — gate approval is always a human action.
Gates & Evidence

Generating deliverables

Every phase generates consulting-grade documents. All are generated on demand from the Generate Deliverables tab in the phase canvas. Download links only appear after generation.

Open the phase workspace and click "Generate Deliverables"
The tab is in the right-hand phase canvas. It shows all documents for this phase with their current status (not generated / Draft / In Review / Signed Off).
/strategic-moves/{id}/phase/{1–5} → Generate tab
Select the document and click Generate
Gate artifacts are marked with a green GATE chip. Generate these first — they're what the gate checks for. Format is HTML preview + Word download for narrative docs; Excel for the Financial Model.
Wait ~10–30s, then preview or download
Nexus assembles the document from your scaffold data, phase inputs, and chat history. Complex documents (Business Case, Financial Model) may take longer. Regenerate any time after major chat updates.
💡 Give Nexus rich context when generating: "generate the charter — sponsor is Sarah Chen (COO), scope is inbound routing only, deadline is Q3." More context = better document.
Gates & Evidence

Evidence Hub

The single document store for a Move — all generated deliverables, uploaded supporting files, and status history, organized by phase.

/strategic-moves/{id}/evidence

Gate artifact
Documents with a blue left border. Required for the phase gate to clear. Must reach "Signed Off" status before the gate can clear. Examples: Program Charter, Current State Assessment, Execution Roadmap.
Working document
Documents with a grey left border. Supporting analysis and design artifacts — tracked and versioned but not blocking the gate. Examples: Stakeholder Map, Risk Register, Root Cause Analysis.
Document status
Draft → In Review → Signed Off. Gate artifacts must reach Signed Off. Working documents can stay at Draft.
📎
Uploading supporting evidence
Upload files via the Evidence Hub directly, or attach them in the Nexus chat with the paperclip icon. Supported: PDF, Word, Excel, CSV, PowerPoint. Uploaded files are indexed by the evidence specialist and attached to the relevant phase automatically.
Control Tower

After a Move completes

When a Move gates out of P5, it transitions automatically to Control Tower. Atlas — the Tower agent — takes over and starts tracking execution health using the metrics you defined in P4.

📡
What Tower does with your Move
Atlas reads the P4 Tower Metrics Plan and begins monitoring the KPIs you defined. If a metric starts deviating from the plan, it surfaces a new pressure card in Intelligence — the cycle closes. A successful Move generates execution evidence that feeds future Moves.
Control Tower

Reading the portfolio

Control Tower shows all active Moves as a portfolio — phase health, gate status, signal pressure, and vendor/system dependencies in a single view.

Portfolio heatmap
A grid of all active Moves showing phase, gate status, and pressure level. Red = blocked or high-pressure. Amber = advisory issues. Green = on track.
Pressure card (Tower)
A signal generated during execution — e.g. a KPI that missed target, a vendor dependency flagged, or a timeline slip. These are fed back into Intelligence and may spawn new Moves.
Atlas
The Tower agent. Answers questions about portfolio status, execution risk, and signal health. "Atlas, which Move is at highest risk of missing P4 this quarter?"

Tips & common mistakes

The most common mistakes new users make across all surfaces.

Setup

✓ Do
Connect at least one operational data source before exploring Intelligence.
Without a connector, Intelligence produces generic industry patterns, not signals specific to your operations.
✗ Don't
Skip the tenant profile because "it's just metadata."
Named executives in the tenant profile flow directly into Move stakeholder maps and charter documents — skipping it produces thin output.

Intelligence

✓ Do
Ask Sentinel to go deeper before originating a Move from a pressure card.
"Sentinel, what's driving the 18% AHT increase?" before clicking "Originate" gives you better scaffold content.
✗ Don't
Dismiss low-severity signals without reading them.
Low-severity doesn't mean low-impact — it may just mean the evidence is early. Some of the highest-value Moves start as amber signals.

Strategic Moves

✓ Do
Talk to Nexus like a colleague, not a form.
"We're cutting AHT by 30%, COO sponsors it, scope is just the routing layer" — fills three fields in one message.
✗ Don't
Leave a Move in P1 or P2 for 30+ days with no gate activity.
Stale Moves surface in Control Tower as portfolio risk signals. Use the P2 Continue/Discontinue decision if the Move needs to be paused.
✓ Do
Regenerate deliverables after significant chat updates.
Document content is assembled at generation time. If you've had three sessions of Nexus inputs since last generating, regenerate before sharing.
✗ Don't
Assume a vague scope is understood by everyone.
Scope disputes are the #1 cause of program failure. Document it explicitly — even if it feels obvious — in field 4 during origination.
