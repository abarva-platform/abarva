01

What is Intelligence?

Intelligence is the AbarVa surface where industry knowledge meets your portfolio reality. It's where you ask "what bets should I be considering?" and get a substantive, sourced answer — not a generic LLM reply.

Most AI tools answer questions from generic training data. Intelligence answers from a shared corpus of industry-specific use cases, patterns, vendors, and proof points overlaid against your tenant's actual data — your initiatives, KPIs, regulatory exposure, and strategic context. The result is reasoning at industry-grade specificity.

"Intelligence is what makes the platform demonstrably know your industry — not just respond to your questions." — Bet-shaping doctrine

Three things make Intelligence different from a chatbot:

①
Citation discipline
Every claim has a source. When Sentinel says "ambient AI delivers 35-50% documentation-time reduction," it cites KLAS Research 2025-Q4 (HIGH reliability). You can click through. You can challenge it. The platform's epistemic humility is visible.
②
Tenant overlay
The same corpus, scored for you. A Boston IDN and a Texas community hospital see the same 23 healthcare AI bets — but ranked differently based on size, segment, regulatory exposure, and current portfolio. Top 3 are CIO-relevant; the rest are browseable.
③
Lane discipline
Sentinel knows what it's not for. Vendor evaluation depth is Source's job. Move shaping is Nexus's job. Sentinel synthesizes patterns and frames bets, then hands off cleanly. No agent does everything.
02

The three substrates Intelligence reads from

Intelligence sits on top of three layered substrates. Understanding what's in each one explains what Sentinel can confidently say versus what it has to qualify.

i. Tenant substrate · what we know about you

Loaded via the AI Initiatives Registry plus your Setup data inventory. Includes your active initiatives (e.g. MH-01 Ambient Documentation at $5.8M measured), per-initiative KPIs, your regulatory exposure, your stakeholder map, your industry-context strategic notes. This is the truth for you.

ii. Industry corpus · what patterns exist

A canonical knowledge layer covering ~23 healthcare use cases, ~28 patterns, ~47 vendors, ~30 named proof points, ~16 regulatory entries — plus parallel sets for retail and financial services. Curated by AbarVa, refreshed quarterly. This is the truth for the industry.

iii. Cross-tenant signal · what peers are doing

Anonymized signal pool from other AbarVa tenants — what use cases they're running, what patterns they observe, what anti-patterns they hit. Activates when 5+ tenants contribute signal. This is the truth for your peer set. (Currently in build; lands as v2 of the corpus.)

✓
How Sentinel uses all three
When you ask "should we be considering Population Health AI?", Sentinel pulls from the corpus (use case definition, patterns, vendors, proof points), scores it against your tenant substrate (your size, your portfolio, your regulatory exposure), and — when peer signal is rich enough — surfaces what comparable IDNs have actually achieved. The answer is grounded in all three.

How to read a pressure card

"Pressure cards" are the attention items Sentinel surfaces on Today. Each one has a fixed shape so you can read it in 10 seconds.

Mockup · Pressure card · Urgent tone
⚠ URGENT · ATTENTION-BLOCKING

MH-07 Model Governance is at risk — and it's blocking 4 candidate bets in clinical.

$0 measured against $1.4M committed annual. This isn't just one stalled program — Model Governance is the foundational substrate for Sepsis EWS, Care Management AI, Clinical Risk expansion, and Imaging AI rollouts. Without an MRM platform live, you can't deploy the SR 11-7-style governance that those clinical AI bets require for FDA SaMD compliance.

Recommended response · re-baseline MH-07 with MRM platform vendor selection. [per AbarVa pattern observation · n=8 IDN deployments]

Open MH-07 in Tower See the 4 blocked bets Ask Sentinel: replan
Tone tag Urgent (red) · Attention (amber) · Opportunity (teal). The visual border picks up the tone color. Sentinel limits Today to 3 cards · one of each tone is the typical mix.
Headline Single Fraunces sentence that compresses the situation into something a CXO can act on. Always names the entity (initiative ID, use case ID, vendor ID).
Body ~80–120 words. Explains why this matters now. Always ends with the recommended response framed as Sentinel's read · not a directive.
Source attribution Closes with [per <source> · <currency>]. Click through to the corpus entry. No source = no card.
Named CTAs Up to three. Always concrete: "Open in Tower", "Shape in Nexus", "Ask Sentinel". Never generic "Learn more".
Below-the-fold tease A "+ N more signals" link at the bottom of the stack — Sentinel surfaces ~7 signals daily but caps Today at 3. The other 4 are browseable.
06

The Pattern → Move flow

Intelligence's job is not to make decisions. It's to surface signals that warrant a Move. Here's how a pattern in the corpus becomes an active Move in your portfolio.

Step 1 · Sentinel
Pattern triggered
Sentinel notices a corpus pattern (P-HC-005 · CMIO sponsorship) is binding for one of your initiatives that doesn't have it. Surfaces in the Patterns tab + Today.
Step 2 · You
Read the evidence
Click through. See the with-vs-without numbers (65–75% adoption with CMIO; 25–40% without · KLAS n=12). Decide if the pattern applies.
Step 3 · Handoff
Hand off to Nexus
Click "Shape as Move" → Nexus opens P0 Originate with the pattern context pre-loaded. Pattern ID becomes a binding constraint on the Move's gate criteria.
Step 4 · Move
Move enters P0
Move is shaped by Nexus, charters at P1, advances through P2-P5. Pattern compliance is checked at each gate. Sentinel watches for pattern-related signals.

The flow is intentional · Intelligence surfaces, you decide, Nexus shapes, Tower observes. No agent acts unilaterally. This is the doctrine — pattern matching is automated, decision-making stays with you.

07

Tenant overlay · what makes this yours

The corpus is canonical industry knowledge. The overlay scores corpus content for your tenant profile. Same corpus, different rankings per tenant.

When Sentinel says "for Meridian, Population Health for ACOs scores 87/100" — that score is composed from seven factor weights:

⚖
Score factor breakdown · UC-HC-MIDDLE-007 for Meridian
ACO contracts active +18 · 8-hospital IDN segment fit +12 · Margin pressure context +10 · CMS quality reporting exposure +8 · Recently entered scaling stage +5 · Not in current portfolio · candidate +15 · Substrate maturity for grounding +19 ·· Total: 87 / 100

Click any score in The Brief to see this breakdown. CIOs trust scoring when they can see what drove it. The score is always derived, never opinion.

A retail tenant with the same corpus would see Population Health filter out entirely (industry mismatch → score 0). A healthcare community-hospital tenant might see it score 65 · same use case, different segment. The corpus stays canonical; the surface adapts.

08

Provenance discipline

Every claim Sentinel makes traces to a source. Currency-dated. Reliability-rated. Stale-flagged. This is what separates Intelligence from a chatbot.

Provenance shows up in three places:

①
Inline · attached to every claim
Format · [per <source name> · <currency-date> · <reliability>] — e.g. "$8-24M annual at IDN scale [per McKinsey 2025-Q4 · HIGH]". The bracketed pill is hoverable; click for the corpus entry with full version history.
②
In the audit footer
Every Brief renders a footer line: "Substrate · 23 use cases · 28 patterns · 47 vendors · 16 regulatory · 14 proof points cited · refresh quarterly · last 2026-Q1". The composer's audit trail.
!
Stale flags
When a corpus claim is past its refresh cadence, Sentinel surfaces an amber flag inline · "vendor financial health is 4 months old; recent changes may not be reflected". The platform's epistemic humility is visible — you don't have to trust uncritically.

There are three reliability ratings:

HIGH · peer-reviewed studies (JAMIA, NEJM Catalyst), public earnings, regulatory filings, established analyst firms (Gartner, KLAS, Forrester) on their core competency.
MED · trade press, vendor case studies (with vendor-curated caveat), conference presentations.
LOW · anonymous forums, vendor marketing without supporting data. Low-reliability claims don't enter the corpus unless corroborated.

09

Common mistakes · do's and don'ts

Watching CXOs use Intelligence for the first time, six things trip people up.

DON'T
Treat the score as gospel. A 87/100 means high relevance per the factor weights · not certainty. Always click through to see which factors drove the score. If the strategic-context match is doing the work, that's softer than a regulatory-alignment match.
DON'T
Skip the anti-patterns. Failure modes are usually more predictive than success patterns. If the binding success pattern is at 65–75% adoption and the anti-pattern is at 70% of failed deployments, the anti-pattern is the load-bearing signal — not the success rate.
DON'T
Take Sentinel's vendor opinion at face value. Sentinel surfaces vendor names + tier classifications + financial-health signals. It does not evaluate vendor fit for your specific contract structure or your buyer dynamic. That's Source's job. Use Sentinel to shortlist; use Source to decide.
DON'T
Ignore the cascade rail. If MH-04 is over-performing and there's a Move Cascade saying it enables three follow-on bets, that's the most concrete forward-look the platform has on your portfolio. Cascades are observed, not theorized.
DO
Ask Sentinel "why this score?" The factor breakdown is the most useful conversation starter. From there, ask "what would change if our regulatory exposure shifted?" — Sentinel will re-score in real time and explain.
DO
Read Today before reading the Brief. Today surfaces what changed since yesterday. The Brief is your quarterly synthesis. Reading them in order gives you the delta before the snapshot.
10

Glossary

Twelve terms you'll encounter across Intelligence. Click any to jump to the canonical corpus entry.

Use Case UC-{INDUSTRY}-{OFFICE}-{N}
A named AI initiative pattern in industry — e.g. UC-HC-FRONT-001 Ambient Clinical Documentation. The corpus has ~23 healthcare, ~22 retail, ~22 finserv use cases canonical at IDN+ scale.
Pattern P-{INDUSTRY}-{N}
A recurring success or failure pattern observable across deployments. Carries quantified with-vs-without numbers + provenance. ~28 healthcare patterns canonical.
Anti-Pattern AP-{INDUSTRY}-{N}
Cross-use-case failure modes promoted from inline failure_modes. Pilot-to-scale gap, model bias drift, IT-imposed workflow framing. The negative-space pattern library.
Vendor V-{INDUSTRY}-{N}
Company providing AI products in this industry. Carries product lines, financial health, share trajectory (gaining / holding / losing), customer roster.
Proof Point PP-{INDUSTRY}-{N}
A named customer deployment with a measured outcome and a HIGH-reliability source. "Cleveland Clinic · DAX · 2024 · 67% physician adoption." Credibility carrier.
Move Cascade MC-{INDUSTRY}-{N}
A sequence of use cases with pre/post conditions. "If you do X and succeed, the natural next bet is Y." Observed evidence + completion rate + handoff failure modes.
Persona PER-{ROLE}-{N}
A named role (CIO, CFO, CMIO, CHRO, CDO) with concerns, objections, KPI focus, hypotheses. Sentinel adjusts voice + content per persona.
Engagement state
Your tenant's current relationship with a use case · in flight · candidate · at risk · scaled · failed. Color-coded across all surfaces.
Lifecycle stage
Where a use case sits on the industry maturity curve · emerging · scaling · mature · declining. Drives The Map's X axis.
Tenant overlay
Query-time scoring layer that adapts canonical corpus content to your tenant profile. Same corpus, different rankings per tenant.
Pressure card
An attention item on the Today surface · urgent / attention / opportunity. Headline + body + named CTAs + source attribution. Three per day; more in the tease.
Binding pattern
A success pattern that's necessary, not optional. If it's not honored, the deployment typically fails. Surfaces as a chip on every relevant use case + as a pre-Move requirement.
