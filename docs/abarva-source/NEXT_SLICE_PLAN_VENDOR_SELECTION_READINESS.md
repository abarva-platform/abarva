# Next Slice: Vendor Selection Readiness

Date: 2026-04-26
Scope: executive readiness planning for moving from BAFO negotiation to selection recommendation
Status: planned

## 1. Purpose

This slice defines the readiness guardrails for recommending one or more vendors for selection.

The output is deterministic planning guidance that keeps the decision auditable and predictable.

## 2. Relationship to RFP Readiness, Vendor Responses, Pricing Normalization, BAFO

Selection readiness consumes all prior deterministic states:

- RFP readiness determines whether requirements were sufficiently specified;
- vendor-response completeness defines comparability and mandatory coverage;
- pricing normalization provides normalized commercial posture;
- BAFO outputs define remaining negotiation blockers and decision confidence.

## 3. Selection Readiness Criteria

Selection can be recommended only when:

- vendor responses are complete or conditionally complete with explicit waivers;
- comparability blockers are resolved or intentionally accepted;
- pricing posture is normalized and documented;
- evidence confidence is sufficient for decision confidence.

## 4. Required Artifacts

Before selection readiness is declared, the following should be present:

- BAFO readiness summary,
- pricing normalization snapshot,
- decision-ready exception register,
- stewardship and governance notes.

## 5. Required Approvals

Selection readiness should reference explicit approver checkpoints:

- procurement sponsor approval,
- finance validation on economic assumptions,
- security review confirmation,
- executive acceptance of residual risks.

## 6. Scorecard Dependency

Selection readiness can consume scorecard status, but this slice does not require scorecard execution changes.

It should track whether a scorecard exists, is complete, and aligned to the active decision framing.

## 7. Pricing Normalization Dependency

Pricing normalization is required for:

- readiness posture on cost certainty,
- commercial trap visibility,
- transition and excluded-services economics.

No additional pricing modeling should be added in this slice.

## 8. Evidence Confidence

Evidence confidence should be explicit at selection-planning level:

- high: deterministic evidence supports recommendation posture,
- medium: recommendation proceeds with conditions,
- low: hold until evidence is refreshed or clarified.

## 9. Risk / Exception Closure

The plan must track unresolved risks with clear next steps:

- unresolved evidence claims,
- incomplete transition commitments,
- unresolved exclusions or assumptions,
- unresolved commercial traps.

## 10. Nexus / Sentinel / Atlas / Steward Behavior

Nexus should expose a concise decision-ready checklist and blocker map.

Sentinel should flag unresolved evidence and unsupported commercial claims.

Atlas should deliver executive framing for portfolio-level impact.

Steward should gate selection-forward movement until required checks are clear.

## 11. What Not to Build

- model calls,
- final vendor selection automation,
- approval engine,
- scorecard generation implementation,
- workflow engine changes,
- document export/import.

## 12. Acceptance Criteria

The plan is sufficient when it:

- maps criteria for "ready / conditional / blocked" selection posture,
- references dependencies from BAFO and pricing/response models,
- defines required artifacts and approvals,
- captures residual risks and exception handling,
- and stays bounded to planning guidance.
