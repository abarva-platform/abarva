Standard Product Scope

AbarVa Source is the sourcing execution layer, not a second data lake.

Source turns approved Knowledge, governed metrics, contract evidence, and opportunity signals into repeatable sourcing work: diagnose, scope, compare, decide, transition, and track value. It complements procurement systems of record; it does not replace them.

Part I

What AbarVa Source Is

Source is the governed workbench for complex technology-services sourcing and vendor decisions. It is built for the work that usually sits between advisory decks, spreadsheets, procurement portals, and scattered contract evidence.

Existing contracts

Optimization before renewal

Source helps teams diagnose renewal exposure, obligations, service gaps, consolidation opportunities, and recoverable value using approved evidence.

New events

Structured sourcing execution

Source supports scope, RFP/RFI preparation, vendor response comparison, pricing normalization, BAFO, decision support, and transition planning.

Governance

Decision support, not authority

Source prepares evidence and decision artifacts. Legal, procurement, finance, risk, compliance, clinical, and executive owners remain accountable approvers.

Product Model

How Knowledge, Cube, aVa, reporting, and Source work together

The split matters. Knowledge stores the truth, Cube defines governed measures, reporting explores the measures, aVa explains the evidence, and Source runs the sourcing workflow.

Layer Owns Does not own
Knowledge Approved facts, relationships, source evidence, baseline versions, lineage, deferred states. Sourcing workflow, vendor selection, custom analytics, or business approvals.
Cube Reusable governed dimensions and measures over approved projections. New facts, model-generated calculations, or bespoke client metrics by default.
Superset Standard analyst drill-down over governed data using a read-only identity. Independent reporting truth or custom dashboards without explicit scope.
Observable Curated narrative views over the same governed metric payload, where included. Metric definitions that diverge from Cube or PostgreSQL.
aVa Evidence-aware explanation, recommendations, and questions bound to the active baseline. Unsupported tenant facts, hidden calculations, or final approval authority.
Source Sourcing diagnosis, RFP/RFI execution, response comparison, decision packages, transition, value tracking. Vendor master, spend truth, contract master, application inventory, or operational metrics.
Included Scope

Two standard workflows

Workflow 01

Existing-contract optimization

Use when there is a renewal, performance issue, consolidation question, scope drift, or service-quality concern. Source frames what is known, what is missing, and what can be negotiated or remediated from the evidence.

  • Renewal and expiration windows
  • Applications, capabilities, and services touched
  • Spend and SLA summaries where supplied
  • Evidence gaps and owner actions
Workflow 02

New sourcing event

Use when the business wants to competitively source, re-platform, consolidate vendors, or modernize a service tower. Source keeps the event disciplined from scope through selection.

  • Scope and RFP readiness
  • Vendor response completeness
  • Pricing normalization and BAFO support
  • Decision package and transition plan
Operating Path

The standard Source journey

AbarVa may identify a sourcing signal upstream, but Source owns the workflow once the signal becomes a sourcing decision path.

01

Signal

Knowledge/Cube surfaces renewal, concentration, service, spend, or transformation exposure.

02

Diagnose

Source confirms evidence, missing inputs, owners, risks, and whether the issue is sourcing-ready.

03

Scope

The team defines towers, services, regions, volumes, constraints, and business outcomes.

04

Compete

RFP/RFI, vendor responses, scorecards, pricing normalization, exceptions, and BAFO.

05

Decide

Executive package, transition path, value ledger, caveats, and approval boundary.

Evidence

Standard inputs Source can use

The standard path works with agreed staged extracts and documents. It does not require clients to grant live access to every source system.

Input Why it matters Standard posture
Vendor and contract inventory Identifies who provides what, renewal windows, obligations, and service scope. Standard.
Application and capability links Shows business impact and migration risk if a vendor or service changes. Standard when approved relationships exist.
Spend summaries Supports exposure, prioritization, and sourcing value sizing. Standard when source lineage reconciles.
Incident and SLA summaries Distinguishes price-only renewals from operational performance problems. Standard at summary level where supplied.
Invoices, POs, rate cards, raw tickets Supports forensic cost, rate, and service analysis. Optional accelerator unless explicitly scoped.
Interviews and workshops Capture business priorities, pain points, decision criteria, and missing evidence. Standard as evidence, with confidence and review state.
Sourcing Patterns

Common technology-services sourcing archetypes

Source should classify the event before generating artifacts. Archetypes determine the evidence needed, evaluation criteria, pricing model, transition risk, and decision package.

AMS: Application managed services

Run/support, enhancements, release management, incident response, SLAs, productivity, automation, and application portfolio coverage.

IMS: Infrastructure managed services

Data center, network, cloud operations, end-user services, resilience, ticket patterns, service levels, and transformation roadmap.

Data and analytics services

Warehouse/lakehouse operations, BI, data engineering, data quality, lineage, platform modernization, and AI-readiness dependencies.

Cloud and platform engineering

Landing zones, platform teams, DevSecOps, FinOps, reliability, migration factories, and managed cloud operations.

ERP and enterprise apps

SAP, Oracle, Workday, CRM, core operations, module support, integration, testing, release cycles, and transformation programs.

AI and data rights

Contract rights, model use, data use, auditability, agent controls, privacy, and AI-enabled service delivery. Often an accelerator.

Scope Discipline

What Source replaces, and what it does not replace

Source can replace scattered manual sourcing work

  • Ad hoc sourcing strategy decks.
  • Repeated RFP/RFI drafting from scratch.
  • Spreadsheet-only response comparison.
  • Untraceable decision rationales.
  • Disconnected value tracking.

Source does not replace accountable systems or owners

  • ERP, CLM, procurement, ITSM, CMDB, monitoring, or finance systems of record.
  • Legal, procurement, finance, risk, compliance, clinical, or executive approval.
  • Client source-system cleanup.
  • Custom analytics unless scoped as an accelerator or analytics-pod service.
  • Near-real-time operational reporting.

Definition of Done

How Source becomes pilot-ready

A polished screen is not enough. Source is pilot-ready only when the sourcing workflow, data lineage, metrics, evidence, and handoffs all bind to the same governed baseline.

Acceptance rule: every Source opportunity, artifact, scorecard, and decision package must carry the tenant key, baseline ID, baseline content hash, projection version, source release ID, review policy version, and evidence references used to support the work.
Gate 01

Signal integrity

The opportunity originates from approved Knowledge/Cube evidence, not from a duplicate Source truth.

Gate 02

Evidence boundary

Unavailable, deferred, conflicting, or incomplete evidence is visible before artifact generation.

Gate 03

Workflow proof

Existing-contract optimization and new-event workflow both preserve stage state and human approvals.

Gate 04

Artifact quality

Generated work products are structured, reviewable, source-bound, and free of unsupported claims.

Gate 05

Analytics boundary

Custom dashboards, forensic analytics, and new connectors are not implied as standard scope.

Gate 06

Rollback

The prior or empty baseline can be restored and Source stops consuming the newer baseline.
