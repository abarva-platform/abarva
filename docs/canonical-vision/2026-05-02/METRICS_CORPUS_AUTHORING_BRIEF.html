Metrics — the gap engine's substrate.

For every industry, every functional domain, every theme — a structured set of metric records with north-star ranges, leader benchmarks, common floors, foundation requirements, vendor landscape, and pattern references. The corpus that makes the gap engine real.

Status Locked, 2026-05-02 Owner Knowledge Layer Codex lane Companion Canonical Design V2 · Cross-Module Brief
What this brief contains The structured contract for authoring 300-600 metric records across three industries (specialty retail, healthcare IDN, financial services) and three functional domains (front office, middle office, back office). The brief specifies the schema, the discipline, and the per-industry per-domain theme structure. The Knowledge Layer Codex authors content against this spec.

01Mission & scope

Author the metric corpus that powers AbarVa's gap engine. For each of the three pilot industries, for each functional domain (front office, middle office, back office), for each theme within a domain, surface the critical metrics that determine whether AI investments in that theme succeed or fail.

Each metric becomes a structured record carrying its definition, why it matters, north-star range at peer scale, leader benchmark, common floor, trajectory signals, foundation requirements, vendor/tooling landscape, common failure modes, and pattern references. The platform's gap engine reads these records and compares them against tenant-current state to surface quantitative gaps, measurement gaps, and trajectory gaps.

Scope numbers

For each of three industries, the brief specifies 4-8 themes per domain, with 8-12 metrics per theme. That's 32-96 metrics per industry per domain × 3 domains × 3 industries — somewhere between 288 and 864 records. Target: ~500 records at meaningful depth in the first authoring pass. Tier-1 priority is 200-300 records covering the highest-priority metrics for the three demo tenants.

Authoring sequence

  • Tier 1 (priority). Top 8-12 metrics per domain per industry. ~200-300 records. Powers the bounded version of the gap engine that ships first.
  • Tier 2. Remaining metrics for full theme coverage. ~200-300 more records. Ships against deeper corpus depth.
  • Tier 3. Vendor-specific metric variants and edge cases. ~100-200 records. Ships as authoring matures.

03One worked example

One full record at depth, to anchor the pattern. The Knowledge Layer Codex authors all 300-500 records to this depth and discipline.

PAT-MET-014 Forecast accuracy at SKU-week Tier 1 · Critical
Aliases
SKU forecast accuracy · SKU-level demand forecast accuracy · WMAPE at SKU-week
Industry · domain · theme
specialty retail · middle office · demand forecasting
Definition
The percentage accuracy of unit demand forecasts at SKU-week granularity, measured as 1 - WMAPE (Weighted Mean Absolute Percentage Error) over a rolling 13-week horizon. Weighted by sales volume so high-velocity SKUs dominate the metric. Window: rolling quarter against forward-looking forecasts.
Why it matters
Forecast accuracy is the leading indicator for inventory turnover, on-shelf availability, markdown spend, and working capital. A 5-point improvement in SKU-week accuracy typically translates to 3-7% reduction in safety stock, 1-2pt improvement in OSA, and 8-15% reduction in markdown depth. It is the single most leveraged middle-office AI metric in specialty retail.
Cohort
Specialty retail · $1-5B annual revenue · multi-channel (store + e-commerce) · 5,000-50,000 active SKUs
North-star range
88-92% (1 - WMAPE) at SKU-week, rolling quarter
Leader benchmark
83-88% · top-quartile peer cohort with mature demand sensing capabilities
Common floor
62-72% · bottom quartile · typical of organizations relying on legacy regression-based forecasting at category-week without SKU-week refinement
Trajectory signal
Peer leaders improving 1.5-3.0 points per year as they integrate demand sensing, weather signals, and marketing-event signals into the forecast pipeline. Laggards static or declining as SKU proliferation outpaces forecast capability.
Measurement difficulty
Moderate. Requires clean SKU-week sales history, point-of-sale data integration, returns netting, and lift-aware adjustment for promotional and marketing events. Most retailers measure forecast accuracy at category-week or department-month, which masks the SKU-level signal that actually drives operational decisions.
Foundation gaps
Data: POS data must reach the analytics warehouse at SKU-store-day grain. Lineage from POS → warehouse → BI must be auditable.
Identity: SKU master must be canonical and de-duplicated. Cross-channel SKU mapping (store SKU → e-commerce SKU) must be solved.
Ops: Forecast must update at least weekly to drive replenishment and allocation. Daily preferred for high-velocity SKUs.
Governance: Forecast accuracy must be reviewed at S&OP cadence with explicit accountability for category management.
Vendors relevant
Blue Yonder Demand Planning — typical leader-range outcomes when properly tuned; vendor lock-in risk on data model. (adjust for tenant: integration cost meaningful for non-BY supply chain estates)
o9 Solutions — strong integrated planning; reaches leader range in 12-18 months when implementation is disciplined
RELEX Solutions — specialty/grocery focus; strong on store-level demand sensing
SAS Demand Planning — established leader; reaches benchmark range; longer implementation cycles
Anaplan + custom forecasting — flexible but requires internal data science capability; outcomes vary widely
Pattern refs
PAT-FM-009 — Inability to measure outcomes (when forecast accuracy isn't tracked at SKU-week, AI demand forecasting moves can't be validated)
PAT-DT-014 — POS data integration gap
PAT-OPS-022 — S&OP cadence and accountability
PAT-RV-007 — Forecast-to-OSA causal chain
PAT-VEN-031 — Demand planning vendor evaluation framework
Refresh cadence
Annual · benchmarks shift slowly as the cohort's overall maturity rises. Vendor entries refresh semi-annually as vendor product evolves.
Corpus version
v1.0.0 · authored 2026-05-02

This is the depth and discipline expected for every Tier-1 metric. Tier-2 records can compress some fields (e.g., briefer vendor lists, shorter why_it_matters) but the schema is the same.

04Authoring discipline

Six rules the Knowledge Layer Codex follows when authoring metric records.

Rule 1 — Industry-standard definitions over invented ones

Every metric must use the canonical industry-standard definition. Forecast accuracy is 1 - WMAPE at SKU-week, not "how close were we." On-shelf availability is units available at SKU-store-day, not "stock-out rate" (which is the inverse and measured differently). When industry conventions vary, name the convention; don't pick arbitrarily.

Rule 2 — Cohort definitions that are specific

Benchmarks only mean something for a defined cohort. "Retail" is too broad — a $50M boutique and a $5B specialty retailer have different north-stars for the same metric. Specify cohort as industry + revenue scale + shape. The platform's gap engine matches tenant scale to the right cohort's benchmarks.

Rule 3 — North-star ranges, not point estimates

"88-92%" reads as honest data. "90%" reads as fabricated certainty. Always cite ranges. Where the data is genuinely thin, say so in the field — "north-star range based on 4-7 published case studies; treat as directional" — rather than inventing precision.

Rule 4 — Foundation gaps named explicitly

If the metric requires data lineage that most enterprises lack, name it. If governance and ops cadence matter, say so. The foundation_gaps field is what powers the gap engine's measurement-gap detection. Vague entries here produce vague gap surfacing.

Rule 5 — Vendor entries with adjustment notes

Don't just list vendors. For each vendor in the metric record, include adjustment notes — when the vendor reaches the leader range, what conditions are required, what the implementation cost typically is, what the vendor lock-in risks are. The platform's vendor landscape submenu reads these.

Rule 6 — Pattern references that connect

Every metric must reference the broader corpus: PAT-FM-* failure modes that affect this metric, PAT-DT-* data/tech patterns required to measure it, PAT-OPS-* operating model patterns, PAT-RV-* realized value patterns. The pattern_refs field is what makes the corpus a graph, not a flat catalog.

Authoring with an LLM as drafting partner Use Claude or GPT-5 as drafting partners for the first pass on each metric. Author with depth research first (industry articles, vendor case studies, peer disclosures, public benchmarks). Treat AI drafts as starting points; verify benchmarks against at least two independent sources before locking. Mark records as draft until verified, verified after independent confirmation, locked after founder review.

08The PAT-MET pattern domain

Each metric record lives at PAT-MET-NNN within the corpus. The PAT-MET-* domain is itself a first-class corpus pattern domain alongside PAT-FM-*, PAT-PRG-*, PAT-AI-*, etc.

How metrics connect to the broader corpus

Pattern domainRelationship to PAT-MET
PAT-FM-* failure modes Many failure modes (especially PAT-FM-009 inability-to-measure) reference specific metrics that, if absent, prevent measurement. Metric records reference back via pattern_refs.
PAT-DT-* data & tech Foundation gaps named in metric records map to PAT-DT-* records. The platform's foundation readiness workflow reads both.
PAT-OPS-* operating model Operating cadence and accountability requirements named in metric records map to PAT-OPS-* records.
PAT-VEN-* vendor patterns Vendor entries in metric records cross-reference vendor corpus records. Vendor records carry the metric capability claims; metric records carry the vendor adjustment notes.
PAT-RV-* realized value Realized value patterns describe how a metric improvement translates to outcomes. PAT-MET → PAT-RV is a one-to-many relationship.
PAT-XF-* cross-industry transfer Some metrics transfer across industries (e.g., AI productivity lift, voluntary turnover). Cross-industry transfer cases reference the source-industry metric record.

Numbering discipline

PAT-MET-NNN where NNN is a global sequential number across all metrics regardless of industry. Numbers don't restart per industry. Reserved blocks: 001-200 retail, 201-400 healthcare, 401-600 financial services, 601-800 cross-industry, 801-999 reserved.

09Delivery & reporting

Authoring workflow

  1. Read this brief plus Canonical Design V2 for context.
  2. Take an industry × domain (e.g., specialty retail × middle office) and produce 8-12 Tier-1 metric records following the schema.
  3. For each record, draft against the schema, verify benchmarks against at least two independent sources (industry reports, vendor case studies, peer disclosures, public benchmarks), mark as verified.
  4. Stage records in corpus/patterns/PAT-MET/ as YAML files (one per metric).
  5. Run the corpus validation script to confirm schema compliance.
  6. Auto-approve and merge batches (4-8 records per PR) once the standard checks pass — schema validation, benchmark sources cited, locks respected, EVENT_LOG updated. Records are marked verified on merge; locked status applies after a record has been retrieved by the platform without revision for 14 days, signaling stability.

Acceptance criteria

  • Schema compliance: every field present, types correct
  • Cohort defined explicitly (industry + scale + shape)
  • Benchmarks as ranges, not points; cited sources where possible
  • Foundation gaps named explicitly and tied to PAT-DT-* / PAT-OPS-* records
  • Vendor entries with adjustment notes (not just lists)
  • Pattern refs connecting to broader corpus
  • Refresh cadence specified

Reporting

Three reports when the authoring milestone is complete
METRICS_CORPUS_AUTHORING_REPORT.md
Coverage: industries × domains × themes covered. Records authored vs. target. Gaps remaining.

METRICS_BENCHMARK_VERIFICATION_LOG.md
For each benchmark, source(s) cited, verification confidence, refresh schedule established.

METRICS_CORPUS_PIPELINE_INTEGRATION.md
Records integrated into the codex pipeline, validation script outputs, samples of cross-references resolved.

Tier-1 milestone

Tier-1 milestone is reached when 200-300 records are authored, verified, locked, integrated into the corpus, and indexed for retrieval. The bounded gap engine ships against this milestone. Tier-2 and Tier-3 follow as ongoing authoring work.

Continuous authoring discipline Records flow from draft → verified → locked automatically. Draft on first authoring; verified when sources are confirmed and merged; locked after 14 days of stable retrieval without revision. The platform retrieves verified and locked records by default. The lane authors continuously without external approval gates; the maturation discipline lives in the records themselves.
