01The vision in one paragraph

AbarVa is the bet-level operating system for AI transformation. Strategic Moves is the registry of those bets; Source is where commitments get made on vendors and spend; Tower observes the portfolio's realized outcomes; Intelligence is the explore layer — where users go to think, see, and decide before committing.

Three substrates feed every Intelligence interaction: what we know about you (tenant context), what patterns exist (corpus), and what the art of the possible looks like for you (industry intelligence at peer scale, contextualized to your tenant). Two outcomes flow out of every Intelligence session: originate new bets, or validate existing ones. The 10 AI failure modes are the spine throughout.

Metrics are the gap engine. Front, middle, and back office each have a defined set of critical metrics. The corpus carries north-star ranges at peer scale. The tenant brings their own metrics — or reveals their absence. The platform compares tenant-current to north-star, surfaces the gaps quantitatively where measurement exists, surfaces the measurement gaps where it doesn't, and references corpus patterns for closing each one. Strategy ingestion ties uploaded strategies to the gaps that matter most.

The lock Bet-level operating system. Three substrates explored to produce two outcomes. Strategy operationalized, not generated. Metrics surface gaps; gaps drive origination and validation.

02Why this exists — the empirical anchor

Every serious analysis of AI failure — MIT Sloan, Gartner, McKinsey, BCG, federal program post-mortems — converges on the same patterns: sponsor disengagement, unclear success criteria, foundation gaps, talent gaps, operating-model resistance, governance blindness, vendor errors, pilot-to-production gaps, inability to measure outcomes, use-case sprawl.

Failures happen at the bet level. Nobody writes post-mortems blaming the parent strategy document. Failures happen where commitments and money meet reality. The platform's defensible value concentrates at the bet level, where the actual problem lives. Intelligence's role is upstream: making sure the bets that get committed are the right ones, well-shaped, with foundations in place, against the right patterns and metrics.

Inability to measure outcomes is one of the 10 failure modes. It is also, empirically, the single most common one. Programs ship; nobody knows whether they delivered. Promises are made; nobody settles them. Metrics-as-gap-engine is not a bonus capability — it is core to addressing the failure mode the corpus identifies most often.

McKinsey's February 2025 article reinforces this position. They name five challenges every enterprise must solve to capture the AI-in-strategy inflection: proprietary data ecosystems, signal-from-noise discipline, executive synthesis, process quality over insight quality, and platform-grade technology investment. AbarVa is built against those five challenges, applied to where the bets actually live.

The platform is layered. Setup is the substrate; Intelligence is the strategic thinking layer; Strategic Moves is the registry of committed bets; Source is where vendor and spend commitments get made; Tower observes the portfolio. Home is the personalized landing.

04Intelligence — the explore layer

Intelligence is where users go to think. Three substrates feed every answer; two outcomes flow from every session; metrics are the connective tissue that makes the substrates concrete and the outcomes measurable.

The three substrates

Substrate one — what we know about you (tenant context). Organization structure, IT landscape, financial profile, prior programs, change-failure record, existing bets, data inventory, uploaded strategy artifacts, and the tenant's current metrics — or the absence of them. Grounded in the private data plane.

Substrate two — what patterns exist (corpus knowledge). Patterns, anti-patterns, solution architectures, decision frameworks, vendor implementations, regulatory frames. And critical metrics per industry per functional domain, with north-star ranges at peer scale. Anchored to the program lifecycle, sourcing lifecycle, and the 10 failure modes.

Substrate three — what art of the possible looks like for you (industry intelligence). Industry intelligence views by functional domain (front office, middle office, back office). What's happening, what's measured, what good looks like, what failure looks like, where peers are leading and lagging. Contextualized to your tenant scale and shape.

The metrics-as-gap-engine For each industry, each functional domain, each theme, the corpus carries critical metrics with their north-star ranges. The tenant brings their own metrics. The platform compares tenant-current to north-star, surfaces measurement gaps where the metric should exist but doesn't, and references corpus patterns for closing each gap. This makes the explore layer concrete and operational, not just informational.

The two outcomes

Originate new bets. When exploration matures into a clear candidate bet — often anchored on a specific gap — the user registers it as a Strategic Move with the Intelligence conversation attached as origination evidence.

Validate existing bets. Existing Strategic Moves can be brought into Intelligence for foundation readiness, pre-mortem, pattern challenge, strategy alignment, or metric-grounded validation. Outputs flow back to the move's evidence ledger.

What's explicitly out of scope

  • Structured AI Strategy Definition workflow that produces enterprise AI strategy from scratch
  • Strategy team replacement claims; the platform supports human strategy work, does not replace it
  • Quarterbacking executive offsites — that's human partner work
  • The four-mode answer toggle as a primary UI control (auto-selected by Sentinel; debug only)

05The Atrium pattern

The platform's screen pattern is the Atrium pattern — our design language for an AI-native operating system. An atrium is a central space in a building where light comes from above, work happens around it, multiple floors look in. Translated to AbarVa: the agent zone is the atrium-light cast on the canvas; the canvas is the work surface where the light falls; the submenus are the rooms you traverse; the navigation is the building's circulation.

The pattern's three properties Persistent zones. Module nav, submenu strip, agent always available, canvas always primary.
Context-driven canvas. The canvas changes based on what the user is doing. Users don't navigate to a separate "chat" surface or "browse" surface — they stay in place; the canvas swaps.
Agent-mediated work. The agent is always there. Output materializes in the agent zone or, when substantial, expands into the canvas adjacent.

The chrome layout

Two horizontal strips at the top (top nav + horizontal submenu). Two vertical zones below (agent + canvas). The agent strip on the left is ambient by default — narrow, persistent, present. The canvas on the right is dominant — where users actually operate. Submenus are horizontal tabs above the work area.

06Three states of the agent zone

The agent zone is elastic. Three states: ambient (~200px, default), engaged (~360px, active dialogue), focus (~520px, deep thinking session). Static layouts force every state into the same proportions; dynamic resize lets each be optimal.

State 1 — ambient (default)
Sentinel ~200px · canvas ~85%

Browsing the canvas. Sentinel is glanceable, available but not stealing focus.

State 2 — engaged (active dialogue)
Sentinel ~360px · canvas reflows

Active dialogue. Sentinel grows; canvas reflows to single-column without losing content.

State 3 — focus (deep thinking session)
Sentinel ~520px · canvas reference panel

Deep thinking. Sentinel dominates; canvas becomes a glanceable reference panel showing only the active artifact. Triggered explicitly only.

State transition triggers Ambient → engaged: user clicks composer, types, or Sentinel surfaces substantive output. Auto-back after 30s inactivity.
Engaged → focus: explicit user action only — chevron click or "enter thinking session." Never auto-triggered.
Any state → module switch: agent state preserved in background. Returning restores prior conversation.

07Click-through behavior

The Atrium pattern's whole value is in what stays put when the user navigates. Strategic thinking is multi-context work; the agent's state must persist across canvas navigation, the submenu's state must persist across agent engagement, and the conversation must never reset because the user clicked somewhere.

User actionWhat changesWhat persists
Click submenu tab Active tab indicator moves; canvas swaps entirely Top nav, Sentinel state (conversation, scroll, composer), agent zone width
Click agent composer / type Sentinel expands ambient → engaged; canvas reflows Top nav, submenu strip, canvas content (just reflowed)
Agent surfaces substantive output Sentinel auto-expands; canvas may receive published cards Submenu, prior canvas content (augmented, not replaced)
Click chevron / enter focus Sentinel takes ~520px; canvas becomes ~100px reference panel Submenu strip and top nav unchanged
Switch module (Intel → Moves) Submenu strip replaced; canvas replaced; agent replaced Prior agent state preserved in background; returning restores it

Browser URL updates on submenu navigation (e.g., /intel/patterns) for shareable deep-linking.

08The seven submenus

1. Today

Purpose: Sentinel's curated entry-state — what's worth your attention right now. Default landing.

Canvas: Three pressure cards ranked by leverage (HIGH/MEDIUM/WATCH), pending-your-decision section, recent-activity section, top metric gaps surfaced from the gap engine.

Sentinel ambient brief: Italic synthesis. "Three things matter today. Vendor C is one signature from unblocking AMS-CDP-2026. Two metric gaps in middle office crossed your action threshold this week."

2. By function

Purpose: Flagship explore-layer experience. Industry intelligence by functional domain, with metric gaps overlaid.

Canvas: Three large domain tiles — Front office, Middle office, Back office — tenant-tailored to industry. Click → theme list at depth → deep-dive with corpus-grounded patterns and metric gap visualization.

Tenant tailoring: Domain themes specific to the tenant's industry. Specialty retail middle office: demand forecasting, replenishment, price/promo, inventory allocation, store labor. Healthcare IDN middle office: ambient documentation, clinical decision support, prior auth, coding quality, population health. Financial services middle office: credit decisioning, fraud detection, AML compliance, market risk.

3. Patterns

Purpose: Corpus pattern catalog. Browseable, filterable, deep-linkable.

Canvas: Filter bar (archetype · failure mode · industry · function), pattern list with mono pattern IDs, click for deep-dive.

Pattern domains: PAT-FM-* (failure modes), PAT-PRG-*, PAT-AI-*, PAT-DT-*, PAT-OPS-*, PAT-VEN-*, PAT-REG-*, PAT-IND-*, PAT-RV-*, PAT-XF-*, and PAT-MET-* (metrics — new).

4. Vendors

Purpose: Vendor landscape intelligence — who plays in what space, at what scale, with what realized outcomes.

Canvas: Filter bar, vendor list, click for deep-dive showing typical pricing, contract patterns, integration patterns, common over-promises, settled outcomes archive.

5. Peer activity

Purpose: What peers in your industry at your scale are doing — anonymized aggregate.

Canvas: Activity feed at anonymized aggregate. "4 of 14 specialty retailers at your scale started ambient documentation pilots in Q1." Filter by domain, event type, pattern, time window.

6. My strategy

Purpose: Where the customer's uploaded AI strategy lives, gets structured, links to registered moves, and gets compared to industry peer patterns AND to the metric gap profile.

Canvas: If no strategy: empty-state with upload affordance. If uploaded: artifact summary, themes section with linked moves and alignment status, empirical challenge section (where strategy assumptions diverge from realized portfolio outcomes), comparative landscape section (where strategy aligns or diverges from peer patterns and from the gap engine's priorities).

7. Sessions

Purpose: Persistent thinking sessions — multi-day strategic thinking that doesn't reset.

Canvas: Session list with name, date, brief summary, candidate moves count, tags. Click to return to session in same state. Multi-user sessions shareable.

09Dynamic right-canvas during chat

The agent zone and the canvas are coupled when Sentinel is engaged in dialogue. Sentinel can publish content to the canvas dynamically based on relevance.

Sentinel publishes to canvas
Pattern citation example

Sentinel mentions PAT-FM-009 in conversation. Canvas highlights that pattern with a thicker border. Conversation and browsing reinforce each other.

What Sentinel can publish to the canvas

  • Pattern citations. Pattern surfaced; canvas highlights or surfaces snippet preview.
  • Document links. Reference card with link, source citation, provenance stamp.
  • Synthesis summaries. Multi-step synthesis as structured card with pinnable points.
  • Candidate move scaffolds. Move-seed cards ready to register.
  • Decision frames. Structured decision card with criteria, evidence, recommendation rationale.
  • Foundation readiness assessments. Gaps, risks, recommendations as structured output.
  • Strategy-to-bet alignment overlays. Comparison with structured callouts.
  • Metric gap surfaces. Specific gap with tenant-current vs. north-star, with corpus pattern references for closing.

10Two outcomes — originate and validate

Origination flow

  1. Candidate identification. Sentinel summarizes problem, value hypothesis, target outcome, foundation considerations, relevant patterns, relevant metric gaps.
  2. Move scaffold. Canvas renders move-seed card with candidate populated.
  3. Confirmation. User reviews, edits, confirms.
  4. Registration. Strategic Move created in P0 with Intelligence session attached as evidence.
  5. Handoff. Nexus picks up at P0 with strategic context already loaded.

Validation flow

  1. Move selection. User selects registered move and chooses "validate in Intelligence."
  2. Context loading. Move's phase, archetype, promise contract, evidence ledger loaded.
  3. Validation type. Foundation Readiness · Pre-mortem · Pattern challenge · Strategy alignment · Metric grounding · Open exploration.
  4. Working session. Persistent session anchored to the move. Sentinel runs analysis.
  5. Outputs. Gaps identified, risks surfaced, recommendations made; outputs flow to evidence ledger.
  6. Decision. Accept · modify design · kill · escalate · originate remediation move (often a foundation-or-measurement-readiness move).

11Metrics & the gap engine

Metrics are the connective tissue between corpus and tenant. The corpus carries critical metrics per industry per functional domain with north-star ranges at peer scale. The tenant brings their current metrics — or reveals their absence. The platform compares, surfaces gaps, and references corpus patterns for closing each one.

The gap engine, conceptually

The gap engine — three flows, three outputs
Corpus + tenant → comparison → gaps

Two inputs (corpus metrics + tenant metrics) feed the gap engine, which produces three classes of gap: quantitative (where measurement exists but trails), measurement (where the metric should exist but doesn't), and trajectory (where rate of improvement lags peer leaders).

What the user sees — the gap surface

In the By function submenu, each domain tile shows a gap-density indicator. Click into a domain → theme list shows critical metrics with tenant gaps overlaid. Click a metric → deep-dive shows tenant-current value, north-star range, peer trajectory, and corpus pattern references for closing the gap.

Gap-driven origination

When a user explores a metric gap, Sentinel can surface: "This gap typically closes through three move archetypes — here are the three relevant to your situation. Want me to scaffold one as a candidate Strategic Move with this gap as anchor?" The metric gap becomes a natural origination trigger, and the resulting move carries the gap into its P2 promise contract as a target metric.

Strategy-to-gap alignment

When a customer uploads their AI strategy, the platform compares the strategy's emphasis to the gap profile. Strategies that emphasize gaps the platform also identifies as high-priority get an "aligned with gap engine" annotation. Strategies that emphasize themes where the tenant has minimal gaps get a "consider re-balancing toward higher-leverage gaps" honest annotation.

What the corpus needs to carry

For metrics-as-gap-engine to work, the corpus carries structured records per metric: name, definition, why it matters, measurement difficulty, north-star range, common floor, leader benchmark, trajectory signal, common failure modes in measurement, foundation gaps preventing measurement, vendor/tooling landscape, realized-value patterns. See METRICS_CORPUS_AUTHORING_BRIEF.html for the full authoring specification.

12What ships first vs. later

Ships first (bounded version)

  • Open thinking mode
  • Foundation Readiness as accessible workflow
  • Move origination from conversations
  • Persistent thinking sessions
  • Cross-portfolio reasoning across registered moves
  • Strategy ingestion (uses existing upload)
  • Today / Patterns / Sessions submenus with bounded content
  • Tenant metric ingestion (manual entry plus CSV upload) for the top 20-30 metrics per industry
  • Gap computation for ingested metrics against tier-1 corpus metric records

Ships against deeper corpus depth (full vision)

  • Browsable industry views by functional domain at depth — requires tier 2 corpus
  • Vendor landscape intelligence at depth — requires tier 3 vendor-specific content
  • Peer activity feed — requires cross-tenant aggregate computation infrastructure
  • Strategy ingestion's empirical challenge logic — requires accumulated realized-value data
  • Sentinel's dynamic canvas-publication capability at full coverage
  • Full metric corpus (300-600 metric records across three industries)
  • Automated metric ingestion via integration to BI / data warehouse / KPI dashboards

Future-state, explicitly out of scope

  • Structured AI Strategy Definition flagship workflow
  • Move Pre-mortem as guided structured workflow
  • Cross-Industry Transfer as guided structured workflow
  • Portfolio Health Check as guided structured workflow

13Pattern application across modules

The Atrium pattern is platform-wide chrome. All six modules share the same top-nav, submenu strip, agent zone, and canvas structure. Each module specifies its own submenu list, agent identity, and canvas content. Detailed per-module specifications are in ATRIUM_PATTERN_CROSS_MODULE_BRIEF.html.

The pattern's discipline holds across all six. Persistent zones, context-driven canvas, agent-mediated work. Submenu clicks swap canvas; agent state preserves; cross-module navigation preserves prior agent state in background. The user moves through the platform fluidly without losing thread.

Codex implication Implementing the Atrium pattern in one module requires building the chrome correctly once. After that, each additional module reuses the chrome and specifies its own submenu list, agent identity, and canvas content. The cost compounds favorably: the first module is the largest investment; every subsequent module is cheaper.
