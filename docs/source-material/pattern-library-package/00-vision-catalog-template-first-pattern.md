## Part 1 · AbarVa Vision

### 1.1 · What AbarVa is

AbarVa is an AI-native enterprise transformation platform. It pairs a curated library of authored patterns with a four-agent intelligence system that runs enterprise AI programs through six disciplined phases, produces decision-grade deliverables at three defined tiers, and reconciles projected outcomes against realized outcomes at program close.

The product is not a SaaS subscription, not a consulting engagement, not a generic AI assistant. It is a **commitment architecture** between AbarVa and each enterprise client — the client contributes observations and outcomes; AbarVa commits to pattern library evolution and outcome accountability; together they build compounding intelligence that no consulting firm or single-vendor AI platform can replicate.

### 1.2 · What AbarVa stands for

Five commitments that sit above every product decision:

**Intelligence over interfaces.** The Fabric — patterns plus agents plus knowledge layer — is the product. The UI renders the Fabric but is not the Fabric. Pattern authoring quality, agent reasoning fidelity, and knowledge layer compounding are the durable investments. Pretty screens without these are theater.

**Honest over confident.** Sparse retrieval is surfaced honestly ("evidence on this is thin"). Pattern citations carry explicit confidence grades. Measured observations are distinguished from authored-from-industry-knowledge content. The platform earns trust through epistemic rigor, not through confident-sounding hallucination.

**Outcomes over outputs.** Every program ends in Phase 6 Verify with dual-ledger reconciliation — projected value versus realized value. Decks without measured outcomes are incomplete. Consulting firms deliver outputs; AbarVa commits to outcomes.

**Compounding over accumulating.** The knowledge layer learns from every engagement. Anonymized observations flow back to pattern authoring. Every subsequent client benefits from prior clients' contributions. The library grows sharper quarterly. This is the structural difference between a platform and a service.

**Client-safe over vendor-convenient.** Tenant isolation is architectural, not contractual. Client data lives in the client's cloud when the client requires it. Claude runs with zero retention. Provenance flows through every citation. No cross-tenant leakage under any circumstance.

### 1.3 · What AbarVa delivers

The platform delivers six compounding capabilities:

**One — Maestro Intake.** Conversational front door with Nexus. Accepts a client's problem in natural language. Classifies against the pattern library (exact match, partial match, no match). Produces GO / REFINE / REDIRECT outcome within a single conversation. Replaces six weeks of consulting-firm scoping with minutes.

**Two — Six-Phase Program Execution.** Every program runs through Origination → Charter → Diagnose → Design → Execute → Verify with four hard gates. Each phase has expected deliverables, gate criteria, and CXO touchpoints. The discipline is non-negotiable; the composition of modules within phases is programmable per archetype.

**Three — Four-Agent Intelligence.** Nexus runs programs (maestro-collegial voice). Sentinel holds the pattern library (research-rigorous). Atlas reads the portfolio Tower (executive-concise). Steward enforces operational integrity (operationally-terse). Each agent has a defined zone, a voice contract, and a role-specific retrieval scope.

**Four — Three-Tier Deliverables.** Rich (12 components, hero artifacts), Outline (8 components, substantive), Stub (6 components, dignified scheduled placeholders). Every deliverable carries a tier badge. No placeholder strings, no em-dash-without-context, no "coming soon" text.

**Five — Five-Dimension Control Tower.** Portfolio-level view across Use Cases, Contradictions, Unowned Risks, Spend Trajectory, Last-Turn-Ago. Atlas composes editorial across the five. Pressure cards surface specific dollar-value issues with decision prompts. Drill-ins resolve to specific programs and deliverables.

**Six — Dual-Ledger Outcome Reconciliation.** Phase 6 Verify produces a reconciliation between Phase 4 projected value and Phase 6 realized value. Variance gets attributed (what worked, what didn't, external factors). Outcome-as-a-service commercial terms attach to this ledger.

### 1.4 · What AbarVa deliberately refuses

Seven things the platform structurally will not do:

**Generic chat.** AbarVa agents do not answer arbitrary questions outside program context. They are not a replacement for ChatGPT or Claude.ai.

**Unprovenanced claims.** Every substantive claim in every agent response carries a citation. No claim without citation. Agents refuse to fabricate citations when retrieval is sparse.

**Cross-tenant leakage.** No client's data ever reaches another client's retrieval surface. Anonymization at the pattern-authoring boundary is architectural.

**Outcome theater.** Phase 6 reconciliation is real measurement, not favorable narrative. If a program underperformed projections, the ledger shows it.

**Pattern velocity at quality cost.** Better to ship 30 patterns at 8/10 than 100 patterns at 5/10. Quality gates are non-negotiable.

**Consulting-firm deck production.** AbarVa deliverables are structured artifacts with citations, decision frames, and reconciliation commitments. Not PowerPoints.

**Vendor lock-in through data hostage.** Client can leave with their full data. Pattern IP stays with AbarVa; client observations stay with the client.

## Part 2 · Pattern Catalog

### 2.1 · What patterns are

A pattern is AbarVa's authored IP unit. A pattern synthesizes fragmented industry knowledge, research, regulatory context, vendor landscape, and observed outcomes into a single structured document that an agent can retrieve, cite, and reason against on every turn.

Patterns are not datasets. Patterns are not templates. Patterns are not playbooks. Patterns are authored frameworks with specific structural components (identity, detection signals, diagnostic questions, interventions, observations) that sit at the intersection of research rigor and executable intelligence.

**Three properties define a pattern:**

**Structural consistency.** Every pattern follows the canonical template (Part 3 of this document). This makes patterns retrievable, citable, and comparable.

**Provenance transparency.** Every claim in a pattern carries explicit provenance — authored from research, observed across deployments, composite aggregation, regulatory citation. The reader knows where each element came from.

**Evolutionary discipline.** Patterns improve through three mechanisms — customer program contributions (Phase 6 outcomes), feedback capture from investor and prospect conversations, industry signal monitoring. Version history is transparent.

### 2.2 · Three pattern tiers

AbarVa's library organizes patterns by abstraction level.

**Tier M — Meta patterns.** AbarVa's own operating model formalized as patterns. Six meta-patterns anchor everything else. These describe how AbarVa thinks enterprise AI transformation should be structured.

**Tier 1 — Craft patterns.** Universal capabilities that apply across verticals (AI Governance, Vendor Sprawl Rationalization, Use Case Portfolio Management, Analytics Modernization, AI-led Product Development Lifecycle). Five to eight patterns at tier-one depth. These are consulting-practice-area equivalents.

**Tier 2 — Capability patterns.** Functional capabilities that apply across industries but are more specific than Craft (Vendor Evaluation, Solution Architecture Alignment, Operating Model Selection, Estimation & Value Quantification, Change Management for AI). Ten to fifteen patterns.

**Tier 3 — Use-case patterns.** Industry-specific or function-specific applications (Ambient Clinical Value Chain Automation, Owned-Brand Margin Recovery, AI Underwriting Modernization, Regulatory Reporting Automation, Demand Forecasting). Fifteen to thirty patterns across three target verticals plus cross-industry.

## Part 3 · Pattern Template

### 3.1 · Why a canonical template matters

Every pattern follows the same structural template. This is non-negotiable for three reasons.

**Retrieval consistency.** Agents retrieve across the library on every turn. Consistent structure lets agents find "detection signals for this situation" or "interventions with evidence" regardless of which pattern is being queried. Inconsistent structure breaks retrieval quality.

**Citation composability.** When Nexus cites three patterns in a response, each citation renders with the same structural semantics. The user trusts citations because they're structurally consistent. Inconsistency erodes credibility.

**Authoring velocity.** A canonical template makes authoring faster over time. Each new pattern inherits the structure and the author focuses on domain content, not document architecture.

### 3.2 · The canonical pattern structure

Every pattern has ten sections. Sections are labeled A through J. Every section is required; sections can be short when evidence is thin but cannot be skipped.

**Section A · Identity**
- Pattern name (canonical, unambiguous)
- Pattern slug (URL-safe identifier)
- Tier (M, 1, 2, or 3)
- Vertical (healthcare, financial-services, retail, cross-industry)
- One-sentence thesis (the pattern's core claim in under 30 words)
- Applicability (when this pattern applies and when it doesn't)
- Regulatory chips (HIPAA, GDPR, SOX, PCI-DSS, EU AI Act, etc.)

**Section B · Context**
- Why this pattern exists (the industry situation that produces the need)
- Adjacent patterns (related patterns in the library — explicit links)
- Predecessor patterns (patterns that typically precede this one in a program)
- Successor patterns (patterns that typically follow)
- Author declaration (authored from industry knowledge / measured across deployments / composite)

**Section C · Detection Signals**
- Six to eight signals that indicate this pattern applies
- Each signal with a description, typical evidence, confidence tier
- Signals organized from strongest (near-certain match) to weakest (possible match)
- Anti-signals (what indicates this pattern does NOT apply)

**Section D · Diagnostic Questions**
- Six to eight questions an agent asks to confirm pattern applicability
- Each question with expected answer ranges and what each range indicates
- Designed for Maestro Intake or Phase 3 CXO interview use

**Section E · Interventions**
- Six to eight levers that move outcomes when this pattern applies
- Each intervention with expected impact, typical difficulty, prerequisite conditions
- Success rate where known (or explicit "authored, not measured")
- Intervention sequencing (dependencies between interventions)

**Section F · Evidence Base**
- Research sources that inform the pattern (cited with provenance)
- Regulatory references where applicable
- Case studies (anonymized where client-derived)
- Quantitative benchmarks with methodology
- Explicit confidence per source category

**Section G · Observations**
- Six-plus structured observations from real program experience
- Each observation with composite tag per File 01 canonical disclaimer
- Each observation carries: situation, action taken, outcome, variance from projection, lesson
- Observations grow over time as programs complete Phase 6

**Section H · Failure Modes**
- Four to six ways this pattern fails in execution
- Each failure mode with detection signal and remediation
- Drawn from File 01 failure modes where applicable
- Framed as "what to watch for" not "what goes wrong" (agent voice)

**Section I · Cross-References**
- Links to related Craft patterns (Tier 1)
- Links to relevant Capability patterns (Tier 2)
- Links to industry-specific Use-case patterns (Tier 3)
- Links to deliverables that typically cite this pattern
- Links to programs using this pattern (tenant-scoped in production)

**Section J · Authoring Metadata**
- Authored by (AbarVa team member or contracted expert)
- Authoring date
- Reviewed by (peer review)
- Expert validated by (domain expert, if AUTHORED-EXPERT)
- Last updated
- Version number
- Status (AUTHORED-EXPERT / AUTHORED-REVIEWED / AUTHORED-DRAFT / etc.)
- Change log (what changed in each version)

### 3.3 · How agents consume patterns

Every agent turn executes the eight-stage per-turn contract from File 08. Patterns flow into that contract at Stage 3 (Fabric attachment) and Stage 4 (composition).

**At Stage 3 Fabric retrieval.** The agent's retrieval query runs semantic match against all patterns in the knowledge layer. Retrieval returns matching patterns with confidence scores, match reasons, and provenance.

**At Stage 4 composition.** The composed prompt inserts retrieved pattern sections into Claude's context. Not the full pattern — the sections relevant to the query. Typical composition for Nexus pressure-testing a program decision might include Section A (identity) + Section C (detection signals) + Section E (interventions) + relevant Section G observations. The composition engine selects sections based on query intent.

**At Stage 5 Claude invocation.** Claude receives the composed prompt with pattern sections labeled and cited. The agent's system prompt instructs Claude to cite patterns verbatim when making claims.

**At Stage 6 response assembly.** Citations in Claude's output resolve to pattern IDs. Each citation renders as a clickable pill or superscript. Confidence grades from Section C or F propagate to citation rendering.

**At Stage 7 UI rendering.** Pattern citations render inline in prose. Click navigates to full pattern page. Evidence drawer shows Section F and Section G in structured detail.

**At Stage 8 feedback logging.** Every turn that cites a pattern logs the citation. This feeds pattern improvement — patterns cited frequently get prioritized for deepening; patterns never cited get reviewed for removal or consolidation.

### 3.4 · How patterns load into the data layer

Patterns are authored as structured Markdown documents but persist across four knowledge-layer stores (per File 03 architecture). The loading pipeline runs the seven-stage ingest from File 11 (scope) with pattern-specific handling.

**Pattern authoring output.** A pattern authored per Section 3.2 above is a single Markdown file with YAML frontmatter containing Section A identity fields and Section J metadata.

**Stage one — commit to authoring repository.** Pattern file commits to `/patterns/` directory in AbarVa's authoring environment. Version control is git; history is authoritative.

**Stage two — schema validation.** Automated check that all ten sections exist, YAML frontmatter is valid, required fields are populated. Fails if schema is violated; author fixes.

**Stage three — registry population.** Section A identity fields write to the Postgres `patterns` table. Primary key, slug, tier, vertical, thesis, applicability, regulatory chips. This is the canonical pattern index.

**Stage four — structured field extraction.** Section C signals, Section D questions, Section E interventions, Section F evidence sources, Section G observations, Section H failure modes each write to dedicated Postgres tables with foreign keys to the pattern. This enables structured queries ("find all patterns with signal matching X" or "find all interventions with impact > Y").

**Stage five — graph relationship construction.** Section I cross-references write to the graph store as typed edges — `RELATED_TO`, `PRECEDES`, `FOLLOWS`, `APPLIES_TO_INDUSTRY`. These edges power cross-pattern navigation and retrieval expansion.

**Stage six — vector embedding.** Each pattern section gets embedded individually (Section A gets one embedding, Section C signals get one embedding each, etc.). Embeddings write to the vector store with metadata linking back to the pattern and section. This supports granular semantic retrieval — a query about "detection signals for vendor overlap" can retrieve the specific Section C block from the Vendor Sprawl pattern without retrieving the full pattern.

**Stage seven — provenance tagging and replication.** Pattern gets tagged with its provenance (authored, version, authoring team, expert-validated-by). If this is a tenant deployment's knowledge layer, the pattern is marked as replicated-from-AbarVa-authoring. Replication carries version for later sync.

**Total loading time for a new pattern.** Fifteen to thirty minutes end-to-end including embedding generation. Patterns can be re-loaded idempotently when updated — Stage two onward re-runs; registry entries update; embeddings regenerate; cross-references refresh.

### 3.5 · How patterns get consumed by the Fabric

Three consumption pathways.

**Pathway A — Direct citation in agent response.** Nexus pressure-tests a program decision. Retrieval returns three patterns matching the context. Nexus composes a response citing the strongest pattern (Section E intervention mapped to the decision). Citation renders as `[pattern: vendor-sprawl-rationalization · HIGH]`. User clicks citation, lands on pattern detail page, sees full Section E.

**Pathway B — Composite evidence in deliverable.** A Phase 4 Business Case deliverable cites multiple patterns in its justification prose. Each citation renders as superscript `[E3]` style linking to evidence drawer. Drawer shows Section F evidence sources per cited pattern.

**Pathway C — Pattern-driven workflow orchestration.** Maestro Intake matches a client's problem to a pattern via Section A identity and Section C signals. The matched pattern's Section E interventions map to the 17-module library. Module composition for the program inherits from the pattern match.

### Section A · Identity

**Pattern name:** The Six-Phase Engagement Architecture

**Pattern slug:** `six-phase-engagement-architecture`

**Tier:** M (Meta — AbarVa's own operating model)

**Vertical:** Cross-industry (applies universally)

**Thesis:** Every enterprise AI program runs through six disciplined phases — Origination, Charter, Diagnose, Design, Execute, Verify — with four hard gates between phases and exactly two CXO touchpoints per program. This architecture produces decision-grade deliverables and measured outcomes where traditional consulting engagements produce decks without accountability.

**Applicability:** This pattern applies to every AbarVa engagement. No program executes without this phase structure. The module composition within phases varies by archetype (Strategic Transformation, Workflow Automation, Platform Modernization, AI Product Enablement, Operational Optimization); the phase structure itself is invariant.

**Does not apply to:** Ad-hoc advisory conversations, point-solution AI deployments without transformation scope, internal tool evaluations without programmatic commitment.

**Regulatory chips:** None at the pattern level. Regulatory requirements attach to specific programs based on industry and use case.

### Section B · Context

**Why this pattern exists.** Traditional consulting engagements have no phase discipline. A $2M McKinsey engagement might produce a "current state assessment" deliverable, a "future state vision" deliverable, and a "roadmap" deliverable — but the transitions between these are soft, the gates are absent, and the relationship between stages is implicit in the partner's judgment rather than structural. Clients get decks; they do not get decision frames with accountability.

AbarVa's six-phase architecture is a deliberate inversion. Phase boundaries are hard. Gate criteria are explicit. CXO engagement points are exactly two (not three, not zero). The architecture forces rigor at each transition and produces an auditable trail of decisions with their underlying evidence.

This pattern also exists because every other meta-pattern in AbarVa's library inherits from it. M2 (module composition) depends on phase assignment. M3 (agent division) anchors agent work to specific phases. M5 (deliverable tiers) depends on phase-specific deliverable expectations. M6 (dual-ledger outcome) runs at Phase 6 against Phase 4 projections. Without M1 authored, none of the others have a coherent home.

**Adjacent patterns:**
- M2 · Seventeen-Module Program Composition (how modules populate phases)
- M3 · Four-Agent Division of Labor (how agents engage with phases)
- M5 · Three-Tier Deliverable System (what each phase produces)
- M6 · Dual-Ledger Outcome Reconciliation (Phase 6 mechanics)

**Predecessor patterns:** None. This is a foundational meta-pattern.

**Successor patterns:** Every other pattern in the library assumes and references this pattern.

### Section C · Detection Signals

Six signals that indicate a program needs this phase architecture applied. Ordered strongest (near-certain match) to weakest (possible match).

**Signal C-1 — HIGH.** Enterprise has an AI transformation initiative of meaningful scope (budget >$500K, cross-functional impact, executive sponsorship). Phase discipline is required whenever the program crosses function boundaries.

**Signal C-2 — HIGH.** Prior consulting engagements on similar scope produced decks without measured outcomes. Client pain with "we got a strategy but not results" is a direct match.

**Signal C-3 — HIGH.** Program requires CXO-level decisions at multiple stages (scope, design, go-live). Without structured CXO touchpoints, decisions drift into informal channels.

**Signal C-4 — MEDIUM.** Program has external accountability (board visibility, investor commitment, regulatory commitment). Hard phase gates produce auditable trail matching accountability requirements.

**Signal C-5 — MEDIUM.** Program spans multiple vendors, systems, or stakeholder groups where consensus is required but hard to force. Phase gates force decision convergence.

**Signal C-6 — LOW.** Program requires ongoing outcome tracking post-deployment. Phase 6 Verify provides the measurement mechanism.

**Anti-signals (this pattern does NOT apply):**
- Program is a point-solution tool deployment (e.g., "install Salesforce Einstein") without transformation scope
- Program is a quick assessment or audit (<4 weeks, no execution) — use lightweight advisory mode instead
- Program is internal AbarVa work that doesn't follow client engagement mechanics

### Section D · Diagnostic Questions

Six questions an agent asks during Maestro Intake or Phase 3 CXO interview to confirm pattern applicability and tune phase depth.

**D-1.** "What is the business outcome you're committing to measure at the end of this program?"
- Expected answer: Specific measurable outcome with baseline and target.
- If absent: Phase 6 Verify has nothing to measure — this is a scoping gap, not a reason to skip phases.

**D-2.** "Who are the executive sponsors and how much of their time can we secure for Phase 3 and Phase 4 touchpoints?"
- Expected answer: Named CXO(s) with 4-8 hours committed across the two touchpoints.
- If absent: Program lacks authority to land decisions — the two-CXO-touchpoint model breaks.

**D-3.** "Have prior AI initiatives in this area produced expected outcomes? If not, what failed?"
- Expected answer: Honest accounting of prior failures — adoption gaps, integration gaps, governance gaps, or outcome measurement gaps.
- Tells the agent which phase to emphasize (Charter scoping, Design validation, Execute change management).

**D-4.** "What regulatory or audit requirements constrain this program?"
- Expected answer: Specific frameworks (HIPAA, SOX, EU AI Act, sector-specific).
- Determines hard-gate evidence requirements and Phase 6 measurement rigor.

**D-5.** "What is the latest this program can produce measurable outcomes?"
- Expected answer: Specific date with business rationale.
- Drives phase-duration allocation and archetype selection.

**D-6.** "What existing programs or initiatives does this connect to or compete with?"
- Expected answer: Related programs with dependencies or conflicts.
- Feeds Portfolio view (M4 Control Tower) and informs module composition.

### Section E · Interventions

The six phases themselves are the intervention. Each phase has a defined purpose, expected deliverables, and gate criterion.

**Phase 1 · Origination**

*Purpose:* Capture the problem, match to pattern library, produce GO / REFINE / REDIRECT outcome.

*Typical modules:* Problem Framing (M2-01). Possibly Stakeholder Map (M2-02) if scope is contested.

*Deliverables:* One-page program brief with archetype classification.

*Gate criterion (soft):* Client agrees to proceed to Charter phase.

*Duration:* Hours to days.

*Agent role:* Nexus leads Maestro Intake conversation. Sentinel matches patterns in parallel. No Atlas or Steward engagement.

**Phase 2 · Charter**

*Purpose:* Establish scope, stakeholders, success criteria, baseline data requirements. Produce signed program charter.

*Typical modules:* Stakeholder Map (M2-02), Success Criteria Definition (M2-03), Baseline Data Request (M2-04).

*Deliverables:* Program Charter (Outline tier minimum, Rich tier for hero programs).

*Gate criterion (HARD GATE 1):* Charter signed by named executive sponsor.

*Duration:* One to three weeks.

*Agent role:* Nexus drafts charter from pattern match. Sponsor reviews. Steward ensures provenance on baseline data requests.

**Phase 3 · Diagnose**

*Purpose:* Run diagnostic against baseline data. Conduct CXO interview. Surface contradictions between stated process and actual data. Produce findings.

*Typical modules:* Diagnostic Instrument (M2-05), Data Analysis + Findings (M2-06), Contradiction Surface (M2-07), CXO Interview Prep + Capture (M2-08).

*Deliverables:* Diagnostic Findings Report (Rich tier for programs with high-stakes decisions downstream, Outline otherwise).

*Gate criterion (HARD GATE 2):* CXO interview completed before findings publish.

*CXO touchpoint 1:* 60-90 minute structured interview by Nexus with CXO sponsor. Contradictions surfaced. Findings reviewed.

*Duration:* Two to six weeks.

*Agent role:* Nexus leads interview preparation and synthesis. Sentinel runs pattern matching against findings. Atlas monitors if findings surface portfolio-level issues. Steward ensures citation provenance on all findings.

**Phase 4 · Design**

*Purpose:* Match findings to solution library. Evaluate vendors or build paths. Produce tradeoff matrix and business case. Validate with CXO. Obtain design approval.

*Typical modules:* Solution Library Match (M2-09), Vendor/Tech Evaluation (M2-10), Tradeoff Matrix + Recommendation (M2-11), Business Case + ROI (M2-12).

*Deliverables:* Decision Memo (Rich tier required — this is the hero deliverable for most programs), Business Case (Rich tier).

*Gate criterion (HARD GATE 3):* Design approved with projected value documented in dual ledger.

*CXO touchpoint 2:* 60-90 minute design validation by Nexus with CXO sponsor. Tradeoffs reviewed. Projected value confirmed.

*Duration:* Three to eight weeks.

*Agent role:* Nexus drives design synthesis and recommendation. Sentinel provides pattern-evidence backing for recommendations. Atlas integrates design into portfolio view. Steward locks decision provenance.

**Phase 5 · Execute**

*Purpose:* Implement the design. Track build progress. Manage change. Catch execution drift early.

*Typical modules:* Implementation Plan (M2-13), Build + Integration Tracking (M2-14), Change Management Plan (M2-15).

*Deliverables:* Execution dashboard (dynamic), change management artifacts (tier varies by scope).

*Gate criterion (soft):* Execution milestones achieved per plan with acceptable variance.

*Duration:* Weeks to months to quarters depending on program scope.

*Agent role:* Nexus monitors progress against plan. Sentinel surfaces emerging patterns (new contradictions, unexpected vendor behavior). Atlas maintains portfolio-level view of execution risks. Steward enforces change documentation.

**Phase 6 · Verify**

*Purpose:* Measure realized outcomes against Phase 4 projections. Document variance. Attribute causes. Contribute observations to pattern library.

*Typical modules:* Outcome Measurement (M2-16), Benefits Realization + Genome Feedback (M2-17).

*Deliverables:* Outcome Reconciliation Report (Rich tier — this is the accountability document and the basis for outcome-as-a-service invoicing).

*Gate criterion (HARD GATE 4):* CXO verification of outcome measurement methodology and results before outcome fee invoice.

*Duration:* Typically 90-180 days post-execution completion.

*Agent role:* Nexus drafts reconciliation with Sentinel's pattern-evidence. Atlas updates portfolio view with realized outcomes. Steward audits the full ledger. Observations flow back to pattern authoring (M6).

### Section H · Failure Modes

Four common ways this pattern fails in execution. Each with detection signal and remediation.

**Failure H-1 · Phase compression under client time pressure.**

*Description:* Client pressure for fast results leads to collapsing Phase 2 Charter or Phase 3 Diagnose into a single week. Scope drifts; contradictions stay hidden; Design proceeds on shaky foundation.

*Detection:* Charter signed within five working days of Origination. Diagnostic instrument run against <50% of baseline data request. CXO interview scheduled within two weeks of program start.

*Remediation:* Nexus honestly signals to sponsor that phase compression risks program failure. Offers alternative: narrow scope to achievable depth within compressed timeline, or extend timeline to maintain depth. Never silently compresses.

**Failure H-2 · Soft gate treated as no gate.**

*Description:* Phase 1-to-2 or Phase 5-to-6 transitions are marked as soft gates. "Soft" gets interpreted as "optional," transitions happen without sponsor alignment, momentum is lost, accountability trails break.

*Detection:* Phase transitions with no documented decision point. Sponsor surprise at phase status.

*Remediation:* Soft gates still require documented transition — "Charter begun," "Verify initiated" — even if not hard-stop. Steward enforces minimum documentation standard.

**Failure H-3 · Missing second CXO touchpoint.**

*Description:* Phase 3 interview happens. Phase 4 validation gets deferred or delegated to ops-level stakeholder. Design ships without sponsor validation. Phase 5 execution lacks authority to resolve obstacles.

*Detection:* Phase 4 Design approved without named-CXO sign-off in decision log.

*Remediation:* Hard gate 3 cannot be cleared without CXO touchpoint 2 completed. Nexus refuses to advance.

**Failure H-4 · Phase 6 skipped or reduced to self-report.**

*Description:* Program completes Phase 5 Execute. Client claims victory. Outcome measurement in Phase 6 becomes "we think it worked, let's invoice." Dual ledger never reconciles. No observations contribute to pattern library.

*Detection:* Phase 6 deliverable is single paragraph or not produced. Outcome fee invoiced without reconciliation report.

*Remediation:* Hard gate 4 requires CXO verification of measurement methodology and results. No outcome fee absent this verification. AbarVa's commercial model structurally resists this failure.
