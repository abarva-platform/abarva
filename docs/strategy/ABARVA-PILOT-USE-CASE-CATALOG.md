# AbarVa — Pilot Use-Case Catalog

**Date:** 2026-05-20
**Status:** GTM catalog — v1.
**Pairs with:** `ABARVA-INVESTOR-DILIGENCE-MEMO-2026-05-20.md`.
**Purpose:** turn the diligence verdict ("prove value with a real paid
pilot") into a concrete, sellable set of pilots — each tied to one painful,
budget-owning CXO decision, with the commercial model, post-pilot
monetization, and founder time all priced in.

---

## 1. The operating principle

We do **not** sell "an AI decision OS." That is too broad to fund and too slow
to prove. We sell **one painful decision, improved in 90 days, with
measurable value** — and we let that pull the platform in behind it.

The test for every pilot:

> **Did we help a CXO make or change a decision worth at least 5–10× the
> pilot fee?**

If a pilot cannot answer that, it is not a pilot — it is a demo with an
invoice.

Two consequences:

- **Lead with Source and Moves.** Buyers already hold budget, renewals,
  vendors, and AI-initiative pain *today*. Intelligence and Tower are real
  expansion surfaces but slower to prove cold.
- **The wedge is a decision, not a module.** The module is what they renew;
  the decision is what they buy.

## 3. The pilot commercial model — 90-day paid pilot, success-backed credit

A pilot is not a discount on the platform and not a free trial. It is a
**90-day paid pilot**: the customer pays monthly, is **not** locked into ARR,
and — only if value is proven — a portion of pilot fees credits into the
annual subscription.

### Pilot tiers

| Tier | Monthly fee | 90-day total | Founder time included | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | $25K/mo | $75K | 10 hrs/mo | 1 module, 1–2 decisions |
| **Standard** | $40K/mo | $120K | 15 hrs/mo | 1 module, 3–5 decisions |
| **Strategic** | $60K/mo | $180K | 20 hrs/mo | 2 modules, or a board/CFO-heavy use case |

**Lead with the Standard tier — $40K/month for 90 days.** It is serious
enough that the customer is genuinely committed, but not so high that
procurement stalls — and it protects the founder's time. Combined Source +
Moves pilots are sold at the **Strategic** tier; a full decision-OS pilot
($75K/mo) only when the customer is serious *and* grants real data access.

**Pricing discipline — do not go below $25K/month** unless it is a logo
worth taking a loss for. The real qualifying test is not whether the buyer
likes the idea; it is whether they will pay enough to put **real decisions,
real data, and real executives** into the process.

### Success-backed conversion credit

- The customer pays monthly during the 90 days. The pilot fee is real
  revenue.
- If the pilot proves value (per the success terms below) and converts to an
  annual module subscription, **50% of pilot fees credit toward year-one
  ARR.**
- If value is not proven, the customer walks — no ARR lock-in. That is the
  point: they did not buy a platform on faith.

### Success terms — defined up front, never vague

A pilot is **successful** when **at least two** of the following are true:

1. Identifies **$1M+** in savings, avoided spend, or risk-adjusted value.
2. Changes, delays, rebids, or measurably improves **at least one executive
   decision**.
3. Produces CXO-approved decision packs for **3+ live events / initiatives**.
4. Creates a **repeatable operating cadence** the team keeps using.
5. The VP Sourcing / CIO / CFO agrees AbarVa **should be used on future
   decisions of this type**.

### How to phrase it to the buyer

> "We do not want you to buy a platform on faith. Run AbarVa for 90 days on
> real decisions. You pay $40K/month during the pilot — that includes
> software, setup, and founder-led advisory. If we prove decision value, we
> convert to an annual module subscription and credit half the pilot fees
> into year one."

Founder-friendly and customer-friendly: the customer's downside is capped at
one quarter; AbarVa's upside is a proven, referenceable conversion.

## 4. Post-pilot ARR — three priced components

On conversion, ARR separates into three lines:

| Component | Pricing logic |
|---|---|
| **Software module** | $250K–$750K ARR per module, by scope, tenants, decision volume |
| **Data / connectors / setup** | One-time $50K–$150K — contract repo, spend, telemetry connectors |
| **Expert advisory layer** | $10K–$25K / month — optional, recommended in years 1–2 while the software carries less of the judgment |

**Expansion** is the real ARR story: a P1 Source customer adds Moves; a Moves
customer adds Tower; each new tenant and each new decision type is upsell.
This is also the data flywheel the diligence memo flagged as the only durable
moat — every pilot must feed anonymized decision/outcome patterns back.

## 5. Founder & expert-advisory time — priced, not free

Right now the founder's strategy time **is part of the product value**. It
must be packaged and bounded — never given away as "support."

**Each pilot includes a fixed advisory envelope** (by tier: 10 / 15 / 20
hours per month) covering:

- 2 executive working sessions
- 1 CXO readout
- 1 artifact review cycle
- 1 value-proof memo

**Beyond the envelope:**

- Additional advisory time billed at **$750/hr**, or
- packaged as an **Executive Advisory add-on at $15K/month** during the
  pilot, or a **$10K–$25K/month** advisory layer once converted.

The discipline: when a customer wants "more founder," that is a **priced
advisory line**, not scope creep on the pilot. As the kernels mature, the
software carries more of that judgment and the advisory line shrinks by
design — but until then it is revenue, and it is named.

## 6. Qualifying a pilot customer

Before a pilot is sold, the buyer must pass discovery. Ask every CIO / CPO /
VP Sourcing / CFO five questions:

1. What IT / vendor / AI decision in the next 90 days makes you nervous?
2. Where do you suspect you are overpaying but cannot prove it?
3. Which vendor award or AI bet would be politically hard to challenge?
4. What evidence would you need to stop, renegotiate, rebid, or kill it?
5. If we helped you avoid one bad decision or find $1M, what would you pay?

> **If they cannot name a specific, painful, dated decision — they are not a
> pilot.** A buyer without a nervous decision has no urgency and no value bar.

## 7. How this closes the diligence gap

The investor memo's "what would make me lead" scenario was: one real
enterprise loads data in under two weeks, runs 2+ decisions through AbarVa, a
CFO/VP says the recommendation changed the decision, Tower captures realized
outcome variance, and the pattern repeats across 3 customers.

This catalog is the machine that produces exactly that evidence:

- **P1 / P4 land the first paid decisions** → "the recommendation changed
  the decision."
- **The §3 success terms on every pilot** → the measurable before/after.
- **P6 + the Tower measurement model from P4** → realized-outcome variance.
- **Three pilots converted** → the repeatable pattern, and the first fuel for
  the cross-customer outcome flywheel.

Sell the decision. Prove the value. Let the platform follow.
