Recommendation

Activate three margin recovery levers in parallel.

Apex's owned-brand margin has compressed 340 basis points over the last 24 monthsE1 against a category that expanded by 80 bpsE2. The gap is not a pricing problem — our consumer price index is in line with the cohortE3 — it is a cost-side erosion compounded by assortment bloat and promotional depth creep.

Three interventions are decision-ready. Each has independent payback. The portfolio effect compounds: executing all three in parallel recovers margin ~40% faster than sequential executionE4, because supplier leverage is highest when volume commitments are being renegotiated alongside SKU consolidation.

Lever 1 — Supplier renegotiation: Top 40 owned-brand suppliers, anchored on Q3 2026 reset cycle. Volume consolidation across 180 SKUs unlocks Tier-1 cost brackets. Projected: $34M-$42M/yrE5.

Lever 2 — Assortment rationalization: 23% SKU reduction on owned-brand tail. Store-level demand modeling shows <2% revenue riskE6; margin-per-linear-foot lift of 14-19%. Projected: $22M-$31M/yr.

Lever 3 — Promotional depth discipline: Cap depth at 22% off for owned-brand (vs. current average 28%E7), with surgical exceptions for competitive response. Projected: $12M-$21M/yr.

Decision log · leading to this memo

How we got here.

Apr 18
Parallel-track execution selected over sequential. Analysis showed 40% faster margin recovery with portfolio leverage. Trade-off: higher operational load in Q3 — mitigated by adding one owned-brand category lead.
Apr 14
Promotional depth cap set at 22%. Alternatives considered: 20% (aggressive, competitive risk), 25% (conservative, lower recovery). 22% balances margin recovery with competitive response flexibility.
Apr 9
SKU reduction target set at 23%. Store-level demand model validated <2% revenue risk across 85% of stores. Higher-density urban stores carry 4-6% risk; mitigated by store-tier-specific assortment.
Apr 2
Scope confirmed: owned-brand only. National-brand margin compression deferred to separate program — different lever structure, different stakeholder set.
Risks and mitigations

Three risks material to decision.

High
Q3 supplier reset timing. Supplier renegotiation window closes end of Q3. If parallel-track execution slips, top-6 savings deferred 6 months.
Mitigation: dedicated category lead, weekly steering, escalation protocol for any supplier milestone slip >10 days.
Med
SKU reduction backlash in urban stores. Higher-density stores show 4-6% revenue risk at target reduction.
Mitigation: tier-specific assortment (urban stores keep 82% of current SKU count vs. 77% average).
Med
Competitive response to promotional depth cap. If primary competitor deepens promotion during transition, owned-brand traffic could compress 2-4%.
Mitigation: surgical exception budget (1.5% of promotional spend), weekly competitive price monitoring.
