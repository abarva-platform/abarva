## Thesis Direction

AbarVa should not frame consulting displacement as a simplistic replacement of experts with AI. The sharper thesis is that AI and workflow software expose a large middle layer of advisory work that is repeatable, artifact-heavy, evidence-hungry, and still sold as bespoke labor. AbarVa's self-interest is to capture that layer as a governed decision operating system. Its partnership model should preserve the roles that consultants, SIs, AI providers, and domain specialists still perform well: trust, executive access, regulated execution, implementation capacity, and specialized judgment.

## Evidence Synthesis

### 1. Enterprise AI is widespread, but scaling remains the bottleneck

McKinsey reports regular AI use in at least one function for 88% of respondents, but most organizations remain in experimentation or pilot phases, and workflow redesign is one of the strongest correlates of meaningful value. Gartner forecasts very large GenAI spend, but also reports CIO movement away from uncertain POCs toward more predictable commercial solutions. Deloitte similarly points to production bottlenecks tied to data, process, technology, strategy, and talent. This supports AbarVa's strategic wedge: clients do not need more abstract AI enthusiasm; they need workflow redesign and operating discipline.

### 2. Consulting's labor model is vulnerable where method is repeatable

BLS data shows management analysts are expensive skilled labor, with a six-figure median wage in 2024 and growth projected through 2034. Microsoft Research shows AI applicability is high in knowledge work and information-heavy tasks. NBER evidence shows AI can improve productivity materially, especially for less experienced workers. This does not mean consultants vanish. It means the work that used to justify layers of junior and mid-level effort becomes increasingly contestable by productized workflows.

### 3. Buyers are questioning traditional consulting value

HFS reports that 65% of enterprises say traditional consulting models fail to deliver real value and that headcount-based contracting is expected to decline. This is one survey, not the whole market, so the conclusion should be tempered. But it aligns with the strategic direction of AI alliances and major consultancies' own shifts toward AI-enabled assets, centers of excellence, and partner ecosystems.

### 4. The market validates partnerships, not isolation

OpenAI's Frontier Alliances with BCG, McKinsey, Accenture, and Capgemini; Accenture's Anthropic partnership; Bain's OpenAI Center of Excellence; BCG's AI ecosystem; Google Cloud's GSI capacity strategy; and Infosys-Anthropic all point to the same structure: enterprise AI deployment requires models, services, implementation capacity, industry knowledge, and change management. AbarVa should not fight that structure. It should own the buyer-side workflow and let partners plug into it.

### 5. AbarVa's specific vector starts with Source

Internal AbarVa Source direction describes Source as an agent-led sourcing and vendor-selection workbench with pattern packs, scorecard governance, artifact generation, decision support, and value tracking. That is the right beachhead because sourcing events are high-value, repetitive enough to encode, and poorly served by either generic procurement suites or one-off advisory decks. AbarVa's product must displace consulting artifacts with governed gates, not replace prose with AI prose.

## Five Steelmanned Counterarguments

### CA1. Consulting demand can rise because AI adoption is hard, not fall because AI exists.

**Steelman:** Most enterprises still struggle with data quality, operating-model redesign, security, change management, and value measurement. If fewer than one-third of experiments reach production and most firms have not scaled, demand for experienced transformation partners may expand.

**AbarVa response:** AbarVa should not claim to replace transformation partners. It should displace repeatable diagnostic, sourcing, artifact, scorecard, and value-governance labor while partnering for specialized implementation and regulated change.

### CA2. Brand trust, board access, and liability still favor incumbents.

**Steelman:** Large clients buy reassurance, political cover, and implementation capacity from firms with global brands, audit-like process discipline, and executive relationships. A software workbench alone cannot sign up to the same risk posture.

**AbarVa response:** The partnership model treats incumbents as channels and execution partners where trust and delivery capacity matter, while AbarVa owns the workflow memory, evidence layer, and repeatable decision operating system.

### CA3. The client may prefer bundled advice and execution over another platform.

**Steelman:** CIOs and procurement leaders are already burdened by tool sprawl. If AbarVa is experienced as another dashboard, it will lose to the firm that absorbs complexity and owns outcomes.

**AbarVa response:** AbarVa must sell fewer surfaces and more governed decisions: client baseline, pattern pack, artifact, gate, scorecard, value ledger, and partner handoff in one controlled motion.

### CA4. AI-native services may compress labor but not necessarily fees.

**Steelman:** Elite firms may capture productivity gains as margin, reinvest in AI assets, and use existing relationships to bundle AI-enabled delivery rather than lower prices.

**AbarVa response:** That is exactly why AbarVa should be explicit about self-interest: it wants to capture part of that margin shift by productizing the operating layer that clients otherwise rent repeatedly from advisors.

### CA5. Outcome-linked models are hard because causality and baselines are disputed.

**Steelman:** AbarVa may want outcome-based economics, but enterprise outcomes depend on messy behavior, governance, budget, vendor performance, and macro conditions. Overclaiming value would destroy trust.

**AbarVa response:** AbarVa should start with evidence-backed projected value, confidence, assumptions, and measurement ownership. Outcome-linked economics should be optional and limited to claims that have a defensible baseline and attribution method.

## Working Claims To Carry Into Chunks

1. AbarVa displaces repeatable consulting method, not all consulting judgment.
2. Source is the first beachhead because sourcing is patterned, high-stakes, and document-heavy.
3. AbarVa's partnership model is strategic control: partners deliver expertise; AbarVa owns workflow memory and evidence authority.
4. AbarVa should be explicit about self-interest: it wants recurring economics, pattern intelligence, and a role in high-value decisions.
5. Pricing should follow decision value, rigor, evidence burden, and value tracking, not generic seat counts.
6. The moat is not prompts; it is pattern intelligence plus evidence provenance across tenant-safe workflows.
7. The ethical posture is anti-waste and anti-opaque claims, not anti-human expertise.
