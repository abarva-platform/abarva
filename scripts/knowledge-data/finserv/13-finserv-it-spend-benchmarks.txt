FINANCIAL SERVICES IT SPEND AND TECHNOLOGY INVESTMENT BENCHMARKS
Source: Gartner IT Spending Forecast 2025, Deloitte FS Technology Survey 2025, IDC Financial Services IT Spending Guide

OVERVIEW AND CONTEXT

IT spend in financial services is one of the highest in any industry segment, driven by regulatory requirements, security demands, data complexity, and competitive pressure from technology-native competitors. In 2024-2025, three forces are reshaping FS IT budgets simultaneously: the shift from run-the-bank to change-the-bank as digital competition intensifies; the emergence of AI as a distinct budget category growing at 34% annually; and FinOps pressure to contain cloud spend as migration costs materialised higher than forecast.

Understanding FS IT spend patterns is critical for benchmarking transformation investments, sizing vendor negotiations, and identifying where a firm is over-spending on run-and-maintain relative to investment in competitive AI capabilities. The gap between leaders and laggards in IT investment allocation — not total spend — is the primary predictor of AI programme outcomes.

AbarVa Genome failure pattern F007 (Misallocated IT Budget — Run Consuming Change): in 58% of FS firms assessed, run-and-maintain consumes above 60% of IT budget, leaving insufficient investment for AI, digital, and competitive capability development. This is a structural constraint on transformation velocity.

IT BUDGET ALLOCATION IN FINANCIAL SERVICES

Budget category breakdown (industry median versus top performers):

Run and maintain (infrastructure, legacy system maintenance, operations, IT support):
    Industry median: 52-58% of IT budget
    Bottom quartile: 64-72% (high legacy burden)
    Top quartile: 38-44% (modernised infrastructure, higher automation)

Change and grow (application development, digital projects, AI, innovation):
    Industry median: 28-34% of IT budget
    Top quartile: 42-48% (larger investment in competitive capability)
    Bottom quartile: 18-24% (insufficient to maintain competitive pace)

Compliance and risk technology (regulatory reporting, surveillance, DORA, MRM):
    Industry median: 12-18% of IT budget
    Regulatory-intensive firms (large banks, EU-regulated): up to 22%

AI and analytics (dedicated AI budget, excluding AI embedded in other categories):
    Industry median: 6-14%
    Leaders (top quartile AI adopters): 18-22%
    Laggards: below 4% dedicated AI spend
    AI budget growth rate: 34% annually — the fastest-growing IT budget category

Cloud spend:
    Cloud as % of total IT spend (2025): 28%
    On-premise: 72% (still dominant but declining at 6% annually)
    Cloud spend growth rate: 24% annually
    Projected cloud as % of IT spend by 2028: 44%
    Note: for cloud-first FS firms already past the transition, cloud may represent 60-75% of IT spend


TECHNOLOGY DEBT IN FINANCIAL SERVICES

Legacy technology debt is the single largest structural cost issue in FS IT:

    Average legacy system age: 22-28 years (mainframe, COBOL, early client-server)
    Annual legacy maintenance cost premium versus modern equivalent: 18-28% higher
    FS industry total estimated technology debt (global, McKinsey 2024): $1.1T
    Average technology debt by firm type:
        Global bank (>$500B assets): $8.4B
        Regional bank ($10-50B assets): $340M
        Asset manager ($50-200B AUM): $48M
        Insurance company (top-50 global): $380M

Legacy maintenance cost categories:
    COBOL programmer premium (60% above Java developer market rate): adds $28,000-$48,000 per developer per year above market
    Batch processing overhead (mainframe batch window limitations): constrains 34% of FS firms from real-time AI deployment
    Integration middleware (API adapters, MQ message brokers for legacy connectivity): adds $1.8-4.2M annually at large banks
    Compliance overhead on legacy (manual controls where automated controls would be standard on modern systems): adds $2.4-6.8M annually

Technology debt impact on AI:
    Firms with high legacy burden (>60% of systems over 15 years old): achieve 42% of AI programme ROI versus low-legacy firms
    Root cause: data quality degradation from batch systems, absent APIs for real-time AI integration, high IT maintenance consuming budget that should fund AI


VENDOR SPEND CONSOLIDATION

Vendor management is an underutilised lever for IT cost reduction:

    Average number of technology vendors (large bank, >$50B assets): 1,200+
    Average number of vendors (top-quartile optimised bank): 600-700
    Asset manager average vendor count ($50B-200B AUM): 280-440
    Vendor consolidation savings achieved: 8-14% of total technology spend
    AI-assisted vendor rationalisation (spend analytics + contract AI): reduces evaluation time 12-18 months to 6-9 months

Vendor categories with most savings opportunity:
    Data vendors: 14-28% savings on renegotiation (Bloomberg, LSEG, FactSet)
    Infrastructure (cloud commitments renegotiated): 12-22% savings with multi-year committed spend
    Professional services (IT outsourcing, SI contracts): 8-18% savings with benchmarking and competition
    Software licenses (ERP, CRM, analytics): 10-24% savings on true-up and consolidation

Vendor contract risk factors:
    Contracts with no exit clause or exit penalty: 61% of FS technology contracts
    Contracts with proprietary data format lock-in: Bloomberg, some core banking vendors
    Contracts with auto-renewal clauses (renewing without active decision): 44%
    Average renegotiation savings per contract cycle: 18-28%

AbarVa Genome failure pattern F019 (Vendor Governance Failure): FS firms without a formal vendor management office (VMO) overpay by 18-24% on technology contracts on average. 52% of FS firms assessed lack a formal VMO with benchmark data.


CYBERSECURITY SPEND IN FINANCIAL SERVICES

Cybersecurity is the fastest-growing IT sub-budget category and is non-discretionary:

    Cybersecurity as % of IT budget (FS industry): 12-18%
    Best-practice target (NIST Cybersecurity Framework, FS-ISAC guidance): 18-22%
    Gap between current and best practice (median firm): 4-8 percentage points

Cybersecurity incident cost data:
    Average cost of FS data breach (IBM Security / Ponemon Institute 2024): $5.9M per incident
    Average cost of ransomware attack (FS): $3.4M per incident (ransom + recovery + regulatory)
    FS cyberattack frequency versus other industries: 300x higher attack rate
    FS firms experiencing at least one significant cybersecurity incident annually: 64%

Major cybersecurity spend categories:
    Endpoint detection and response (EDR): 22% of cybersecurity budget; CrowdStrike Falcon dominant
    Network security and SIEM: 18%; Palo Alto Networks Cortex, Splunk Enterprise Security
    Identity and access management (IAM): 16%; Okta, CyberArk, Microsoft Entra
    Cloud security posture management (CSPM): 12%; Wiz, Prisma Cloud, Microsoft Defender for Cloud
    AI-native cybersecurity: 14% and growing; Darktrace (unsupervised AI), Vectra AI, Abnormal Security

AI cybersecurity tools in FS:
    Darktrace (AI autonomous threat detection): 22% market share in large FS; learns normal network behaviour and detects anomalies
    CrowdStrike Falcon AI: ML-based endpoint protection; fastest threat detection in industry (sub-1 minute)
    Microsoft Sentinel (AI SIEM): 34% of Azure-primary FS firms; tight integration with Azure AD and M365
    Abnormal Security (AI email security): 18% of large banks; GenAI-based email threat detection

    Life insurance IT spend as % of premium: 3.8-6.4%, median 5.2%

Insurance AI priorities:

    Underwriting AI: telematics, satellite imagery, IoT data for property risk assessment

FINTECH TECHNOLOGY SPEND AND VALUATION BENCHMARKS

FinTech firms show dramatically different IT investment patterns versus incumbents:

    FinTech IT spend as % of revenue: 18-28% (technology is the product)
    Developer cost as % of total cost: 42-58% (versus 12-18% at traditional FS)
    FinTech cloud-first adoption: 94% (born in cloud; no legacy burden)
    FinTech AI deployment speed: first production AI in 6 months average (versus 18 months traditional FS)

FinTech valuation impact of AI:
    Premium-AI-enabled FinTechs: 6-8x revenue multiple
    Traditional FS firm with equivalent AI capability: 2.4x revenue multiple
    Gap: markets value AI-native architecture 2.5-3.5x higher than retrofitted AI
    Implication: FS incumbents cannot achieve FinTech valuation multiples through AI alone — require business model change to capture full value

KEY METRICS FOR BENCHMARKING IT INVESTMENT HEALTH

AbarVa diagnostic framework for IT investment health:

    Run vs. change balance: above 60% run spending is a structural constraint; target 42-48% run
    AI budget as % of IT: below 6% signals under-investment; 14-22% signals AI-mature organisation
    Legacy as % of application portfolio: above 50% legacy (15+ year old systems) creates structural AI capability gap
    Cloud as % of IT: below 20% in 2025 signals modernisation lag; target 35-45% by 2027
    Technical debt ratio: annual legacy maintenance cost divided by revenue; above 3% creates material drag on competitive investment
    Cybersecurity as % of IT: below 12% is insufficient given FS threat landscape; target 18-22%

Investment health scoring (AbarVa 100-point scale):
    90-100: technology-advantage position; AI-leading
    70-89: competitive but investment acceleration needed
    50-69: at-risk; transformation required in 2-3 years
    Below 50: existential technology risk; emergency programme required


IT INVESTMENT PRIORITISATION AND GOVERNANCE

FS firms allocate IT budgets under significant competing pressures: regulatory compliance mandates consume non-discretionary budget; legacy maintenance absorbs 60-70% of technology spend; and AI/innovation investment must compete against both. The governance frameworks for technology investment allocation determine whether firms can break the cycle:

IT budget governance models in FS:
    Traditional IT governance (CIO-led, annual planning): 52% of FS firms
        Annual budget cycle; limited ability to re-prioritise mid-year; waterfall project governance
        AI investment in this model: allocated once annually; often underfunded vs. need
    Product-based IT governance (portfolio/product model): 34% of FS firms
        Technology investment aligned to product lines (digital banking, wealth management, capital markets)
        Product owners with technology budget authority; quarterly re-prioritisation
        AI investment: embedded in product roadmaps; more agile re-allocation
    Platform-first governance (shared platform with product-layer investment): 14% of FS firms
        Shared data, cloud, and AI platform funded centrally; product-specific features funded by business
        Best model for AI: platform (Snowflake, Databricks, Salesforce) funded at scale; product AI layered on top
        Firms with platform governance: highest AI ROI in AbarVa dataset (4.2x average vs. 2.8x traditional governance)

IT budget allocation shift 2020-2025:
    Regulatory and compliance technology: from 18% to 24% of IT budget (DORA, EU AI Act driving increases)
    Cloud infrastructure: from 8% to 22% (migration from on-premise to cloud)
    AI and analytics: from 4% to 14% (fastest-growing category)
    Legacy maintenance offset: from 72% to 60% (declining but still majority of IT spend for most FS firms)
    Key observation: AI investment growth has been funded primarily by legacy maintenance reduction and operational efficiency savings — not by new budget allocation in most cases


TECHNOLOGY INVESTMENT ROI MEASUREMENT IN FINANCIAL SERVICES

Technology ROI measurement is systematically underdeveloped in FS. 62% of FS firms cannot quantify the return on their largest technology investments:

ROI measurement maturity:
    FS firms with formal technology investment ROI tracking: 38%
    FS firms tracking technology investment against pre-agreed business KPIs: 28%
    FS firms relying solely on project delivery metrics (on time, on budget) as measure of success: 34%
    AbarVa assessment: technology investment measured only on delivery metrics — not business outcomes — is the leading governance failure in FS IT programmes

Technology ROI by investment category (measured at 24 months):
    Core banking modernisation: 1.4-2.2x (long payback; high cost; multi-year benefit accrual)
    CRM (Salesforce FSC with proper change management): 3.2-4.8x
    Data platform (Snowflake + Databricks + Collibra): 2.8-4.2x
    Fraud AI: 3.8-6.2x (quantifiable fraud loss reduction)
    Operations automation (reconciliation, reporting, close): 4.2-7.4x (highest ROI category in AbarVa data)
    GenAI productivity tools: 2.4-4.1x (productivity gain, with measurement framework in place)
    Regulatory technology: 1.8-2.8x (compliance cost avoidance + penalty avoidance)

The measurement gap costs FS firms real money:
    Without ROI tracking, average technology investment renewal rate at year 3: 62% (38% of programmes discontinued)
    With ROI tracking showing positive returns: 88% renewal rate
    Lost compounding value of discontinued programmes: AbarVa estimates $2.4-$6.8M per discontinued programme (future value of unrealised year 3-5 benefits)


EMERGING TECHNOLOGY SPEND IN FINANCIAL SERVICES 2024-2025

Specific technology categories driving incremental spend:

Quantum-safe cryptography (post-quantum migration):
    FS firms with PQC migration project started: 18%
    Estimated cost for large global bank PQC migration: $48-$120M over 8-12 years
    Urgency: NIST PQC standards published August 2024; regulatory guidance from DORA and US CISA requires roadmap documentation by 2026
    Current spend: $2.4-$8.8M annually for planning and early migration (networking, PKI infrastructure)

Digital asset infrastructure:
    FS firms with tokenisation or digital asset infrastructure investment: 34%
    Primary use cases: tokenised bond settlement (JPMorgan Onyx: $1T+ settled via blockchain), repo settlement (DTCC Project Lithium), tokenised fund units (BlackRock BUIDL fund)
    Annual spend on digital asset technology (large bank): $12-$48M
    Revenue opportunity: tokenised asset custody fees ($0.28/basis point) and settlement services

Quantum computing exploration:
    FS firms with quantum computing exploration budget: 22%
    Primary use cases being explored: portfolio optimisation (D-Wave quantum annealing), option pricing (quantum Monte Carlo), cryptography migration planning
    Annual spend (exploration stage): $840K-$2.4M for dedicated quantum team
    Realistic production timeline: 8-12 years for meaningful FS quantum advantage

AI infrastructure spend (incremental):
    GPU compute for ML training (new budget category 2023-2025): $840K-$4.8M annually for large FS firm
    LLM API costs (Azure OpenAI, AWS Bedrock): $420K-$1.8M annually
    MLOps platform (Databricks, Azure ML, DataRobot): $280K-$1.2M annually
    Total AI infrastructure incremental spend: $1.5-$7.8M annually (above existing data platform costs)

AbarVa Genome pattern F019 (Technology Without Business Case): 44% of FS technology investments above $1M are made without a formal business case approved by a business owner. Technology investments approved solely by the CIO or CDO without business ownership have 2.4x higher abandonment rate within 36 months. Every technology investment above $500K should have a named business owner, pre-agreed success metrics, and a 24-month ROI checkpoint — regardless of whether the investment is mandated (regulatory) or discretionary (AI).
