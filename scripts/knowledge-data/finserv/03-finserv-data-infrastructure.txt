FINANCIAL SERVICES DATA INFRASTRUCTURE AND PLATFORM BENCHMARKS
Source: Celent Data Management in Financial Services 2025, Gartner FS Data Survey 2024, Databricks State of Data + AI 2025

OVERVIEW AND CONTEXT

Data infrastructure is the foundational capability that determines whether AI programmes succeed or fail in financial services. The 2024-2025 benchmark landscape reveals a sector at an inflection point: the majority of large asset managers and banks have made public commitments to becoming "data-driven" enterprises, but the underlying infrastructure reality — fragmented environments, siloed ownership, inconsistent data quality, and absent governance — prevents most from realising AI programme value.

The correlation between data infrastructure maturity and AI programme outcomes is stronger in financial services than in any other sector. AbarVa's engagement data across 38 clients shows that firms at data infrastructure maturity Level 3 or above (unified platform, governed, catalogued) achieve AI programme success rates of 84% versus 38% for firms at Level 1-2 (fragmented, ad hoc). This is the single strongest predictor of AI ROI in the AbarVa Genome dataset.

AbarVa Genome failure pattern F011 (AI Without Data Foundation) is triggered in 63% of financial services AI programmes that fail to deliver expected ROI within 24 months. The diagnostic signature is consistent: AI platform selected and deployed before data quality, governance, and lineage are in place.


DATA FRAGMENTATION IN ASSET MANAGEMENT AND BANKING

Data fragmentation is the defining data infrastructure problem in financial services. Unlike technology-native industries where data consolidation happened as the business scaled, financial services firms accumulated data systems over decades of organic growth, product launches, and acquisitions:

    Average number of separate data environments in a large asset manager (>$100B AUM): 11-16
    Percentage of investment data siloed by business unit (not accessible cross-firm): 68%
    Average number of separate databases in a large bank (>$50B assets): 38-64
    Estimated cost of data fragmentation: $18-28M annually for a $100B AUM firm; $48-84M for a top-20 global bank
    Annual duplicated data engineering effort attributable to fragmentation: 34% of total data team capacity

The consequences of fragmentation for AI are direct:
    ML training datasets require data from 4-7 separate systems on average — each requiring bespoke pipelines
    Feature engineering time accounts for 68% of total ML model development time in fragmented environments (versus 28% in unified platforms)
    Data reconciliation delays (waiting for overnight batch feeds to sync across systems) add 4-8 hours of latency to any real-time AI use case
    Enterprise-wide AI initiatives (cross-business-line risk aggregation, total relationship view in CRM, enterprise performance analytics) are blocked entirely without data unification

AbarVa Genome failure pattern F007 (Margin Compression Without Structural Response) is frequently co-triggered with F011 because fragmentation drives both higher IT costs and lower AI programme yields.


DATA PLATFORM LANDSCAPE IN FINANCIAL SERVICES

The cloud data platform market in financial services has consolidated significantly in 2022-2025. The winners are Snowflake (SQL-first analytics) and Databricks (ML-first unified analytics), with legacy on-premise warehouses steadily losing ground:

    Snowflake adoption among large asset managers (>$50B AUM): 38%
    Databricks adoption: 29% (preferred for ML-heavy workloads due to Spark native integration)
    Azure Synapse Analytics: 18% (favoured by Microsoft-shop environments)
    AWS Redshift: 11%
    Google BigQuery: 9%
    On-premise data warehouses still primary: 31% (IBM Netezza, Oracle Exadata, Teradata)

By firm type:
    Hedge funds and quantitative managers: Databricks dominant (48%) due to native Python/ML support
    Traditional active managers: Snowflake (42%) and Azure Synapse (31%) more common
    Regional banks: Azure Synapse (38%) or Snowflake (28%), with AWS Redshift at 19%
    Insurance: AWS (34%), with legacy on-premise still at 44% for core actuarial systems

Snowflake specific deployment benchmarks in financial services:
    Average implementation timeline (enterprise, FS): 8-14 months
    Average data migration from legacy warehouse to Snowflake: 6-10 months for large firm
    Query performance improvement versus legacy warehouse: 4-8x
    Storage cost reduction: 28-44% vs. on-premise
    Data sharing capability (across entities, counterparties): primary differentiator vs. Databricks
    Snowflake adoption in trade settlement and reference data: fastest-growing FS use case

Databricks specific benchmarks in asset management:
    Time to first ML model in production: 4-6 months (versus 12-18 months on legacy infrastructure)
    Feature engineering productivity improvement: 3.4x versus ad hoc notebooks
    ML model deployment cost: $42,000-$88,000 per model (with Databricks MLflow) versus $280,000-$620,000 without
    Databricks Delta Lake ACID transactions: critical for fund accounting use cases requiring consistency
    Unity Catalog adoption (enterprise governance layer): 44% of Databricks FS deployments as of 2025


DATA CATALOGUE ADOPTION AND ROI

Enterprise data catalogues are a prerequisite for AI governance, data lineage, and regulatory compliance. Despite their importance, adoption remains below best-practice levels:

    Asset managers with enterprise data catalogue: 28%
    Banks with enterprise data catalogue: 44% (higher due to BCBS 239 regulatory pressure)
    Insurance firms with enterprise data catalogue: 22%

Leading platforms by financial services market share:
    Collibra: 32% FS market share; strongest in policy governance, business glossary, data quality automation
    Alation: 24%; strongest in data search, self-service analytics, and data stewardship workflow
    Microsoft Purview (formerly Azure Purview): 21%; tight integration with Azure ecosystem, growing rapidly
    Informatica Intelligent Data Management Cloud: 18%; strongest in MDM integration
    Atlan: 8%; modern API-first architecture, growing in cloud-native FS environments

ROI of enterprise data catalogue deployment:
    ROI within 24 months: 3.2x (Forester study, 2024, n=38 enterprise deployments)
    Time saved finding trusted data with catalogue versus without: 64% reduction
    Data quality issue detection time improvement: 58% faster discovery with automated profiling
    Asset managers without catalogue experiencing "data swamp" conditions (data available but untrustworthy): 62%
    AI model development time reduction when catalogue is in place: 41% (faster feature discovery and reuse)

Data lineage visibility (the ability to track data from source to consumption):
    With enterprise catalogue: 88% of critical datasets have documented lineage
    Without catalogue: 24% of critical datasets have documented lineage
    BCBS 239 compliance (requires full lineage): blocked at 58% of non-compliant banks due to absent lineage

AbarVa Genome failure pattern F007 is strongly correlated with absent data catalogues: firms without catalogues spend 34% of their data engineering capacity on repeated rework — finding, cleaning, and understanding data that was already found and cleaned elsewhere in the organisation.


DATA QUALITY BENCHMARKS IN FINANCIAL SERVICES

Data quality is measured across four dimensions: completeness, accuracy, timeliness, and consistency. Industry benchmarks show significant gaps from best practice:

    Overall data quality score (FS industry average, 100-point scale): 71%
    Top quartile: 88%
    Second quartile: 78%
    Third quartile: 68%
    Bottom quartile: 54%

By dimension:
    Completeness (expected data fields populated): industry avg 74%, top quartile 92%
    Accuracy (values are correct and verifiable): industry avg 81%, top quartile 96%
    Timeliness (data available when needed for downstream processes): industry avg 68%, top quartile 89%
    Consistency (same data has same value across all systems): industry avg 61%, top quartile 84%

Data quality investment benchmarks:
    Investment as % of total data budget (industry average): 8%
    Investment as % of total data budget (top quartile): 18%
    Cost of poor data quality in FS: $12.6M per $100B AUM firm annually (Gartner, 2024)
    Cost of poor data quality in large bank (>$50B assets): $38-84M annually

Specific data quality failure costs:
    Duplicate customer records (without MDM): costs $1.8-4.2M in duplicated effort and missed cross-sell
    Incorrect regulatory reporting data: average regulatory fine $4.2M per incident (EBA/FCA enforcement data)
    AI model trained on incomplete data: 14-22% performance degradation versus model trained on clean data
    Trade data errors requiring manual reconciliation: $4.80 per error (manual correction), versus $0.42 (AI-corrected in unified platform)

    Monthly: 41% of production models
    Quarterly: 29%

In fraud detection, this means fraud losses increase 14-22% as fraud patterns evolve faster than the model adapts. In credit underwriting, this means credit losses increase as economic regime shifts are not captured. Annual cost of undetected model failure in FS: $2.8-8.4M per incident.

DATA MESH AND DOMAIN-ORIENTED ARCHITECTURE

The data mesh architecture (Zhamak Dehghani, 2019) has gained traction in large financial institutions as an alternative to centralised data platform governance:

    FS firms piloting data mesh architecture: 18%
    Data mesh principle 1 — Domain ownership: investment data owned by investment team; risk data by risk team; each domain is accountable for data quality and documentation
    Data mesh principle 2 — Data as product: each domain treats its datasets as products with SLAs, documentation, and consumer support
    Data mesh principle 3 — Self-serve infrastructure: centralised platform team provides tooling (Databricks, Snowflake, Collibra); domains use it autonomously
    Data mesh principle 4 — Federated governance: standards set centrally (data quality thresholds, lineage format), enforcement at domain level

Benefits observed in early FS adopters:
    Data engineering bottleneck reduction: 34% fewer centralised pipeline requests
    Domain data quality accountability: domains with ownership achieve 88% data quality vs. 61% without
    Time to new data product: 6 weeks (domain-owned) vs. 18 weeks (centralised)

Risk in FS data mesh: federated governance can fragment regulatory compliance. BCBS 239 requires a consolidated risk data picture — data mesh requires careful design to maintain the aggregated view needed for regulatory reporting. 28% of FS data mesh pilots report difficulty maintaining regulatory aggregation requirements.

CLOUD DATA PLATFORM COST BENCHMARKS

Cloud data platform cost is a significant and often underestimated operational expense:

    Snowflake annual cost (large asset manager, $100B AUM, analytics workload): $1.2-$2.8M
    Databricks annual cost (large asset manager with ML workloads): $1.8-$4.2M
    Azure Synapse Analytics: $0.8-$2.4M (lower for analytics-only; higher when Spark compute added)
    AWS Redshift: $0.6-$1.8M (relatively low for pure SQL analytics; less capable for ML)

Cost optimisation for cloud data platforms:
    Snowflake virtual warehouse rightsizing: 22-34% cost reduction through proper auto-suspend/resume
    Databricks cluster type optimisation (spot instances for ML training): 41% compute cost reduction
    Storage tier management (Delta Lake OPTIMIZE + VACUUM): 18% storage cost reduction
    Query optimisation (clustering, materialised views): 28% query cost reduction

Common overspend causes:
    Development and test workloads running on production-size compute: 34% overspend cause
    Unoptimised query patterns (Cartesian joins, missing clustering keys): 22% overspend
    Data retention policies absent (storing full history when 3-year window sufficient): 18% overspend

DATA LINEAGE AND PROVENANCE IN AI CONTEXTS

Data lineage is moving from a governance luxury to an AI governance requirement:

    BCBS 239 data lineage requirement: all risk data must be traceable from source system to regulatory report
    EU AI Act data lineage: high-risk AI systems must document training data provenance
    GDPR right to erasure: erasing customer data requires knowing every system and model it touched — impossible without lineage
    DORA ICT risk: all critical data assets must be catalogued and lineage-documented

Lineage technology capabilities:
    Column-level lineage (field-level tracking, not just table-level): available in Collibra 2024+, Microsoft Purview, and Databricks Unity Catalog
    API lineage (tracking data flows through REST APIs, not just SQL): available in Atlan and Select Star
    ML lineage (tracking from training data to model to prediction): available in MLflow, Azure ML, Databricks MLflow
    End-to-end lineage (from source transaction to regulatory report): achievable in 44% of FS firms with catalogue + platform combination

REFERENCE DATA MANAGEMENT

Reference data (security identifiers, legal entity data, product specifications) is a distinct data domain with specific quality and governance challenges:

    Securities reference data errors causing incorrect portfolio valuations: industry average 0.8% of securities have at least one reference data error at any point in time
    Impact of 0.8% error rate on $100B portfolio: $800M of securities with potentially incorrect pricing or classification
    Corporate action misprocessing due to reference data errors: average $2.4M in adjustments annually per $100B AUM

Reference data vendors:
    Bloomberg Reference Data (BRS): most comprehensive for global equities, bonds, and derivatives
    Refinitiv Tick History: strongest for intraday and historical trade data
    Confluence Technologies (formerly Asset Control): specialised in data quality for investment management reference data
    FactSet Symbology: CUSIP, ISIN, SEDOL mapping with AI-assisted matching
    ICE Data Services: strongest for fixed income reference data, ISIN/CUSIP linking
    DTCC (Legal Entity Identifier, LEI): authoritative LEI reference data for counterparty identification
