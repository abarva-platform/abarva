REGIONAL AND COMMUNITY BANKING COST-TO-INCOME AND PERFORMANCE BENCHMARKS
Source: FDIC Quarterly Banking Profile Q4 2024, Federal Reserve Bank Performance Data 2025, S&P Global Market Intelligence Banking Benchmark

OVERVIEW AND CONTEXT

Cost-to-income ratio is the primary operational efficiency benchmark for US regional and community banks. Unlike asset managers, where fee compression drives C/I pressure primarily from the revenue side, banking C/I pressure comes from both sides simultaneously: deposit costs rose 180-240 basis points in 2023-2024 as the Federal Reserve tightened, while revenue diversification and non-interest income growth have proven structurally difficult for banks below $50B in assets.

The 2024-2025 environment presents a specific challenge: post-rate-cycle normalization is reducing net interest margin (NIM) from the 2023 peaks, and banks that invested in technology and headcount during the 2021-2022 growth period are now managing bloated fixed cost structures as revenue compresses. AI-driven cost reduction has moved from a 5-year strategic ambition to an immediate operational priority for banks in the $5B-$50B asset range.

AbarVa Genome failure pattern F007 (Fixed Cost Base Misaligned to Revenue Environment) is the most frequently triggered pattern in banking diagnostics, present in 68% of regional banks assessed with assets between $5B and $30B.
