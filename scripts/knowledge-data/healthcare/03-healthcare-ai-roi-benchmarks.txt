IMPLEMENTATION SUCCESS FACTORS — RANKED BY IMPACT ON OUTCOME

Factor 1 — Named C-suite executive sponsor (COO or CFO level): 3.2x higher programme success rate vs. IT-only sponsorship
Factor 2 — Clean data foundation established before AI deployment: 2.8x higher ROI; data quality score must be >65 on AbarVa 0-100 scale
Factor 3 — Baseline metrics locked and signed on Day 0 with vendor: required for any valid ROI claim; absent in 61% of AI contracts
Factor 4 — Phased rollout (1 use case proven before adding second): 2.1x less likely to be abandoned; organizations that go broad simultaneously show 44% higher abandonment rate
Factor 5 — Outcome accountability clauses in vendor contract: 1.9x better vendor performance outcomes; only 34% of AI contracts contain measurable outcome commitments
Factor 6 — CDO or CDIO role filled before launch: present in programmes with 84% success rate; absent programmes show 38% success rate
Factor 7 — Clinical champion (non-IT physician or nurse leader): critical for adoption; programmes without clinical champion: 62% end-user rejection rate
Factor 8 — Post-deployment optimization budget (10-15% of initial): programmes with optimization budget achieve 1.6x ROI of those without

MEASURING AI ROI — METHODOLOGY

REVENUE IMPACT MEASUREMENT:
Net revenue improvement: baseline denial rate x baseline revenue vs. post-AI denial rate x same revenue base
Attribution rule: changes in revenue during the first 90 days after AI go-live are attributed 80% to AI (Hawthorne effect and parallel improvements adjust the remaining 20%)
Comparison methodology: preferred approach is year-over-year same-period comparison; cohort comparison (AI users vs. non-users); statistical significance threshold: p < 0.05

COST IMPACT MEASUREMENT:
Staff time reduction: direct measurement via time study before and after AI deployment (100-sample minimum)
Vendor cost reduction: actual invoices compared; travel nurse spend compared quarter-over-quarter
Infrastructure cost: cloud cost dashboard comparison period-over-period

COMMON ROI MEASUREMENT ERRORS:
Error 1: Attributing all denial rate improvement to AI (other factors change simultaneously)
Error 2: No baseline locked on Day 0 — allows retrospective cherry-picking of favorable baseline period
Error 3: Measuring gross savings without subtracting AI investment and operational cost
Error 4: Reporting ROI at 12 months when payback period is 18+ months; programme cancelled prematurely
Error 5: Vendor-reported ROI vs. health system-verified ROI: vendors report 3.2x average; health system verification yields 2.1x average (significant gap)
