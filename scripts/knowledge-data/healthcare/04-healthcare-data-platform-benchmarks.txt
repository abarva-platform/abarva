HEALTHCARE DATA PLATFORM AND CLOUD INFRASTRUCTURE BENCHMARKS
Source: HIMSS Analytics 2025, Gartner Healthcare IT Survey, AbarVa Data Maturity Assessments, CHIME CIO Survey 2025

DATA READINESS BENCHMARKS — ABARVA 0-100 SCALE

Distribution across assessed health systems (n=340+ assessments):
- Score > 70 (AI-ready): 8% of health systems
- Score 50-70 (foundation building, 12-24 months from AI-ready): 29%
- Score 35-50 (significant gaps, 2-4 years from AI-ready without intervention): 41%
- Score < 35 (pre-foundation, AI deployment premature): 22%

Key insight: 63% of health systems that announce AI strategies are operating below Score 50 — meaning the data foundation cannot support the programmes they are committing to

IMPACT OF DATA READINESS SCORE ON AI OUTCOMES:
Score > 65: 2x more AI use cases in production within 24 months vs. Score 50-64
Score 50-64: median programme outcomes (baseline for comparison)
Score 35-49: 44% higher programme cost than projected due to mid-project data remediation
Score < 35: 68% of AI initiatives stall at proof-of-concept; never reach production

SCORING DIMENSIONS (AbarVa Data Readiness Model):
1. Data Completeness (0-20 points): % of structured clinical and financial data accessible
2. Data Quality (0-20 points): error rate, duplicate rate, referential integrity
3. Data Architecture (0-20 points): modern vs. legacy; streaming vs. batch-only
4. Data Governance (0-20 points): ownership, definitions, policies
5. Data Access and Democratization (0-20 points): self-service analytics, API availability
Total: 100 points

TOP DATA READINESS BLOCKERS (by frequency in assessed health systems):
1. No enterprise data catalogue: 67% of assessed organisations
2. Epic/EHR data not integrated with enterprise data warehouse: 61%
3. Master Patient Index (MPI) duplicate rate above 3%: 54%
4. Multiple conflicting definitions for the same metric (e.g., "encounters" means different things in 3 systems): 51%
5. No self-service analytics for clinical/operational users: 48%
6. Data quality score below 70% on critical datasets: 44%
7. No real-time data pipeline (batch ETL only): 41%
8. No data governance committee with enforcement authority: 38%
9. Patient data in 6+ siloed source systems with no unified patient ID: 34%
10. No FHIR API layer for interoperability: 31%

MASTER PATIENT INDEX (MPI) QUALITY BENCHMARKS

Average MPI duplicate rate (no active deduplication programme): 4.8%
Top quartile (with MDM programme + probabilistic matching): 1.2%
Industry median: 3.1%
Critical access hospitals: 6.4% duplicate rate (least governance infrastructure)
AI model accuracy degradation at 3% duplicate rate: 40-60% reduction in model precision
AI model accuracy degradation at 5% duplicate rate: >70% reduction — model effectively unusable for individual-level predictions

MPI REMEDIATION COSTS:
Cost to assess and remediate MPI (per 1M records, one-time project): $0.8-1.4M
Ongoing annual deduplication and maintenance cost: $0.3-0.6M per 1M records
Duration of remediation programme: 8-14 months
Vendors: Verato (probabilistic matching), Rhapsody, InterSystems HealthShare, IBM MDM
Health systems with active MDM programme: 34%
Health systems with no MPI monitoring (passive state): 44%

GENOME FAILURE PATTERNS IN DATA INFRASTRUCTURE

F019 — Data fragmentation unresolved: 68% of health systems cannot connect clinical, financial, and operational data into a unified patient timeline; blocks AI that needs longitudinal data
F027 — ERP fragmentation blocks AI data layer: occurs when billing is in a separate system (Lawson, PeopleSoft, Infor) from Epic; joins fail or are incorrect; 71% of AI deployments limited
F003 — Teradata or legacy data warehouse EOL: forces crisis migration; 38-47% cost premium and 18-24 month delay in AI programme
F034 — No data quality baseline at programme start: if data quality not measured on Day 0, impossible to attribute quality improvements or justify remediation spend
F041 — Pilot data ≠ production data: 44% of AI pilots use specially curated datasets; production data has 2.8x higher error rates; causes model performance degradation at scale

DATA ARCHITECTURE EVOLUTION — ROADMAPS BY STARTING POINT

FROM TERADATA / LEGACY ON-PREMISE DW:
Timeline to AI readiness: 24-36 months
Phase 1 (months 1-6): current state assessment; identify AI use cases that must wait for migration vs. those deployable now
Phase 2 (months 6-18): cloud data warehouse deployment (Snowflake or Azure Synapse); parallel running with Teradata; historical data migration
Phase 3 (months 18-30): Teradata decommission; real-time pipeline added; first AI use cases deployed
Phase 4 (months 30+): streaming pipeline, lakehouse architecture, advanced AI
Total migration cost: $3.8-9.2M per $1B revenue + $0.6-1.2M/year ongoing

FROM EPIC CLARITY (BATCH ETL ONLY):
Timeline to AI readiness: 12-18 months
Phase 1 (months 1-3): establish Azure Synapse or Snowflake as enterprise DW; begin ingesting Epic Clarity tables (2,000+ tables require prioritization)
Phase 2 (months 3-9): MPI deduplication, data quality programme, metric standardization
Phase 3 (months 9-15): first AI use case in production (recommendation: denial prediction — uses only Epic Clarity; does not require real-time)
Phase 4 (months 15-24): add real-time HL7 feeds for clinical AI; integrate non-Epic data sources
Total investment: $1.8-4.2M first 18 months

FROM CLOUD-NATIVE (PATTERN C):
Timeline to AI readiness: 6-12 months (infrastructure already in place)
Focus areas: data quality validation, FHIR pipeline testing, model training, governance establishment
First AI use case in production: 6-9 months
Total investment: $0.8-2.4M (infrastructure ready; investment is in use case development)

DATA PLATFORM VENDOR BENCHMARKS (2025)

SNOWFLAKE IN HEALTHCARE:
Healthcare customers: 1,200+ (fastest growing data platform in healthcare)
Key capability: multi-cloud (runs on Azure, AWS, GCP) — eliminates cloud lock-in
Healthcare Data Cloud: pre-built healthcare data models; HIPAA BAA standard
HL7 FHIR integration: Snowflake + Azure Health Data Services pipeline is most common Epic → AI path
Pricing: $2-6/credit; typical healthcare DW runs $120,000-480,000/year at scale
AI/ML integration: Snowflake Cortex (LLM functions inside SQL); Snowpark (Python ML inside Snowflake)
Key weakness: not native streaming; requires Kafka or similar for real-time (Snowpipe Streaming is near-real-time, not true streaming)

DATABRICKS IN HEALTHCARE:
Healthcare customers: 600+ (growing; preferred by data science-led organizations)
Key capability: unified analytics + ML + streaming in one platform (lakehouse)
Delta Lake: open-source storage format; ACID transactions; schema enforcement
MLflow: open-source ML lifecycle management (experiment tracking, model registry, deployment)
Pricing: $0.22-0.55/DBU; typical healthcare ML workload $180,000-640,000/year
AI/ML integration: best-in-class; train, deploy, and monitor ML models natively
Key weakness: steeper learning curve than Snowflake; requires ML engineering talent

AZURE SYNAPSE ANALYTICS:
Healthcare customers: 2,400+ (boosted by Epic Azure partnership)
Key capability: native Epic integration via Azure Data Factory; part of Microsoft ecosystem
Azure Fabric (2025): Microsoft's unified analytics platform replacing Synapse; combines Power BI, Data Factory, Synapse, and OpenAI
Pricing: bundled with Azure enterprise agreements; often included in existing Microsoft spend
Key weakness: less multi-cloud flexibility than Snowflake; Databricks is preferred for heavy ML workloads

OMOP CDM AND DATA STANDARDIZATION:
OMOP (Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership) Common Data Model: used by 400+ health systems for research and AI
Key benefit: standardizes disparate EHR data into consistent format; enables cross-institution AI model comparison
OMOP adoption trend: growing as health systems join research networks (PCORNet, TriNetX, N3C)
AI models trained on OMOP CDM: portable across institutions; 2.4x faster deployment at second site
Investment to implement OMOP: $280,000-840,000 one-time; $80,000-180,000 annual maintenance

AI TRAINING DATA MANAGEMENT

SYNTHETIC DATA GENERATION:
Synthetic patient data: generated by AI to mimic real patient distributions without PHI
Use cases: AI model training when real data volume is insufficient; vendor demos without PHI exposure; testing without compliance risk
Vendors: Syntegra, MDClone, Simulated Outcomes (TriNetX subsidiary)
Synthetic data quality: 92-96% statistical fidelity to real data (for structured tabular data)
Regulatory status: FDA guidance issued 2024 for synthetic data use in device studies; ONC guidance pending
HIPAA compliance: synthetic data is not PHI (if properly generated); no BAA required for pure synthetic data

FEDERATED LEARNING IN HEALTHCARE:
Federated learning: AI model trains on distributed data without centralizing PHI
Use case: multi-site model training across health systems; each site keeps its data; model weights are shared
Healthcare deployments: 24 known federated learning programmes (as of 2025)
Federated learning platforms: NVIDIA FLARE, PySyft, OpenFL
Patient data volume accessible via federated learning: equivalent of 8-12 health systems pooled (vs. 1 system in siloed approach)
Research impact: multi-site sepsis model trained via federated learning shows 18% higher AUC than single-site model
