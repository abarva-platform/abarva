RETAIL DEMAND FORECASTING AND INVENTORY AI BENCHMARKS
Source: Gartner Supply Chain AI Report 2025, Blue Yonder Customer Data 2024-2025, NRF Supply Chain Survey 2025, Relex Solutions Benchmark Report 2025

OVERVIEW

Demand forecasting AI is the highest-ROI, fastest-payback AI investment available to retail in 2024-2025, with a median payback period of 6-9 months. It is also the strategic prerequisite for nearly every other AI programme — pricing AI, supply chain AI, promotion AI, and replenishment AI all require the data quality improvements and unified inventory foundation that demand forecasting forces an organisation to build. Retailers who deploy demand forecasting first achieve 4.2x ROI on subsequent personalisation vs. 1.8x for retailers who start with personalisation first. Despite this evidence, 39% of $1B+ retailers have deployed demand forecasting AI as of 2025, leaving the majority operating on statistical methods that achieve 74% accuracy at the SKU/store level.

ABARVA GENOME FAILURE PATTERNS — DEMAND FORECASTING

F007: FORECASTING AI ON DIRTY DATA — Demand forecasting AI deployed without data quality remediation. Inventory accuracy is 68% (industry average below best practice). AI predictions are no better than statistical baseline. Prevalence: 64% of retail demand forecasting deployments. Fix: 3-month data quality sprint is prerequisite.

F011: SKIPPING DEMAND FORECASTING FOR PERSONALISATION — Retailers deploy personalisation first (often due to marketing team influence). Without demand forecasting and unified inventory data, personalisation recommendations generate stockouts on promoted items. Personalisation ROI is 41% lower. Fix: Demand forecasting is Sequence 1.

F019: PROMOTIONAL CALENDAR NOT INTEGRATED — AI deployed without promotional calendar integration. AI misses promotion weeks, generating 18% over-forecast for non-promo weeks and 24% under-forecast for promo weeks. Fix: Promotional calendar is a mandatory data source at implementation.

MACHINE LEARNING MODELS IN DEMAND FORECASTING

MODEL TYPES AND PERFORMANCE:
- Classical statistical (ARIMA, Holt-Winters): 74% accuracy; still used by 44% as baseline
- Gradient boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM): 88-90% accuracy; fast training; most common AI upgrade from statistical
- Neural networks (LSTM, Transformer-based): 90-94% accuracy; best for complex patterns; requires 36+ months of data
- Ensemble models (combination of multiple models): 91-94% accuracy (top quartile); used by 48% of AI-active retailers
- Foundation models for forecasting (TimesFM, Chronos, Lag-Llama): emerging; 88-92% accuracy with less historical data

MODEL SELECTION CRITERIA BY USE CASE:
- Stable, high-velocity SKUs (grocery ambient): gradient boosting is sufficient and computationally efficient
- Seasonal fashion SKUs: LSTM neural network handles trend + seasonal interaction better
- New product introduction: foundation models outperform (less reliance on historical data)
- Intermittent demand (long-tail SKUs): Croston, IMAPA models; gradient boosting underperforms on sparse data

RETRAINING AND MODEL LIFECYCLE:
- Model retraining frequency (best practice): weekly for gradient boosting; monthly for neural networks
- Model drift detection: AI monitors forecast accuracy continuously; triggers retraining when accuracy drops > 3pp
- A/B testing of new models: top-quartile retailers run 2-3 model variants in parallel; select best by category
- Model versioning (MLflow, Databricks): 68% of AI-active retailers manage forecast model versions formally
- Data science FTE for demand forecasting AI maintenance: 2-4 FTE ongoing per $5B retailer
