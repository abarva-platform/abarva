SAP ECC TO S/4HANA MIGRATION — RETAIL BENCHMARKS
Source: SAP Partner Network 2025, Deloitte SAP Migration Study 2025, AbarVa Engagement Data

OVERVIEW

SAP ECC 6.0 end of mainstream maintenance is January 2027, with extended premium maintenance available only through 2030 at significant cost premium. As of Q1 2025, 54% of $1B+ global retailers run SAP as their primary ERP, making this the single most consequential IT infrastructure decision for the majority of large-format retailers over the next 24-36 months. Despite the deadline being widely known, 38% of SAP retail customers have no migration plan in place — the highest share of any industry vertical. The primary cause: underestimation of timeline, data complexity, and the organisational change required to move from ECC customisations to S/4HANA standard processes.

MIGRATION TIMELINE BENCHMARKS

Average migration timeline for $5B+ retailer: 32-42 months from programme kickoff to production go-live
Greenfield implementation (new clean build): 6-9 months longer than brownfield on average
Brownfield (system conversion, in-place): preserves customisations but carries forward technical debt

MIGRATION APPROACHES: GREENFIELD VS. BROWNFIELD

GREENFIELD (NEW SYSTEM BUILD):
- 34% of retail S/4HANA migrations
- Highest risk during transition (parallel run required)
- Cleanest architecture — eliminates ECC technical debt entirely
- Longest timeline: 36-54 months for complex retailers
- Best for: retailers with significant process redesign objectives or highly customised ECC
- Risk: 2.4x higher scope change rate than brownfield

BROWNFIELD (SYSTEM CONVERSION):
- 48% of retail S/4HANA migrations
- Preserves existing customisations (customer-specific ABAP code)
- Faster and cheaper initial migration: 18-28% lower cost than greenfield for equivalent scope
- Risk: carries technical debt forward; customisations need review for S/4HANA compatibility
- Best for: retailers who need speed to meet 2027 deadline and have stable processes
- Technical debt rationalisation post-migration: typically required within 24 months

HYBRID (SELECTIVE DATA MIGRATION):
- 18% of retail S/4HANA migrations
- Mid-market approach: new system with curated historical data migration
- Allows leaving behind data quality issues from ECC
- Typical timeline: 14-22 months
- Requires robust data mapping and validation programme

SAP RISE COMMERCIAL ANALYSIS

RISE CONTRACT STRUCTURE:
- RISE is SAP's cloud migration programme: includes S/4HANA licence + hosting + basis support + upgrade management
- Contract structure: 5-year minimum; per-user pricing + consumption (for additional modules)
- Price per user per month ($5B retailer, 500 SAP users): $180-380 depending on module scope
- Annual RISE cost: $8-14M/year (vs. $4-6M/year on-premise maintenance)
- RISE benefit 1: built-in AI (Joule, Business AI) included — no separate AI licence
- RISE benefit 2: automatic upgrades (always on latest S/4HANA) — eliminates upgrade project every 5 years
- RISE benefit 3: SAP manages infrastructure, security patching, basis — reduces IT headcount need by 4-6 FTE

RISE RISK FACTORS:
- Vendor lock-in: RISE makes migration to non-SAP even harder than on-premise (contract commitment)
- Limited customisation: RISE standard processes; custom ABAP heavily restricted
- Data sovereignty: data stored in hyperscaler (AWS/Azure/GCP) — may conflict with some data residency requirements
- Cost transparency: consumption-based components add 15-28% to base subscription in practice
- Exit cost: data portability and migration from RISE to alternative is complex and expensive

RISE vs. CLEAN CORE vs. PRIVATE CLOUD (DECISION FRAMEWORK):
- RISE (public cloud): best for retailers who can adopt standard processes, want managed service, and plan no heavy customisation
- Private Cloud Edition (managed, customer's hyperscaler): best for retailers needing data control or restricted customisation
- On-premise (self-managed): only for retailers with specific regulatory/customisation requirements; declining rapidly
