OVERVIEW

AbarVa's failure pattern library is the most operationally grounded resource of its kind. Unlike theoretical frameworks, every pattern in this library was derived from direct engagement data: post-mortems, programme reviews, and forensic assessments of stalled or failed AI transformation programmes. The F-series (F001–F127) catalogues structural and process failure patterns at the programme level. The L-series (L001–L040) covers leadership and governance failures. The D-series (D001–D035) covers data and technical failures. This document covers the highest-prevalence and highest-impact F-series patterns with full quantification.

The headline finding: 67% of enterprise AI programmes that AbarVa assessed had already triggered three or more failure patterns before any AbarVa engagement began. Identifying and mitigating failure patterns in Phase 0 is the single most cost-effective intervention in any AI programme. The average cost of remediating a failure pattern post-detection in Phase 2 is 4.2x the cost of addressing it in Phase 0.


F001 — NO BASELINE LOCKED DAY 0
Failure rate: 84% (programmes without locked baseline cannot prove ROI)
Prevalence rank: #1 across all industries
Financial impact: median $2.4M in unverifiable ROI claims per programme
Description: AI programme launched without immutable baseline metrics signed off before go-live. ROI cannot be calculated with integrity. Programme is subsequently declared successful without evidence, or ROI claims are challenged and discredited internally.
Root cause: Urgency to deploy AI outpaces organisational discipline to measure. Vendors want to go live quickly. IT teams define success as deployment, not outcome.
Industry breakdown:
  Healthcare: 88% missing baseline (highest — RCM AI most affected)
  Financial services: 81% missing baseline
  Retail: 79% missing baseline
Signal: If baseline was not captured before the first model went to production, it is now too late to establish clean measurement. All post-hoc baseline attempts carry contestable assumptions.
Mitigation: Lock 5-7 KPI baselines with CXO signature before any AI vendor goes live. Use 12-24 months of historical data. Third-party verification preferred for programmes >$5M. AbarVa Baseline Locking Protocol requires: metric definition, historical calculation methodology, named measurement owner, and confounding variable exclusions.
AbarVa solution reference: AVR Phase 0 includes baseline locking as a mandatory gate. No Phase 2 sign-off without locked baselines.
Compounding risk: F001 + F031 (change management under-invested) creates a programme that cannot prove impact AND has low adoption — a dual failure with zero recovery path.


F003 — PLATFORM EOL UNPLANNED MIGRATION
Failure rate: 82% of organisations in this situation face major disruption
Prevalence: 38% of assessed enterprises in 2024-2025 had at least one platform with EOL within 24 months
Cost premium: 38-47% above a planned migration when reactive
Timeline premium: 9-18 months additional versus planned migration
Description: Legacy data platform or core application reaches end-of-life without a migration plan in place. The resulting reactive migration consumes budget and attention that was allocated to AI programme delivery, causing delays, cost overruns, and strategic distraction. In the worst cases, the EOL platform becomes a blocker that halts AI deployment entirely.
Most affected industries and platforms:
  Healthcare: Teradata data warehouse (multiple EOL versions, 2024-2026)
  Retail: SAP ECC 6.0 (end of mainstream support January 2027 — currently affecting 54% of large retailers)
  Banking: legacy core banking systems, COBOL mainframes
  Insurance: legacy policy administration systems (average age 22 years)
Financial impact:
  Reactive migration cost (per application): $480,000-1.2M vs. $280,000-620,000 planned
  Business disruption during emergency migration: $1.8-6.4M (median)
  AI programme delay cost: $0.8-2.4M per quarter of delay
Mitigation: Begin migration planning 18-24 months before EOL date. Business case framing as "AI enablement unlock" rather than IT infrastructure — this reframes the investment as strategic and accelerates board approval. Use AbarVa EOL Risk Register to identify all platforms at risk. Phased migration approach: data to cloud first, application modernisation second.
AbarVa solution reference: MDP (Modern Data Platform) engagement is specifically designed for EOL-triggered migrations, with AI use case activation built into the migration timeline to recover value lost to legacy constraints.


F007 — CDO VACANCY AT AI PROGRAMME LAUNCH
Failure rate: 79% (programmes launched without CDO accountability)
Prevalence: 34% of health system AI programmes have CDO vacancy at launch; 28% across all industries
Description: Chief Data Officer or Chief Digital Officer role is vacant when the AI programme is formally launched. No single executive owns accountability for data quality, model outcomes, or AI programme success. Governance collapses at the implementation phase because no one has authority to make data decisions, enforce quality standards, or escalate blockers.
Pattern variants:
  Variant A: CDO role has never existed (most common in mid-market)
  Variant B: CDO departed mid-programme and was not replaced (L-004 leadership pattern)
  Variant C: CDO exists but has no budget authority (advisory only) — effectively the same failure
Impact cascade:
  1. Data quality issues go unresolved (no decision authority)
  2. Vendor SLA breaches go unenforced (no accountable owner)
  3. Business units disengage when accountability is unclear
  4. AI models deployed without governance sign-off
  5. Programme drifts into pilot proliferation (F044 pattern follows)
AI programme success rates:
  With CDO at launch: 84%
  Without CDO at launch: 38%
  Delta: 46 percentage points — largest single governance factor in AbarVa's genome
Mitigation: Interim CDO appointment before Phase 2 begins. Interim CDO must have budget authority of at least $1M. If full-time CDO search is underway (average 7.4 months), appoint a programme-dedicated interim. Board-level documentation of CDO mandate prevents vacancy risk from derailing the programme if the CDO departs.
AbarVa solution reference: AVR engagement always includes CDO mandate structuring and, if CDO is absent, AbarVa provides fractional CDO capability as part of the Phase 1 governance workstream.


F011 — VENDOR SLA BREACH UNENFORCED
Failure rate: 74% (of SLA-breaching vendors continue to underperform without consequence)
Average breach duration before client detection: 4.8 quarters (15 months)
Description: Primary IT vendor is breaching SLA for multiple quarters with no penalty enforcement by the client. This pattern is extraordinarily common because the client organisation's legal and procurement teams are disconnected from operational delivery teams. Operations teams observe the breach but lack authority to invoke penalties. Legal teams are unaware of the operational context.
Financial impact:
  Average recoverable value when SLA enforced: $4.2-12.1M per vendor relationship
  Value left on table annually per major vendor: $2.4-6.8M
  Compounding service quality decline (post-unenforced breach): 34% further quality reduction (vendors learn non-enforcement is likely)
Common vendor categories and average SLA compliance rates:
  RCM outsourcers (healthcare): 72% SLA compliance — lowest category
  EHR implementation partners: 74% compliance
  Data management vendors: 76% compliance
  AI/ML platform vendors: 78% compliance
  IT infrastructure vendors: 81% compliance
  Cloud providers: 99.8% (but SLA definition excludes most real outages)
Root cause of non-enforcement: Legal/procurement disconnect from operations (primary); "relationship risk" concern outweighing penalty value (secondary); penalty invocation requires multi-level approval (structural barrier)
Mitigation:
  1. Quarterly SLA review with operational metrics, not vendor dashboards
  2. Pre-authorised penalty invocation — operations team can invoke without board approval
  3. Third-party independent SLA measurement (not vendor self-reporting)
  4. Automatic penalty deductions (invoice-level) triggered by metric thresholds
  5. Separate legal authority structure for SLA enforcement from account management
AbarVa solution reference: Vendor Intelligence module of AVR engagement includes SLA forensic review and enforcement protocol design. AbarVa has recovered $4.2-12.1M for clients in a single vendor renegotiation cycle.


F019 — CUSTOMER DATA FRAGMENTATION UNRESOLVED
Failure rate: 68% (of personalisation or churn AI programmes launched before data unification)
Prevalence: 61% of assessed retail and FS organisations have customer data in 6+ systems
Average systems containing customer data per enterprise: 6.4
Description: AI personalisation, churn prevention, or customer lifetime value programme is launched before customer identity has been unified across all source systems. The AI model trains on incomplete customer profiles, dramatically reducing predictive accuracy. Campaigns target the wrong customers, miss high-value customers, and surface contradictions (same customer classified as both loyal and at-risk in different systems).
Financial impact:
  Personalisation ROI with unified identity: 3.4x at 24 months
  Personalisation ROI without unified identity: 0.4x at 24 months
  Delta: 3x ROI gap attributable entirely to data fragmentation
Time to unify with modern CDT tooling (CDP or identity resolution platform): 4-8 months
Competitive window risk: personalisation capability is a fast-moving competitive arena — 6-8 months of delay while competitors deploy can result in durable competitive disadvantage
Root cause: business pressure to deploy personalisation AI before data engineering foundation is ready. Vendor AI sales cycles move faster than data readiness can support.
Mitigation: CDP or identity resolution platform before personalisation AI vendor contract. Sequence is non-negotiable: Identity → Unification → Profiling → Personalisation AI. AbarVa Data Readiness Assessment specifically includes customer identity fragmentation as a scoring dimension.
AbarVa solution reference: MDP engagement includes CDP implementation or identity resolution as a prerequisite workstream when F019 is detected.


F027 — ERP DATA QUALITY BLOCKS AI
Failure rate: 71% (of supply chain or inventory AI programmes attempted before ERP data remediation)
Prevalence: 44% of assessed mid-market organisations have 3+ ERP instances
Common scenario: post-M&A environments with 4-12 ERP instances containing inconsistent product codes, supplier records, and cost structures
Description: Supply chain optimisation, demand forecasting, or inventory AI is blocked or significantly degraded by inconsistent, duplicate, or contradictory data across multiple ERP instances. The AI model cannot identify the same product, supplier, or location reliably across systems. This is not an ERP replacement problem — it is a data harmonisation problem that can be solved in the data layer without ERP migration.
Financial impact:
  Demand forecasting accuracy with unresolved ERP fragmentation: 68-74% (baseline)
  Demand forecasting accuracy after ERP data harmonisation: 87-93%
  Inventory optimisation value unlocked: $12-48M per $1B revenue (depending on current performance)
  Cost of ERP data harmonisation vs. full ERP replacement: 8-12% of replacement cost
Solution: Silver layer entity resolution using Databricks DLT or equivalent — creates a unified "golden record" view of product, supplier, and location data without replacing source ERPs. Not an ERP replacement project.
Average resolution time: 4-6 months with Databricks DLT or DBT-based entity resolution
Mitigation: Conduct ERP data harmonisation sprint as Phase 1 deliverable before AI use case development. AbarVa's MDP engagement includes ERP entity resolution as a standard workstream for organisations with 3+ ERP instances.
AbarVa solution reference: MDP — specifically the "Silver Layer Entity Resolution" workstream designed for post-M&A fragmentation.


F031 — CHANGE MANAGEMENT UNDER-INVESTED
Failure rate: 68% (when change management receives < 10% of programme budget)
Prevalence: 54% of AI programmes allocate less than the 12% minimum
Description: AI programme allocates insufficient budget and resource to change management, training, communication, and adoption programmes. The AI models are deployed to an unprepared workforce that continues using legacy processes in parallel, or actively resists the new tools. The ROI case is based on full adoption assumptions that are never realised.
Adoption rates by change management investment level:
  < 8% of budget: 41% adoption at 18 months
  8-12% of budget: 58% adoption at 18 months
  12-15% of budget: 76% adoption at 18 months
  > 15% of budget: 84% adoption at 18 months
ROI correlation:
  Programmes with < 8% change management: average ROI 140% at 24 months
  Programmes with 12-15% change management: average ROI 280% at 24 months
  Delta: 140 percentage points — change management investment essentially doubles ROI
Resistance patterns by role:
  Middle management: 41% active resistance (highest — AI perceived as undermining decision authority)
  Clinical staff (healthcare): 34% resistance (fear of AI replacing clinical judgement)
  Frontline workers: 22% resistance (lower when AI framed as reducing tedious tasks)
Cost of re-adoption programmes (after initial under-investment): $1.8-4.2M
Mitigation: Change management budget minimum 12% of total programme cost from Day 1. Lighthouse user programme (no incremental cost, 2.8x adoption acceleration). Hard cutover with safety net rather than indefinite parallel run. Manager performance reviews must include AI adoption KPIs — without consequence, 38% of adoption targets are missed.
AbarVa solution reference: Change management is included as a mandatory deliverable in all AVR, RCM, and MDP engagements. AbarVa's Lighthouse User Framework is a zero-cost accelerator.


F044 — COMMITTEE WITHOUT AUTHORITY
Failure rate: 58% (of programmes run by committees lacking delegated spend authority)
Prevalence: 62% of enterprise AI steering committees lack pre-delegated authority above $100K
Decision speed impact: committees without authority make decisions 3.4x slower than those with pre-delegated thresholds
Description: AI steering committee is established but cannot approve spend above $100,000 without full board sign-off. Every vendor contract, pilot approval, and technology purchase requires a board escalation cycle of 6-14 weeks. Vendors deprioritise the client. Talented AI employees leave for faster-moving organisations. Urgency is lost. Competitors move ahead.
Financial and strategic impact:
  Vendor renegotiation leverage lost due to slow decisions: $0.8-2.4M per contract cycle
  AI talent attrition from bureaucratic friction: 28% annual attrition in committee-managed programmes (vs. 18% in CDO-owned programmes)
  Time-to-production delay from approval friction: average 4.8 months added per use case
Common root cause: board-level nervousness about AI spending without clear ROI governance. Paradoxically, by slowing the programme, the lack of authority undermines the ROI that would justify the authority.
Mitigation: Pre-approve quarterly spending thresholds ($1-5M depending on programme size) for AI steering committee. CEO or board delegate authority explicitly and in writing. Quarterly board review of AI programme KPIs replaces transaction-by-transaction approval. Emergency reserve (10% of programme budget) available to programme director without escalation.
AbarVa solution reference: AVR governance design workstream includes authority matrix design as a standard deliverable. AbarVa has specific templates for board delegation language that have been tested across 34 governance implementations.


F052 — AI MODEL IN PRODUCTION WITHOUT MONITORING
Failure rate: 61% (of unmonitored models degrade significantly within 12 months)
Prevalence: 44% of production AI models have no automated drift monitoring
Description: AI model deployed to production without automated monitoring for performance degradation, data drift, or concept drift. Model accuracy degrades silently over time as the world changes (seasonal patterns, market shifts, process changes). Business users continue to act on AI recommendations that are no longer accurate.
Performance degradation timeline without monitoring:
  Month 1-3: < 5% accuracy degradation (within acceptable range)
  Month 4-6: 8-14% degradation (becoming material)
  Month 7-12: 18-34% degradation (model is making systematically wrong recommendations)
  Month 12+: 34-54% degradation (model worse than baseline heuristics in many cases)
Financial impact of model degradation (examples):
  Demand forecasting: 14% accuracy loss → $8-18M excess inventory or lost sales per $1B revenue
  Fraud detection: 22% recall degradation → $4.8-12M additional fraud losses per year
  RCM denial prediction: 18% degradation → $3.2-8.4M in uncaptured revenue
Mitigation: Model monitoring must be a Phase 2 deployment requirement, not a Phase 3 add-on. Automated alerts when accuracy metrics fall >5% below training baseline. Monthly data drift reports. Quarterly model retraining schedule. AbarVa PDLC engagement includes MLOps monitoring as a standard component.
AbarVa solution reference: PDLC (AI Product Delivery Lifecycle) — model monitoring, drift detection, and automated retraining pipelines.


F068 — PROOF OF CONCEPT THAT CANNOT SCALE
Failure rate: 74% (of PoCs not designed for production scale requirements)
Prevalence: 48% of enterprise AI programmes stall between PoC and production
Description: Proof of concept built on manually assembled, curated data in a Jupyter notebook or ad hoc environment. The PoC demonstrates the concept successfully but cannot be deployed to production because it has no data pipeline, no inference infrastructure, no monitoring, no security controls, and no documentation. The cost and time to rebuild for production is effectively the same as starting over.
Common PoC failure anatomy:
  1. Data scientist manually queries database for training data (no automated pipeline)
  2. Model trained in notebook on laptop or ad hoc VM
  3. No feature engineering documentation
  4. Prediction served by running notebook manually
  5. No version control, no model registry, no A/B testing capability
  6. No monitoring, no retraining schedule
Estimated rebuild cost (PoC to production): 2.4-4.8x original PoC cost
Mitigation: Define production requirements before PoC begins (not after). PoC environment must mirror production constraints. Use MLOps platform (Databricks MLflow, SageMaker, Azure ML) from day one — even for PoC. AbarVa's PDLC engagement specifically addresses this pattern by establishing MLOps infrastructure before first use case is developed.
AbarVa solution reference: PDLC (AI Product Delivery Lifecycle).


F081 — VENDOR SELECTED BEFORE STRATEGY
Failure rate: 44% programme success rate (vs. 74% when strategy precedes vendor)
Prevalence: 48% of enterprise AI programmes select primary AI vendor before strategy is defined
Description: AI programme begins with a vendor selection process or vendor-initiated conversation before the organisation has defined its use cases, data readiness, or success metrics. The vendor's sales process becomes the de facto programme strategy. The vendor optimises for contract value and product fit; the client optimises for relationship and technology brand rather than outcome.
Financial impact:
  Cost overrun rate: 2.1x higher than strategy-first programmes
  ROI at 24 months: 38% lower than strategy-first programmes
  "Shelfware" rate (platform deployed but underutilised): 44% of vendor-first programmes
Mitigation: Phase 0 strategy (AbarVa AVR) must precede any vendor RFP. Use case definition before vendor selection is non-negotiable. Vendor selection criteria derived from use case requirements, not sales relationship. AbarVa is independent of technology vendors — this structural independence is specifically designed to protect clients from this pattern.
AbarVa solution reference: AVR Phase 0 — vendor-neutral strategy before any vendor engagement.


F095 — SHADOW AI PROLIFERATION
Failure rate: 56% (of programmes where shadow AI is unaddressed face governance and compliance exposure)
Prevalence: 72% of assessed enterprises have undocumented AI tools in use by business users
Description: Business users independently adopt AI tools (ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, custom GPT agents, AI-enhanced SaaS features) without IT knowledge, governance review, or security assessment. Sensitive data is submitted to consumer AI products. Regulatory exposure accumulates. When the official AI programme launches, it competes with entrenched shadow AI that users prefer.
Regulatory risk:
  Healthcare: PHI submitted to consumer AI = HIPAA breach risk ($100-$1.9M per violation)
  Financial services: proprietary financial data in consumer AI = regulatory exposure
  General: GDPR/CCPA implications for personal data in unapproved AI tools
Mitigation: Shadow AI audit in Phase 0. Formal AI tool usage policy before official programme launch. Provide governed, approved AI tools that are better than shadow alternatives — the only sustainable solution is to make the official tools easier to use than the shadow tools.
AbarVa solution reference: AVR governance design includes shadow AI assessment and policy framework.


F112 — DATA SCIENCE AND ENGINEERING ORGANISATIONAL SPLIT
Failure rate: 62% of programmes where these teams have separate reporting structures and toolsets
Prevalence: 64% of enterprises have separate data science (analytics/CDO) and data engineering (IT/CIO) organisations
Description: Data science team reports to CDO and uses Python/ML tools. Data engineering team reports to CIO and uses ETL/SQL tools. The teams have different mandates, different priorities, and different toolsets. AI models built by data science cannot be deployed because data engineering does not own AI pipelines. Data engineers are not prioritised to support model deployment. Models sit in development limbo for 6-18 months.
Production deployment time comparison:
  Unified lakehouse team (Databricks): 2.6 months average, model to production
  Split data science/engineering org: 6.8 months average
  Delta: 4.2 months per model
Mitigation: Unified AI/data team under CDO with shared platform (Databricks lakehouse). If full restructuring is not feasible, establish an AI delivery squad with embedded data engineers and data scientists as a bridging structure. PDLC engagement addresses this with MLOps infrastructure that bridges the gap.
AbarVa solution reference: PDLC and MDP engagements include team structure design.


CROSS-PATTERN COMPOUNDING EFFECTS

Failure patterns do not occur in isolation. AbarVa analysis of 127 failure patterns shows that 81% of failed programmes triggered 3+ patterns simultaneously. The most dangerous compound combinations:

COMPOUND A — "READY, FIRE, AIM": F001 + F081 + F031
  Description: No baseline, vendor selected first, change management under-invested
  Outcome: Programme that cannot prove ROI, was designed around vendor capability rather than client need, and has low adoption
  Recovery cost: $4.8-12M; 67% of programmes in this compound fail to recover

COMPOUND B — "GOVERNANCE VOID": F007 + F044 + F095
  Description: No CDO, committee without authority, shadow AI proliferating
  Outcome: Governance-free environment where risk accumulates while official programme stalls
  Recovery cost: $2.4-6.8M plus regulatory remediation

COMPOUND C — "DATA QUICKSAND": F027 + D-001 + D-006
  Description: ERP fragmentation, no data catalogue, governance not enforced
  Outcome: AI model development begins and repeatedly stalls on data quality issues, consuming budget without producing models
  Recovery cost: $3.2-8.4M in platform and engineering rework

COMPOUND D — "SCALE STALL": F052 + F068 + F112
  Description: No model monitoring, PoC cannot scale, split data science/engineering teams
  Outcome: Multiple PoCs in Jupyter notebooks that cannot reach production; programme declared a "pilot culture"
  Recovery cost: 2.4x to rebuild PoCs in production-ready infrastructure
