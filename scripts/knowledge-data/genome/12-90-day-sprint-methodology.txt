90-DAY SPRINT AND QUICK WIN METHODOLOGY
Source: AbarVa Phase Gate Framework, AbarVa Genome — Programme Survival Patterns
Updated: 2025.Q1

OVERVIEW

The 90-day quick win is the most critical single event in an AI programme beyond initial mandate creation. Its impact on programme survival is quantified precisely: programmes with a 90-day quick win continue at 78% rate through 24 months; programmes without continue at only 38%. This 40-percentage-point difference represents the organisational psychology of AI investment — boards, sponsors, and teams all need visible evidence before they will sustain commitment through the difficulty of Phase 2 and 3.

The 90-day sprint methodology is not about moving fast for the sake of speed. It is about selecting a use case and a scope that is genuinely achievable in 90 days, building it rigorously, and communicating the result effectively to the stakeholders who make Phase 3 investment decisions. A technically excellent 90-day result that is communicated poorly has the same programme survival rate as no result at all.

Key findings:
  90-day quick win → 78% programme continuation at 24 months
  No 90-day quick win → 38% programme continuation
  Delta: 40 percentage points — largest single Phase 2 success factor
  Phase 2 investment approval rate after 90-day win: 84%
  AI team retention through Phase 2 after visible win: 94%
  Failure reason #1 for 90-day sprints: use case selected without confirming data availability


THE 90-DAY IMPERATIVE — WHY THIS WINDOW MATTERS

BOARD PSYCHOLOGY:
  Average board attention span on strategic initiative: 2-3 quarterly cycles
  Typical board meeting frequency: monthly to quarterly
  Board demand for early evidence: most boards expect progress signal within first budget cycle
  Without 90-day evidence: AI programme competes with other initiatives for continued support; board defaults to scepticism
  With 90-day evidence: board transitions from "investor monitoring uncertainty" to "investor backing proven direction"

SPONSOR CONFIDENCE:
  Executive sponsors face internal political pressure proportional to the investment size
  Without visible progress: sponsor is defending an investment with no evidence; political position weakens
  With visible progress: sponsor has a success story; internal credibility grows; programme gains allies
  The 90-day win is the sponsor's political insurance policy

WORKFORCE ENGAGEMENT:
  Human psychological engagement peaks at programme launch (excitement) and valleys at month 3-4 (reality of difficulty)
  Without 90-day win: engagement valley becomes permanent disengagement; talent starts seeking other opportunities
  With 90-day win: engagement valley converts to restored motivation; team sees its work validated
  AbarVa finding: 94% of AI teams stay through Phase 2 after a visible 90-day win; 61% without

BUDGET CYCLE ALIGNMENT:
  Many organisations run quarterly budget reviews
  Without Phase 2 evidence by quarter 2: Phase 2 budget is at risk in quarterly review
  With Phase 2 quick win by day 90: Phase 2 budget renewal is routine; Phase 3 discussion begins
  Financial discipline: the 90-day structure forces the programme to operate at budget rhythm, not IT project rhythm

COMPETITOR VISIBILITY:
  Competitor AI deployments typically become public or visible to customers within 90 days of launch
  Without 90-day win: leadership begins questioning the pace of their own programme vs. competitors
  With 90-day win: leadership has their own evidence to counter competitive narrative


DEFINING THE RIGHT 90-DAY WIN

Not every use case is appropriate for a 90-day sprint. Selection criteria are more important than execution — choosing the wrong use case makes failure likely regardless of team quality.

SELECTION CRITERIA (ALL FIVE REQUIRED):

Criterion 1 — MEASURABLE (vs. locked baseline):
  Must produce a specific numeric improvement in a KPI that was baselined in Phase 0
  Acceptable: "Denial rate improved from 9.8% to 7.4% in the first 30 claims cohort processed by AI"
  Not acceptable: "The AI model is working well" or "users are positive"
  Non-negotiable: if there is no locked baseline, the 90-day win cannot be validated

Criterion 2 — VISIBLE (board-level comprehension):
  Must be something a board member can understand without technical explanation
  Test: can you explain the win in one sentence without using the words "algorithm", "model", "dataset", or "pipeline"?
  Example that passes: "AI denied claims screening reduced first-pass denial rate by 24% in pilot cohort"
  Example that fails: "Our gradient boosting ensemble achieved 0.89 AUC on the hold-out test set"

Criterion 3 — ATTRIBUTABLE (clearly caused by AI):
  Must be demonstrably caused by the AI intervention, not by other changes during the period
  Risk: concurrent operational changes contaminate attribution
  Mitigation: control group comparison; baseline period that excludes concurrent changes; narrow scope where AI is the only variable

Criterion 4 — CREDIBLE (real data, not pilot cherry-pick):
  Must be based on representative real-world data, not curated best-case scenarios
  Credibility destroyers: using hand-selected "easy" data; using historical data that was pre-cleaned; using a time period that was abnormally favourable
  Board reaction to cherry-picked results: 68% of sponsors become sceptical of subsequent results when early results are found to be unrepresentative

Criterion 5 — SCALE-ABLE (replicable across the organisation):
  The 90-day win should demonstrate capability that can be replicated at scale
  It must not be dependent on: exceptional individual effort, special circumstances, or one-time data availability
  Scale-ability test: "Could we do this for all 100 hospitals/stores/branches with the same approach?"


QUICK WIN SELECTION BY INDUSTRY

HEALTHCARE:

Best 90-day win options:
Option A: Denial scrubbing AI (highest recommended)
  Use case: AI reviews claims before submission and flags likely denials for correction
  Data requirement: Epic Clarity billing data (typically available and accessible)
  Achievable improvement: 15-25% denial rate reduction in first 90 days
  Board visibility: CFO-level — direct revenue impact is immediate and quantifiable
  Data availability risk: LOW (Epic Clarity is the standard healthcare data source; AbarVa has pre-built extraction queries)
  Technical complexity: LOW-MEDIUM (supervised classification on structured claims data)
  Timeline confidence: HIGH — this use case has been delivered in 90 days in 28 of 31 AbarVa healthcare engagements

Option B: Prior authorisation time reduction
  Achievable improvement: 40-60% reduction in PA cycle time
  Board visibility: COO/CMO — operational efficiency and physician satisfaction
  Data risk: MEDIUM (requires PA workflow system access; not always integrated with Epic)
  Timeline confidence: MEDIUM — depends on PA system access

Avoid for 90-day sprint:
  Sepsis early warning (complex; requires real-time data pipeline; 4-6 months minimum)
  Readmission prediction (requires 12+ months of outcome data to validate)
  Clinical documentation AI (requires physician workflow change; 90 days is too fast for adoption)

FINANCIAL SERVICES:

Best 90-day win options:
Option A: AML/fraud false positive reduction (highest recommended)
  Use case: ML model reduces false positive rate in transaction monitoring; fewer unnecessary investigations
  Achievable improvement: 30-50% false positive reduction in 90 days
  Board visibility: COO/Chief Risk Officer — operational and regulatory significance
  Data availability risk: LOW (transaction monitoring system data is accessible)
  Technical complexity: MEDIUM (ML classification on transaction features)

Option B: Trade reconciliation or breaks reduction
  Use case: AI identifies and resolves reconciliation exceptions automatically
  Achievable improvement: 60-80% reduction in manual reconciliation exceptions
  Board visibility: CFO/COO — direct time and cost saving
  Data availability risk: LOW (reconciliation system data well-structured)

Avoid for 90-day sprint:
  Credit underwriting model (regulatory model risk approval required; 6-12 months)
  Customer churn prediction (requires unified identity; 4-6 months data work first)
  Market risk model (regulatory validation timeline incompatible with 90 days)

RETAIL:

Best 90-day win options:
Option A: Demand forecasting improvement for one category (highest recommended)
  Use case: AI forecast for top-volume category (e.g., beverages, cleaning products) replaces statistical baseline
  Achievable improvement: 10-15% MAPE improvement (from 26% to 22%) in 90 days
  Board visibility: COO/CFO — inventory cost and availability
  Data availability risk: LOW (POS data + inventory data typically available)
  Scope discipline: limit to 1 category and 50-100 pilot stores — do not attempt full range in 90 days

Option B: Markdown optimisation for seasonal clearance
  Use case: AI recommends markdown timing and depth for end-of-season clearance
  Achievable improvement: 8-12% reduction in markdown depth; 15-20% better sell-through
  Board visibility: CFO/GMM — direct margin impact visible in seasonal results
  Data availability risk: LOW (POS + inventory + pricing history)

Avoid for 90-day sprint:
  Personalisation AI (requires identity resolution; 4-6 months before meaningful personalisation)
  End-to-end supply chain optimisation (requires multi-system integration; 6-12 months)
  New store site selection (requires external data integration and validation; 6+ months)

MANUFACTURING:

Best 90-day win options:
Option A: Predictive quality detection on one production line
  Use case: AI detects quality defects from sensor data before product leaves the production stage
  Achievable improvement: 20-35% reduction in defect escape rate
  Board visibility: COO/quality leadership — direct scrap and rework cost
  Data availability risk: MEDIUM (requires sensor data connectivity to cloud — often the blocker)

Option B: OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness) prediction on one machine class
  Use case: AI predicts machine maintenance need from vibration/temperature sensor data
  Achievable improvement: 12-18% reduction in unplanned downtime
  Data availability risk: MEDIUM-HIGH (sensor data must be accessible and clean)


SPRINT DESIGN FRAMEWORK

Week-by-week execution template:

WEEK 1-2: FOUNDATION AND DATA AUDIT
  Day 1-3: data access confirmed; source systems identified; access credentials established
  Day 3-5: data quality audit of training dataset — completeness, accuracy, freshness assessment
  Day 5 (gate): go/no-go decision based on data quality
    GO: proceed if data quality > 80% completeness on required fields
    NO-GO: alternative use case selected or data remediation sprint initiated (not a 90-day sprint)
  Day 5-10: baseline KPI confirmed from source data; discrepancy with Phase 0 baseline documented
  Day 10-14: model training environment established (Databricks / SageMaker / Azure ML provisioned)

WEEK 3-6: MODEL DEVELOPMENT
  Week 3: feature engineering — which data elements predict the outcome?
  Week 4: initial model training; first accuracy assessment on training data
  Week 5: model validation on hold-out test set; accuracy vs. business requirement threshold confirmed
  Week 6: model review with business subject matter expert — do predictions make business sense?
    Key check: model must pass "sanity check" by domain expert — if predictions are consistently counter-intuitive, stop and investigate before proceeding

WEEK 7-8: VALIDATION AND CONTROLLED ENVIRONMENT TESTING
  Week 7: model deployed in controlled environment (not production); predictions generated on recent real data
  Week 7: side-by-side comparison — AI prediction vs. actual outcome for past 30-day period
  Week 7-8: business user review — do AI recommendations align with expert judgement?
  Week 8: accuracy threshold test — must exceed minimum viable accuracy before production deployment
    Healthcare denial: >85% precision on flagged claims (false positives cost operations money)
    Retail forecast: MAPE improvement >8% vs. baseline (credible improvement)
    Fraud: >90% recall on known fraud cases (missing fraud is more costly than false positives)

WEEK 9-10: PRODUCTION DEPLOYMENT (LIMITED SCOPE)
  Week 9: production deployment to pilot scope (one hospital, one store, one product category)
  Week 9: AI and manual process run in parallel (no hard cutover yet — safety validation)
  Week 10: monitor AI recommendations vs. human decisions; confirm AI quality in production environment
  Week 10: document first production results against locked baseline

WEEK 11-12: MEASUREMENT AND COMMUNICATION
  Week 11: extract measurement data from source systems; compare to locked baseline
  Week 11: calculate preliminary ROI (extrapolated from pilot results to full programme scale)
  Week 12: board/CXO presentation — results with evidence
    Presentation must be: led by business owner (not IT/data science), visual (one chart of baseline vs. achievement), financial (dollar impact quantified), credible (methodology shown), future-facing (Phase 3 roadmap shown)
  Day 90: programme continuation decision — expected outcome: Phase 3 approval and expanded budget


90-DAY WIN FAILURE PATTERNS

FAILURE A: USE CASE SELECTED WITHOUT DATA AUDIT
  Description: team selects compelling use case in Phase 0; sprint begins; data audit in week 1 reveals critical data unavailable
  Result: sprint reset at week 2-3; timeline overruns 90 days; political capital lost
  Prevalence: 31% of 90-day sprints face significant data discovery problems
  Mitigation: data audit is Week 1 Gate 1 — non-negotiable go/no-go before sprint continues
  AbarVa practice: data audit checklist completed before use case is finalised in Phase 0 discovery

FAILURE B: SCOPE TOO AMBITIOUS
  Description: team selects most ambitious version of use case rather than minimum viable version
  Example: full RCM AI suite (denial + PA + coding) instead of denial scrubbing only
  Result: technical development extends past 90 days; incomplete work shown to board; confidence lost
  Mitigation: explicitly scope the smallest replicable version of the use case; all other capabilities are Phase 3

FAILURE C: VENDOR DEPENDENCY
  Description: 90-day sprint depends on an external AI vendor delivering their product
  Risk: vendor delivery delays push past 90 days; client has no control over timeline
  Mitigation: 90-day win must be deliverable by internal team + AbarVa team without vendor dependency
  Exception: if vendor integration is pre-built and reliable, may include — but vendor must commit to 60-day delivery
  AbarVa rule: no 90-day sprint is permitted to have critical path dependency on a vendor

FAILURE D: TECHNICAL SUCCESS, COMMUNICATION FAILURE
  Description: AI model performs well; results are real; but presentation to board is technical and unconvincing
  Symptom: board says "that's interesting" instead of "approve Phase 3 budget"
  Root cause: data science team presents; business language absent; financial impact not quantified
  Mitigation: board presentation must be: designed by communications lead, presented by business owner, contain one primary financial outcome number, and show Phase 3 roadmap

FAILURE E: NO SAFETY NET FOR HARD CUTOVER
  Description: team deploys directly to production without parallel run; early error causes business disruption; trust in AI collapses
  Mitigation: always implement 30-60 day parallel run before hard cutover; parallel run builds confidence; hard cutover happens after parallel validation
  Note: parallel run beyond 90 days becomes a failure pattern itself (see F031 extended parallel run)

90-DAY SPRINT RESOURCE REQUIREMENTS

The 90-day sprint requires a focused team for 90 days. Trying to run a 90-day sprint as a part-time activity fails in 78% of attempts — 90 days is already the maximum timeline for in-scope use cases.

MINIMUM TEAM FOR 90-DAY SPRINT:
  ML Engineer (1 FTE for 90 days): model development, feature engineering, model deployment
  Data Engineer (0.5-1 FTE for 90 days): data pipeline, source data extraction, Silver layer preparation
  Data Scientist (0.5 FTE): exploratory analysis, feature selection, model validation
  Business Translator (0.25 FTE): domain expert validation; sanity check on model predictions; user acceptance
  Programme Manager (0.25 FTE): sprint tracking, stakeholder communication, board presentation preparation
  Total: approximately 3 FTE equivalent for 90 days

  Traditional consulting equivalent: $580,000-1.2M
  Internal team only (if capable): $180,000-340,000 in loaded team cost

ROI on 90-day sprint investment:

  ROI on sprint investment: 6.6-26x in year 1 alone
  Strategic value (Phase 3 approval): $18-38M in Phase 3 investment unlocked by 90-day evidence


90-DAY SPRINT COMMUNICATION STRATEGY

The communication strategy is as important as the technical delivery. A technically excellent 90-day result presented poorly has the same programme survival rate as a poor result.

INTERNAL COMMUNICATION TIMELINE:
  Week 1: launch communication — "We're starting a 90-day AI sprint; here's what we're trying to achieve and how we'll measure success"
  Week 6: mid-sprint check-in to stakeholders — "We're on track; here's what the data is showing early"
  Week 10: preview for executive sponsor — "Here's what the result will look like; prepare for board presentation"
  Week 12: board presentation and business team announcement

BOARD PRESENTATION FORMAT:
  Slide 1: The problem we set out to solve (baseline KPI, financial consequence)
  Slide 2: What we built and how it works (one paragraph, no technical jargon)
  Slide 3: The result — KPI vs. baseline, one clear chart
  Slide 4: Financial impact (annualised, conservative estimate)
  Slide 5: What comes next — Phase 3 roadmap and budget request
  Total: 5 slides; 20 minutes maximum; business owner presents (not data science team)

KEY LANGUAGE FOR 90-DAY BOARD PRESENTATION:
  "Before AI: denial rate 9.8%. After AI in our 90-day pilot: 7.4%. At current claims volume, this improvement is worth $8.4M annually."
  "We chose the smallest, most credible version of this use case for the first 90 days. The full programme will achieve [X] in [timeframe]."
  "The data proves this works. We are asking to invest $[X] to scale this across [scope]. Expected return: $[Y] in [timeframe]."
  Do NOT say: "Our model achieved 0.89 AUC on the hold-out test set" — board does not know what this means

BUSINESS TEAM ANNOUNCEMENT (POST-BOARD APPROVAL):
  Announce by the business unit leader, not IT or data science
  Language: "Our team has achieved X using AI in 90 days. Starting [date], all [role] will use the AI tool for [process]. [Manager name] will support the transition."
  This announcement implicitly communicates: this is real, the leadership is committed, this is mandatory
  Do not position as optional: "You can try the AI tool if you like" produces 28% adoption; "We are using the AI tool from [date]" produces 76% adoption


SPECIFIC QUICK WIN EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

For a 90-day win to be credible and program-sustaining, the evidence must meet specific standards:

EVIDENCE STANDARD (ABARVA MINIMUM):
  1. Comparison to locked baseline (not to estimated or remembered baseline)
  2. Same metric definition before and after (no cherry-picking more favourable metric post-hoc)
  3. Representative sample (not cherry-picked "easy" data)
  4. Real production data (not test data or historical simulation)
  5. Financial impact quantified using confirmed revenue/cost figures (not estimates)
  6. Timeline documented (sprint started [date]; baseline measured [date]; result measured [date])

EVIDENCE ENHANCERS (OPTIONAL BUT POWERFUL):
  Control group comparison: comparing AI-enabled cohort vs. same-period non-AI cohort strengthens attribution
  Statistical significance: if sample is small, confidence interval should be reported
  Third-party observation: having a neutral observer (AbarVa, finance team, internal audit) verify the result is significantly more credible
  Vendor confirmation: if the AI vendor also confirms the result against their own metrics, credibility increases

EVIDENCE THAT UNDERMINES CREDIBILITY (AVOID):
  Sample size too small (< 30 transactions/claims/predictions): board will correctly question statistical validity
  Time period too short (< 30 days): insufficient to show stable performance
  Comparison to manually curated "best-case" baseline: if the baseline was created after the AI result to maximise the delta, this will be detected
  Reporting model performance (AUC, accuracy) instead of business outcome: not meaningful to board
