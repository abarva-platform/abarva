DATA READINESS AND AI PROGRAMME OUTCOME CORRELATION
Source: AbarVa Assessment Database — 340+ engagements
Assessment Framework: AbarVa Data Readiness Score (DRS), Version 3.2
Updated: 2025.Q1

OVERVIEW

Data readiness is the single most predictive factor in AI programme outcomes outside of governance. AbarVa has assessed 340+ organisations using the Data Readiness Score (DRS), a composite 0-100 index built from four domains. The headline finding: only 8% of assessed organisations are genuinely AI-ready, while 63% have gaps significant enough to cause material AI programme underperformance if not addressed before use case deployment begins.

The most expensive mistake in enterprise AI programmes is selecting an AI vendor or use case before assessing and addressing data readiness. Organisations that invest in data readiness before platform selection achieve 2.8x better AI programme outcomes than those that select the platform first. This is not a theoretical claim — it is directly observable in outcome data across 340+ engagements.

Correct investment sequence:
  Phase 0: Baseline and data readiness assessment
  Phase 1: Governance + data quality + platform selection (informed by readiness)
  Phase 2: Use case development on solid foundation
  Wrong sequence: Platform selection → Use case development → (discover) data quality gaps → rework → (miss) governance → delay


ABARVA DATA READINESS SCORE (DRS) FRAMEWORK

The DRS is a 0-100 composite score across four domains:

Domain 1: Data Quality (30% weight)
  Measures: completeness, accuracy, consistency, timeliness of critical datasets
  Sub-components: record completeness %, field accuracy rate, cross-system consistency, data freshness SLA compliance
  Scoring: 0-30 points
  AI-ready threshold: 22+ points (≥73% quality on critical datasets)

Domain 2: Infrastructure Maturity (25% weight)
  Measures: cloud adoption, data platform modernity, streaming capability, storage architecture
  Sub-components: cloud migration %, data platform generation, streaming vs. batch capability, data catalogue existence
  Scoring: 0-25 points
  AI-ready threshold: 18+ points (cloud-first with modern lakehouse)

Domain 3: Data Governance (25% weight)
  Measures: governance framework, MDM, data ownership, policy enforcement, lineage tracking
  Sub-components: governance framework existence and enforcement, MDM coverage, data ownership clarity, automated quality gates
  Scoring: 0-25 points
  AI-ready threshold: 18+ points (enforced governance with MDM)

Domain 4: Talent and Capability (20% weight)
  Measures: data literacy, data team skills, self-service analytics, ML capability
  Sub-components: data literate workforce %, data team seniority, self-service adoption, ML/AI in-house capability
  Scoring: 0-20 points
  AI-ready threshold: 14+ points (strong data literacy with ML capability)

Total AI-ready threshold: 70+ (sum of domain thresholds)


SCORE DISTRIBUTION ACROSS ASSESSED CLIENT BASE

Score > 70 (AI-Ready): 8% of assessed organisations
Score 50-70 (Foundation Building): 29% of assessed organisations
Score 35-50 (Significant Gaps): 41% of assessed organisations
Score < 35 (Pre-Foundation): 22% of assessed organisations

Industry breakdown of DRS distribution:
  Financial services (above 50): 44% — highest readiness across industries
  Technology companies (above 50): 38%
  Retail (above 50): 32%
  Healthcare (above 50): 24% — lowest, due to EHR fragmentation and governance gaps
  Manufacturing (above 50): 28%

The healthcare finding is significant: despite heavy investment in EHR systems (Epic, Cerner), the data generated by those systems is rarely in a form suitable for AI without significant preparation. Epic data in particular requires substantial transformation work — Clarity SQL access, Caboodle mart configuration, or FHIR API normalisation — before it is AI-model-ready.


OUTCOME CORRELATION BY SCORE BAND

SCORE > 65 (AI-READY):
AI use case deployment rate: 2x the 35-50 band at 24 months
Programme cost vs. forecast: 94% on or under budget (highly predictable programmes)
Model accuracy at initial deployment: 88% average (vs. 62% in the 35-49 band)
ROI at 24 months: 280-380%
90-day milestone achieved: 84% of the time
Model production deployment time: 2.6 months average
Programme success rate: 78%
Key characteristic: data quality and governance infrastructure already in place; AI development focuses on model building, not data remediation

SCORE 50-64 (FOUNDATION BUILDING):
AI use case deployment rate: 1.4x median
Programme cost vs. forecast: 12% over budget average
Model accuracy at initial deployment: 76% average
ROI at 24 months: 140-210%
90-day milestone achieved: 61% of the time
Model production deployment time: 4.8 months average
Programme success rate: 61%
Key characteristic: some data quality work required in parallel with AI development; budget and timeline overruns are predictable; readiness investment of $2-4M in parallel with Phase 2 improves outcomes

SCORE 35-49 (SIGNIFICANT GAPS):
AI use case deployment rate: 0.7x median (below average — many use cases stall)
Programme cost vs. forecast: 44% over budget average (material cost overrun)
Model accuracy at initial deployment: 62% average (borderline useful)
ROI at 24 months: 60-110%
90-day milestone achieved: 38% of the time
Model production deployment time: 7.2 months average
Programme success rate: 38%
Key characteristic: data remediation consumes AI programme resources; use cases stall waiting for data; governance conflicts emerge; vendor selection frequently reversed
AbarVa recommendation: do not begin AI use case development at this score level; invest in moving to 50-64 first (12-18 months, $4-12M depending on organisation size)

SCORE < 35 (PRE-FOUNDATION):
68% of AI initiatives stall at proof-of-concept (cannot reach production)
Deployed models: 0.3x the AI-ready band
Cost overrun: 2.1x original programme estimate
ROI: negative in 44% of cases; positive ROI median 28% (far below capital cost of programme)
Model production deployment time: 11.4 months average (when achieved at all)
Programme success rate: 14%
Key characteristic: fundamental infrastructure and governance gaps make AI deployment counterproductive; data quality issues are discovered mid-model development; governance conflicts halt deployment approvals
AbarVa recommendation: full data foundation programme before any AI use case. Phase 0 and Phase 1 only. Attempting AI use cases in this band is statistically likely to produce waste.


DOMAIN-LEVEL OUTCOME CORRELATIONS

DATA QUALITY DOMAIN (0-30 POINTS):
  Score < 15: AI model accuracy 54% average — models frequently worse than heuristics
  Score 15-22: AI model accuracy 72% average — useful but below industry benchmarks
  Score > 22: AI model accuracy 88% average — meets or exceeds business requirements

Specific data quality thresholds for AI models:
  Completeness: > 92% field completeness required for reliable model training
  Below 85% completeness: model accuracy degrades 18-34%
  Consistency: cross-system field consistency > 88% required for entity resolution
  Healthcare: Epic Clarity data typically requires 3-4 months of quality remediation before AI use

INFRASTRUCTURE DOMAIN (0-25 POINTS):
  Score < 12: batch-only, on-premise — real-time AI use cases impossible; training data pipelines manual
  Score 12-18: hybrid cloud, some automation — AI possible but slow and expensive
  Score > 18: cloud-native, streaming capable — AI development 2.6x faster and 44% cheaper

Cloud migration status correlation with AI programme speed:
  On-premise only: average 14 months to first AI model in production
  Hybrid (partial cloud): average 9 months to first AI model in production
  Cloud-native: average 5 months to first AI model in production

GOVERNANCE DOMAIN (0-25 POINTS):
  Score < 12: no enforced governance; shadow analytics proliferating; model approvals ad hoc
  Score 12-18: governance exists but not enforced; data catalogue incomplete; MDM absent
  Score > 18: enforced governance; data catalogue with >80% coverage; MDM active; clear data ownership

Governance score vs. AI programme success rate:
  Governance score < 12: 28% programme success rate
  Governance score 12-18: 52% programme success rate
  Governance score > 18: 81% programme success rate

TALENT DOMAIN (0-20 POINTS):
  Score < 8: no ML capability internally; data literacy < 20%; self-service analytics absent
  Score 8-14: emerging ML team; data literacy 20-40%; BI tools deployed but underused
  Score > 14: capable ML team; data literacy 40%+; self-service covers 50%+ of analytics needs

Talent score correlation with AI programme velocity:
  Talent score < 8: first use case requires 18+ months; vendor dependency high
  Talent score 8-14: first use case 9-14 months; hybrid vendor/internal
  Talent score > 14: first use case 4-8 months; internal delivery capable


DATA READINESS IMPROVEMENT ROI

Moving from DRS 35-40 to DRS 60-65:
  Required investment: $4-12M (varies by organisation size and domain gaps)
  Required timeline: 12-18 months
  Primary investments: cloud migration, data catalogue, MDM, data quality programme, governance framework
  ROI on readiness investment: 4.2x (through downstream AI programme performance improvement)
  Calculation basis: programmes at DRS 60-65 deliver 2.1x better AI ROI than programmes at DRS 35-40; the incremental AI ROI attributable to the readiness investment exceeds the readiness investment by 4.2x

Moving from DRS 60-65 to DRS 70+:
  Required investment: $2-6M additional
  Required timeline: 6-12 additional months
  Primary investments: advanced MDM, feature store, streaming pipeline, AI governance layer
  ROI: 1.8x marginal improvement in AI programme ROI; primary benefit is speed to production

Readiness investment as percentage of total AI programme:
  Top quartile programmes: 18-24% of total budget allocated to readiness
  Bottom quartile programmes: 4-8% of total budget allocated to readiness
  Bottom quartile result: 2.1x higher total programme cost (skipping readiness is expensive — paid for with rework)


TOP 5 DATA READINESS BLOCKERS

Blocker 1: No enterprise data governance framework (prevalence: 61%)
  Impact: No authority to enforce data quality standards; AI models trained on unvalidated data
  Resolution: Governance framework design + CDO appointment. Timeline: 3-6 months. Cost: $0.4-1.2M
  AbarVa deliverable: Governance Framework Design (AVR Phase 0 + Phase 1)

Blocker 2: Multiple conflicting master data sources — customer, product, financial (prevalence: 54%)
  Impact: AI models receive contradictory training signals; entity resolution fails; churn/personalisation AI degraded
  Resolution: MDM implementation + entity resolution sprint. Timeline: 4-8 months. Cost: $1.2-4.8M
  AbarVa deliverable: MDM and Entity Resolution (MDP engagement)

Blocker 3: Data lake without data catalogue (prevalence: 48%)
  Impact: 74% of data becomes undiscoverable within 18 months; data engineering rediscovers existing datasets repeatedly
  Resolution: Collibra, Alation, or Microsoft Purview deployment in parallel with lake. Timeline: 2-4 months. Cost: $0.4-1.6M
  AbarVa deliverable: Data Catalogue Implementation (MDP engagement)

Blocker 4: No self-service analytics for business users (prevalence: 44%)
  Impact: Business users rely on IT for all data questions; shadow analytics proliferates; data literacy remains low
  Resolution: Semantic layer (dbt) + governed BI (Power BI, Tableau, Looker). Timeline: 3-6 months. Cost: $0.6-2.4M
  AbarVa deliverable: Self-Service Analytics Layer (MDP engagement)

Blocker 5: Data quality score < 70% on critical AI training datasets (prevalence: 41%)
  Impact: AI model accuracy degrades proportionally; 10% completeness gap = 18-34% accuracy degradation
  Resolution: Data quality programme targeting critical datasets; Great Expectations or dbt test automation. Timeline: 3-6 months. Cost: $0.8-2.4M
  AbarVa deliverable: Data Quality Programme (MDP Phase 1)


SECTOR-SPECIFIC READINESS PATTERNS

HEALTHCARE:
  Primary blocker: EHR data fragmentation (Epic + 2-6 other systems); HL7/FHIR normalisation required
  Average DRS: 41 (below the significant gaps threshold)
  Most common gap: Governance domain (CDO absent 34% of the time; no enforced data quality standards)
  Quick wins: Epic FHIR API unlocks structured clinical data for RCM AI in 2-4 months
  Hardest problem: clinical notes in unstructured text (NLP required; adds 6-12 months to AI readiness)

FINANCIAL SERVICES:
  Primary blocker: Legacy core banking data in COBOL/mainframe formats; MDM for customer identity
  Average DRS: 52 (highest across industries — but still below AI-ready threshold for most)
  Most common gap: Infrastructure domain (mainframe/COBOL not compatible with ML training pipelines)
  Quick wins: GL/transactional data typically clean; fraud detection and reconciliation AI can begin while legacy migration continues
  Hardest problem: real-time fraud detection requires sub-second data freshness — mainframe batch feeds are incompatible

RETAIL:
  Primary blocker: Post-M&A ERP fragmentation (4-12 ERP instances); POS data quality varies by store
  Average DRS: 46 (significant gaps band)
  Most common gap: Data quality domain (ERP inconsistencies; historical sales data distorted by stockouts)
  Quick wins: POS data for demand forecasting is typically available and relatively clean for top categories
  Hardest problem: customer identity across in-store, e-commerce, and loyalty platforms (F019 pattern)

MANUFACTURING:
  Primary blocker: OT/IT integration gap — factory floor sensors produce data in proprietary formats not connected to enterprise data platform
  Average DRS: 43 (significant gaps)
  Most common gap: Infrastructure domain (OT data not in cloud; streaming not implemented)
  Quick wins: ERP-based demand and supply data; predictive maintenance on equipment with existing sensors
  Hardest problem: real-time quality AI requires sub-minute sensor data streaming to cloud


READINESS ASSESSMENT PROCESS (ABARVA PHASE 0)

Week 1-2: Stakeholder interviews (CDO, CIO, business unit leaders, data engineering team, data science team)
Week 2-3: Technical assessment (data platform review, source system audit, governance documentation review)
Week 3-4: Data sampling and quality analysis (selected critical datasets profiled against AI-ready thresholds)
Week 4: DRS calculated; gap analysis documented; remediation roadmap proposed with costs and timeline

Output:
  DRS score with domain breakdown
  Heat map of gap severity by domain and priority dataset
  Readiness roadmap: sequenced investments to reach AI-ready threshold
  "Start here" recommendation: which use cases can start now vs. which require readiness work first

Typical Phase 0 readiness finding:
  Average DRS across all assessed organisations: 44
  Average number of significant blockers identified: 3.2 per organisation
  Average estimated remediation investment: $6.8M
  Average estimated ROI on remediation investment: 4.2x (through improved AI programme performance)

AbarVa's standing recommendation: no AI use case should proceed to Phase 2 development until DRS is above 50 for that use case's specific data domains. Exemptions only for 90-day quick wins on data that is already clean.


DATA READINESS BY AI USE CASE TYPE

Each AI use case has different data readiness requirements. The following minimum DRS thresholds are required by use case to achieve > 80% model accuracy at deployment:

CLASSIFICATION USE CASES (Denial prediction, Fraud detection, Churn prediction):
  Minimum DRS overall: 55
  Critical domains: Data Quality (22+), Governance (18+)
  Key requirements: labelled historical outcomes (minimum 12 months); consistent feature definitions; <3% duplicate rate on primary entity
  Data quality minimum: 92% completeness on feature fields; 88% consistency across systems
  If below threshold: model accuracy falls to 62% average — likely below business requirement

REGRESSION / FORECASTING USE CASES (Demand forecasting, Cost prediction):
  Minimum DRS overall: 50
  Critical domains: Data Quality (20+), Infrastructure (16+)
  Key requirements: clean time series (24 months preferred); no data gaps > 2 weeks; seasonality data available
  Stockout correction required: if historical sales data has > 3% stockout periods, demand correction required before training
  If below threshold: MAPE improvement typically 40-60% of potential (significant but suboptimal)

REAL-TIME AI USE CASES (Real-time fraud, Sepsis detection, Dynamic pricing):
  Minimum DRS overall: 65
  Critical domains: Infrastructure (22+), Data Quality (22+)
  Key requirements: streaming pipeline active (Kafka/Event Hubs); data freshness < 2 minutes; feature store with real-time write
  Non-negotiable: no batch-only pipeline for real-time use cases — D-005 pattern must be resolved before development begins
  If below threshold: real-time use case is technically impossible; must defer until streaming capability is built

GENERATIVE AI USE CASES (RAG, Document processing, Ambient documentation):
  Minimum DRS overall: 45 (lower threshold — GenAI is less sensitive to structured data quality)
  Critical domains: Governance (16+) — PII management is primary requirement for GenAI
  Key requirements: PII and PHI classification complete; approved data sources identified; prompt/output logging in place
  If below threshold: regulatory risk (HIPAA, GDPR) from uncontrolled PII in GenAI training or context

NLP USE CASES (Clinical notes processing, Contract analysis):
  Minimum DRS overall: 50
  Critical domain: Infrastructure (18+) — unstructured text processing requires cloud NLP services
  Key requirements: document storage in cloud object storage; text extraction pipeline; annotation/labelling infrastructure
  Healthcare NLP: de-identification of PHI before any NLP model training (HIPAA requirement)


DATA READINESS INVESTMENT SEQUENCING

The sequence of data readiness investments matters as much as the investments themselves. Organisations that invest in the wrong order spend more and achieve less.

WRONG SEQUENCE (MOST COMMON):
  1. Select cloud data platform (Snowflake/Databricks) — first because vendor sales
  2. Migrate data to platform — creates Bronze layer without governance
  3. Begin use case development — encounter data quality issues; stall
  4. Add data catalogue retroactively — expensive, incomplete
  5. Attempt MDM after data is in lake — 3x more expensive retroactively
  6. Governance never fully established — AI models run on ungoverned data

RIGHT SEQUENCE (ABARVA STANDARD):
  1. Use case definition (Phase 0) — determines which data domains need readiness
  2. Governance framework (CDO, policy, ownership) — before data moves anywhere
  3. Data quality assessment and remediation on target domains — before platform migration
  4. Platform selection and provisioning — informed by use case data requirements
  5. Catalogue and governance tools deployed in parallel with platform — never after
  6. MDM for primary entities — before use case models begin
  7. Use case development — on a prepared, governed, high-quality data foundation

Time saved by correct sequence vs. wrong sequence: 4-8 months
Cost saved by correct sequence vs. wrong sequence: $2.4-6.8M in rework avoidance
AbarVa finding: every organisation AbarVa has assessed that followed the wrong sequence needed a data remediation sprint before AI models could reach production


DATA READINESS AND COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE

The relationship between data readiness and competitive advantage is non-linear. Organisations in the 35-49 DRS band are not simply "a bit behind" organisations in the 65+ band — they are operationally unable to deploy the AI use cases that create competitive differentiation. The competitive gap is not proportional; it is categorical.

Competitive readiness tiers:
  DRS > 65 (AI-Ready): can deploy all AI use case categories including real-time; building competitive moat
  DRS 50-64 (Foundation Building): can deploy classification and forecasting AI; real-time is 6-12 months away; competitive parity at best
  DRS 35-49 (Significant Gaps): AI deployment is slow and expensive; competitors with DRS > 65 are deploying 3x faster; competitive disadvantage growing
  DRS < 35 (Pre-Foundation): AI is effectively impossible at meaningful scale; every pilot stalls on data issues; competitors moving decisively ahead

Time to competitive disadvantage by industry (if DRS < 50 and not improving):
  Healthcare: 18-24 months before AI-capable competitors achieve measurable denial rate, documentation, and operational advantage
  Financial services: 12-18 months before AI-capable competitors achieve fraud, credit, and operational cost advantage
  Retail: 12-18 months before AI-capable competitors achieve demand accuracy and personalisation advantage
  Manufacturing: 24-36 months (longer cycle, less real-time-dependent)

The urgency argument: AbarVa's Phase 0 includes a competitive readiness analysis that shows the CEO/board where their organisation sits in the DRS distribution relative to their identified peer group. When a CEO sees that 38% of their peer group is already above DRS 65 and deploying production AI while their organisation is at DRS 44, the readiness investment becomes strategic urgency, not IT infrastructure.
