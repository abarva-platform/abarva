OVERVIEW

This document provides specific, quantified peer outcome benchmarks across industries for AI transformation programmes. These benchmarks are sourced from AbarVa's engagement database (340+ programmes, anonymised), cross-validated with publicly disclosed outcomes from earnings calls, investor presentations, and industry research. They are used by AbarVa consultants to establish peer context in client engagements — showing what comparable organisations have achieved with structured AI programmes.

The most important use of these benchmarks is the peer comparison conversation: when a CEO, CFO, or board is evaluating whether to invest in AI, the most motivating data point is not theoretical ROI but the specific outcome a comparable competitor has achieved. "Your closest competitor has reduced denial rate from 12.4% to 6.8% using AI" is more persuasive than any ROI model.

Key headline findings:
  AbarVa structured programme ROI at 24 months: 280% (vs. 110% industry unstructured)
  AbarVa programme success rate: 61% (vs. 34% industry average)
  Healthcare top quartile time to first AI use case: 8 months
  FS top quartile: 6 months; Retail top quartile: 5 months
  Worst-case outcome (multiple failure patterns active): $3.2-4.8M lost before abandonment

WORST-CASE OUTCOMES (ACTIVE FAILURE PATTERNS)

CASE W-1: HEALTHCARE — PROGRAMME ABANDONED ($3.2M LOST)
  Pattern combination: F001 (no baseline) + F007 (CDO vacant) + L-007 (vendor before strategy)
  Sequence: AI vendor selected based on sales presentation; CDO role vacant throughout; no baseline established
  Progress: AI vendor deployed model; no one knew if it was working; no baseline to compare against
  Month 14: CFO challenged ROI claim; programme team could not produce evidence; programme defunded
  Recoverable cost: $3.2M programme investment; zero verifiable ROI
  Key lesson: absence of baseline, CDO, and strategy created a programme that could not prove its value

CASE W-2: FINANCIAL SERVICES — PLATFORM SHELFWARE ($4.8M PLATFORM, 6% UTILISATION)
  Pattern: L-007 (vendor selected before strategy) + DP-001 (platform built without use cases)
  Sequence: IT-led initiative selected Databricks based on vendor presentation; data science team was not engaged; no use cases defined
  Progress: Databricks provisioned and populated with data; data science team had 12 use cases in Jupyter notebooks; none deployed
  Month 24: $4.8M platform investment; 6% utilisation by data science team
  Key lesson: technology without strategy produces expensive infrastructure with no ROI

CASE W-3: RETAIL — PERSONALISATION WITHOUT IDENTITY RESOLUTION ($0.4x ROI)
  Pattern: F019 (customer data fragmentation) + L-008 (pilot proliferation)
  Sequence: personalisation AI vendor contracted; 6.4 average systems with customer data not unified
  Progress: personalisation AI ran on fragmented customer profiles; 28% of customers had 2+ profiles; segments were wrong
  Month 18: personalisation campaigns targeting the wrong customers; customer complaints about irrelevant messaging
  ROI: 0.4x (vs. 3.4x potential with identity resolution); $6.8M investment with $2.7M return
  Key lesson: personalisation AI requires identity resolution first; F019 is non-negotiable prerequisite

PEER BENCHMARKING CONVERSATION FRAMEWORK

AbarVa uses peer benchmarks in client conversations according to a structured framework designed to maximise persuasion while maintaining credibility.

Step 1: Establish current state (baseline)
  "What is your current [denial rate/forecast accuracy/fraud loss ratio]?"
  "How do you track this metric, and what data source do you use?"
  [This establishes that the client knows their own number and how it is measured]

Step 2: Reveal peer context (the gap)
  "The industry average is [X]. Your number is [Y]. You are [above/below] average by [Z%]."
  "The top quartile is [W]. If you reached top quartile, the financial impact would be [$A]."
  [This creates the quantified competitive gap]

Step 3: Show what AI achieves (the possibility)
  "Comparable organisations — [healthcare system/bank/retailer] with similar characteristics — have improved [metric] from [start] to [end] using AI in [timeframe]."
  "That is [X% improvement] worth [$Y] on your revenue/cost base."
  [This makes the improvement concrete and specific, not theoretical]

Step 4: Connect to the investment (the decision)
  "A programme to achieve this for your organisation would require [investment] with expected ROI of [range] at 24 months."
  "The question is not whether AI can achieve this — the peer data shows it can. The question is whether you act now or continue to let the gap widen."
  [This reframes the decision as timing, not viability]

Credibility requirements for this conversation:
  All peer benchmarks must be verifiable (AbarVa engagement data + public sources)
  Do not overclaim: use ranges, not point estimates; acknowledge variation by organisation
  Do not invent urgency: the urgency must come from real competitive data, not manufactured fear
  AbarVa peer database: current as of 2025.Q1; updated quarterly with new engagement outcomes
