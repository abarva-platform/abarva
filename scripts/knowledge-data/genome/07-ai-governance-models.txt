AI GOVERNANCE MODELS AND ACCOUNTABILITY FRAMEWORKS
Source: AbarVa Genome — 340+ engagements, MIT Sloan AI Governance Study 2025

GOVERNANCE MODEL COMPARISON

MODEL 1: AI STEERING COMMITTEE (CEO-SPONSORED)
Structure: CEO, CFO, CDO, COO, business unit heads meet monthly
AI KPIs reviewed against baseline with financial consequences
Spend authority: pre-delegated to committee for AI programme
Success rate: 2.1x vs. no governance committee
Best for: organisations >$5B revenue, enterprise-wide AI

MODEL 2: CDO ACCOUNTABILITY MODEL
Structure: CDO owns all AI outcome metrics with P&L consequences
CDO bonus structure includes AI ROI delivery components
Success rate: 88% programme continuation at 24 months
Rare but highest-performing model
Risk: CDO departure → programme at risk (F-pattern L-004)
Mitigation: Board-level documentation of programme mandate

MODEL 3: CENTRE OF EXCELLENCE (CoE)
Structure: dedicated AI CoE provides platforms, standards, and skills; business units own use case delivery and ROI
Success rate: 74%
Best for: organisations >$3B revenue with multiple business units
Most scalable model for complex organisations
Key risk: CoE becomes bottleneck if not resourced adequately (6-8 FTE minimum)
Staffing: AI architects, ML engineers, data stewards, business translators

MODEL 4: FEDERATED MODEL
Structure: AI capability distributed across business units, loose central coordination
Success rate: 52%
Risk: duplication, inconsistent standards, data fragmentation
Common outcome: 34 parallel AI pilots with no shared platform or data (Genome F-pattern F044)
Best for: early-stage organisations below $1B revenue

GOVERNANCE FAILURE PATTERNS

FAILURE 1: COMMITTEE WITHOUT FINANCIAL AUTHORITY
AI committee cannot approve spend > $100K without board sign-off
Decision speed: 3.4x slower
Vendor relationships suffer — vendors deprioritise slow-decision clients
Fix: pre-delegated authority to $1-5M per quarter for AI steering

FAILURE 2: MONTHLY MEETING WITHOUT CONSEQUENCE
Monthly steering reviews AI KPIs, but no consequence for missing targets
64% of targets missed without consequence are not recovered
Fix: quarterly performance review with explicit manager accountability tied to AI KPIs

FAILURE 3: GOVERNANCE WITHOUT DATA GOVERNANCE TRACK
AI governance committee exists but data governance has no representation
Result: AI programme succeeds, data quality degrades, AI starts to fail at month 18
Fix: data governance lead as standing member of AI steering committee

FAILURE 4: RETROSPECTIVE GOVERNANCE
AI models deployed, then governance built around them
Result: unapproved models in production, explainability gaps, regulatory exposure
Fix: governance framework before first model goes to production

KPI MEASUREMENT CADENCE
Quarterly review only: 62% of AI KPI targets maintained at 12 months
Weekly operational + quarterly strategic: 88% of AI KPI targets maintained
Annual only: 38% maintained
Best practice: automated weekly AI performance dashboard + quarterly CXO review
