2 · Two scenarios this brief covers

Scenario A · Existing IT contract optimization

"We have $35M/yr in AMS with Wipro. Contract is 2 years from renewal. Are we paying market? Where's the leverage?"

This is not a competition. It's a renegotiation, possibly with a stalking horse (a credible alternative quote to keep the incumbent honest).

CXO expectation: 8–15% savings without a transition. Tool's job is to surface what's overpriced, what's miscoped, and what's contractually broken so you can walk into the renegotiation with evidence.

Scenario B · AMS/IMS consolidation across portfolios

"We have 4 AMS vendors, 3 IMS vendors, and 6 BU-level outsourcing deals. Time to consolidate to 2–3 strategic partners."

This is a competitive sourcing event. New RFP, full TCO comparison, BAFO, contract award, transition.

CXO expectation: 15–22% blended savings + structural cleanup (single SoW model, unified SLAs, common governance, exit-clause parity). The tool's job is to model the consolidation properly — not just sum the spend.

3 · The Trust Gate — run this before stage one TARGET-STATE

The audit surfaced a failure that no stage-level test would catch but that destroys the product's credibility in the first 90 seconds: the same portfolio reports different numbers on different surfaces.

Observed, live: for one Apex tenant in one session — the Events surface showed 3 events / $74.0M at stake; the Portfolio surface showed 2 events / $39.0M total value; the Decision Queue showed "23 decisions." Three independent scorecards, three answers.

This maps directly onto the CXO's first two questions (§5): "Can I trust what this says?" and "Will the Board believe the savings number?" If the headline event count and value don't agree across surfaces, the executive cannot trust the $2.4M hidden-cost catch or the $9.8M BAFO swing either — and won't.

The gate (3 checks, < 5 minutes, before any stage work)

  • One number. Open the same tenant on Decisions, Portfolio, and an event canvas. Event count and portfolio value must agree to the dollar. Any drift = fail.
  • One vocabulary. A given event's stage label is identical everywhere (card, rail, kanban band). No event is "BAFO" here and "Evaluation & Pricing" there. Three names for one state = fail.
  • One source of truth, cited. Click any headline number through to its computation/evidence. A figure you can't trace is a figure you can't sign.
Gate rule: if the numbers don't reconcile, stop the test. Trust scores 1, and per the mindset below the tool "doesn't get a second event." Fix the canonical-scorecard issue first, then resume. Everything downstream inherits this number's credibility.

4 · The two surfaces — how a CXO should navigate TARGET-STATE

In the target state Source is two top-level surfaces plus the event canvas. A clean walk has exactly one path; a CXO should never wonder which of several near-identical "home" pages they're on.

SurfaceThe one jobWhat the CXO does here
Decisions (the home)What needs deciding todayScan the queue sorted by risk × value; Open decision → on the one that matters
PortfolioThe full event table + the one scorecardAnalyze the book of work; start a new event
Event canvasWork one event to a defensible closeMove through the 11 stages; the rail shows done · current · next, not all 11 at once
Artifacts (scorecard · report · vendor · compare)Evidence reached from an eventNever a top tab; opened in context
Navigation probe: count the home pages. If "Queue," "Events," and "Portfolio" are three peer tabs showing overlapping views of the same events, the surface hasn't been consolidated — and the Trust Gate above is the symptom. Target = Decisions · Portfolio (two tabs); everything else is reached from an event.

5 · The CXO testing mindset

When a CXO opens a sourcing tool, they're asking five questions in this order:

  1. Can I trust what this thing says? (= evidence chain on every claim, and one consistent number everywhere — see the Trust Gate)
  2. Will the Board believe the savings number when this is over? (= CFO-defensible value math, no hand-waving)
  3. Will the contract protect us 3 years from now? (= governance, audit, exit, change-control)
  4. Will my best people use it, or work around it? (= does it match how sourcing actually runs — and is it calm enough to live in daily)
  5. What does it catch that I would have missed? (= the proof the tool is paying for itself)

If the tool fails any of #1–3, it doesn't get a second event. If it fails #4–5, it gets replaced by a spreadsheet within 6 months.

6 · Stage-by-stage CXO playbook

For each of the 11 stages: what the CXO needs, what they'll ask, the pass-bar (minimum to keep credibility), the exceed-bar (the new standard), failure modes to probe — and a Experience bar TARGET-STATE: the calm-and-clarity test for that surface. The 11 stages remain the canonical model; the canvas simply shows done · current · next rather than all 11 at once.

Stage 1 · Strategy

Need Is this a real competition or a renewal in disguise? What archetype? What's the realistic savings range?

Questions:

  • "What's my BATNA if no vendor moves?"
  • "Show me the 3 archetypes and why we're picking this one."
  • "What's the savings range based on similar deals — not this tool's guess?"
  • "Who's the executive sponsor and their decision authority on day 60?"
  • "What kills this deal if I'm honest about my political constraints?"

Pass Memo names trigger, sponsor by name, savings range with confidence band, 2–3 show-stoppers called out.

Exceed Market benchmarks for similar consolidations; surfaces dissenting view; names political constraints the sponsor won't say aloud.

Probe Generic "competitive sourcing event" memo; savings as a number not a range; no tech-debt or M&A overhang.

Experience Memo reads in plain executive language — no "deterministic," no "Step 0," no agent codenames. One primary action ("Open event") is obvious.

Stage 2 · Scope

Need What's in/out by app/service/geo/BU. Incumbent baseline. Demand profile.

Questions:

  • "App inventory in scope — criticality, incumbent, cost, SLA, volume?"
  • "What's out, and why? What's the risk of leaving it?"
  • "Ticket/incident/change baseline last 12 months, per app, with the volume curve?"
  • "Demand in year 2–3 — steady or growth?"
  • "What happens to scope if we divest the eCommerce BU next year?"

Pass Inventory tied to real CMDB/financial system. Baseline numbers cite source + as-of date. Scope rationale per app. Divestiture scenarios flagged.

Exceed Auto-detects scope inconsistencies; models 2–3 variants; surfaces hidden retained-org assumptions.

Probe Portfolio is a list of names with no criticality; volume stale/missing; no scope-change cost model.

Experience The scope readiness state is shown once, not as a readiness counter + artifact counter + evidence ramp all restating it. Evidence states beyond "usable / not yet" are tucked away, not a 7-row glossary on screen.

Stage 3 · RFP

Need Defensible weighted evaluation criteria; vendor instructions tight enough to enforce; anti-gaming pricing template.

Questions:

  • "Eval rubric with weights — who signed off?"
  • "Are SLA definitions enforceable, or marketing language?"
  • "How does the pricing template stop cost hiding in transition, ramp, change-control, T&M?"
  • "Q&A protocol — symmetric to all bidders?"
  • "What evidence do we require — case studies, financials, references, sub-contractors?"

Pass Criteria SMART. Weights total 100, agreed pre-RFP. SLA template has measurement method, exclusions, credit/penalty. Pricing template forces apples-to-apples.

Exceed "What if" scenarios (volume ±20%, scope shift, M&A); auto-detects gaming patterns; symmetric Q&A log.

Probe Buzzword criteria ("partnership," "innovation"); template lets vendors add lines/footnotes; no T&M cap.

Experience The RFP artifact opens without a wall of export buttons; controls that aren't usable yet ("Mark Complete" on a not-started draft) are hidden, not greyed.

Stage 4 · Vendor Responses

Need Completeness check + compliance flagging. Who's serious, who's not.

Questions:

  • "Which vendors submitted complete vs. incomplete? On what specifically?"
  • "Non-compliance flags — incapability or sandbagging?"
  • "Appetite signals — generous, defensive, walking-the-line?"
  • "Conflicts of interest? Sub-contractor overlaps? IP concerns?"

Pass Completeness matrix per vendor per section. Non-compliance flagged material/non-material. Q&A cycle logged.

Exceed Heat map of essays-vs-crisp answers (comfort signal); detects copy-paste between proposals (collusion / shared-sub signal).

Experience Vendor records use human names and clean status — no raw IDs like vendor_contracts:ven:apex:009 in the buyer's view; the provenance link ("Where did this come from?") stays, the internal key does not.

Stage 5 · Evaluation

Need Evidence-backed scoring. Identify BATNA. Find the spread.

Questions:

  • "Each material score with the evidence and the reviewer?"
  • "Where do reviewers disagree? Capture the dissent."
  • "Spread between #1 and #2 — tight (pricing leverage) or wide (real differentiation)?"
  • "Who's my BATNA if #1 walks in negotiation?"

Pass Every material score has written rationale citing evidence. Reviewer identity + timestamp per score. Dissent visible at recommendation level. BATNA explicit.

Exceed Auto-flags scoring inconsistency; weight sensitivity analysis; captures "what the reviewer would change in hindsight."

Probe Scores without rationale; single reviewer per criterion; no dissent; "BATNA" is just the next-highest scorer.

Experience The scorecard page carries one status, not four stacked conveyors (shell badge + lifecycle strip + readiness meter + approval counter). Empty tables say so; no confident header over zero rows.

Stage 6 · Pricing Normalization Load-bearing

The single biggest CXO test. Get this wrong and the rest is decoration.

Need TCO normalized across vendors, apples-to-apples. Savings bridge from baseline to each vendor's run-rate.

Questions:

  • "TCO bridge: baseline → each vendor's curb-to-curb cost → transition → year-1/3/5 run-rate?"
  • "Hidden costs — transition, ramp, dual-run, exit, governance, retained-org, change-control unit rates?"
  • "FX, indexation (CPI/COLA), volume bands, onshore/offshore mix — normalized across vendors?"
  • "Apples-to-apples per-unit rate — per FTE, ticket, app, server, seat?"
  • "The iceberg — 30% visible pricing + 70% invisible (governance, change, exit)?"
  • "Sensitivity: volume −20%, who's most exposed? Scope +10%, who has best change-order rates?"

Pass TCO table normalized with footnotes. Hidden costs as line items. Per-unit rate table by role/location. Multi-year shape with indexation. Currency normalized; FX stated.

Exceed Iceberg 30/70 view; sensitivity ribbon (volume ±20%, scope ±10%, FX ±5%); detects pricing games ("lowest year-1 but steepest escalator — 3-yr cost 9% higher"); 95th-percentile benchmark alerts; CFO-ready savings bridge.

Probe TCO is total contract value, not normalized; transition cost missing or buried in year-1; T&M compared but not volume-modeled; no indexation normalization; "hidden cost" categories that aren't actually surfaced.

Experience The normalized number here is the same number that rolls up to the portfolio scorecard and the value ledger — one computation, consistent everywhere (the Trust Gate, made concrete at the highest-stakes stage). The word "deterministic" never appears; the math is shown instead.

Stage 7 · BAFO Negotiations

Need Move every commercial lever, document every concession trade, walk away credibly.

Questions:

  • "What levers, on what evidence? Price, term, volume commit, scope flex, payment, SLA, governance, exit, benchmarking."
  • "Walk-away on each lever? Target? Opening ask?"
  • "Concession ledger — we asked X, vendor gave Y, we offered Z?"
  • "BATNA strength — is the second bidder credible or are we bluffing?"
  • "Cadence — single round or iterative? In-person or written?"

Pass Per-lever target/walk-away/opening. Concession ledger. Every ask justified by benchmark, second-bid, or market data. Risk allocation matrix with $ caps.

Exceed Quantified envelope ("$100M → $87M moves NPV by $X over 3 years"); surfaces vendor's BATNA; detects desperation ("moved 14% in 24hrs — push governance + exit, not price"); pre-mortem ("if we sign, what breaks in 18 months?").

Probe BAFO ask is "we want a lower number" without lever specificity; no concession tracking; team without unified walk-away; "final" accepted without exit/audit/benchmark clauses.

Experience "Advance to next stage" reads as a plain action (not "Promote to BAFO" stage-machine language); the agent rail is a narrow aide, not a 45%-of-screen panel competing with the negotiation workspace.

Stage 8 · Executive Decision

Need A CFO-and-Board-defensible recommendation. Evidence-backed, dissent-captured, missing-data-acknowledged.

Questions:

  • "Recommendation, and the one-sentence reason?"
  • "Citations on every material claim — scores, pricing math, risk?"
  • "The dissent — what did the loudest contrarian say, why are we overriding?"
  • "What do we NOT know? Assumption risks?"
  • "If a director asks 'second-best option,' can I answer in 30 seconds?"
  • "Human-approval record — who, when, what reviewed, what reason?"

Pass Brief has recommendation, evidence summary, missing data, risks, dissent, approval record, next steps. Every material claim cited. Dissent a real section. Approval record names human, timestamp, reason. Downloadable DOCX + PDF, full text not truncated.

Exceed Auto 1-slide Board summary; 3 likely Board questions + pre-baked cited answers; "if we say no, here's plan B"; tracks pre-decision approvals through the chain.

Probe "Award / proceed" verdict at stage 3 (L6 bug — verify gone); brief truncated mid-sentence; dissent sanitized or absent; approval record without reason; no machine-readable audit log.

Experience The report is titled in buyer language, not "Deterministic event report." Export is one calm menu, not five peer buttons in the header. The packet reads like a document, not a system dump.

Stage 9 · Selection / Award

Need Lock the award. SoW airtight. Transition obligations enforceable.

Questions:

  • "Commercial terms in the contract = BAFO, line by line?"
  • "What changed between award and signature — every diff, who approved each?"
  • "Is the SoW enforceable by a junior contract manager 18 months out?"
  • "Transition obligations binding with penalties for slip?"
  • "Kill-switch if KPIs miss in year 1?"

Pass Term sheet → MSA → SOW alignment matrix. Audit, SLA credit/penalty, change control, exit, benchmarking, indemnification, IP/data, termination clauses. Every commercial drift from BAFO documented + approved.

Exceed Auto-diff BAFO commitments vs. contract language; "standard clauses you're missing" alerts; cost-of-weakening estimates ("audit clause 6 months not 12 — $X lost recovery").

Experience Stage label is consistent with every other surface; the rail shows this stage as current with done-behind and next-ahead, not all 11 nodes at once.

Stage 10 · Transition

Need KT plan signed off. Go-live readiness gates. Risk register live.

Questions:

  • "KT plan with milestones, owner, evidence-of-completion criteria?"
  • "Go-live readiness scorecard — who signs off on what?"
  • "Risk register — red/amber, who owns burn-down?"
  • "Incumbent dependencies? Exit clause invoked on what dates?"

Pass Time-bound milestone plan with owner and evidence. Go-live gates with sign-off chain. Risk register active.

Exceed Auto-detects KT slip ("Milestone 12 is 80% but evidence 30 days old — probable real status 60%"); cross-checks vendor staffing vs. onboarding ("said 47 FTEs by week 6; HR shows 31").

Experience Status is conveyed once per item; no duplicate readiness/lifecycle/gate badges telling the same story three ways.

Stage 11 · Value

Where the Board comes back and asks "Did we actually save what you promised?"

Need Baseline locked. Savings model agreed with CFO. Credible realized-savings tracking.

Questions:

  • "Locked baseline — agreed with CFO, signed at contract start?"
  • "Projected savings — in the model the CFO approved?"
  • "Realized savings — what we actually saved, with evidence?"
  • "Variance — why?"
  • "CFO-attestable — could I sign my name to this number?"

Pass Value ledger entry per stage transition with FK to decision approval. Baseline + projected + realized + variance, each with evidence. CFO attestation captured (signature, date, scope).

Exceed Auto-flags savings evaporation ("Variance −8% in 60 days — investigate change orders"); ties realized to BAFO commitments ("committed 22% offshore; current 14% — shortfall traceable to staffing"); quarterly Board pack auto-generated.

Experience Zero implementation language on this CFO-facing page — no computeBaseline(), no "runtime," no "seed." The value number equals the portfolio number equals the normalized stage-6 number. One truth, end to end.
