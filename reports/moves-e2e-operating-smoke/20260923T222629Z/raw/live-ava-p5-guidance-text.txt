What the client team should do next

The immediate priority is closing the six missing required evidence items before Tower acceptance can be confirmed. These aren't optional — they are gaps that would force the delivery team to return to the program team during execution, which fails the P5 handoff test.

Missing required evidence to collect now:

Knowledge base ownership and freshness — who owns the KB, what is the update cadence, and is there a named steward?
Call transcript and recording availability — retention policy, access controls, and volume confirmed?
PHI, privacy, security, and audit controls — privacy impact assessment complete, audit log design confirmed?
Human-in-the-loop decision model — which decisions require human review, escalation path named?
Model risk and responsible AI controls — AI Governance Council sign-off, monitoring plan named?
Measurement owner and cadence — who owns outcome measurement post-launch, at what cadence?

Sessions to run

Three working sessions close the gap cleanly: (1) a Privacy and Security review with the clinical and IT security owners to close the PHI, HITL, and audit controls items together; (2) a Model Risk and AI Governance session — noting that the 2025 sepsis alert was declined for exactly this reason (missing monitoring plan and unnamed clinical owner), so this session must produce named owners and a monitoring plan, not just a submission; (3) a Tower Measurement Handoff session with the receiving Tower lead to lock the metric contract, confirm cadence, and produce the explicit acceptance statement.

Open blockers and caveats

The Data Governance Lead role is vacant — this is a named dependency for measure reconciliation and stewardship and must be resolved or formally waived before Tower accepts. Kona Coast is design-scope only and excluded from launch scope due to the legacy platform's weekly feed cadence; the handoff package must state this boundary explicitly so the delivery team does not scope it into wave 1. Three value inputs remain zero and unvalidated pending actuary and finance sign-off — the Tower metric contract must not treat these as realized value. Executive sponsor and program lead are not recorded on the engagement row; Tower acceptance requires a named receiving-party individual, which means this gap must be closed before the final gate.

Metrics going to Tower

The Tower metric contract should carry: care-gap closure rate (baseline 41.2%, 418,000 attributed lives, 1,142,000 open gaps), care-management search-and-reconciliation time (baseline 31 of 53 minutes per case), and caseload closure volume. Each metric needs a named owner, measurement cadence, and escalation threshold. The three unvalidated value inputs must be flagged as pending actuary/finance sign-off — not included as committed targets.
