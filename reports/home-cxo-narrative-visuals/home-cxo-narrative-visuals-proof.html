CXO gate: FAILED (overall 4/5, bar 4.4)

Before

This narrative is built on a source-backed synthetic demo context for a Meridian-style integrated delivery network and health plan; it is planning-grade and is not real Meridian production data. The story it tells is deliberate: the enterprise context layer is the hero, and member service Agent Assist in the contact center is one worked example of what that layer unlocks. Across nine represented domains, the context reads well on breadth and evidence coverage, with 62 readable records each backed by a citable evidence reference. What the context makes clear is that Meridian runs a mature but fragmented current-state estate: Epic clinical systems (Epic Clarity, Epic Caboodle), a claims administration platform, eligibility and benefits, a CRM member case platform, a contact center platform, an enterprise knowledge base, on-prem SQL Server reporting marts, a DB2 or Netezza-style integration warehouse where applicable, and heavy reporting through Tableau, SAS, and Power BI. The declared direction is an AWS plus Databricks lakehouse with medallion architecture, Unity Catalog-style governance, PHI controls, human-in-the-loop review, and audit controls. Those platform elements are target-state, not current production. The decision implication for leadership is straightforward: high-value bets such as Agent Assist depend on a governed context spine that does not yet exist, so the near-term work is evidence capture, baseline setting, and relationship validation rather than premature automation claims.

After

Meridian Health operates as an integrated delivery network and health plan spanning clinical operations, health plan operations, quality, finance, contact center, and enterprise data and analytics. What Nexus understands today is a wide operating picture — business functions with named executive owners, a large application estate anchored by Epic and legacy reporting, a substantial vendor base, and a pipeline of candidate initiatives from lakehouse foundation to cost transparency. The complication is that analytics remain on-premise and fragmented, medallion certification and formal data governance are not in place, and validated cross-domain relationships are absent, so the connective view between systems, data, and outcomes is not yet proven. The pattern that becomes visible is concentration: the same patient and member identity, claims, and clinical data that would ground a contact-center Agent Assist example also grounds payment integrity, cost transparency, and provider quality — one governed foundation serves many bets, not one. For a CIO or CDAO this means funding the identity spine, medallion certification, and governance operating model as shared infrastructure rather than per-use-case plumbing, and treating transcript and PHI controls as a gating decision before any AI touches member conversations. A CFO should note that expected-value framing for the lakehouse foundation exists but has no measured baseline, so value must be validated before it is claimed. The recommended next action is to confirm evidence owners, KPI baselines, and integration readiness for CRM, claims, eligibility, and Epic-derived data in a working session. This is source-backed synthetic demo context for discovery and framing, not real Meridian production data.
