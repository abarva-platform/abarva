## Vendors in BAFO

Three vendors advanced from Round 2 to BAFO. All have completed initial pricing submissions; final BAFO due 2026-05-30.

### Wipro (incumbent)

**Round 2 score:** 78.4
**Initial BAFO submission:** 2026-04-12
**Annual price submitted:** $1.78M (vs. current $3.2M for in-scope apps; 44% reduction)
**Apex floor:** $1.40M
**Negotiation positions:**

- **Apex pushing on:** transition assistance terms (90 days at no cost; current submission is 60 days), exit assistance language (currently weaker than Apex template), MFN clause (Wipro resisting), capability matrix (Apex wants explicit named-resource commitments for tier-1 apps)
- **Wipro pushing on:** scope freeze enforcement (wants explicit out-of-scope language for legacy POS modernization), penalty cap on SLA breach (wants annual cap; Apex wants per-incident exposure)
- **Locked terms:** 5-year term, 3-year initial commit + 2-year renewal option, mutual SLA framework, transition timeline (12 months for full transition)
- **Open issues:** 4 — pricing floor, transition assistance period, exit assistance language, MFN

**Apex assessment:** Wipro's incumbent position is double-edged. They know the apps; the 2023 history creates trust deficit. Aggressive pricing reflects their need to win this back. Risk: if they win at this price they'll squeeze elsewhere (change requests, scope arguments).

## Apex's BAFO discipline (locked in P2 charter)

- **Floor pricing:** $1.40M annual minimum acceptable. Below this, Apex walks.
- **Transition-risk threshold:** if any vendor scores transition-risk red on >2 of the 22 in-scope apps, Apex doesn't advance them regardless of price.
- **Exit assistance:** must include cooperative transition with no time limit, data export rights, and price-locked extension during exit.
- **Scope freeze:** the 22 apps are the 22 apps. Any expansion request requires sponsor-level approval.
- **Vendor concentration:** if a single vendor wins, Apex maintains a "second-source readiness" plan documented in the contract (no second vendor, but readiness to onboard one if needed).

## Open contradictions in the negotiation

1. **Wipro 2023 baggage vs. incumbent advantage.** The 2023 attempted consolidation paused after $9.2M spend, with Wipro on the other side. Carlos Rivera personally absorbed the failure publicly. Hiring Wipro again is a reputational re-engagement that the COO and parts of the board will scrutinize. Has to be defensible on objective criteria.
2. **Pricing vs. capability fit.** Infosys is the strongest fit; Wipro is the lowest priced (with some risk). The CFO is pushing on pricing; the CIO is pushing on fit. The current expectation is that BAFO compresses the gap.
3. **Apex's stated $2.2M annual savings target vs. realized savings risk.** Even if a vendor lands at $1.40M annual, the realized savings target depends on the transition completing within 12 months without service-level degradation. The 2023 program had $4.2M projected savings that became zero realized; the Investment Committee will scrutinize the realized-savings methodology.
