## Regional Banking — Structural Environment (2025-2026)

### Interest Rate Environment and NIM Dynamics

The Federal Reserve held the federal funds rate at 4.25-4.50% after three consecutive 25bp cuts in late 2024. Regional banks entered 2026 with a structurally challenging NIM environment: deposit repricing pressure (accumulated CDs and money market fund flows repricing higher) is compressing margins faster than earning asset yields are declining. Banks with high CD concentrations and low core deposit ratios are most exposed.

First Capital's NIM declined 14bp in FY2025 to 4.12%. Peer median for $3-7B asset community/regional banks is 3.85-4.20% (FDIC call report data). First Capital's positioning is above peer median but compressing.

**Industry implication for First Capital:** NIM recovery requires either rate increases, deposit mix improvement (toward non-interest bearing and lower-cost core), or asset repricing through loan mix shift. Digital transformation is the lever for improving core deposit acquisition and retention.

### Credit Quality Cycle

Commercial real estate — particularly office — continues as the primary credit quality concern across the regional banking industry. Per Moody's Q1 2026 banking outlook, office CRE delinquency rates at community banks rose 18bp YoY to 2.1% nationally. Multifamily stress has emerged in select markets (Sun Belt oversupply).

C&I credit quality remains broadly stable, though watch list formation rates increased at most banks with middle-market exposure, reflecting economic uncertainty and higher financing costs.

**First Capital's specific exposure:** NPL ratio 0.82% (vs 0.54% FY2024) with office CRE as primary driver (3 credits in workout, representing $42M exposure). CECL reserves reflect management's scenario weighting. NPL trajectory is the primary near-term stock performance concern.

### Regulatory Environment

**OCC Supervisory Focus (2026):** The OCC has published its semi-annual risk perspective emphasizing (1) BSA/AML program maturity at community banks, (2) commercial real estate concentration management, (3) interest rate risk governance, and (4) cybersecurity resilience. First Capital's examination findings align exactly with OCC's stated priority areas — the MRAs are not idiosyncratic; they reflect industry-wide deficiencies the OCC is actively pursuing.

**Basel III Endgame:** The U.S. implementation of Basel III endgame capital requirements was reproposed in mid-2024. Banks with $100B-$700B in assets (Category III/IV) face the most significant RWA increases. First Capital ($4.2B total assets) is below the initial threshold but monitoring closely as OCC signals the rule may ultimately apply to larger community banks. Estimated RWA increase if applied: 8-12%.

**CFPB Section 1033 (Open Banking):** The final rule requiring banks to provide consumer data portability (via API) applies to banks with >$850M in assets. First Capital is subject. FDX API compliance must be demonstrated by 2027. Jack Henry Banno has FDX-compliant APIs; core banking modernization dependency for deeper compliance.

### Competitive Landscape

**Direct regional competitors:** Carter Bankshares ($4.8B assets, VA-NC-SC), Atlantic Capital Bancshares ($2.9B, GA-NC), S&T Bancorp ($9.6B, mid-Atlantic), Eagle Bancorp ($11.2B, DC metro).

**Non-bank competition:**

- Registered Investment Advisors (independent) competing aggressively for wealth management relationships at fee rates 20-40bp below institutional wealth arms like First Capital Wealth

**Consolidation environment:** Regional bank M&A has rebounded from the SVB-era pause. Community banks in the $3-8B range are common acquisition targets. First Capital's CRA Satisfactory rating (vs. Outstanding) limits certain M&A optionality; technology gap is a valuation discount.

## Wealth Management — Industry Context

### Fee Compression and Fiduciary Standards

Wealth management fee compression has continued: average advisory fee across industry fell from ~0.87% AUM in 2022 to ~0.78% in 2025 (Cerulli 2025 report). First Capital charges ~0.87% on discretionary AUM — above market but sustainable if client outcomes justify. Fee transparency under DOL Fiduciary Rule evolution requires documented fee disclosure.

### Digital Wealth Experience Gap

Client expectations for digital wealth experience have been shaped by Schwab Intelligent Portfolios, Betterment, and Fidelity's digital platform. Independent RIAs using Orion, Envestnet, or Addepar have generally better client portal ratings than bank-affiliated wealth arms. First Capital's 42% portal adoption vs. competitors' 70-80% reflects this structural gap.

### Cross-Sell Opportunity with Banking Clients

Bank-affiliated wealth management has a structural advantage in cross-sell — access to the bank's consumer and commercial customer base. Benchmark data (Cerulli 2025) shows top-performing bank wealth affiliates achieve 28-35% penetration of bank consumer households. First Capital is at 18% — suggesting significant untapped opportunity if technology, referral incentives, and advisor capacity are aligned.

## Technology Investment — Peer Comparison

Regional banks with $3-7B in assets are investing heavily in technology to address legacy core replacement, digital experience gaps, and regulatory requirements simultaneously. Celent's 2025 banking technology survey shows:
- Core banking replacement: 23% of banks in this tier have active replacement projects; 41% are evaluating
- Digital banking investment: average $2.8M/year in FY2025 (up 28% from FY2023)
- BSA/AML enhancement: driven by heightened OCC and FinCEN examination standards; average $1.8M/year in FY2025

First Capital's $20.6M total FY2026 IT spend (8.5% of revenue) is above the peer median of 8.1% — reflecting the investment phase. The portfolio of 5 concurrent major programs is aggressive by peer standards and reflects the consequence of deferred investment in the 2019-2022 period.

## AI and Emerging Technology

### Banking-Specific AI Applications (2025-2026)

**Credit decisioning:** AI-assisted underwriting has moved mainstream for consumer lending. Banks using AI models show pull-through rate improvement of 8-15pp and loan processing time reduction of 30-50%. First Capital's 67% pull-through rate (vs. 72% target) is addressable with AI-assisted underwriting in MortgageCadence.

**BSA/AML:** Natural language processing and behavioral analytics for transaction monitoring are mature. Industry false-positive rates for legacy rule-based systems average 85-90%; AI-augmented systems achieve 30-40%. First Capital's 31% false-positive rate reflects a legacy rule-based system with partial tuning — further AI augmentation could bring this to ≤20%.

**Wealth Management:** Generative AI for financial planning content, client communication drafting, and portfolio commentary is being adopted at leading RIAs. First Capital Wealth is not yet using AI tools for advisor productivity.

**Fraud Detection:** Real-time AI fraud detection at the card issuing level is now standard for Tier-1 processors. First Capital processes through Visa DPS which has AI fraud scoring built in; the main gap is internal first-party fraud detection, which Actimize handles sub-optimally.

### Governance and Risk for AI

OCC's 2024 guidance on third-party model risk and the Federal Reserve's AI governance principles both require robust model risk management for AI systems. First Capital's 78% model validation coverage and SharePoint-based model inventory are below the requirements emerging from OCC guidance. The Model Risk Management program must be upgraded before significant AI deployment can occur safely.
