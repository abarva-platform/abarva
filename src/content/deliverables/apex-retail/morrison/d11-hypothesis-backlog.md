## Executive summary

The hypothesis backlog catalogues the **14 hypotheses** generated during intake and refined through Phase 2 diagnosis, each tested against the financial baseline (D07), the pain point register (D08), the root cause analysis (D09), and the benchmark cohort (D10). Of the 14, **9 are validated**, **3 are rejected**, and **2 are deferred to Phase 5** as second-cycle considerations [E31]. The nine validated hypotheses cluster into four families — supplier rationalisation (H1-H4), assortment rebuild (H5-H8), promotion discipline (H9-H11), and capability and data (H12-H14). The Phase 3 prioritisation decision, taken 2026-03-05 by Marcus T. and Katherine P., sequences the validated hypotheses into three intervention tracks that run in parallel rather than sequentially, with the capability and data hypotheses (H12-H14) supporting all three tracks rather than forming a fourth [E31]. The deferred hypotheses (H7 and H13) address opportunities whose recovery value is real but whose execution risk is better handled after the program's 14-month window closes.

## The 14 hypotheses

Each hypothesis was authored in a standard structure: claim, measurable test, evidence source, validation status, and lever linkage. The hypotheses were not generated top-down from the pattern library; they emerged bottom-up from the D04 intake synthesis, the D08 operator pain points, and the D10 benchmark comparison, then cross-checked against the Owned Brand Margin Recovery pattern's canonical lever catalog [E9]. The list below groups them by cluster for readability. The validation status column distinguishes validated (green), rejected (the evidence pointed the other way), and deferred (validated in principle but carried to Phase 5).

## Supplier rationalisation hypotheses (H1-H4)

**H1 · Volume consolidation on the top-3 suppliers will reduce unit cost by 400-600 bps on consolidated volume.** *Validated.* Counterfactual model using diversified-cohort unit costs [E5], cross-checked against benchmark cohort Practice 1 [E28]. Evidence: medium-to-high confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 1 (supplier renegotiation).

**H2 · Tier-2 supplier activation in 3 categories will unlock competitive pressure on Tier-1 incumbents.** *Validated.* Six categories analysed; three show ready-to-activate tier-2 suppliers with specification-compatible capacity [E26]. Medium confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 1 sub-workstream.

**H3 · Dual-sourcing 4 critical SKUs will reduce unit-cost exposure by 15-25% on that subset.** *Validated.* Benchmark cohort evidence: two top-quartile peers use dual-sourcing structurally [E28]. Medium confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 1 implementation detail.

**H4 · A supplier-led trade-fund negotiation discipline will reduce promotional depth creep by 30-50 bps independent of depth governance.** *Validated.* Direct evidence from pricing-analyst interview [E25] and baseline promotional depth attribution [E7]. High confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 3 (promotional depth discipline) with a supplier-side mechanism.

## Assortment rebuild hypotheses (H5-H8)

**H5 · Long-tail SKU rationalisation (bottom 40%) will lift margin-per-linear-foot by 14-19% with <2% revenue risk.** *Validated.* Driver analysis [E6] and store-tier demand modelling; benchmark cohort Practice 2 confirms the mechanism [E28]. High confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 2 (assortment rationalisation).

**H6 · Pack-size consolidation on 12 SKUs will reduce production complexity and unlock 60-90 bps margin on those SKUs.** *Validated.* Pain point D08 #9 and benchmark cohort spec consolidation practice [E28]. Medium confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 2 sub-workstream.

**H7 · Planogram redesign across 1,200 stores will lift owned-brand attachment by 8-12%.** *Deferred to Phase 5.* Validated in principle but the execution envelope exceeds the 14-month Morrison window; deferred pending Phase 5 continuation decision. Maps to a possible Phase 5 program.

**H8 · Subcategory exit (orphan SKUs, discontinued launches) will recover 30-50 bps of mix drift.** *Validated.* Pain point D08 #10 and baseline mix decomposition [E21]. High confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 2 implementation detail.

## Promotion discipline hypotheses (H9-H11)

**H9 · Capping promotional depth at -22% across the owned-brand book will recover 60-85 bps of compression.** *Validated.* Price elasticity modelling [E7] and benchmark cohort comparison [E28]. Medium-to-high confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 3 (promotional depth discipline).

**H10 · A margin-gated promotional calendar will structurally prevent future depth creep.** *Validated.* Benchmark cohort Practice 3 [E28] and pain point D08 #12 [E25]. High confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 3 governance mechanism.

**H11 · Competitive price monitoring moved from biweekly manual to near-real-time will reduce reactive depth events by 40%.** *Validated.* Pain point D08 #13 and quantified reactive-event volume [E25]. Medium confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 3 data dependency (overlaps with H14).

## Capability and data hypotheses (H12-H14)

**H12 · A gross-margin governance forum spanning merchandising, supply chain, and pricing will close the capability gap identified in RCA.** *Validated.* RCA upstream cause analysis [E26]. High confidence. Maps to the D19 delivery-plan governance construct, not a standalone lever.

**H13 · Unifying the six disconnected supplier cost masters will cut unit-cost visibility lag from T+28 to T+7.** *Deferred to Phase 5.* Pain point D08 #16 is real, but the required systems work is a multi-quarter effort outside Morrison's 14-month envelope. Maps to a possible Phase 5 data program.

**H14 · Closing the T+14 category margin reporting gap to T+7 will enable proactive promotional depth decisions.** *Validated.* Pain point D08 #15 [E25] and upstream cause analysis [E26]. Medium confidence. Maps to Phase 3 Lever 3 data dependency (not a standalone lever; executed as part of Lever 3's reporting stand-up).

**Rejected hypotheses:** H15 (national-brand margin compression was tested as a candidate lever and rejected — out of owned-brand scope per D01 charter); H16 (loyalty program margin impact — tested and found non-material to owned-brand); H17 (store-format resegmentation — real opportunity but untied to the owned-brand margin problem). Rejected hypotheses are held in the backlog archive with their rejection evidence; they do not re-enter the Phase 3 design.

## Prioritisation and Phase 3 sequencing

The nine validated hypotheses were sequenced into three parallel tracks on 2026-03-05 [E31]. **Track 1 (Supplier renegotiation)** executes H1, H2, H3, and H4 together, anchored on the Q3 2026 supplier reset window. **Track 2 (Assortment rationalisation)** executes H5, H6, and H8 together, with store-tier segmentation protecting urban-store revenue. **Track 3 (Promotional depth discipline)** executes H9, H10, and H11 together, with H14's reporting work embedded as a Track 3 dependency rather than a separate track. H12 (governance forum) becomes the coordination mechanism across all three tracks and is established in Phase 4 Sprint 1.

The parallel-track sequencing is the single most consequential decision in the deliverable. The portfolio-compounding analysis estimates **+30-40% faster margin recovery** vs. sequential execution of the same nine hypotheses, because supplier leverage is highest when volume commitments are being renegotiated alongside SKU rationalisation [E9][E32]. The operational cost of parallel execution — higher Q3 load on category managers, one dedicated category lead added — was ratified as acceptable by Marcus T. The sequencing decision feeds directly into D15 (intervention portfolio) and D16 (business case), with the compounding lift explicitly modelled in the high-scenario $22M/yr projection.

The 2 deferred hypotheses (H7 planogram redesign, H13 supplier cost master unification) are not shelved — they are scheduled. Both enter the Phase 5 continuation roadmap as second-cycle candidates, with validation and scoping refreshed after the Morrison program delivers its Phase 4 outcomes. The Morrison team's discipline on deferrals was noted approvingly in the 2026-03-05 workshop minutes: the program resisted the temptation to absorb every validated hypothesis and instead sized the envelope to what the 14-month window can credibly deliver.

## Risks and mitigations

- **[High]** **Parallel-track execution creates concentrated Q3 2026 operational load.** Three workstreams executing simultaneously through the supplier reset window could overwhelm category managers. Mitigation: dedicated category lead added; weekly steering committee; escalation protocol for any track-level slip >5 days.
- **[Medium]** **Portfolio-compounding estimate is pattern-library-derived, not Apex-specific.** The +30-40% lift is a pattern library benchmark [E9][E32] rather than an Apex measurement. Mitigation: D16 business case treats the compounding lift as a high-scenario element; low-scenario business case assumes zero compounding (additive-only recovery).
- **[Medium]** **Deferred hypotheses may drift from the Phase 5 roadmap.** H7 and H13 have real recoverable value; if deferred too long they lose context. Mitigation: D24 outcome measurement plan explicitly carries H7 and H13 as named Phase 5 candidates with refresh triggers at month 9.
