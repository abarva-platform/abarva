## Executive summary

The change management package scaffolds behaviour, communication, training, and adoption across the 1,200-store network and the corporate functions upstream of it so the three parallel workstreams execute without margin-erosive rework. The package was approved by Katherine P. on **2026-05-20** and identifies **four role-impact tiers** with tier-specific communications, training, and adoption telemetry [E68][E73]. Morrison's change challenge is unusual: category managers are asked to **cut SKUs** (historically threatening given headcount/SKU-count coupling [E12]), the CMO's brand team to **trust the assortment discipline**, and the pricing function to **govern promotional depth** against a calendar that limits tactical reaction space. Training cascades reach store level by **Week 4 (2026-05-27)** with a targeted >80% completion rate — the Phase 5 gate threshold [E60][E70].

## Stakeholder impact analysis

The analysis tiers stakeholders by **degree of behavioural change** required, not seniority. **Tier 1** (Corporate decision-makers, ~8): sponsor pair, Diane R., Reese A., Lena G., Director of Pricing, two Finance leads. Change is about sustained sponsor discipline through execution. **Tier 2** (Function leaders, ~24): category leaders, pricing managers, merchandising managers, supply chain operations leads, brand directors, store ops VPs. They translate levers into day-to-day operating choices. **Tier 3** (Category operators, ~160): buyers, pricing analysts, supply chain planners, merchandising analysts, brand managers. Change is immediate and visible — new playbooks, cadences, decision rights. **Tier 4** (Store operators, ~3,200 across 1,200 stores): store managers, assistants, department leads. Change is narrow but high-volume — execute the depth caps, flag cut-execution issues, maintain in-store house-brand merchandising [E68].

The 15%-per-category SKU depth cut ceiling (the brand-equity fence surfaced by Katherine P. and corroborated by Lena G. [E11]) is the single most consequential change-management design choice. It is a design constraint on the assortment lever but it dictates the framing of every store and operator conversation: "no category loses more than 15% of its SKUs; long-tail SKUs carry the weight of rationalisation" [E11][E68].

## Communication plan · by stakeholder group

**Tier 1** is conversational and high-frequency. Weekly 30-minute sponsor syncs (Marcus T. + Katherine P. + Diane R. + Nexus orchestrator) plus the Monday 0800 Nexus telemetry package. Unstructured sponsor exchanges outside this cadence are discouraged — they create conflicting messaging downstream [E69].

**Tier 2** is biweekly structured. A 60-minute function-leader forum every second Friday, chaired by workstream pair-owners, rotating by workstream (Supplier in Week 2, Assortment in Week 4). Between forums, a condensed telemetry package [E69].

**Tier 3** is weekly written + biweekly live. A Monday 3-4 paragraph narrative-voice update (authored by a Nexus content lead, reviewed by workstream co-owners for voice consistency) and biweekly 45-minute team briefings. Tier 3 operators often sit at the intersection of two workstreams — a category buyer is affected by Supplier and Assortment simultaneously — so voice consistency matters [E69].

**Tier 4** cascades through existing Apex store channels (Monday district huddle, mid-week store-manager email), not a Morrison-specific channel. Training materials flow through the existing Apex learning platform. A Morrison-specific store channel would add noise to a saturated network [E68][E69].

## Training + enablement

Three tracks matched to workstream content. **Supplier Renegotiation** (Tier 2-3, no store content): 4-hour volume-tier contracting workshop, 90-min dual-sourcing overview. **Assortment Rationalisation** (Tier 2-4): 3-hour cut-methodology + 15%-ceiling workshop for Tiers 2-3, 45-min store-manager execution briefing. **Promotional Depth Discipline** (Tier 2-3): 2-hour margin-gated calendar workshop, 60-min T+7 dashboard session [E70].

The cascade is sized for completion by **2026-05-27**, three days before the gate. Completion is tracked via the Apex learning platform; >80% is the Gate 4 criterion [E60]. A 90% stretch target was set and not enforced — 80% with quality completion beats 90% with box-ticking, and the marginal 10% on a 1,200-store network is operationally costly to chase [E70].

## Adoption metrics

Adoption is measured at three levels. **Training completion** (throughput): pulled from the learning platform with 24-hour lag. **Behavioural signals** (leading): resistance-shaped entries on the blocker register, Tier 3 briefing attendance, Tier 2 forum decision throughput, and a 3-sentence qualitative sponsor-confidence read from the Nexus orchestrator after every Tuesday forum, published in the following Monday telemetry package [E71]. **Outcome signals** (lagging): margin recovery, cut execution without consumer-visible signal, promotional depth holding at cap — these belong to D24, not D22.

Adoption metrics are inputs to outcome metrics, not substitutes for them. A program with 95% training completion and no margin recovery has an adoption or a design failure; D22 does not discriminate between the two [E71].

## Resistance patterns + mitigations

The package pre-registers **five resistance patterns** from the Owned Brand Margin Recovery pattern library, validated against Morrison's stakeholder map [E72][E9]. **Pattern 1 · SKU-count-as-identity:** category buyers whose team size is coupled to SKU count interpret rationalisation as headcount risk. Mitigation: CFO-signed 12-month decoupling commitment communicated at Tier 3 Week 2. **Pattern 2 · Brand-equity-as-veto:** marketing blocks specific cuts. Mitigation: the 15% ceiling is the veto envelope — inside, cuts proceed without marketing re-sign-off; outside, Katherine P. is the named approver. **Pattern 3 · Supplier-relationship-as-asset:** supply chain leaders resist on relationship cost. Mitigation: Diane R. personally owns Supplier A and B renegotiations. **Pattern 4 · Promotional-depth-as-share-defence:** pricing managers resist the -22% cap. Mitigation: the margin-gated calendar is a clearer decision framework than the cap; the cap is a consequence, not a rule. **Pattern 5 · Reporting-lag-as-excuse:** operators use T+14 lag to defer decisions. Mitigation: WS3 collapses to T+7 by Sprint 2; communicated as operator benefit, not governance imposition [E72].

Patterns track in the blocker register via a "resistance-shaped" tag. Sprint 1 logged two entries, both in WS2 (SKU-count-as-identity and brand-equity-as-veto). Both resolved inside Sprint 1 using the pre-registered mitigations [E72][E66].

## Post-go-live support

Post-Phase-4 support is scoped for **60 days beyond the 2026-05-30 gate**, covering Phase 5 ramp. The Nexus content lead and the function-leader forum cadence persist; the Tier 3 weekly written update continues through the 60-day window and tapers to biweekly thereafter. A Nexus change-management business-partner is embedded through 60 days, then transitions to advisory. A 30-day pulse survey (5 questions) targeting Tiers 2-3 publishes to the sponsor pair inside two weeks of close [E70][E73].

The 60 days are a **bridge**, not a continuation. D24 attestation assumes Morrison's change-management footprint is materially removed by Day 60 and Apex retains the adoption muscle independently.

## Risks and mitigations

- **[High]** **Training completion tracks <80% on 2026-05-27.** Gate 4 fails and Phase 5 slips. Mitigation: completion reviewed daily from Week 3; retrieval wave triggers if Week 3 tracking shows <65%; store-ops VP owns the retrieval.
- **[Medium]** **Pattern 1 persists despite decoupling letter.** WS2 execution slows if category buyers remain SKU-count-defensive. Mitigation: 12-month quarterly reaffirmation cadence, second reaffirmation in Phase 5 Q3.
- **[Medium]** **Nexus content lead bottleneck on Tier 3 updates.** Weekly narrative-voice updates for three workstreams is non-trivial. Mitigation: a second content lead funded from contingency if Week 3 output quality slips; co-owners share authorship monthly from Phase 5.
