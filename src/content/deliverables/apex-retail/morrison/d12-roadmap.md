## Executive summary

The Morrison roadmap sequences nine validated hypotheses (D11) into a **three-workstream parallel track** that runs from **2026-05-06 (Phase 4 launch)** through **2027-Q1 (Phase 5 attestation window)** on a **36-month horizon** [E31][E40]. The first 14 months are the committed program envelope — supplier renegotiation, assortment rationalisation, and promotional depth discipline executing concurrently rather than sequentially — and the trailing 22 months are the attestation, second-cycle, and deferred-hypothesis (H7, H13) window. The **$6.8M capital envelope** is phased **55% in the first six months** and tapers thereafter [E41]. Six gates (G1-G6) separate phases; every gate has a CFO-level pass/no-pass criterion tied to measurable bps recovery or leading indicators. The roadmap deliberately avoids a serial "supplier-first, then assortment, then promo" shape because the portfolio-compounding analysis (D11) shows parallel execution recovers margin **30-40% faster** [E32] — a decision ratified by Marcus T. on 2026-03-18 [E40].

## 36-month horizon

The horizon spans four distinct execution windows. **Window 1 · Phase 4 build (2026-05-06 to 2026-09-30)** — all three workstreams stand up; supplier contract redraft cycles kick off in parallel with assortment Wave 1 and the promotional calendar rebuild. **Window 2 · Phase 4 early harvest (2026-10-01 to 2027-01-31)** — first measurable bps recovery on supplier and promo workstreams; assortment Wave 2 rolls out. **Window 3 · Phase 5 attestation (2027-02-01 to 2027-06-30)** — dual-ledger reconciliation with Apex Finance; sponsor attestation against D16 envelope; second-cycle decisions on H7 and H13. **Window 4 · Continuation optionality (2027-07-01 to 2029-04-30)** — carried as an option, not a commitment, for deferred hypotheses and residual gap-to-median closure [E40]. The horizon framing is deliberately longer than the 14-month committed envelope because the Phase 5 attestation and continuation decisions are where the program's real value accretes.

## Lever sequencing rationale

The decision to run three levers in parallel rather than in sequence is the roadmap's single most consequential choice. The pattern-library analysis in D11 quantifies the compounding at **+30-40% faster recovery** vs. a sequential rollout of the same nine hypotheses [E9][E32]. The mechanism is specific: **supplier volume leverage is highest when SKU rationalisation is concurrent**, because consolidating volume onto fewer SKUs (Lever 2) compounds the volume-tier pricing improvements from supplier consolidation (Lever 1). A sequential rollout loses that compounding because by the time Lever 2 (assortment) activates, the Lever 1 (supplier) contracts have already locked pricing at the pre-rationalisation volume band. Lever 3 (promo) is additive and does not compound with 1 and 2, but its dependency on the T+7 category margin reporting (H14) means it has to run in parallel with supplier contract data unification work anyway [E40]. The operational cost of parallel execution — concentrated Q3 2026 load on category managers — was accepted by Marcus T. on 2026-03-18 with one added category lead as the mitigation [E42].

## Milestone + gate calendar

Six named gates separate windows. **G1 · Phase 4 launch** (2026-05-06) — three workstreams stood up; each has a named lead, a 14-day sprint cadence, and a week-1 artifact (supplier negotiation dossier, assortment SKU-tier inventory, promo calendar current-state map). **G2 · 90-day supplier first-close** (2026-08-06) — first renegotiated supplier contract signed; target 1 of 3 Tier-1 suppliers; gate criterion: unit-cost recovery on consolidated volume ≥ 300 bps (pattern-library low bound) [E41]. **G3 · Assortment Wave 1** (2026-09-30) — first four categories rationalised; gate criterion: revenue risk <2% with SKU count reduction on target (15% urban / 23% suburban tier-weighted) [E41]. **G4 · Promotional calendar live** (2026-10-15) — margin-gated calendar operational; first depth event subjected to the -22% cap; T+7 reporting online (H14 dependency) [E40]. **G5 · Phase 4 exit** (2027-01-31) — all three workstreams at steady-state; cumulative bps recovery reconciled to Apex Finance; no-go criterion: <120 bps recovered by this date triggers kill-switch review (D15) [E41]. **G6 · Phase 5 attestation** (2027-Q1) — dual-ledger attestation; go/no-go on continuation. Every gate has a named CFO sign-off slot; every gate has a pre-populated pass/no-pass checklist in the governance forum backlog.

## Dependencies + critical path

The critical path runs through **supplier contract redraft → first-close signature** in WS1 because the 90-day G2 gate is the earliest measurable-impact checkpoint and because the assortment Wave 1 rollout's SKU consolidation targets assume the supplier volume tiers will be ready to absorb them. The second critical dependency is the **T+14 → T+7 category margin reporting** work embedded in WS3; if this slips past 2026-09-15 the promotional calendar cannot operate with the -22% depth cap as designed [E40]. Non-critical but watched: the governance forum standing instruction (H12) must be ratified by G1; without it the cross-workstream coordination defaults to ad-hoc sponsor emails. The dependency map is held in the D19 delivery plan; this roadmap surfaces only the critical edges.

## Resourcing envelope

The resourcing envelope is tight by design. Each workstream has one lead (Diane R. sponsors WS1 supplier, Reese A. sponsors WS2 assortment, a pricing-analytics director sponsors WS3 promo) plus one execution owner; one program manager coordinates across workstreams; one dedicated category lead was added to absorb the Q3 2026 parallel-execution load — the explicit mitigation Marcus T. approved on 2026-03-18 [E42]. Maestro orchestration provides the governance forum cadence, the dual-ledger reconciliation, and the daily progress signal feeds. The capital phasing — 55% in H1 because the supplier contract redraft and assortment Wave 1 rollout consume most capex in the first six months — is deliberate to front-load the critical path; H3 and H4 spend is largely platform stabilization and attestation tooling [E41].

## Risks and mitigations

- **[High]** **Parallel-track execution concentrates Q3 2026 operational load on category managers.** If the added category lead is not sufficient, any two of the three workstreams could slip. Mitigation: weekly steering committee with 5-day escalation SLA; G2 90-day supplier first-close acts as a leading indicator — if G2 slips >30 days the D15 kill-switch convenes automatically.
- **[Medium]** **Gate criteria are calibrated to pattern-library bounds, not Apex-specific counterfactuals.** G2's 300 bps unit-cost recovery threshold is the low bound of Practice 1 (D10); Apex-specific response may undershoot. Mitigation: G2 has a pre-announced re-forecast trigger if initial supplier response is in the 250-300 bps band; re-forecast does not halt but updates the D16 envelope.
- **[Medium]** **Capital phasing 55/30/15 assumes no re-scope.** If sponsor appetite shifts mid-program, the phasing must be replanned — H1 spend is largely committed by 2026-08. Mitigation: Marcus T. standing commitment in D17; Phase 5 continuation capital is separate and not drawn against the $6.8M envelope.
