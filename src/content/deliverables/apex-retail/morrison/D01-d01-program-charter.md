## Executive summary

Morrison is a committed program to recover **340 basis points** of owned-brand gross margin erosion observed across Apex Retail Group's portfolio over the preceding 24 months [E1]. Sponsor **Marcus T. (CFO)** has authorized a **$6.8M capital envelope** against a projected **$14-22M/yr steady-state impact** with a **9-14 month payback horizon**. **Katherine P. (CMO)** joins as co-sponsor to guarantee brand-equity guardrails through assortment and promotional decisions [E4]. Scope is intentionally bounded to the owned-brand portfolio across 1,200 stores; national-brand margin questions are deferred to a successor program with a different lever structure. This charter locks scope, success definition, governance, and phase gates so the Phase 2 diagnostic work has a stable frame to push against.

## Why this program exists

Apex Retail Group operates at $18B in annualized revenue across 1,200 stores. The owned-brand portfolio — eight priority categories spanning packaged goods, household essentials, and personal care — has been the strategic margin vehicle since the 2022 re-platform. Between FY24 Q1 and FY26 Q1, aggregate owned-brand gross margin compressed by **340 bps** [E1]. The compression was not uniform: two categories (packaged-goods and household essentials) absorbed disproportionate loss, while personal care held flat. Pricing discipline was not the driver. Apex's consumer price index tracked cohort medians within 20 bps throughout the window [E3]. The compression is upstream in cost-to-serve and downstream in promotional depth.

The trigger for formal program activation was the **FY26 Q1 gross-margin attestation** delivered to Apex's audit committee on 2026-01-09, which flagged owned-brand margin as the single largest drag on enterprise operating-margin guidance. Marcus T. moved within four business days to commission a structured recovery program. The choice to frame the work as a **pattern-matched program** rather than an ad-hoc margin initiative was deliberate: it connects the effort to AbarVa's Transformation Genome pattern library, specifically `owned-brand-margin-recovery`, which codifies the canonical lever set and evidence thresholds drawn from comparable programs across the cohort [E9].

## Scope in / scope out

**In scope:**
- Owned-brand gross margin across all eight priority categories, measured against the FY24 baseline
- Supplier cost structure within the owned-brand manufacturing base
- Assortment architecture for owned-brand SKUs (SKU-per-linear-foot, long-tail management)
- Promotional depth policy applied to owned-brand
- Inventory turns and markdown governance tied to owned-brand
- Store-tier differentiation where assortment interventions diverge (urban vs. suburban density)

**Out of scope (Phase 1):**
- National-brand margin compression — acknowledged as a separate strategic question with different lever structure and stakeholder set; deferred to a successor program
- Private-label expansion (new category entry) — strategic roadmap question, not a margin-recovery lever in the current window
- Digital/e-commerce margin (separate P&L line; smaller absolute bps movement in the baseline)
- Pricing architecture at the national-brand or cross-banner level
- Loyalty program economics (touches margin indirectly but has its own program owner)

The in/out boundary was ratified on **2026-01-14** [E3] and re-affirmed on **2026-01-16** when Katherine P. confirmed co-sponsorship. The decision to defer national-brand work was the single most consequential scope call of Phase 1: it prevents scope creep into a problem that would require a different cohort of interventions and would dilute Marcus's executive attention.

## Success definition

The primary success metric is **owned-brand gross margin recovery measured in basis points against the FY24 baseline** [E8]. The charter adopts a **tiered success definition**:

- **Threshold success:** recover 180 bps (matches cohort median) within 18 months of program kickoff
- **Target success:** recover 240 bps (exceeds cohort median, approaches top-quartile trajectory) within 18 months
- **Stretch success:** recover the full 340 bps, returning to the FY24 baseline, within 24 months

Supporting metrics — unit cost (bps), promotional depth (bps), SKU-per-LF (count), and inventory turns (turns/yr) — are tracked under D03 Success Metric Tree and contribute leading-indicator visibility into whether the primary metric is trending correctly. Gate advancement at each phase depends on supporting-metric traction, not just ultimate margin delivery, because margin is a lagging reveal.

Financial coherence: the **$14-22M/yr steady-state impact** framing in the capital envelope aligns with the primary-metric tiered success definition. $14M corresponds to threshold recovery applied to the FY25 owned-brand revenue base; $22M corresponds to the target recovery tier. The stretch tier is excluded from the business case baseline to preserve conservatism in committee messaging [E9].

## Governance

Governance operates on a **biweekly cadence** ratified by Diane R. (SVP Supply Chain) on 2026-01-22 [E2]. The structure:

- **Steering committee** — Marcus T. (chair), Katherine P., Diane R., meets biweekly on Thursdays
- **Working committee** — category leads, supplier relationship owners, pricing analytics lead, meets weekly on Tuesdays
- **Nexus orchestrator agent** — maintains program artifact state, surfaces contradictions, tracks decision log, prepares committee packets
- **Escalation path** — any blocker unresolved > 5 days at working level escalates to Marcus; > 10 days escalates to the Apex CEO

Decision authority is held by Marcus for capital and scope; Katherine holds veto authority over assortment and promotional interventions that materially affect owned-brand equity perception [E4]. This dual-sponsor structure is specific to Morrison — it exists because the program's most consequential lever (assortment rationalization) cuts across the CFO/CMO boundary and a single-sponsor structure would invite downstream deadlock.

## Risk callouts

- **Sponsor alignment drift (tier: High).** The CFO wants cost-side recovery; the CMO wants brand-equity protection. Without disciplined decision architecture, the two vectors will pull program decisions apart mid-execution. **Mitigation:** dual-sponsor structure with explicit veto domains (Marcus: capital and scope; Katherine: brand-equity and promotional assortment). Contradiction surfaces in the intake synthesis (D04) are logged, not suppressed.
- **Scope creep into national-brand territory (tier: Medium).** As the diagnostic surfaces cross-brand cost drivers, pressure will build to expand scope. **Mitigation:** charter boundary re-affirmed at each phase gate; any scope-expansion proposal requires a written amendment and committee vote, not an inline decision.
- **Benchmark cohort representativeness (tier: Medium).** The 6-retailer composite cohort informs target tiers; if the cohort is mis-scaled, success thresholds drift. **Mitigation:** cohort composition reviewed in Phase 2 D10 Benchmark Comparison before Phase 3 business-case lock [E2].
