## Executive summary

The Morrison stakeholder map tiers every party with influence over or dependency on the **340 bps owned-brand margin recovery program** [E1]. Two decision-makers — **Marcus T. (CFO, primary sponsor)** and **Katherine P. (CMO, co-sponsor)** — hold voting authority; **Diane R. (SVP Supply Chain)** operates the execution spine. Tier-2 influencers span merchandising, finance, store operations, and brand strategy; Tier-3 affected populations include category buyers, store managers, and supplier relationship owners. Governance runs on a **biweekly steering cadence** [E2] with an escalation path that resolves 95% of working-level blockers without committee intervention. This map is the routing layer for every subsequent decision; every Phase 2-4 deliverable references it.

## Key facts

- **Phase:** 1 · Intake & Framing
- **Owner:** Diane R. · SVP Supply Chain (stakeholder methodology owner)

## Sponsor and co-sponsor

**Marcus T. · CFO (Primary sponsor).** Marcus holds capital authority, program-scope authority, and final sign-off on the business case. He has framed the margin compression as upstream cost-to-serve erosion compounded by promotional depth creep; this framing is recorded verbatim in the intake synthesis [E3]. His decision tempo is fast: the charter approval took four business days from committee flag to signed artifact. His attention cost is high — steering-committee agendas must arrive 48 hours in advance with pre-reads under 6 pages.

**Katherine P. · CMO (Co-sponsor).** Katherine joined as co-sponsor on 2026-01-16 after scoping conversations with Marcus clarified that assortment and promotional levers would intersect her brand-equity perimeter. She holds veto authority over assortment rationalization depth and promotional-depth policy changes that materially affect owned-brand equity perception [E4]. Her framing: owned-brand is a brand-equity asset, not a margin play; aggressive assortment cuts will signal retreat to the consumer. This framing creates the primary productive tension in the program — and is the reason for dual-sponsorship rather than a CFO-only structure.

The pairing is deliberate. A margin-recovery program with only a CFO sponsor will over-optimize on cost-side levers and under-weight brand consequence. A program with only a CMO sponsor will protect brand at the expense of margin recovery velocity. Dual-sponsorship with explicit veto domains is the governance answer to the underlying lever architecture.

## Decision authority

Decision authority is partitioned into three domains, ratified on 2026-01-22:

- **Capital and scope domain (Marcus).** Any decision that changes the capital envelope, extends the program duration beyond the 14-month horizon, or alters the in/out scope boundary requires Marcus's sign-off. This includes approval of the business case (D16) and the decision memo (D17).
- **Brand-equity and commercial domain (Katherine).** Any decision that changes promotional depth policy, cuts assortment depth below a published threshold per category, or modifies owned-brand positioning requires Katherine's sign-off. Katherine holds veto authority; if exercised, the decision returns to the working committee for re-formulation, not to Marcus for override.
- **Execution domain (Diane R.).** Any decision on supplier sequencing, operational workstream staffing, or delivery cadence is Diane's call within the committed capital and brand-equity frames. Escalation to Marcus or Katherine only when a decision crosses a domain boundary.

This partitioning resolves the most common governance failure mode observed in pattern-comparable margin recovery programs: the deadlock that occurs when a single-sponsor structure tries to absorb brand-equity decisions the sponsor is not positioned to own.

## Affected populations

Tier 3 — parties affected by program decisions but not formally consulted in decision sessions. Three populations carry the largest absorption load:

- **Category buyers (~45 operators).** Assortment rationalization will reshape their SKU portfolios. Training cascades and new workflow discipline required. Change management plan (D22) allocates 12 training hours per buyer over six weeks.
- **Store managers (~1,200 operators).** Store-tier assortment differentiation means managers in urban high-density stores will retain more owned-brand SKUs than suburban counterparts. Communication tiering via Katherine's team; store-visit cadence for high-variance stores.
- **Supplier relationship owners (~40 operators).** Supplier renegotiation wave will compress normal cadence. Contract-by-contract sequencing with Alex W.'s team; legal support from Theo's team.

Downstream populations — store-level associates, owned-brand product developers, packaging and visual merchandising — receive communication but do not carry decision absorption. A full role-impact matrix appears in D22 Change Management Package.

## Communication cadence

Four cadences run in parallel:

- **Steering committee · biweekly** — Marcus, Katherine, Diane; 60 minutes; decision artifacts and phase-gate reviews. Pre-read 48 hours ahead.
- **Working committee · weekly** — Reese, Jon, Priya, Alex, Mo, Damien, Nico, with Diane chairing; 90 minutes; operational dispatch and evidence triage. Minutes to steering the same day.
- **Tier-2 influencer briefings · monthly** — async written brief to all Tier-2 parties summarizing state of play, open decisions, and upcoming asks. Reduces committee bandwidth consumption by pushing context-setting into writing.
- **Store and supplier communications · phase-gated** — tied to Phase 4 change-management waves, not to the continuous governance cadence.

Cadence discipline is owned by Ravi N. (Transformation Office) with Nexus orchestrator agent support. The Nexus agent maintains the decision log, surfaces contradictions between Tier-1 and Tier-2 positions, and prepares committee packets. This is the first Apex program where the orchestrator agent carries formal PMO duties; the pattern is load-bearing for future programs and its efficacy is measured under D24 Outcome Measurement Plan.

## Risk callouts

- **Katherine's veto exercised mid-diagnosis (tier: Medium).** If Phase 2 RCA surfaces assortment cuts deeper than Katherine's equity threshold tolerates, veto exercise could reset Phase 3 design timing. **Mitigation:** Sonia E.'s consumer insights team runs parallel brand-equity modeling during Phase 2; Katherine is briefed on intermediate findings at weekly one-on-ones with Diane, avoiding surprise at the steering committee.
- **Marcus attention dilution (tier: Medium).** Apex's FY26 operating plan has three other CFO-sponsor programs in flight. If Morrison loses attention share, decision tempo slows. **Mitigation:** biweekly cadence is floor, not ceiling; Ravi monitors attention signals and escalates if three consecutive steering meetings run under 45 minutes or see cancellation.
- **Tier-2 influencer saturation (tier: Low).** Eleven Tier-2 parties is a wide surface; coordination cost grows non-linearly. **Mitigation:** async monthly brief replaces most synchronous touchpoints; working committee is the primary synchronous forum; Tier-2 attendance is by-decision, not by-default.
