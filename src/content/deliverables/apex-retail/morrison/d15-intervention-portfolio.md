## Executive summary

The Morrison intervention portfolio selects **three levers from the eight-lever owned-brand-margin-recovery pattern catalog** [E9] and executes them as a **parallel track** rather than sequentially, sized to the $6.8M capital envelope and the $14-22M/yr steady-state impact target. **Lever 1 · Supplier renegotiation** targets 140-200 bps recovery through volume-tier contract restructuring across the top-3 Tier-1 incumbents, tier-2 supplier activation in 3 categories, and dual-sourcing on 4 critical SKUs [E45][E46]. **Lever 2 · Assortment rationalisation** targets 80-120 bps recovery through long-tail cuts (bottom 40% of volume), pack-size consolidation on 12 SKUs, and 8 subcategory exits — bounded by the CMO's brand-equity fence (≤15% per-category cut in urban stores, ≤23% in suburban) [E47]. **Lever 3 · Promo discipline** targets 80-110 bps recovery through a -22% depth cap, a margin-gated promotional calendar, and T+7 category margin reporting (H14) [E48]. The portfolio's **expected recovery of 300-430 bps** reconciles to the 340 bps compression (D07) and closes 40-55% of the gap-to-cohort-median (D10). The portfolio has named **kill-switch criteria** per lever; any lever failing its 90-day leading indicator triggers a governance forum review with a ratified pause-or-replace decision path [E49].

## Portfolio overview

The portfolio was constructed by mapping the nine validated hypotheses (D11) to the eight-lever pattern catalog [E9], then selecting the three levers with the clearest combination of impact envelope, feasibility inside the 14-month window, and evidence confidence from Phase 2. Two other catalog levers — **supplier cost master unification** (mapped to H13) and **planogram redesign** (mapped to H7) — were explicitly deferred to Phase 5 because their execution envelopes exceed the Morrison window; those deferrals are held in the portfolio as named second-cycle candidates, not as dropped options [E31]. The **remaining three catalog levers** (national-brand scope, loyalty program margin, store-format resegmentation) were tested as H15-H17 and rejected on scope or materiality grounds during the Phase 2 hypothesis workshop [E31]. The portfolio therefore represents the filtered intersection of **pattern-library completeness**, **Phase 2 evidence**, and **Phase 3 execution envelope** — not an inherited template.

The **parallel-execution decision** (ratified by Marcus T. on 2026-03-18 [E40]) is the portfolio's structural choice. Sequential execution — supplier first, then assortment, then promo — loses the portfolio-compounding effect quantified in D11 at +30-40% faster recovery [E32]. The compounding mechanism is lever-specific: Lever 1 (supplier) and Lever 2 (assortment) compound because volume-tier supplier pricing is highest when SKU rationalisation is concurrent; Lever 3 (promo) is additive to both but has a data dependency (H14's T+7 reporting) that has to run in parallel regardless. The portfolio therefore runs three workstreams concurrently from G1 onward with the governance forum (H12) serving as the cross-workstream coordination mechanism rather than a fourth lever [E45].

## Lever 1 · Supplier renegotiation

**Target recovery · 140-200 bps** (expected contribution to total portfolio). **Capital allocation · $3.1M** (primarily contract legal, tier-2 supplier due diligence, and platform for trade-fund tracking) [E46]. **Owner · Diane R. (SVP Supply Chain).** **Gate · G2 2026-08-06 (90-day first-close).**

The lever executes four sub-workstreams in parallel. **Sub-WS 1.1 · Volume-tier contract restructure** — renegotiate the top-3 Tier-1 supplier contracts from fixed-unit-cost primitives to tiered volume structures with annual rebates, mirroring cohort Practice 1 [E28]. Target: 400-600 bps unit-cost improvement on consolidated volume (maps to H1) [E46]. **Sub-WS 1.2 · Tier-2 supplier activation** — activate specification-compatible tier-2 suppliers in 3 of 6 assessed categories; target dual-source or tier-2-preferred status on 20-30% of category volume by G5 (maps to H2). **Sub-WS 1.3 · Dual-sourcing critical SKUs** — establish structural dual-sourcing on 4 critical SKUs; target 15-25% unit-cost reduction on that subset (maps to H3) [E46]. **Sub-WS 1.4 · Supplier-led trade-fund discipline** — embed depth-commitment negotiation timing into category review cycle; target 30-50 bps depth-creep independent reduction (maps to H4). The lever is **high confidence** because three of four sub-WSs have direct cohort-practice evidence [E28][E46], and the remaining sub-WS (1.4) has pain-point-level evidence [E25]. The leading indicator for G2 is **first renegotiated contract signed on at least 1 of 3 Tier-1 suppliers by day 90** with unit-cost recovery ≥ 300 bps on consolidated volume [E46]. A miss on this indicator triggers the kill-switch review (below).

## Lever 2 · Assortment rationalisation

**Target recovery · 80-120 bps.** **Capital allocation · $1.9M** (largely category analytics tooling, store-tier segmentation work, and change-management for category leads) [E47]. **Owner · Reese A. (VP Merchandising, Owned Brand)** with **Diane R. air cover** for the first two category rollouts. **Gate · G3 2026-09-30 (Wave 1 rollout).**

The lever executes three sub-workstreams in parallel but inside the CMO's brand-equity fence. **Sub-WS 2.1 · Long-tail rationalisation** — rationalise the bottom 40% of owned-brand SKUs by volume across the 8 compressing categories; target 14-19% margin-per-linear-foot lift with <2% revenue risk (maps to H5) [E47]. **Sub-WS 2.2 · Pack-size consolidation** — consolidate 12 SKUs with pack-size redundancy; target 60-90 bps margin on that SKU subset (maps to H6). **Sub-WS 2.3 · Subcategory exit** — exit orphan / discontinued-launch subcategories; target 30-50 bps mix drift recovery (maps to H8). The **brand-equity fence** — ratified by Katherine P. on 2026-03-12 — caps per-category cut depth at **15% in urban stores** and **23% in suburban stores**, with the bounded variation reflecting different basket dynamics and loyalty mechanics by tier [E11][E47]. The lever is **high confidence** because H5 and H8 have high-confidence Phase 2 evidence and cohort Practice 2 is directly transferable [E28]; the brand-equity fence constrains the upper bound of recovery but not the lower bound. The leading indicator for G3 is **4-of-8 categories through Wave 1 rationalisation by day 150** with revenue risk tracking <2% and no consumer-research signal flag from the Katherine P. brand-health dashboard [E47].

## Lever 3 · Promo discipline

**Target recovery · 80-110 bps.** **Capital allocation · $1.8M** (primarily T+7 reporting rebuild, competitive price monitoring tooling, and margin-gated calendar logic implementation) [E48]. **Owner · Pricing Analytics Director** (workstream lead; sponsor surface is Marcus T. via the governance forum). **Gate · G4 2026-10-15 (calendar live).**

The lever executes three sub-workstreams in parallel. **Sub-WS 3.1 · Depth cap** — cap promotional depth at -22% across the owned-brand book; target 60-85 bps recovery (maps to H9) based on price-elasticity modelling [E7][E48]. **Sub-WS 3.2 · Margin-gated promotional calendar** — require a category margin threshold to be met before any depth event is approved; mirrors cohort Practice 3 [E28] and structurally prevents depth creep (maps to H10). **Sub-WS 3.3 · T+7 category margin reporting** — rebuild the category margin reporting pipeline from T+14 to T+7 (maps to H14); this is a data-infrastructure dependency that also enables H11's near-real-time competitive price monitoring [E48]. The lever is **medium-high confidence**: H9 and H10 are well-evidenced but depth-cap enforcement typically encounters category-manager resistance in pattern-library precedents [E9], and the T+7 reporting rebuild is a non-trivial data-engineering dependency [E48]. The leading indicator for G4 is **margin-gated calendar operational and T+7 reporting online by day 165** with the first live depth event gated at -22% cap and no manual override; an override event triggers a governance forum review within 48 hours [E48].

## Confidence + sequencing

The three levers have distinct confidence profiles and sequencing rationales. **Lever 1 (high)** is sequenced first into execution because its 90-day first-close gate (G2) is the earliest measurable impact and because supplier contract redraft cycle latency is the longest on the critical path. **Lever 2 (high, bounded)** is sequenced in parallel because the compounding with Lever 1 is only captured if SKU rationalisation is concurrent with supplier volume renegotiation; the brand-equity fence constrains the upper bound of recovery but is a confidence boundary, not a confidence reduction [E50]. **Lever 3 (medium-high)** is sequenced in parallel because the T+7 reporting dependency forces it into the Q3 2026 window and because its recovery is additive (not compounding) with the other levers; running it sequentially would delay recovery without capturing offsetting compounding [E50]. The confidence ladder is captured in D17 (decision memo) as the sponsor-attested confidence framing for the $6.8M capital commitment; the ladder is material to the business case because it calibrates the low/base/high scenarios in D16 [E50].

## Kill-switch criteria

Each lever has a named kill-switch criterion that pauses the lever and triggers a governance-forum review rather than silently underperforming. **Lever 1 kill-switch** — no Tier-1 supplier contract signed by day 120 (30 days past G2), OR signed but unit-cost recovery <250 bps; pause triggers a replacement-path review (tier-2 acceleration, scope compression, or capital reallocation to Lever 3) [E49]. **Lever 2 kill-switch** — Wave 1 rollout shows consumer-perception signal drag in Katherine P.'s brand-health dashboard (>1 standard deviation below trend), OR revenue risk exceeds 2.5% at category level; pause triggers a cut-depth re-calibration or a store-tier narrowing [E49]. **Lever 3 kill-switch** — T+7 reporting slips past day 195 (30 days past G4), OR depth cap encounters >3 override events in the first 60 days of operation; pause triggers either a reporting-rebuild replan or a calendar-logic rewrite with tighter enforcement [E49]. The kill-switch protocol is **pause, not cancel** — a paused lever re-enters the governance forum agenda weekly until the decision owner (Marcus T., Katherine P., or the relevant workstream sponsor) ratifies either resumption, re-scope, or replacement. Pattern-library precedent: programs with named kill-switches recover 2.1× faster from lever underperformance than programs without [E9][E49].

## Risks and mitigations

- **[High]** **Kill-switch ratios assume pattern-library base rates on override events and cycle-time latency.** Apex's actual response curve may differ. Mitigation: kill-switch thresholds have a re-calibration trigger at G1+30 days based on early signal; governance forum has authority to re-baseline without sponsor escalation [E49].
- **[High]** **Brand-equity fence in Lever 2 may under-size recovery if consumer tolerance proves higher than the 15%/23% thresholds.** Upside leakage if actual tolerance is 25-30%. Mitigation: Katherine P.'s dashboard is the re-calibration signal; if Wave 1 shows no consumer drag at the fence, Wave 2 can stretch the cap by up to 5 percentage points with CMO sign-off [E47].
- **[Medium]** **Portfolio-compounding estimate (+30-40%) is pattern-library-derived, not Apex-specific.** If compounding is lower, the high-scenario ($22M/yr) does not materialise. Mitigation: D16 treats the compounding lift as upside-only; low-scenario assumes additive-only recovery at $14M/yr [E32][E50].
