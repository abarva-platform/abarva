## Executive summary

The delivery plan converts the Phase 3 intervention portfolio (D15), business case (D16), decision memo (D17), and risk register (D18) into an executable Phase 4 build-out running **2026-04-30 → 2026-05-30**. The plan resolves three questions: how the parallel tracks scope inside 30 days, who owns what, and what must be true on 2026-05-30 to move from BUILD to Phase 5 attestation. Morrison carries a **$6.8M capital envelope** against a **$14-22M/yr steady-state impact target**; Phase 4 is the interval in which the three levers (supplier renegotiation, assortment rationalisation, promotional depth discipline) transition from designed to in-flight [E9][E30]. ## Phase 4 scope envelope

Phase 4 is **build-out, not steady-state operation**. The envelope is narrow by design: stand up the three workstreams, execute Sprint 1, close it with defensible telemetry, and leave Sprint 2 sequenced for Phase 5 handoff. In scope: RACI ratification, workstream charters, sprint cadence lock, D22 change package, D24 measurement plan, and the governance forum (H12) [E60]. Out of scope: 1,200-store planogram work (H7, deferred per D11), supplier cost master unification (H13, deferred), and any Phase 5 attestation activity. Phase 4 ships only what Phase 4 can finish — attestation creep is a governance failure, not a stretch goal.

The 30-day window is deliberately short. The Phase 3 debate contemplated a 45-day Phase 4; Marcus T. compressed to 30 on the view that longer windows invite scope drift while the levers are fresh [E60]. The compression tightens the gate criteria (below) beyond a typical build-phase gate.

## Workstream structure

Three workstreams run in parallel, each with a named pair of decision owners. **WS1 · Supplier Renegotiation** is co-owned by Diane R. and a category-lead deputy; carries H1-H4, anchored on the Q3 2026 supplier reset window. **WS2 · Assortment Rationalisation** is co-owned by Reese A. and Lena G.; the Lena pairing is deliberate — she is the CMO's interlocutor on the 15%-per-category brand-equity fence [E11]. **WS3 · Promotional Depth Discipline** is co-owned by the Director of Pricing and a Finance BP; carries H14's T+14 → T+7 reporting stand-up as an embedded dependency rather than a separate track [E31].

The governance forum (H12) is not a fourth workstream but a weekly cross-workstream steering construct chaired by Marcus T. Tuesdays at 0900, with Katherine P. every second week. It resolves cross-workstream dependencies inside a week rather than letting them migrate to the Phase-gate review [E60].

## Resourcing + staffing

Of the $6.8M envelope, **$1.1M** is allocated to Phase 4 build-out: one added category lead for WS2 ($180K loaded, pro-rated), reporting contractor for WS3 (~$240K), CMO-side training/comms vendor for D22 (~$120K), retained Apex allocations at 40-80% (Diane R. 50%, Reese A. 70%, Pricing Director 60%, Lena G. 25%) [E62]. The added category lead is the most consequential choice; without the added bandwidth, WS2 Sprint 1 would have landed at 60-65% target velocity per comparable-pattern data [E62].

Nexus Maestro orchestrates across workstreams and owns the weekly telemetry package. A Nexus delivery lead is embedded full-time, a content lead supports D22, and data engineering supports the WS3 reporting stand-up. The Nexus footprint is sized for removal at Phase 5 — every Nexus role has a documented Apex shadow owner [E60].

## Sequencing + critical path

The critical path runs through WS1. Supplier Renegotiation generates the volume-commitment data WS2 needs to size SKU cuts without breaking supplier contracts, and WS3 needs to size margin-gated promotional depth caps [E61]. If WS1 slips, WS2 and WS3 slip behind it; WS2 or WS3 slipping alone leaves WS1 unaffected.

Sprint 1 (Weeks 1-2) executes: WS1 volume-tier contract template drafts for Supplier A and Supplier B; WS2 long-tail SKU freeze and category-by-category cut-target modelling; WS3 T+14 → T+7 reporting pilot on packaged goods and household essentials. Sprint 2 (Weeks 3-4) executes: WS1 Supplier C contract template and dual-source activation for 4 critical SKUs; WS2 first-category cut execution in packaged goods; WS3 margin-gated calendar rollout on household essentials [E61].

## Gate criteria for Phase 5

Phase 4 closes 2026-05-30 against four hard gates. **Gate 1:** WS1 signed term sheets with Supplier A and Supplier B on volume-tier restructure plus a tier-2 supplier MoU on one activation category. **Gate 2:** WS2 has executed the first category cut in packaged goods with no consumer-visible signal in store-tier weekly reads. **Gate 3:** WS3 has a margin-gated calendar ratified by the sponsor pair and has capped one live event at -22% as pilot. **Gate 4:** D22 training has cascaded to store-level by 2026-05-27 with >80% completion on the 1,200-store network [E60].

A failed gate does not auto-pass to Phase 5. D24 attestation assumes all four gates closed; a failure triggers either a 10-day gate-specific extension or a descoping conversation owned by Marcus T. [E60].

## Contingencies

The envelope carries a **12% contingency reserve** ($816K) ring-fenced against three named triggers [E63]. **Trigger 1:** Supplier C redraft past 2026-05-22 (already flagged at-risk in Sprint 1 [E66]) — contingency funds a parallel tier-2 draft so WS1 does not stall on a single supplier. **Trigger 2:** CMO-side brand-equity pushback forces a smaller first-category cut than modelled — contingency funds additional brand research and a second rehearsal cycle. **Trigger 3:** WS3 reporting slips past T+10 on first close — contingency funds a second contractor wave and a bridge reporting layer.

The reserve is not for scope expansion. Any release requires Marcus T. authorisation with a documented trigger match; scope expansion using reserve dollars is prohibited by the D17 memo [E60].

## Risks and mitigations

- **[High]** **WS1 Supplier C redraft slips past Sprint 1 closeout (flagged 2026-05-13 [E66]).** Critical-path risk: WS2's first cut loses volume-commitment visibility and must derisk or delay. Mitigation: parallel tier-2 draft funded from contingency; Sprint 2 first stand-up item.

- **[Medium]** **Added category lead ramp may not hit Sprint 2 velocity.** Pattern ramp is 2-4 weeks; Morrison allows 2-3. Mitigation: Reese A. backstops at 70%; Sprint 2 handoff checklist authored during Sprint 1.
