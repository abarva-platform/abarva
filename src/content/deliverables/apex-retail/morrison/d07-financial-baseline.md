## Executive summary

Apex's owned-brand gross margin compressed **340 basis points** over the twenty-four months ending January 2026, with compression concentrated in eight of the twelve active owned-brand categories [E1]. The decline is not a pricing failure — shelf-price discipline held within 40 bps of the cohort median throughout the window [E3][E20]. Approximately **220 bps of the compression is cost-side** (unit cost inflation outrunning negotiated recovery and a 6–9% supplier-concentration premium), **85 bps is promotional depth creep**, and the residual **35 bps is mix drift** inside the owned-brand basket. Packaged goods and household essentials absorbed the heaviest damage; personal care and frozen prepared held flat. This baseline sets the measurement floor against which the three-lever Morrison program will be attested, and frames the **$14–22M/yr** steady-state impact target as recoverable — not aspirational.

## Key facts

- **-340 bps** aggregate owned-brand gross-margin compression over 24 months, baseline lock date 2026-02-04 [E1]
- **8 of 12** owned-brand categories in compression; packaged goods (-510 bps) and household essentials (-480 bps) lead the decline [E20]
- **~65% cost-side, ~25% promotional depth, ~10% mix** — decomposed attribution of the compression [E21]
- Measurement cadence: monthly, with 24-month rolling window; attested against Apex Finance closing ledger [E8]

## What the owned-brand margin looked like before Morrison

The baseline uses a rolling 24-month trace of Apex Finance closing-ledger margin, reconciled to internal category ledgers [E21]. Owned-brand (house-label) SKUs were isolated from national-brand inventory using the master-data flag that Apex's merchandising team maintains; the 312 SKUs in scope generated $2.1B of 2025 revenue, or 11.7% of total Apex revenue. Gross margin on the owned-brand book opened the window at 32.8% (February 2024) and closed at 29.4% (January 2026). National-brand margin moved -40 bps across the same window, and the cohort benchmark for owned-brand categories lifted +80 bps [E2]. Apex is therefore **420 bps behind a cohort that is moving in the opposite direction**, not merely drifting inside a sector-wide soft patch.

## Category-level decomposition

Of the twelve active owned-brand categories, eight show statistically significant compression (p < 0.05 against a two-year control baseline) [E20]. Packaged goods (dry grocery, snacks, beverages) dropped **510 bps** on the back of supplier renegotiation stalling through two contract cycles. Household essentials (paper, cleaning, hygiene) dropped **480 bps**, driven almost entirely by promotional depth expanding from -18% to -29% average markdown as the CMO's team defended share against national-brand depth reductions in that subcategory. Frozen prepared (-90 bps), dairy ambient (-140 bps), and canned goods (-290 bps) round out the declining set. Personal care held flat (+10 bps). Fresh bakery and specialty condiments actually expanded (+60 and +80 bps respectively), which the baseline notes as proof that category-level recovery is achievable inside the Apex operating model.

## Cost structure analysis

The cost-side decomposition is the most consequential finding in the baseline. Landed unit cost inflation across the 24 months totaled **9.4%** on a weighted basis, against negotiated cost recovery of only **3.8%** — a 560 bps gap translating to **~220 bps of the margin compression**. Inside that gap, the single biggest driver is supplier concentration: the top three suppliers hold **62% of owned-brand COGS** [E5], and the unit-cost premium vs. a diversified supplier base is +6–9% across five categories. Freight, packaging, and contract-manufacturing fees each contributed 20–40 bps individually; none are dominant, but collectively they explain the remainder of the cost-side gap. Labor allocation was not a material contributor in the owned-brand window, since most owned-brand production is outsourced to contract manufacturers.

## Revenue waterfall

Decomposing the revenue side: starting gross margin of 32.8% → (-85 bps) promotional depth creep (the depth ramp from -18% to -27% on household essentials and packaged goods) [E7] → (-35 bps) mix drift (long-tail owned-brand SKUs grew as a share of the basket, and those SKUs carry 150-200 bps lower margin than the top-quartile SKUs) [E6] → (-220 bps) unit cost inflation net of negotiated recovery → ending margin 29.4%. The waterfall is additive in bps rather than compounded, and ties to the month-closed ledger within a 3 bp reconciliation band [E21]. Apex Finance signed off on the decomposition methodology on 2026-02-04.

## Competitive margin benchmarks

A six-retailer composite cohort, matched on revenue band ($12B-$24B), store format, and owned-brand program maturity, placed Apex in the **3rd quartile** on owned-brand gross margin, with a **180 bps gap to cohort median** and **310 bps gap to top quartile** [E2]. The detailed cohort analysis sits in D10; the baseline's use of the cohort is narrower: it establishes that the 340 bps compression is not a sector effect. Four of the six cohort members **expanded** owned-brand margin over the same 24 months; two held flat. None compressed at Apex's magnitude. The benchmark view thereby converts the problem framing from "tough environment" to "Apex-specific capability gap," which the CFO has used in sponsor comms since the 2026-02-11 prioritization review [E22].

## Gaps and data caveats

Three caveats frame the baseline's confidence. First, one owned-brand category (specialty beverages, launched mid-2024) has only 15 months of clean history and is excluded from the compression attribution but included in the current margin level. Second, the promotional depth attribution uses a price-elasticity model calibrated on 2023-2025 data [E7]; its confidence is graded medium because the 2026 price environment is still evolving. Third, the supplier concentration premium uses a diversified-cohort counterfactual [E5]; the 6–9% range captures model uncertainty but should be reread as Phase 3 supplier negotiations actualize. Apex Finance and the Morrison team agreed on 2026-02-04 that these caveats do not invalidate the baseline for decision purposes, but will be revisited at Phase 4 baseline re-lock before outcome attestation begins.

## Risks and mitigations

- **[Medium]** **Supplier concentration premium range is wide (6–9%).** The counterfactual model cannot fully isolate category effects from supplier effects. Mitigation: re-estimate after two Phase 3 supplier negotiations close, tightening the Phase 4 baseline re-lock.
- **[Medium]** **Promotional depth elasticity may shift as competitors respond.** Depth discipline is one of the three Morrison levers; competitive response is modelled but unobserved. Mitigation: monthly competitive price monitoring built into the outcome measurement plan (D24).
- **[Low]** **Owned-brand master-data flag has historical drift.** ~3% of SKUs were reclassified during the window. Mitigation: re-run the trace with a conservative (stricter) flag; delta was 12 bps, within the reconciliation band.
