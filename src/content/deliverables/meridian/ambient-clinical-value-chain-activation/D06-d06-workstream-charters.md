## Executive summary

Phase 2 of MRD-01 decomposes the value-chain activation mandate into **four parallel workstreams** so documentation-time baseline, burnout measurement, vendor-integration reality check, and CMIO/CMO alignment run concurrently across the **2026-02-03 → 2026-03-03** window without colliding at steering [E16][E20]. Each has a named lead, weekly cadence, and explicit contract with the Phase 3 decision memo (D17). **WS1 Documentation Governance** (Dr. Morales + Theo J.) owns note-template drift, vendor-integration commissioning, and the ambient-to-CDI feedback loop design. **WS2 Clinician Burnout Measurement** (Dr. Larsson + Kevin B.) protects the clinician-facing lens and wires MBI-HSS and pajama-time signals into the steering packet. **WS3 Vendor Rationalisation Candidates** (Priya Raman + Dr. Morales) builds the evidence inventory the D17 consolidation question will reason against, without pre-committing Phase 2 to a rationalisation answer. **WS4 CMIO/CMO Alignment Cadence** (Sarah Chen + Dr. Larsson) sustains the four-principal governance architecture under Phase 2 evidence pressure. The four-workstream structure is the Phase 2 design call that executes value-chain framing without drifting into premature vendor rationalisation [E3][E6].

## Workstream structure for Phase 2

The Phase 2 charter runs four concurrent workstreams because the ambient-clinical-value-chain pattern has four distinct diagnostic surfaces that move on different rhythms [E7]. Documentation governance moves in daily Epic-integration sprints. Burnout measurement moves on monthly MBI-HSS waves and weekly pajama-time cuts. Vendor rationalisation moves with monthly vendor-council cadences. CMIO/CMO alignment moves on the biweekly steering rhythm. Forcing all four onto a single workstream would starve the slower surfaces or slow the faster ones to match.

The four workstreams share a single **Nexus orchestrator agent** surface as their cross-workstream coordination layer, preventing the classical failure mode of divergent framings crystallising before steering can reconcile them [E16]. The orchestrator maintains a shared contradiction log, surfaces cross-workstream dependencies at the Monday working committee, and prepares a consolidated steering packet every second Thursday. Each workstream lead writes into the shared orchestrator surface, so evidence inventory stays single-source through Phase 2.

## WS1 · Documentation governance

**Lead:** Dr. Morales · CMIO. **Co-lead:** Theo J. · Director, Clinical Informatics Engineering. **Deliverables owned:** D06 Documentation-Time Baseline, D08 Pain Point Register (documentation subsections), D11 Hypothesis Backlog (documentation hypotheses).

WS1 carries the heaviest diagnostic load. Its Phase 2 contract has three commitments [E11][E15]. **First**, validate the Epic Signal baseline methodology against the 18-month trace and freeze the 2025 Q4 baseline on 2026-02-11 [E1]. **Second**, audit note-template drift across Abridge, DAX Copilot, and Suki outputs at a 2,400-note, 90-day sample with specialty-specific rubrics [E11]. **Third**, scope the ambient-to-CDI feedback loop architecture at design-brief depth — sufficient to support Phase 3 intervention sizing but short of vendor commissioning commitments. WS1 operates on a daily stand-up with Theo J., a weekly deep-working session with Dr. Morales chairing, and biweekly vendor-council touchpoints that feed WS3.

## WS2 · Clinician burnout measurement

**Lead:** Dr. Larsson · CMO. **Co-lead:** Kevin B. · Director, Epic Signal Analytics. **Deliverables owned:** weekly pajama-time dashboard, monthly MBI-HSS burnout wave, D08 Pain Point Register (clinician subsections).

WS2 protects the clinician-facing lens. Its Phase 2 contract: **no Phase 3 intervention recommendation enters D15 without WS2 sign-off** on clinician cognitive-load implications — the operational expression of Dr. Larsson's co-sponsor veto authority [E4]. Its first deliverable is a weekly pajama-time dashboard cut by specialty, site, and ambient-vendor coverage, piped into the steering packet every second Thursday. Its second is the 2026-02-25 MBI-HSS wave [E2], giving Phase 2 a repeat-measurement point against the 2026-01-12 baseline — the earliest credible window without survey-fatigue contamination. WS2 also owns the clinician-voice layer of D08: 22 structured clinician conversations (12 primary care, 6 specialty, 4 outpatient procedural) across the five largest ambulatory sites and two highest-volume hospital-medicine services.

## WS3 · Vendor rationalisation candidates

**Lead:** Priya Raman · VP Revenue Cycle. **Co-lead:** Dr. Morales · CMIO. **Deliverables owned:** D10 Benchmark Comparison, vendor-capability inventory, D17 Decision Memo Phase 3 input packet.

WS3 holds the vendor-rationalisation question open through Phase 2 without answering it. Its contract: **assemble the evidence the Phase 3 decision memo will reason against**, not pre-commit the program to a rationalisation answer. This is Dr. Morales's concession in the charter decision log [E6]: his dissent on rationalisation timing is preserved, and the Phase 3 gate re-examines the question with Phase 2 evidence in hand. WS3 produces three artefacts: a vendor-capability inventory comparing Abridge, DAX Copilot, and Suki across 14 integration surfaces; D10 Benchmark Comparison (five MA-heavy peer IDNs) [E10]; and a switching-cost and transition-risk model on the two most consolidation-plausible candidates (primary-care only, specialty only), giving the Phase 3 gate a quantitative frame without foreclosing the decision.

## WS4 · CMIO/CMO alignment cadence

**Lead:** Sarah Chen · CIO. **Co-lead:** Dr. Larsson · CMO. **Deliverables owned:** biweekly CMIO/CMO/CIO alignment log, Phase 2 → 3 gate decision packet.

WS4 is the governance-integrity workstream. Its contract: **prevent the four-principal authority partition from fragmenting under Phase 2 evidence pressure**. As WS1 surfaces note-template drift and WS3 assembles vendor-capability evidence, Dr. Morales's prior rationalisation argument becomes easier to make, which could push CMIO and CMO into oppositional positions heading into the Phase 3 gate [E6]. WS4 runs a biweekly 45-minute CMIO/CMO/CIO triad on the opposite Thursday from steering, giving the three principals a low-stakes surface to reconcile framings before they land at the four-principal steering. WS4 also owns the Phase 2 → 3 gate packet: a 6-page decision brief consolidating WS1-3 findings into three Phase 3 options (status-quo-three-vendor, primary-care-consolidation, full-rationalisation) with evidence citations against each, arriving at steering on 2026-03-03.

## Reporting into the steering committee

All four workstreams report into the **biweekly Thursday steering committee** via a consolidated Nexus orchestrator packet. The packet arrives 48 hours ahead and follows a fixed shape: section 1 summarises WS1-4 progress against Phase 2 contract commitments; section 2 lists open contradictions between workstreams (the orchestrator is required to surface, not suppress, these); section 3 lists decisions the committee owns this meeting; section 4 lists decisions workstream leads have taken under their Tier-2 authority since the prior steering.

The reporting structure is deliberately asymmetric: WS1 and WS3 route more decisions to the committee because their work surfaces capital-scope and vendor-scope questions; WS2 and WS4 take more decisions at the workstream level because their surfaces are clinician-facing and governance-procedural. Mark L. (Transformation Office) owns packet discipline; the Nexus orchestrator maintains packet state between meetings.

## Risks and mitigations

- **[Medium]** **Workstream contradictions calcify before steering surfaces them.** Parallel diagnosis across four workstreams produces divergent framings by design; if the Nexus orchestrator fails to surface contradictions, the steering committee receives a falsely consensual packet and decisions drift. **Mitigation:** section 2 of every steering packet is explicitly the open-contradictions list; Mark L. reviews the section with Dr. Morales 24 hours before packet release; any contradiction suppressed from the packet is flagged in the working-committee minutes.
- **[Medium]** **WS3 evidence assembly drifts into a de-facto consolidation recommendation.** The workstream lead assignment (Priya Raman + Dr. Morales) creates a structural bias toward consolidation because Dr. Morales has argued for it publicly [E6]. **Mitigation:** Sarah Chen chairs WS3 quarterly review; WS3 artefacts are explicitly scoped as evidence-for-Phase-3-decision, not recommendation; D10 and D17 re-state the three Phase 3 options as open.
- **[Medium]** **WS2 clinician-voice layer overweights recent burnout.** 22 structured clinician conversations conducted inside a 30-day window may bias toward acute pain visible at the time of interview. **Mitigation:** WS2 triangulates clinician voice against Epic Signal baseline [E1] and MBI-HSS wave [E2]; the three signals must converge for a pain point to enter D08 as high-severity.
