## Executive summary

The Meridian intervention portfolio selects **three levers from the eight-lever ambient-clinical-value-chain pattern catalog** [E7] and executes them as a **parallel track** rather than sequentially, sized to the $4.2M capital envelope and the $8-14M/yr steady-state value-at-stake target [E9][E46]. **Lever 1 · Documentation governance + CDI integration** targets a **25-35 minute primary-care pajama-time reduction** by Phase 4 exit through unified ambient-to-CDI telemetry, specialty-specific template governance, and a single vendor-council cadence (H4, H6, H10) [E28][E47]. **Lever 2 · Clinician-facing workflow redesign** targets a **3-5 point HCC capture lift on the MA panel** and a **4-7 point MBI-HSS emotional-exhaustion reduction** through HCC feedback-loop commissioning, quality-measure capture at encounter level, and shift-specific pajama-time interventions (H5, H7, H9) [E28][E29][E47]. **Lever 3 · Vendor rationalisation (pilot-first, decision-deferred)** executes the D13 ambient consolidation pilot at 18 sites and preserves the three-vendor operating envelope through Phase 4, with the rationalisation decision routed to Phase 5 ratification (H12 held open) [E6][E42][E46]. The portfolio's **expected steady-state value** is **$8-14M/yr** (low-to-high scenarios) against the $4.2M capital envelope, closing 55-75% of the gap to cohort-median integration maturity [E9][E28]. Each lever has named **kill-switch criteria**; any lever failing its 90-day leading indicator triggers a governance-forum review with a ratified pause-or-replace decision path [E45].

## Portfolio overview

The portfolio was constructed by mapping the seven validated hypotheses (D11) to the eight-lever `ambient-clinical-value-chain` pattern catalog [E7][E31], then selecting the three levers with the clearest combination of clinician-outcome envelope, feasibility inside the 14-month Phase 4 window, and evidence confidence from Phase 2. Two other catalog levers — **ambient coverage extension to in-basket and portal-message documentation** (mapped to H8) and **specialty-specific ambient consolidation** (mapped to H3) — were explicitly deferred to Phase 5 because their execution envelopes exceed the Phase 4 window: H8 requires Epic in-basket modernisation beyond the $4.2M envelope, and H3 exposes urgent care and ambulatory surgery to a 6-9 month ambient-coverage gap during Suki sunset [E31]. Those deferrals are held as named second-cycle candidates in the Phase 5 continuation roadmap, not as dropped options. The **remaining three catalog levers** (full three-way consolidation, full ambient withdrawal, clinician-coaching-only with no technology change) were tested as H1 and an earlier candidate and rejected on Phase 2 evidence [E31]. The portfolio therefore represents the filtered intersection of **pattern-library completeness**, **Phase 2 evidence**, and **Phase 3 execution envelope** — not an inherited template.

The **parallel-execution decision** (ratified by Sarah Chen on 2026-03-18 with Dr. Larsson co-signing [E40]) is the portfolio's structural choice. Sequential execution — documentation governance first, then workflow redesign, then rationalisation pilot — loses the portfolio-compounding effect quantified in D11 at **+25-35% faster outcome realisation** [E32]. The compounding mechanism is lever-specific: **Lever 1's CDI-queue volume reinforces Lever 2's HCC feedback loop** because CDI resolutions feed the vendor engines that then suggest more completely on subsequent encounters, creating a positive cycle; **Lever 2's feedback loop in turn reinforces Lever 1's template governance** because HCC-relevant template updates flow from CDI-observed gaps. Lever 3's pilot is structurally parallel-only — its empirical value is in comparing consolidation-at-the-margin against the integration-driven lift that Levers 1 and 2 deliver across all 220 sites, which requires Levers 1 and 2 to be running concurrently [E30][E42]. The portfolio therefore runs three workstreams concurrently from G1 onward with the governance triad (H10 unified vendor-council cadence + H11 CMIO/CMO/CIO alignment) serving as cross-workstream infrastructure rather than a fourth outcome lever [E20].

## Lever 1 · Documentation governance + CDI integration

**Target outcome · 25-35 min/clinician/day pajama-time reduction in primary care; CDI-queue routing volume from ~40/week baseline to 140-180/week.** **Capital allocation · $1.8M** (primarily unified ambient-to-CDI telemetry commissioning, Epic integration-engineering labor, specialty-specific template governance tooling, and vendor-council coordination platform) [E46]. **Owner · Dr. Morales (CMIO) with Theo J. (Director, Clinical Informatics Engineering) as execution lead.** **Gate · G2 2026-08-25 (90-day CDI first-signal) and G4 2026-11-30 (template governance cadence live).**

The lever executes three sub-workstreams in parallel. **Sub-WS 1.1 · Unified ambient-to-CDI telemetry (H4)** — commission a single telemetry surface spanning Abridge, DAX Copilot, and Suki that pipes HCC suggestions, DRG specificity flags, and quality-measure gaps into a unified CDI queue; mirrors D10 Practice 1 directly [E28]. Target: CDI-queue routing volume from ~40/week to 140-180/week by G5 [E15][E25]. **Sub-WS 1.2 · Specialty-specific template governance board (H6)** — replace three parallel vendor-council cadences with a single quarterly board ratifying template updates on a shared Epic-library spine; target: template-selection error rate from 23% baseline to 11-14% residual by G5 [E11]. **Sub-WS 1.3 · Unified vendor-council coordination cadence (H10)** — Dr. Morales chairs a single weekly cadence replacing three parallel ones, cutting cross-vendor decision latency from median 18 days to median 5 [E24]. The lever is **high confidence** because H4 has direct cohort-practice evidence (two peers) and H6 and H10 have both cohort evidence (four peers) and pain-point-level evidence at Meridian [E11][E24][E28]. The leading indicator for G2 is **CDI-queue routing ≥80/week/100 ambient-clinicians by day 90**; a miss below 55/week triggers the kill-switch review.

## Lever 2 · Clinician-facing workflow redesign

**Target outcome · 3-5 point HCC capture lift on MA panel; 4-7 point MBI-HSS emotional-exhaustion reduction; HEDIS + Stars quality-measure capture from 34% to 55-65%.** **Capital allocation · $1.4M** (primarily HCC feedback-loop commissioning on Abridge and DAX Copilot, quality-measure documentation capture pipeline at encounter level, shift-specific clinician coaching, and Epic-integration middleware) [E46]. **Owner · Dr. Larsson (CMO) with Kevin B. (VP, Clinical Operations) as execution lead for clinician-facing surfaces and Priya Raman (VP Revenue Cycle) as execution lead for the HCC and quality-measure surfaces.** **Gate · G3 2026-10-15 (HCC feedback loop ratified) and G5 2027-03-31 (Phase 4 exit outcome reconciliation).**

The lever executes three sub-workstreams in parallel. **Sub-WS 2.1 · HCC-capture feedback loop (H5)** — commission the feedback loop from CDI-query resolution back into each ambient vendor's suggestion engine, on Abridge (primary care) and DAX Copilot (specialty) as priority deployments; Suki inherits the feedback loop as Phase 4 H2/H3 readiness builds toward a Phase 5 decision [E12][E13][E14]. Target: 3-5 point HCC capture lift on MA panel by G5 [E28]. **Sub-WS 2.2 · Quality-measure documentation capture (H7)** — route ambient-identified care-gap documentation directly into the HEDIS + Stars quality-measure workflow at encounter level, following D10 Practice 3 [E28]. Target: capture rate from 34% baseline to 55-65% by G5. **Sub-WS 2.3 · Shift-specific pajama-time interventions (H9)** — night and weekend rotation-specific coaching and workflow redesign, targeting the uneven pajama-time coverage pain surfaced by 9 of 14 primary-care clinicians in Phase 2 [E23]. Target: primary-care night-shift pajama time brought within 10 minutes of day-shift by G5. The lever is **medium-high confidence** because H5 and H7 have cohort evidence but feedback-loop commissioning requires vendor-council bilateral readiness that has not been fully stress-tested at Meridian [E12][E13], and H9 is a clinician-coaching intervention whose outcome signal depends on clinician engagement that cannot be pre-wired [E21][E23]. The leading indicator for G3 is **feedback loop commissioned on 2 of 3 vendors with 1-point HCC capture lift measured on 60-day trailing window**; a miss below 0.5-point lift triggers the kill-switch review.

## Lever 3 · Vendor rationalisation (pilot-first, decision-deferred)

**Target outcome · Phase 5 rationalisation decision textured with Meridian-specific consolidation evidence from the D13 pilot; Phase 4 outcome contribution is zero by design.** **Capital allocation · $1.0M** (including $0.6M D13 pilot operational cost and $0.4M reserved for Phase 5 transition planning) [E42][E46]. **Owner · Sarah Chen (CIO) with Priya Raman (VP Revenue Cycle) as execution lead on the pilot, Dr. Morales (CMIO) chairing the 2027-Q3 rationalisation ratification workshop.** **Gate · D13 pilot month-6 go/scale/kill (2026-12-15) and G6 Phase 5 attestation (2027-Q1 to 2027-Q3).**

The lever executes on a deliberately deferred timeline. **Sub-WS 3.1 · D13 ambient consolidation pilot** — 18 sites, 6-month instrumented window, Abridge-anchored primary-care consolidation inside the pilot boundary while three-vendor envelope is preserved system-wide; full design in D13 [E42][E43][E44]. **Sub-WS 3.2 · Phase 5 rationalisation ratification workshop preparation** — carry the pilot outcome, Dr. Morales's standing dissent, and the D10 Peer-3 decomposition (~70% integration-driven / ~30% consolidation-driven) into the 2027-Q3 workshop as structured evidence; the workshop itself decides between Option A (Abridge primary-care consolidation), Option B (DAX Copilot specialty consolidation), and Option C (three-vendor preserved with redesign) [E6][E30][E54]. **Sub-WS 3.3 · Vendor-council transition readiness** — maintain standing bilateral readiness with all three vendors through Phase 4 so that any Phase 5 rationalisation decision can execute on a 9-12 month horizon rather than a 14-20 month one [E12][E13][E14]. The lever is **medium confidence** because the pilot has kill-switch protection and the Phase 5 decision is deferred to when Meridian-specific evidence exists, but the fundamental outcome — whether Meridian should consolidate or preserve three vendors — is not resolved by this lever in Phase 4 by design [E6][E42]. The leading indicator for the pilot month-3 interim readout is the three-criterion gate in D13 [E45].

## Confidence + sequencing

The three levers have distinct confidence profiles and sequencing rationales. **Lever 1 (high)** is sequenced first into execution because its G2 90-day CDI-queue first-signal is the earliest measurable impact and because CDI-telemetry is the structural prerequisite for Lever 2's HCC feedback loop [E15][E47]. **Lever 2 (medium-high)** is sequenced in parallel because the compounding with Lever 1 is only captured if HCC feedback-loop commissioning happens concurrently with CDI-telemetry stand-up; the feedback loop has no signal without CDI queue routing, and CDI queue routing has no closure without the feedback loop [E47]. **Lever 3 (medium)** is sequenced in parallel because the D13 pilot's empirical value depends on the integration-driven lift being present across the non-pilot sites — the pilot measures consolidation-at-the-margin against the integration baseline, which requires Levers 1 and 2 running [E30][E42]. The confidence ladder is captured in D17 (decision memo) as the sponsor-attested confidence framing for the $4.2M capital commitment; the ladder calibrates the low/base/high scenarios in D16 [E49][E50].

## Kill-switch + escalation

Each lever has a named kill-switch criterion that pauses the lever and triggers a governance-forum review rather than silently underperforming [E45]. **Lever 1 kill-switch** — CDI-queue routing below 55/week/100 ambient-clinicians at G2 (day 90), OR vendor-council coordination cadence showing three consecutive weeks of unresolved cross-vendor blockers; pause triggers a replacement-path review (Epic-integration acceleration, scope compression to two-vendor telemetry, or capital reallocation to Lever 2) [E45][E47]. **Lever 2 kill-switch** — HCC capture lift below 0.5-point on 60-day trailing window at G3, OR feedback loop commissioning blocked by vendor-council non-readiness on more than one vendor; pause triggers either scope narrowing (Abridge-only feedback loop, defer DAX Copilot and Suki to Phase 5) or a capital reallocation to Lever 1's template-governance depth [E45]. **Lever 3 kill-switch** — D13 pilot month-3 interim readout fails the three-criterion gate; pause the pilot, re-instate DAX Copilot and Suki at the 18 sites, feed the pilot-retrospective into the Phase 5 packet as Meridian-specific negative-evidence [E42][E45]. The kill-switch protocol is **pause, not cancel** — a paused lever re-enters the governance forum agenda weekly until the decision owner (Sarah Chen for Lever 1 and 3; Dr. Larsson for Lever 2) ratifies either resumption, re-scope, or replacement. Pattern-library precedent: programs with named kill-switches recover 2.1× faster from lever underperformance than programs without [E7][E45]. Escalation pathways: workstream lead → governance triad → four-principal steering → Sarah Chen 48-hour turnaround if the steering cannot resolve.

## Risks and mitigations

- **[High]** **Kill-switch thresholds assume pattern-library base rates on CDI-queue ramp and feedback-loop commissioning cadence.** Meridian's actual response curve may differ — CDI-team capacity, Epic-integration cycle time, and vendor-council readiness could each undershoot the pattern-library median [E7][E45]. Mitigation: kill-switch thresholds have re-calibration triggers at G1+30 days based on early signal; governance triad has authority to re-baseline without sponsor escalation if the re-calibration stays within the D16 base-case envelope [E9][E49].
- **[High]** **Portfolio-compounding estimate (+25-35%) is pattern-library-derived, not Meridian-specific.** If compounding is lower, the high-scenario ($14M/yr) does not materialise [E32]. Mitigation: D16 treats the compounding lift as upside-only; low-scenario assumes additive-only outcome realisation at $8M/yr steady-state, which is defensible on the integration-only recovery component [E9][E49].
- **[Medium]** **Lever 3 pilot outcome may not produce a decisive Phase 5 rationalisation signal.** If the D13 pilot finishes in a mixed-signal state (2 of 4 scale-out triggers met), the 2027-Q3 ratification workshop inherits an ambiguous decision context rather than a textured one. Mitigation: pilot design includes structured readout format that explicitly separates consolidation-specific and integration-specific outcome components [E42][E44]; even a mixed-signal pilot produces better evidence than the Phase 3 baseline.
