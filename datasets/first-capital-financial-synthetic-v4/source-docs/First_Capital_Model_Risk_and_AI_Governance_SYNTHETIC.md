# Model Risk and AI Governance Readout (Synthetic)

AI pilots touching fraud, AML, lending, HR, customer servicing, and wealth supervision require model inventory registration, SR 11-7 style validation, explainability evidence, access controls, and monitoring plans before production scale.

Current gaps include overdue validation slots, inconsistent evidence citations, missing restricted-data attestations, weak prompt logging for some copilots, and unclear ownership where business-led AI workflows depend on technology data products.

## Executive Context

This synthetic source is intended to behave like a board or steering-pack excerpt, not a demo placeholder. It names the business objective, the affected operating areas, the technology dependencies, the evidence needed for governance, and the metric path that a CXO would use to decide whether to scale, hold, restructure, or stop the work.

## Business Metrics and Value Chain

The relevant value chain connects strategic objective, benchmarked metric gap, capability, initiative, future-state process, adoption, and realized outcome. The source should be read with the business-metric layer: revenue, margin, unit cost, productivity, quality, risk, customer experience, cycle time, and control evidence. For First Capital Model Risk and AI Governance, the value claim is not accepted unless it can be tied to baseline, target, forecast, observed result, and finance or business-owner attestation.

## Current-State Constraints

Current-state constraints include fragmented ownership, inconsistent source lineage, manual reconciliation, system-of-record ambiguity, vendor dependencies, change saturation, control evidence gaps, and unclear decision rights. These constraints are intentionally captured because they are the reasons an executive insight should recommend sequencing, evidence collection, adoption work, or kill/hold decisions rather than simply celebrate a technology launch.

## Future-State Process And Adoption

The future-state design should specify which work changes, which persona owns the changed workflow, what controls remain human-approved, what evidence is captured automatically, and how adoption will be measured. Adoption is treated as a first-class signal: active usage, workflow completion, exception rate, quality guardrails, training readiness, stakeholder sign-off, and operational handoff all influence whether value can be claimed.

## Evidence And Governance

Evidence should be sufficient for an executive challenge. Useful evidence includes source system extracts, financial assumptions, metric definitions, benchmark vintage, control approvals, model or AI governance notes, data-quality checks, test results, adoption telemetry, service reliability history, and named owner sign-off. If a downstream insight cannot cite this evidence path, the right answer is to name the gap instead of filling it with a generic recommendation.

## Questions This Source Should Help Answer

- What business metric is this work supposed to move?
- Is the benchmark gap large enough to justify action?
- Which capabilities, systems, data products, and vendors are in the dependency chain?
- Which risks or missing evidence block scale?
- What would the future process look like after the Move?
- Who must approve the value claim before it becomes board-ready?
