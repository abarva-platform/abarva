## Section 1. Business purpose

Supplier will provide application managed services for airline operations,
airport operations, corporate shared services, data and integration support,
and related service desk escalations. Buyer entered the agreement to stabilize
core application support, reduce incident backlog, improve change quality, and
create a measurable productivity glidepath.

## Section 2. In-scope services

1. L2/L3 application support for approximately 640 named applications in the
   AMS service catalog.
2. Airline operations applications covering disruption operations, airport
   station operations, crew operations, maintenance planning, and operations
   analytics.
3. Corporate shared-services support for finance, treasury, procurement, HR,
   legal operations, and enterprise reporting.
4. Data and integration support for batch interfaces, API support, file-transfer
   operations, and data quality triage.
5. Incident, request, problem, change, release, and knowledge-management support
   under the governance cadence in Section 11.

## Section 3. Out-of-scope services

Supplier excludes product engineering for new digital channels, ERP
transformation projects, cloud platform engineering, cybersecurity operations,
and business-process ownership. Work outside the service catalog must be
handled through the change-order process unless Buyer and Supplier agree to add
the work to a priced catalog line.

## Section 4. Baseline volumes

The run baseline used for pricing assumes:

| Driver | Contract baseline |
|---|---:|
| Monthly AMS tickets | 7,420 |
| P1 incidents per month | 31 |
| P2 incidents per month | 265 |
| Monthly changes | 690 |
| Emergency changes per month | 31 |
| Reopened incidents | 4.8% |
| Supported applications | 640 |

Supplier may request commercial relief when actual volume exceeds baseline by
more than 12% for two consecutive quarters, but only if Supplier demonstrates
that automation, problem management, and knowledge-base obligations have been
performed.

## Section 5. Pricing schedule

Base recurring services are priced at **$3,200,000 per month** for the 2026 run
year, excluding approved pass-throughs and change orders. The contract permits
approved pass-through charges for third-party tools, buyer-directed travel, and
premium coverage events, provided invoice-level backup is supplied.

## Section 6. Staffing commitment

Supplier commits to staff the following run teams unless Buyer approves a
re-baseline:

| Tower | Committed FTE | Coverage commitment |
|---|---:|---|
| Airline operations apps | 54 | 24x7 with named weekend escalation |
| Corporate shared services | 38 | 18x5 with month-end close coverage |
| Data and integration support | 22 | 18x5 plus overnight critical escalation |

Monthly staffing attestations must show committed FTE, observed FTE, location
mix, shift coverage, and open vacancies. Persistent underfill is a service
governance issue and may create credits if tied to missed service levels.

## Section 7. SLA exhibit

Supplier agrees to the service levels below:

| SLA | Target | Credit mechanism | Cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 incident restore | 95% within 4 hours | monthly credit if missed | 4% of monthly service fee |
| P2 incident restore | 92% within 12 hours | monthly credit if missed | 3% of monthly service fee |
| Change success | 98% successful changes | operational review only | no direct credit |
| Knowledge article freshness | 95% reviewed every 180 days | corrective action plan | no direct credit |

The contract does not define a chronic-miss multiplier for P1 restore or change
success. Earn-back is allowed after two compliant months unless Buyer disputes
root-cause closure.

## Section 8. Automation and productivity

Supplier proposed an 11% reduction in ticket handling effort by year three
through knowledge automation, resolver-group routing, incident clustering, and
runbook automation. The contract does not include an automatic run-rate
reduction unless Buyer and Supplier agree to a productivity credit schedule
through amendment.

## Section 9. Change-order process

Out-of-scope demand is priced through change orders. Supplier must classify each
change order as one-time, recurring run, pass-through, or demand-baseline
adjustment. Buyer may reject change-order charges that lack request owner,
service catalog mapping, price basis, and acceptance evidence.

## Section 10. Benchmarking and audit rights

Buyer may benchmark fees and service performance once per contract year after
year two. Supplier must provide role mix, location mix, invoice backup, SLA
history, change-order ledger, automation benefit ledger, and staffing
attestations within 20 business days of request.

## Section 11. Governance

Supplier and Buyer will operate weekly service reviews, monthly operating
reviews, and quarterly executive business reviews. The quarterly business
review must cover service performance, ticket trend, change quality, staffing,
automation progress, invoice variance, service credits, change orders, and open
risks.

## Section 12. Renewal and termination

The agreement renews for one 24-month extension unless Buyer gives written
non-renewal notice by **2026-09-30**. Buyer may reserve renewal rights while
requesting cure plans, benchmark support, or commercial renegotiation. Supplier
must support transition assistance for up to 180 days following expiration or
termination at the then-current rate card unless superseded by a transition
SOW.

## Section 13. Known commercial exceptions

1. Tooling pass-throughs require invoice backup but are not hard-capped.
2. Change-order demand is not automatically converted into catalog pricing.
3. P1 service credits are capped at 4% of monthly service fee.
4. Change-success SLA has no direct credit.
5. Productivity commitments require later amendment to affect run-rate.
