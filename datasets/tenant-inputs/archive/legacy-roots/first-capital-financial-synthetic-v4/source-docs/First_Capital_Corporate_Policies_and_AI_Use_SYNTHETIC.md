# First Capital Financial Corporate Policies and AI Use Context

Synthetic source document for context-layer ingestion.

## Policy Context

First Capital's AI and data modernization agenda is governed by model risk management, fair lending, privacy, information security, data classification, records retention, third-party risk, and sourcing policies. These policies matter because many proposed AI use cases touch regulated decisions, customer data, employee data, payments, fraud, AML, lending, wealth, or operational controls.

The model risk policy follows SR 11-7 style expectations. Any model or AI workflow that influences credit, AML investigation prioritization, fraud scoring, collections, pricing, customer eligibility, complaint routing, employee decisioning, or financial reporting must have business ownership, validation plan, testing evidence, monitoring, explainability, and production approval before scale.

The data classification policy treats customer identifiers, account records, transaction history, credit attributes, sanctions-screening evidence, SAR narratives, wealth holdings, employee records, and authentication data as restricted. Restricted data cannot be used in unapproved generative AI tools. Retrieval-augmented AI systems must preserve source citations and access controls by role.

The third-party and sourcing policy requires AI vendors to disclose hosting, subprocessor, data-retention, model-training, audit, breach-notification, and exit provisions. AI contract renewals must show adoption, value realization, control readiness, and evidence posture before expansion.

## Controls That Affect AI Use Cases

- Fraud, AML, and sanctions AI require validation evidence, false-positive monitoring, investigator override logging, and regulatory exam readiness.
- Lending and credit AI require fair-lending review, adverse-action evidence, explainability, and monitored model drift.
- Copilot and coding assistants require restricted-data prevention, repository controls, and secure SDLC telemetry.
- Wealth and advisor AI require supervision evidence, suitability controls, communication retention, and FINRA/SEC review.
- HR AI requires bias assessment, employee-data controls, and legal approval.

## Sourcing Implications

The CIO and CFO should not approve AI scaling from usage metrics alone. First Capital should tie every AI expansion to policy readiness, model-risk status, value proof, and defensible source evidence.
