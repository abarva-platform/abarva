## THE CONCEPT

Every product in this suite was previously a $2-6M consulting engagement.
Each is now a preconfigured workflow that any client can activate in hours, not months.

The pattern is consistent across all three products:
1. Client loads their data (guided, templated, confidence-scored)
2. Abarva applies proprietary pattern recognition from real prior engagements
3. Client walks through a structured workflow — not a blank prompt
4. Output is a board-ready artifact, not a chat transcript
5. Maestro anchors the engagement — the platform does the analysis

These are not features. They are standalone products that happen to share the Abarva platform.

## PRODUCT 2 — Future of Work: Autonomous Enterprise

### The Problem This Solves

Every employee in every enterprise organization spends 30-45 minutes per day on interruptible administrative work — IT tickets, HR queries, policy lookups, scheduling, compliance questions, onboarding tasks. For a 10,000-person organization, that is 75,000+ hours per week of capacity consumed by tasks that AI can resolve in seconds.

The question is not whether to automate this. The question is: what is the right architecture, what governance ensures it runs safely, and what do employees do with the recovered capacity?

This replaces a $3-5M workforce transformation advisory engagement.

### The Architecture Pattern (Built From Real Engagement Experience)

The production-proven architecture — using whatever orchestration stack the client already has:

```
AUTONOMOUS ENTERPRISE — 5-LAYER ARCHITECTURE

Layer 5 — Employee Entry Point
The front door. Where every employee starts.
Options: Existing ITSM chat (ServiceNow/Moveworks) | Microsoft Teams | Web | Mobile
Natural language. No training required.
Captures intent → routes to correct layer.

Layer 4 — AI Reasoning Engine (Claude)
The brain. Handles complex, multi-step requests.
Decomposes work across specialized agents.
Human-in-loop at approval gates.
Specialized agents: HR · IT · Finance · Facilities · Research/Mission

Layer 3 — Workflow Control Plane (ServiceNow or equivalent)
The governor. Nothing moves without an audit trail.
SLA tracking · Approvals · Compliance · Escalation
Simple requests close here. Complex requests invoke Layer 4.

Layer 2 — Enterprise Data & Knowledge Layer
The context. Where the answers live.
Existing data platforms (Palantir, Fabric, Snowflake, Databricks)
Legacy systems (Hadoop, Teradata, on-prem databases)
Systems of record accessible via governed API or export

Layer 1 — Systems of Record
Unchanged. Never touched directly by AI.
ERP · HR systems · Finance · Epic/EHR · CRM
AI reads and writes through governed middleware only.
```

## PRODUCT 3 — Analytics Modernization Intelligence (AMI)

### The Problem This Solves

Every large enterprise has an analytics estate that evolved over 15-20 years. It is a maze of DataStage jobs, SAS scripts, Tableau workbooks, stored procedures, and reporting tools — most of which no one fully understands, most of which cannot be changed without risk, and all of which represent a dependency that blocks AI adoption.

The classic approach: hire a large team, spend 6-12 months mapping the estate, produce a rationalization report, and then wait 2 years for funding to modernize.

The Abarva approach: ingest the estate, build an intelligent map of it, identify what to rationalize, what to modernize, and what to retire — in weeks, not months. Then run the modernization with confidence, not guesswork.

This is built directly from the playbook developed in a real production engagement — adapted, generalized, and made repeatable.

### Who Buys This

- CIO or CDO with legacy analytics debt
- VP Data Engineering trying to migrate from legacy to cloud
- Analytics platform team moving from on-prem to cloud-native
- Any org with: DataStage, SAS, Tableau, Informatica, SSRS, Crystal Reports, BOBJ, or similar

### What AMI Does

Ingests the analytics estate — every job, every script, every workbook, every stored procedure — and builds an intelligent map that answers:
- What does this thing do? (in plain English — no reading code required)
- What does this thing depend on? (lineage map — upstream and downstream)
- Who uses this? (usage data from logs and platform analytics)
- What would break if this changed? (impact assessment, automated)
- Is this redundant? (duplicate detection across the estate)
- Is this worth modernizing, or should it be retired?
