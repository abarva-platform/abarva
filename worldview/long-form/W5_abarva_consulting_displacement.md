## Executive Thesis

AbarVa should be explicit: it has a commercial interest in displacing a portion of traditional consulting. But the attractive target is not all consulting, and it is not human judgment. The target is the middle layer of consulting work that has become repeatable enough to encode but is still sold as if every engagement were bespoke: fact-base assembly, sourcing design, artifact production, scorecard governance, vendor comparison, commercial normalization, risk surfacing, executive memo construction, and value tracking.

That distinction matters. If AbarVa claims that AI replaces consultants, it will sound naive. If it claims that software can absorb the repeatable operating method underneath many advisory engagements, it is describing a real strategic inflection point.

McKinsey's 2025 State of AI survey reports broad AI use, but also that most organizations have not scaled AI across the enterprise and that workflow redesign is central to value capture ([S01](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai/)). Gartner forecasts $643.9 billion of GenAI spending in 2025 and notes CIO movement away from uncertain proof-of-concept work toward commercial solutions with more predictable value ([S02](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-03-31-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-genai-spending-to-reach-644-billion-in-2025)). Deloitte describes agentic AI as a move from augmenting knowledge to augmenting execution, but also points to data and production barriers ([S16](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/technology-management/tech-trends/2025/tech-trends-ai-agents-and-autonomous-ai.html)).

The implication for AbarVa is simple and uncomfortable: enterprise buyers still need help, but they should not have to rent the same advisory operating system repeatedly from different firms.

## The Strategic Inflection Point

Grove's question is not whether the old model is dead. It is whether the basis of competition has changed before the incumbents admit it. In consulting, the old basis of competition combined brand, partner judgment, client access, pyramided labor, proprietary methods, and deliverable polish. AI does not weaken all of those equally. It weakens the parts that are labor-intensive, information-heavy, repetitive, and hard for clients to inspect.

The consulting pyramid was a clever machine. Junior people gathered data, maintained trackers, drafted decks, chased source files, built comparison matrices, and converted senior judgment into presentation form. Middle layers managed coordination and quality. Partners translated uncertainty into confidence for executives. The top of that pyramid still matters. The bottom and middle are changing.

Microsoft Research analyzed 200,000 Bing Copilot conversations and found high AI applicability in knowledge work, office/admin support, computer/mathematical work, and information-heavy roles ([S12](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/working-with-ai-measuring-the-occupational-implications-of-generative-ai/)). NBER's study of 5,179 customer-support agents found a 14% average productivity increase from generative AI assistance, with larger gains for novice and low-skilled workers ([S20](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)). PwC reports a 56% wage premium for AI skills, which suggests the labor market is repricing capability rather than simply deleting jobs ([S04](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/services/ai/ai-jobs-barometer.html)).

That is the inflection point. AI does not just reduce effort. It compresses the learning curve for repeatable knowledge work and shifts value toward those who own context, workflow, evidence, and judgment.

## Why Source Is The First Beachhead

AbarVa Source is the right first displacement vector because sourcing is a patterned, high-value decision workflow. Every serious technology-services sourcing event asks similar questions:

- What is in scope?
- What baseline data exists?
- Which inputs are missing?
- What artifact is ready to release?
- What vendor claims are supported?
- Are pricing assumptions comparable?
- Has the scorecard been approved and locked?
- What commercial traps remain?
- What decision should the executive make?
- What value is projected, and who will measure it?

Traditional advisory can answer these questions. Procurement suites can record some of the downstream process. Generic AI can draft prose. None of those categories naturally owns the whole governed decision workflow.

That is AbarVa's opening. Source should become the event-side intelligence layer for complex technology-services decisions: pattern packs, stage gates, scorecard governance, artifact generation, commercial normalization, decision memos, value ledger entries, and audit trail. It should complement procurement systems of record rather than replacing them. It should partner with advisors and SIs rather than pretending implementation is easy. But it should own the decision memory.

The HFS survey is useful here because it captures a buyer mood: 65% of enterprises in the study said traditional consulting models fail to deliver real value, and 83% said AI-powered consulting delivers greater value than traditional approaches ([S08](https://www.hfsresearch.com/press-release/ai-powered-consulting-forces-reckoning-65-of-enterprises-say-traditional-models-no-longer-deliver-value-2/)). Treat that as directional, not universal. The more important signal is that buyers are increasingly willing to challenge effort-based consulting economics.

AbarVa should not answer with cheaper consultants. It should answer with a different unit of value: governed decisions.

## Partnership Is The Model, Not A Compromise

The market is already telling us that enterprise AI will be partner-mediated. OpenAI announced Frontier Alliances with BCG, McKinsey, Accenture, and Capgemini to help customers define strategy, integrate systems, redesign workflows, and scale deployment ([S09](https://openai.com/index/frontier-alliance-partners/)). Accenture and Anthropic announced a multi-year partnership with approximately 30,000 Accenture professionals to be trained on Claude and joint offerings around ROI, workflow redesign, and change management ([S10](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/accenture-and-anthropic-launch-multi-year-partnership-to-drive-enterprise-ai-innovation-and-value-across-industries)). Bain expanded its OpenAI partnership around a Center of Excellence and client AI transformation work ([S11](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2024/bain-and-company-announces-expanded-partnership-with-openai-to-accelerate-delivery-of-ai-solutions-and-meet-fast-growing-client-needs/)).

This is not noise. It is market structure.

The reason is obvious: enterprise AI requires models, data, security, process redesign, executive alignment, stakeholder change, implementation, measurement, and support. No single actor owns all of that credibly. Google Cloud's partner strategy says GSIs play a major role in helping enterprise customers plan, execute, and manage digital transformations on Google Cloud ([S22](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/partners/idc-reports-on-google-cloud-gsi-partners/)). Infosys and Anthropic describe agentic AI collaboration for regulated and complex industries ([S24](https://www.infosys.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/advanced-enterprise-ai-solutions-industries.html)).

AbarVa's partnership model should embrace this reality while protecting its own control point.

The division of labor should be clear:

- AbarVa owns the workflow, evidence, pattern memory, artifact lifecycle, scorecard governance, decision ledger, and value ledger.
- Strategy partners help with executive framing, operating model, board narrative, and transformation choices.
- Implementation partners wire data, cloud, ERP, agents, security, and process change into production.
- Specialist partners contribute legal, procurement, benchmark, regulated-domain, or category expertise.
- AI providers supply model capability and platform primitives.

This is not a peace offering to incumbents. It is a power move. AbarVa lets partners deliver value through the platform while preventing the workflow memory from disappearing into partner-owned decks and spreadsheets.

## The Thompson Lens: Aggregate Buyer Workflow Attention

Thompson's aggregation logic is useful because enterprise expertise markets are fragmented on the supply side and coordination-heavy on the demand side. The buyer does not want to manage model providers, cloud providers, SIs, niche advisors, procurement tools, security reviews, contract terms, stakeholder meetings, and executive narratives as separate chaos streams. The buyer wants a trusted interaction layer.

AbarVa can be that layer if it owns buyer workflow attention. Not attention in the consumer sense. Attention in the executive-operating sense: where the buyer goes to see what is true, what is missing, what decision is ready, what risk is unresolved, and which partner should act next.

That creates leverage. If AbarVa controls the event state and evidence model, partners plug into AbarVa's context rather than dragging clients back into each firm's private methodology. The platform reduces search costs, coordination costs, memory loss, and vendor sprawl. It also creates a demand-side data advantage: AbarVa learns which patterns recur, which claims fail, which gates matter, which interventions work, and which value assumptions prove fragile.

The aggregator does not need to perform every service. It needs to own the trusted workflow where services are selected, sequenced, and evaluated.

## The Christensen Lens: Start Where Incumbents Are Overserving

AbarVa should not begin by trying to replace top-tier CEO counsel. That is not the vulnerable flank. Incumbents are strongest where the work involves board trust, political navigation, regulatory credibility, crisis pressure, and enterprise-scale execution.

The weaker flank is the middle: recurring decisions that need rigor but not a full bespoke advisory machine. Sourcing events. AI use-case portfolio governance. Vendor rationalization. Commercial risk normalization. Program evidence reviews. Executive decision packets. These jobs are too important for casual AI prompting and too repetitive for premium labor each time.

That is a Christensen-shaped opening. AbarVa begins with workflows that are underserved by procurement suites and overserved by traditional advisory. The product may look less prestigious than strategy consulting, but it competes on a different basis: persistence, repeatability, evidence, speed, and governance. Over time, pattern intelligence improves, partners attach, and AbarVa moves upmarket into more strategic events.

Disruption here is not an explosion. It is margin migration. Work once packaged as billable analyst and manager time becomes embedded product capability.

## The Moat: Pattern Intelligence Plus Evidence Provenance

AbarVa's moat cannot be prompts. Prompts will leak. Models will improve. Large firms will build internal tools. Procurement suites will add AI features. The defensible asset is pattern intelligence plus evidence provenance.

Pattern intelligence means AbarVa knows the shape of recurring decisions: stages, required inputs, diagnostic questions, common risks, artifact templates, scorecard defaults, gate criteria, commercial traps, negotiation levers, value assumptions, and failure modes. Evidence provenance means every claim, recommendation, risk flag, and value movement can be traced to source material, owner, date, confidence, and approval state.

Anthropic's research shows business API usage is automation-heavy and context-constrained ([S18](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report?cam=claude)). Its later Economic Index work shows usage remains concentrated in a small number of tasks ([S19](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)). That supports a practical lesson: enterprise AI impact will not come from generic capability alone. It will come from putting capability into the right recurring contexts.

Consulting firms have this memory in people, practices, and reused materials. AbarVa's opportunity is to make it software-native and tenant-safe.

## Pricing And Commercial Posture

AbarVa should not price Source as a generic seat tool. Seats measure access. Source should measure decision rigor and value at stake.

The commercial logic should follow:

- event size and strategic consequence;
- required evidence depth;
- workflow rigor;
- number and complexity of vendors;
- artifact and scorecard governance burden;
- commercial normalization complexity;
- executive decision support;
- value tracking and measurement needs;
- optional partner orchestration.

This is where AbarVa must remain disciplined. Do not invent market benchmarks. Do not imply realized savings without measurement. Do not claim a vendor price is good or bad without comparable scope, baseline, assumptions, and evidence. AbarVa's pricing power should come from defensibility, acceleration, rework reduction, and risk visibility.

Outcome-linked pricing is attractive but dangerous. The steelman against it is strong: enterprise outcomes have contested causality. Vendor performance, retained-team behavior, implementation quality, business adoption, and macro conditions all affect results. AbarVa should begin with projected value, confidence levels, assumptions, evidence, measurement owner, and variance risk. It can pursue outcome-linked economics only when baseline and attribution are strong enough.

That restraint is not weakness. It is how AbarVa avoids becoming the same opaque advisory model it is challenging.

## Incumbent Response And Risk

Incumbents will copy the visible surface. Accenture reports fiscal 2025 revenue of $69.7 billion, new bookings of $80.6 billion, partner-driven work at 60% of revenue, and $5.9 billion of generative AI bookings ([S13](https://www.accenture.com/cr-en/about/company/integrated-reporting-financial)). BCG describes a broad AI collaboration ecosystem ([S15](https://www.bcg.com/capabilities/artificial-intelligence)). Bain, Infosys, TCS, Deloitte, Capgemini, and others are all positioning around AI-enabled transformation.

They can build dashboards. They can wrap internal copilots around delivery methods. They can bundle AI-enabled consulting into existing relationships. AbarVa's defense is not a prettier interface. It is counter-positioning.

Incumbents benefit when method remains private, labor remains billable, and the client rents the same operating memory repeatedly. AbarVa benefits when method becomes transparent, reusable, client-owned, and evidence-bound. If AbarVa can make clients believe that decision memory should belong to them, not the advisor, incumbents face a harder copy problem. They can imitate features, but fully imitating the posture may undermine their economics.

## The Ethical Line

AbarVa should not build its story on contempt for consultants. That is lazy and strategically wrong. Consulting can create real value. Many transformations need judgment, trust, stakeholder navigation, regulated execution, and implementation capacity. WEF's Future of Jobs work describes both job creation and displacement by 2030, and its workforce strategy chapter shows upskilling and automation moving together ([S06](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/2-jobs-outlook/), [S07](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/4-workforce-strategies/)).

The ethical line is not anti-consultant. It is anti-waste. Anti-opaque claims. Anti-unmeasured value. Anti-decision memory loss. Anti-billing models that reward effort without defensible progress.

AbarVa earns legitimacy if it helps clients and partners move work up the value stack: less manual artifact assembly, more validation; less slide production, more tradeoff clarity; less private methodology, more client-owned evidence; less speculative ROI, more measured value confidence.

## Strategic Close

The desired end state is not AbarVa as a cheaper consultancy. It is AbarVa as a partner-mediated decision intelligence platform.

Clients use AbarVa to run high-value decisions with evidence, gates, artifacts, agents, value logic, and memory. Partners plug in to provide strategy, implementation, benchmarks, change management, and domain expertise. AbarVa captures the workflow data, pattern improvements, and commercial control point.

The line is the business:

AbarVa should displace consulting where consulting is repeatable method sold as bespoke labor. It should partner where consulting is judgment, trust, regulated execution, and change capacity.
