## Core Thesis

Software and consulting are not simply being destroyed by AI. They are being restructured around executable expertise. The old economic grammar was seats, billable hours, implementation phases, and expert judgment packaged as decks or custom code. The new grammar is workflow control, evidence, evaluation, agentic execution, governance, and outcomes.

The profit pool shifts toward platforms and services that can turn expertise into governed action. Generic leverage - junior research, boilerplate delivery, first-pass coding, template PMO work, documentation, status synthesis, and low-context support - is exposed. High-context work - architecture, trust, regulated change, proprietary data, executive judgment, and operating accountability - remains valuable and may become more valuable.

## Key Evidence Extracted

- Gartner's April 2026 forecast puts worldwide IT spending at $6.31655 trillion in 2026, software at $1.443621 trillion, and IT services at $1.870197 trillion. This supports a restructuring thesis rather than a demand-collapse thesis.
- IDC's April 2026 infrastructure forecast points to AI infrastructure spending of $487 billion in 2026, making compute and memory a new operating constraint for software and services buyers.
- McKinsey's 2025 survey shows broad AI use but limited enterprise-scale capture: 88% report AI use in at least one function, about one-third are scaling enterprise-wide, and 23% are scaling agents somewhere in the enterprise.
- Developer productivity evidence is mixed. GitHub Copilot experimental evidence shows large speed gains in bounded tasks, while METR's 2025 RCT found experienced open-source developers 19% slower in mature repositories.
- Stack Overflow's 2025 survey shows high AI exposure but practical skepticism: 66% report frustration with AI answers that are almost right and 45.2% report more time debugging AI-generated code.
- NBER's support-agent study shows AI can raise productivity in repeatable knowledge work, especially for less experienced workers, suggesting AI compresses apprenticeship and codifies high-performer behavior.
- BLS projections complicate simple displacement claims: software developers/QA/testers and management analysts are projected to grow, while computer programmers are projected to decline.
- Services incumbents are publicly moving toward AI-led transformation, AI-aware workforces, agent repositories, managed services, and AI bookings. This validates urgency but does not prove the legacy pyramid is safe.
- SaaS leaders are repositioning around agents, copilots, data clouds, and workflow platforms. This implies the seat model will be joined by usage, agent, and outcome pricing pressure.
- NIST, OWASP, and the EU AI Act make clear that agentic software brings risk, compliance, and auditability needs. Governance becomes a product and services revenue pool.

## Five Steelmanned Counterarguments

1. Demand growth protects the incumbents. Gartner forecasts rising software and IT services spending; BLS projects growth in software and management analyst jobs. If budgets grow, incumbents may adapt without major pain.
2. Enterprise complexity is a moat. Legacy systems, politics, procurement, change management, cybersecurity, and regulated accountability make trusted human intermediaries hard to replace.
3. AI productivity is not reliably proven. METR's RCT and Stack Overflow survey friction show that AI can add review, debugging, and context-recovery costs, especially in mature systems.
4. Incumbents can absorb the disruption. Accenture, Capgemini, Infosys, TCS, Microsoft, Salesforce, ServiceNow, and SAP are already embedding AI in platforms, delivery, and training.
5. Regulation slows autonomous substitution. NIST, OWASP, and the EU AI Act raise the cost of replacing human judgment in high-risk workflows and create demand for governance services.

## Synthesis Against Counterarguments

The counterarguments are strong, but they mostly defend differentiated, high-context, high-trust work. They do not defend generic labor leverage, generic SaaS seats, generic implementation documents, or low-context support. The restructuring thesis therefore survives in a precise form: total demand can rise while the monetization basis changes. The industry grows, but its old grammar stops matching the work.
