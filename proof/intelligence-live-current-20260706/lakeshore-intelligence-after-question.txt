How should the CIO prioritize AI and automation across HR, finance, treasury, legal, and shared services?
aVa

Lakeshore Holdings has enough loaded evidence to give the CIO a clear prioritization posture — and it points firmly toward a sequenced proof model rather than a broad parallel launch.

Treasury and Finance are the only Phase 1 bets worth funding now. The Kyriba global cash and payments rollout has $18.9M in measured value (enterprise evidence), 85,000 payments/month, and active control gaps — bank connectivity uncertified, payment-format defect unresolved, SOX signer evidence incomplete. That is not a reason to slow down; it is the exact proof sequence the CIO needs to run. The finance close and reporting layer (SAP/BlackLine/Hyperion substrate) is the natural companion: M365 Copilot finance shows $86M committed against $2.86M realized, which means scaling before the semantic layer is certified compounds the gap rather than closing it.

ServiceNow finance support is a shape-next candidate — 940 monthly users, $3.08M measured, source-backed evidence status. It earns Phase 2 investment once ticket-volume baselines and repeat-contact drivers are confirmed.

HR and Legal belong in discovery, not scale. The enterprise record has a 16-day contract review cycle-time baseline (Legal Ops owner) and Workday HCM in the system inventory, but process volumes, CLM/eBilling matter data, and policy taxonomies are not loaded. Recommending scale in either function without that evidence would be fabricated precision.

The CIO's governing principle here: prove Finance-attested outcomes in Treasury/Finance first, reuse the governance and context assets in ServiceNow next, and fund HR/Legal discovery in parallel — not as pilots, but as evidence-loading exercises that earn a scale decision later.
